## 0.0.313 · 2026-11-06 · Alter Ego (C64) · air with Claude

- **A key read straight after an interrupt belongs to the main program.**
  The lockstep tells the interrupt's reads from the main program's by
  the stack: an interrupt is over once the stack pointer is back above
  its frame. It checked that only as each instruction ran. When the
  timer came as a `JSR` to GETIN ended, the KERNAL's handler ran first
  and left by its exit, a hook on the machine, and GETIN's own hook
  read the keyboard's buffer with no instruction between. The lockstep
  still counted the interrupt as running, gave the read to it, and the
  port never saw the key: one session of 15 lost a RUN/STOP in a scene,
  at frame 26,538 of 48,591. The lockstep now checks the stack at every
  read and every stop as well (`kit/c64/lockstep.js`), and
  `kit/c64/test_lockstep_kernal_exit.js` makes the timer meet that `JSR`
  83 times in 20,000 passes.
