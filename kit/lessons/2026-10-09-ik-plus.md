## next · 9 October 2026 · IK+ · air with Claude

**Draw a small graphics table before naming it.** IK+ stamps five-byte
shapes from a table of 30 bytes onto a fighter who has been knocked
down. The annotation named them knock-down marks from the routine that
copies them, and coverage counted the table as done. Read as pixels,
they are six digits, 1, 2, 4, 5, 8 and 0: the game paints the points of
the hit on the fallen fighter. Once they were digits, what the game
paints could be compared with what it scores, and a live test of each
attack showed two of them paint half the points they add. A name taken
from the code that moves bytes says what they are for, not what they
show: when a routine copies bytes into graphics, draw them before the
comment names them, however small the table.
