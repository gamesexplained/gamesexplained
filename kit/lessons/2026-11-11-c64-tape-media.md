## next · 2026-11-11 · Shadowfire (C64) · air with Claude

- **Every C64 tape starts in the KERNAL's format, and that part reads the
  same on all of them.** The C64's media reader refused a tape, so none
  of the ten tape games had a `media.json`. `kit/c64/tape.py` now reads a
  TAP as pulses, and `kit/c16/media.py` reads the KERNAL's format with
  the same code and its own machine's pulse lengths. On the ten tapes
  every KERNAL block's checksum came out right. Squirm's one file (14,151
  bytes) and Aztec Challenge's two (613 and 28,671) are in their games'
  listings byte for byte, and those two tapes hold nothing else.
- **The other eight go on in a turbo format, and hardly two share one.**
  Each is a bit a pulse in two lengths: 280 and 528 cycles on
  Shadowfire, 296 and 576 on Kane, 216 and 312 on Gribbly's Day Out, 384
  and 576 on Into the Eagle's Nest, 272 and 488 on Aaargh!, 376 and 736
  on The Sentinel, 496 and 952 on Rick Dangerous, 384 and 728 on North &
  South. Only one pair looks to share a loader: the boot files of Rick
  Dangerous and North & South are both 156 bytes at `$029F`, and 132 of
  the bytes are equal. So a reader for the eight is a table of formats
  that grows a loader at a time, and until a format is in it the
  stretch is listed as an item with its two pulse lengths, to be
  answered by hand.
- **Run a scrambled loader's own code to read it.** Shadowfire's boot
  program is written in undocumented opcodes and its turbo loader is
  stored EORed in the header. Disassembling the tape's bytes gives
  nothing. Running the boot program's 101 bytes in `kit/cpu/cpu6502.js`
  until it jumped left the loader in the clear at `$0351`: CIA 2's timer
  A loaded with `$0180`, bits lowest first, `$E3` hunted a bit at a
  time, `$ED`, two addresses, the bytes, ten blocks. That is the first
  row of `TURBO`.
- **With no checksum, the listing is the proof, and a picture is a quick
  one.** This format has none. Of the ten blocks, three are in the
  listing byte for byte (36,863 bytes, 19 bytes, and the 8,180 of 8,184
  that the listing holds). Three more are the loading picture, and
  drawing them gave the screenshot the run had saved from the web.
- **A block the scan leaves open may still be the part.** Block 9 has
  14,079 bytes and the listing holds 8,175 of them, all equal. The rest
  are screen areas the game draws as it runs, which the listing leaves
  out, so the scan's 90 % was never reached. Before answering such an
  item by hand, count the listed bytes that differ: none did.
- **A tape holds what no snapshot does.** Shadowfire's loading picture
  is on the tape and in no listing, because the game loads over it. So
  are 3,072 bytes below its bitmap that nothing uses. The ledger lists
  both; the picture is a candidate for the Graphics tab.
- **A loader can rewrite itself as it loads.** Kane's loader reads
  chunks, and some land on its own code: one changes how many pulses
  make a byte (the operand at `$034B`, 9 to 10 and back), one the
  header's length (`$03A4`), one turns the `ROL` at `$0347` into a
  `ROR`, so later bytes come lowest bit first. No table row describes
  that. Reading it needs the loader's own code run against the pulses,
  which the kit does not do; Kane's turbo part stays one stretch.
