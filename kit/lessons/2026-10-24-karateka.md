## 0.0.230 · 2026-10-24 · Karateka (Apple II) · air with Claude

- **Take a mechanic's figures for the copy from the tested port, not
  from the tables read by hand.** A routine that indexes a table with a
  value from a range reads every byte the range reaches, and the last of
  them may not belong to the table at all. Karateka's guards decide
  whether to step in from a table at `$BFF2 + x`, with `x` from 7 to 11,
  which reads `$BFF9`-`$BFFD`: a byte of `$80`, then the stage's own
  entry jump (`4C 00 B7`) and the stage's number. Read by hand, the table
  looked like five chances, and the page said a guard ten columns away
  steps in 72 times in 256. The port, run against the game's own code on
  the kit's 6502 over every random number, gave 183, the jump's high
  byte. The two had to agree before the number went on the page, and
  only the run said which bytes the routine reads.
