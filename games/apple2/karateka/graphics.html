1984 · Brøderbund Software · Apple II

Karateka

The Apple II has no sprites. Every fighter in Karateka is three pictures, his legs, his torso and his head, put together on the hidden hi-res page each frame by the engine's draw_figure. On this page they move by the game's own routines, from the listings, on the kit's 6502.

01 · Everything that moves

Every fighter moves through the same poses, by the same routines

The game has no timer: a frame lasts as long as its drawing takes, about eight a second on the cliff with the hero standing. Here every figure takes a frame eight times a second.

loading the listings

The hero, the guards and Akuma are each the working figure ($20-$2F) given a command (command, $6540), moved a frame along its sequences (animate, $6400) and drawn (draw_figure, $6811), as a stage's main loop does, over the engine's listing with the stage's laid over it: the cliff's for the hero, the stage's for a guard, the last stage's for Akuma, whose figure its own routine draws ($BC00). The commands are the ones the keys and the guards' brain send: the bow, the stance, a step, a kick or a punch at a height, a fall. Mariko walks by the engine's $7E33, over the title's pictures. The hawk's six pictures are drawn by the stage's shape routine ($1903), at the rows the palace's hawk_draw gives them ($BD57). The page is cleared with $80 each frame, black, as the stage's $1900 clears it.

A fighter's moves are two lists of poses, one for the legs (legs_seq, $6300) and one for the torso (torso_seq, $63BE), stepped a pose a frame, forwards or backwards. A command only chooses where in the lists to start and which way to step: the bow, the stance and standing up are the same few poses run forwards and back, and a step back is the step forward run backwards. Each entry of the legs' list also says how many columns the figure moves on that frame (legs_step, $6360), so the feet carry the figure along. Codes in the lists start the blows: at a kick's code the legs and torso take the kick's pose for the height asked for, and at a punch's the torso takes the punch's.

The guards are the hero drawn the other way round, each byte of every picture turned round through a table of 256 reversed bytes (bit_reverse, $0980), with headgear in place of his head. Akuma moves by the same lists, but the last stage draws him from pictures of its own: his robe and legs, his body and arms and his head, for every pose he takes. He never stands or bows, and the pictures for those poses point at the start of the engine's.

The hero is orange and white and the guards blue and white, from the same pictures. A dot lit on its own shows blue or orange by whether it falls on an even or an odd dot across the screen, so a fighter's colours come from where he stands: his byte column and the scene's dot offset ($50) added together. A column further along, the hero would be blue and a guard orange. The standing pictures are drawn a dot out of step with the stance's, so the column a fighter moves as he takes the stance keeps his colours. Before a fall the game moves a fighter back a column when the sum is the wrong way for the falling pictures: odd is right for the hero, even for anyone else ($6717).

02 · The pictures

Legs, torsos and heads, and the pictures only one figure uses

Every picture a figure is built from, each drawn alone by the stage's shape routine. Pick one to see what the listing says it is.

Pick a picture.

Each picture is two bytes, its height in rows and its width in bytes, then its rows, top first, each byte seven dots and the colour bit, already as the screen holds them. The legs, the torsos, the hero's head and headgear types 0 and 6 are the engine's ($8000-$9FFF), with the masks and the stars; headgear types 1 to 5 are each stage's own at $BE00; Mariko's are each load's own, from $1530, one set in the title and the cliff, another in the palace and the dungeon, and a third in the last stage; the hawk's are the palace's; Akuma's for the fight are the last stage's, and those for his chamber the engine's. A picture that serves several poses is shown once. Each is drawn at byte column 1, where most of the hero's come out orange; a column along, their lone dots would turn blue. Masks are drawn here as they are stored, their set dots lit: the game ANDs them on, so where a mask is clear it cuts a dark hole for the picture after it.

The second half of the long step forward has no pictures of its own: legs poses $1C-$20 are poses $1B-$17 drawn mirrored, the other foot forward (legs_mirror, $62BE). Every legs pose also has a mask, a picture of mostly set dots with the feet left clear, drawn with AND under a fighter's feet so the floor's pattern does not show through him (feet_mask_lo, $60A8).

03 · How a fighter is drawn

Masks first, then the legs, the head and the torso

One fighter's part of a frame, as a stage's loop draws it: the band he stands in cleared, the mask under his feet, then draw_figure, stopped at each picture.

The stage's three calls for one fighter ($6780 or $678C, $6786 or $6789, then $6783) run on the kit's 6502 for the pose shown, stopped at each call to the stage's shape routines ($1903, $1906, $1909 and $190C), with the bytes each step changed in yellow. The drawing mode is $0F: 0 ORs the picture onto the screen, $FF ANDs it, which is how a mask cuts.

For the stance's arms, the punches and the guards' poses draw_figure draws a mask first, and a smaller one for standing and bowing ($69DC). Then the legs, the head's mask and the head or the headgear, and the torso last, on top. Every picture is ORed onto the screen, so the black around a figure never hides what is behind it; only the masks clear anything. Before a fighter is drawn again, the band he stood in two frames before is cleared to black and the floor's pattern put back (erase_strip, $6797), and how the whole frame is drawn on the hidden page and shown is on How it works.
