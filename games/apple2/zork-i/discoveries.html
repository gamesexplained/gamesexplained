1980 · Infocom

Zork I

What the code does that the game never tells the player. A line of sixty commas leaves the game reading one letter of every line, until the disk is booted again.

01 · The parse buffer

Bug: sixty commas leave the game reading one letter of every line

A comma is a word, and the parse buffer has room for 59 words. The 60th writes over the text buffer's size, and from then on the interpreter keeps only the first letter of whatever the player types.

The interpreter's own copy and split ($1D69, $130B), ported to the page and held byte for byte to the code in the kit's 6502 simulator for lines of 0 to 110 commas. The buffers are the story's (story $2396 and $2486, memory $4F96 and $5086), shown as the first line leaves them; the second line is split from that memory, as if the story changed none of it in between. Live, sixty commas and then open mailbox gave I DON'T KNOW THE WORD 'O'.

The story tells the interpreter how many words it may write, in byte 0 of the parse buffer: 120. The buffer is 240 bytes long, though, and each word takes four after a two-byte head, so only 59 fit. The 60th word's length and position go into the two bytes after the buffer, which are the first two of the text buffer: its size, 100, becomes 1, the length of a comma.

Every later line is copied into the text buffer up to its size, so the game sees one letter. open mailbox arrives as o, and the story answers I DON'T KNOW THE WORD 'O'. Some one-letter words still work: n walks north, and q quits. restart, restore, and save arrive as r and s, and s means south, so the only way back to a working game is to quit and boot the disk again (live).

At 63 words the interpreter's index into the buffer goes past 255 and wraps to 0 ($12EB). The 64th word's length lands in byte 0, the limit, and its position in byte 1, the count of words, so the count can never equal the limit and the split never stops early again. With 63 commas the count comes out at 64, and the story reads a 64th word made of commas and memory: it prints a screenful of the game's memory as I CAN'T USE THE WORD '...' HERE. (live).

02 · Fighting

Bug: a knockout always ends in death

A villain who knocks the player out is meant to get one to three free rounds before the player comes round. The count of those rounds never goes down, so the villain goes on swinging, in the same turn, until a blow kills.

routine DO-FIGHT, story $E16E: L03 the last blow's result, L06 the rounds left
$E1A8  call      VILLAIN-BLOW L05 L06 -> L03   a villain strikes
$E1AF  jz        L03 ?~$0E1B8                   the player is dead?
$E1B2  store     [L03] #00                      then the round is over
$E1B5  jump      $0E1C8
$E1B8  je        L03 #02 ?~$0E17E               knocked out?
$E1BD  random    #03 -> sp
$E1C1  add       #01 sp -> L06                  then 2 to 4: one to three more rounds
$E1C5  jump      $0E17E                         the next villain
$E1C8  jz        L03 ?rtrue                     dead: stop
$E1CB  jz        L06 ?rtrue                     never knocked out: stop
$E1CE  dec       L06                            lowers local 2, 3 or 4, and never L06
$E1D0  jz        L06 ?~$0E17B                   rounds left? then fight again
$E1D4  rtrue

Story $E16E, as the story's listing shows it. Live, an unarmed player waited in the Troll Room in 40 games. The troll knocked the player out 28 times, and each time, in the same turn, he hesitated up to five times and then Conquering his fears, the troll puts you to death.

The Z-machine's dec takes the number of a variable, and the listing writes [L06] for a variable named directly. Written without the brackets, as here, the operand is read first, and its value is the number of the variable to lower. After a knockout L06 holds 2, 3, or 4, so the instruction lowers L02, L03, or L04, counters that the next round sets afresh. L06 keeps its value, and the round starts again (story $E17B).

Against a player who is out cold, every blow is a hesitation or a killing blow (VILLAIN-BLOW, story $E35D), so the rounds can end only in death. Infocom's later source lowers the count itself, so there a knockout costs the player one to three rounds (what differs from the source).

03 · The Timber Room

Bug: carrying the sack into the Timber Room crashes the game

The Timber Room checks what the player carries before every command, because the passage beyond it is too narrow for anything big. A container stops the check: it calls itself to look inside, starts again from the first thing the player holds, finds the same container, and calls itself again, until the Z-machine's stack is full.

routine NO-OBJS, story $CC4C: L01 why the room was called, L03 the thing looked at
$CC57  get_child    WINNER -> L03 ?$0CC5B     the first thing the player carries
$CC5B  store        [EMPTY-HANDED] #01
$CC5E  jz           L03 ?~$0CC64              nothing more?
$CC61  jump         $0CC8A
$CC64  test_attr    L03 #0E ?~$0CC75          a container?
$CC68  call         NO-OBJS L01 L03 -> sp     then look inside it ... from the first thing again
$CC6F  jz           EMPTY-HANDED ?~$0CC83
$CC72  jump         $0CC8A
$CC75  get_prop     L03 #09 -> sp             its size
$CC79  jg           sp #05 ?~$0CC83           bigger than 5?
$CC7D  store        [EMPTY-HANDED] #00        then the passage is shut to the player
$CC80  jump         $0CC8A
$CC83  get_sibling  L03 -> L03 ?$0CC87        the next thing carried
$CC87  jump         $0CC5E

Story $CC4C, the routine of the Timber Room and the Drafty Room, as the story's listing shows it. Live, with the brown sack the first thing carried, look in the Timber Room stopped the game with the call at story $CC68 1,025 deep. With the sack left at Ladder Bottom, look worked.

The routine passes the container to itself as its second argument and never reads it: every call starts again from the player's first possession (get_child WINNER). The check passes over small things and stops at the first thing bigger than size 5, so the game crashes only when a container comes before anything big. The lamp, size 15, is big enough to stop it, and the sack is size 3. The thing taken last comes first, so walking in with the sack taken after the lamp crashes the game at the first command, and walking in with the lamp taken after the sack does not. The Drafty Room shares the routine, and the bug.

On the Apple II each call takes room on the Z-machine's stack, which holds 178 words ($1708), and the push that does not fit is the interpreter's one fatal error: a BRK into the Monitor, with no message (the next section).

04 · Errors

Bug: a fatal error drops into the Monitor without its message

The interpreter holds the text ZORK INTERNAL ERROR!, but nothing prints it. Every fatal error is a BRK, which stops the 6502 and leaves the player in the Apple II's Monitor.

From the listing: the text at $21D3, which nothing refers to, and the one fatal error at $21E7, with the places that call it.

The interpreter calls $21E7 for a story whose header says it needs something this interpreter lacks (bit 0 of header byte 1), an opcode it does not know, a je with one operand, a property too long for get_prop or put_prop, and a Z-machine stack that runs over or under. Zork I's own story reaches one of them: a container carried into the Timber Room overflows the stack (the section above). Eight places call the error, and each reaches the same single BRK, while the message that would explain it fills the twenty bytes just before it.

05 · The troll

Bug: disarming the troll breaks every "narrow" in the game

When the troll has lost his axe and does not pick it up again, the story changes his description to a cowering troll. The instruction it uses writes to a table instead of to the troll, and the address it works out is inside the text of the word "narrow", which the whole game shares.

TROLL-FCN, story $8CDA: the troll without his axe, in the player's room
$8D3B  storew   #99 #0B s_a_troll_is_here     word 11 of a table at $0099: story $00AF
$8D41  print    "The troll, disarmed, cowers in terror, pleading for his life in the guttural tongue of the trolls."

abbreviation 26, story $00AC
before  4C D7 5E 9C 80 A5    "narrow "
after   4C D7 5E 8E 7F A5    "narroizx'.1 "     $8E7F: the address of "A troll is here.", halved

Story $8D3B and the text of abbreviation 26 (story $00AC), as the story's listing shows them; the second row is the text decoded after the store. Live, once the troll had cowered, the cellar was a dark and damp cellar with a narroizx'.1 passageway leading north.

storew writes a word into a table, given the table's address, an index, and the value. The story gives it the troll's object number, 153 ($99), as the address, so the word lands at 153 + 2 × 11, story $00AF. put_prop with the same operands would have set the troll's property 11, his description, as the routine does everywhere else; Infocom's later source has PUTP here.

Story $00AF is the fourth byte of the text that abbreviation 26 stands for, narrow , and every string in the game that holds the word holds it as that abbreviation. The two bytes change the word's last letters and clear the bit that ends its text, which then runs on into the next abbreviation's bytes. From then on every "narrow" prints as "narroizx'.1", from the path north of the house to the cellar.

06 · Shaking

Bug: shaking the open sack freezes the game

Shaking an open container is meant to tip out what is in it. The loop that empties it puts each thing back into the same container, so there is always a first thing left to move, and the game never answers.

V-SHAKE, story $7D7C: something open with something in it
$7DDF  test_attr    PRSO #0D ?~rfalse     open?
$7DE3  get_child    PRSO -> sp ?~rfalse   anything in it?
$7DE7  get_child    PRSO -> L01 ?~$07DF1  the first thing in it
$7DEB  insert_obj   L01 PRSO              moved ... into the same container
$7DEE  jump         $07DE7
$7DF1  print_ret    "All of the objects spill onto the floor."

Story $7D7C, as the story's listing shows it. Live, from the kitchen: open sack, then shake sack. The game printed nothing more and took no more commands.

insert_obj moves an object into another and makes it the first thing there, so moving the first thing into its own container leaves it first, and the loop runs on. Infocom's later source empties the container into the room, with a routine of its own, SHAKE-LOOP. A closed sack only rattles: It sounds like there is something inside the brown sack. The same loop catches anything open that can be taken and holds something, unless its own routine answers shake first, as the bottle's does.

07 · The trophy case

Bug: a treasure inside a container in the trophy case scores nothing

The routine that adds up the trophy case looks inside every container it finds, adds up what is in it, and throws the sum away. A treasure left inside the brown sack in the case earns no points.

routine OTVAL-FROB, story $898C: L01 = 183 (the trophy case), L02, L03
$8993  get_child    L01 -> L02 ?$08997    the first thing in the case
$8997  jz           L02 ?~$0899C          nothing more?
$899A  ret          L03                   then the total
$899C  get_prop     L02 #0C -> sp         its property 12
$89A0  add          L03 sp -> L03         added to the total
$89A4  get_child    L02 -> sp ?~$089AE    does it hold anything?
$89A8  call         OTVAL-FROB L02 -> sp  add up what is inside it ... onto the stack
$89AE  get_sibling  L02 -> L02 ?$089B2    the next thing in the case
$89B2  jump         $08997

Story $898C, as the story's listing shows it. Live, from the living room with the case open: the egg put straight into the case raised the score from 15 to 20; the egg put into the brown sack, and the sack into the case, left it at 15.

The case's total is worked out again after every take in the living room and every put into the case: the points scored elsewhere (global 129) plus whatever this routine returns (story $8814). For a container in the case it calls itself, which is right, but the result goes onto the Z-machine's stack and stays there: the next instruction moves on to the container's sibling. The routine keeps its total in L03, and only the treasures lying in the case itself are ever added to it.

The sack has no property 12, and the story's default for it is 0, so a sack full of treasures is worth nothing in the case. Taking the treasures out of the sack and putting them in the case one by one scores them. The points for finding them are paid either way, the first time each is taken (where the 350 points come from).

08 · The bottle

Bug: throwing the lamp at the bottle breaks the lamp instead

The bottle's routine runs whenever the bottle is in a sentence, and for throw it takes away the thing thrown without asking what it was. Thrown at the bottle, the lamp is taken away, the game says the bottle shatters, and the bottle stays on the table.

BOTTLE-FUNCTION, story $AB1E
$AB21  je           PRSA #60 ?~$0AB49     throw?
$AB25  remove_obj   PRSO                  the thing thrown, whatever it is
$AB27  store        [L01] #01
$AB2A  print        "The bottle hits the far wall and shatters."
$AB45  new_line
$AB46  jump         $0AB7E
  ...
$AB7E  jz           L01 ?rfalse
$AB81  jin          WATER PRSO ?~rfalse   was there water in it? if not, the throw goes on

Story $AB1E, as the story's listing shows it. Live, in the kitchen with the lamp: throw lamp at bottle gave The bottle hits the far wall and shatters. and The lamp has smashed into the floor, and the light has gone out. The kitchen then held a broken brass lantern, and the bottle, with its water, on the table.

When the routine has done its part and found no water to spill, it returns false, so the game goes on to the lamp's own routine, which smashes it. The player has lost the lamp. Thrown at something else, the bottle shatters as it should, and an empty one goes on to You're not carrying the glass bottle. Infocom's later source runs the shattering only when the bottle is the thing thrown.

09 · The skeleton

Bug: looking at the skeleton brings down the ghost's curse

Disturbing the dead adventurer in the maze angers his ghost, who sends the player's treasures to the Land of the Living Dead. The skeleton's routine never asks what the player did, so examine skeleton brings the curse too.

SKELETON, story $947A, whole
$947B  print      "A ghost appears in the room and is appalled at your having desecrated the remains of a fellow adventurer. ..."
$950E  new_line
$950F  call       ROB HERE #D6 #64 -> sp   every treasure in the room, to object 214
$9517  call       ROB #FC #D6 -> sp        and every treasure the player carries
$951E  rtrue

Story $947A, as the story's listing shows it. Object 214 is the Land of the Living Dead, and 252 ($FC) the player. Live, in the maze room with the skeleton: examine skeleton brought the ghost, and the bag of coins that lay there was gone.

An object's routine is asked first about any sentence that names it, and it chooses which verbs to answer. The skeleton's answers them all, examine among them. Infocom's later source lets only nine verbs through: take, rub, move, push, raise, lower, attack, kick, and kiss.

10 · The Loud Room

Secret: in the Loud Room, even quit only echoes

While the Loud Room is loud, it reads the player's lines itself and answers each with its last word, twice. Only a way out, bug, and echo do anything, so the player cannot save, restore, or quit there.

>quit
quit quit ...
>save
save save ...
>hello there
there there ...
>bug
That's only your opinion.
>w
Round Room

Live, from the Loud Room with the reservoir full, as it is at the start (LOUD-ROOM-FCN, story $B248).

The room drops the rest of the player's line on arrival and reads lines with a prompt of its own until one leaves. w, e, and u leave, for the Round Room, the Damp Cave, and the Deep Canyon, and the new room is only named, not described. echo is the puzzle's answer: The acoustics of the room change subtly., and the room is quiet from then on, its platinum bar free to take. Infocom's later source lets save, restore, and quit through as well.

11 · The interpreter's arithmetic

Bug: the interpreter divides wrongly by a negative number

Before a division the interpreter makes both numbers positive, and when the second is negative it negates the first one instead. 7 divided by -2 comes out as 0.

This is the interpreter's own code, run on the page in the kit's 6502 simulator on the listing's bytes: the numbers go where the interpreter leaves an instruction's operands ($82-$85), and the run starts at the handler ($111D, $114F or $10E8) and stops where it stores the result ($0B55). Run the same way in the emulator, 17 sums gave the same answers as the page, and a test holds the page to them.

The Z-machine works in 16-bit signed numbers, and its division rounds towards zero. The interpreter divides without signs ($15C1), so it first counts the negative operands and makes them positive ($160F). The mistake is in the second step: for a negative divisor it negates the dividend instead ($1633). 7 ÷ -2 becomes -7 ÷ -2, which is 65,529 ÷ 65,534 without signs, which is 0. With both negative, the dividend is negated twice and the divisor not at all, so -7 ÷ -2 is 0 too.

A multiplication comes out right anyway, because a 16-bit product wraps the same way whatever the signs. mod never puts the sign back ($114F), and its remainder loses its top bit in the divide routine, so 7 mod -3 is 32,761, which is $FFF9 without bit 15. Dividing by zero gives -1, with no error.

Zork I never notices. The story's six divisions divide by 2, 4, 5 or 70, and its two mods take a counter that only goes up.

12 · The player

Secret: the player's object is called the cretin

The player is object 252, and its short name is cretin. The word that names it is adventurer; the word cretin belongs to another object, you.

Objects 252 and 75 as the object table in the listing holds them. Live: kill cretin asks WHAT DO YOU WANT TO KILL THE CRETIN WITH?, and hello cretin answers THE YOU BOWS HIS HEAD TO YOU IN GREETING.

Global 38 holds the player's object in play, 252. Its property 18, the nouns that name it, holds one word, advent, the first six letters of adventurer. The words a player is likelier to use for themselves (me, myself, self, and cretin) name object 75, whose short name is you. It is one of the objects inside object 23, with the walls, the air, and the pair of hands. Saying hello to it prints its short name where the player's name would go (hello).

xyzzy and plugh, words from the older Colossal Cave Adventure, both get A HOLLOW VOICE SAYS 'CRETIN'.

13 · Greetings

Secret: every tenth hello to the sailor gets a different answer

Zork counts every hello sailor. Most get Nothing happens here.; the tenth, the twentieth and every tenth after get something else.


    
The story's rule (story $766E): global 81 counts, and its remainders after dividing by 20 and by 10 choose the answer. Live, twenty in a row from West of House: the 10th and the 20th gave the two answers here, and the other eighteen NOTHING HAPPENS HERE.

  

  
The sailor is object 22, another of the objects inside object 23 that are with the player everywhere, and the routine for hello gives the sailor a branch of its own. hello on its own gets a greeting from a table (HELLO., live). An object with attribute 20, as the player and object 75 have, gets The ... bows his head to you in greeting. Anything else gets I think that only schizophrenics say 'Hello' to a ..., the mailbox included (live).
