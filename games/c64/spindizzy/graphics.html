1986 · Electric Dreams Software

Spindizzy

Everything that moves in Spindizzy is a sprite the game builds again as it moves: Gerald, the inhabitants, the jewels and the lifts. Before the video chip shows one, the game cuts away whatever scenery stands in front of it.

01 · Everything that moves

Every shape, built by the game's own code

Gerald and the two kinds of inhabitant, the jewel, and a lift for each of the twelve switch numbers. The game builds their sprites again on every other pass of its main loop, about 30 times a second at the slow speed. The I button swaps Gerald's shape, as the I key does in play.

The shapes are drawn by the game's own code in the built site.

Each tile is a machine of its own: the game's routines on the kit's 6502 in the page, over the listing's bytes, with nothing in front to hide what they draw. Gerald and the inhabitants are drawn by project and draw_object ($A835, $A535) as the main loop draws Gerald. The jewel and the lifts are built into the piece table by the game's own piece builder from a room record of three bytes each and run by run_pieces ($A0D3). The I button runs change_shape ($7223) on every tile's memory. The sprites take the colours the game gives them: purple and yellow for every sprite ($D025 and $D026, set by init_and_title), and the sprite's own colour.

Gerald is two sprites laid one over the other: a multicolour one for his body in purple, yellow and his own cyan, and a hires one in black for his outline, which the video chip draws in front. Their shapes come from a set of 384 bytes, four frames of 48 bytes for each sprite, 16 rows of 3 bytes. The game keeps three sets. The one Gerald uses is at $C800, and change_shape rotates all three through it when I is pressed: the top, then the ball, then the disc on an axle, then the top again.

The inhabitants are drawn by the same routine from the two sets Gerald is not using, kind 0 from $7800 and kind 1 from $7980, in light red. So an inhabitant never has Gerald's shape, and pressing I changes theirs along with his.

Which of the four frames draw_object takes depends on where Gerald is across the screen: bits 5 and 6 of his screen column, which is his X minus his Y. The frame moves on at every 32 of the column, and his sprites step 8 pixels across at every 128, so he turns through all four frames between one step and the next. He spins only while he moves across the screen. Standing still, or rolling straight down it with X and Y rising together, he holds one frame.

A jewel is one multicolour sprite, built once from the 48 bytes of jewel_picture ($C180) when the room is entered. After that jewel_piece only adds 1 to its colour on each run of the pieces, so it flashes through all sixteen colours about twice a second. A lift is two multicolour sprites in black, purple and yellow, each made from a piece of 32 bytes in symbol_pieces ($C000-$C17F). The two pieces are chosen from the bits of the lift's switch number in the same way draw_switch_symbol chooses them for the box in the panel, so a lift carries the symbol of the pad that works it. A lift with no switch carries the symbol for 0, which the box never shows: for switch 0 it draws nothing.

02 · Behind the scenery

A sprite is cut to fit behind what stands in front of it

Gerald rolls past a block in room (53, 61), seen in the F7 view. The video chip would draw him over the block. The game cuts him first.

Room (53, 61), built by the game's routines on the kit's 6502 as the Map tab builds it, with Gerald moved to and fro along a line one cell from its front edge. On every other pass run_pieces runs the room's two lifts, then project and draw_object draw Gerald, then sort_sprites orders the sprites, as the main loop does. The mask is $C300-$C37F as build_sprite_mask leaves it, light where Gerald may show. The outline marks the part draw_object reads.

The room is a bitmap and Gerald is a pair of sprites over it, so on its own the video chip would show him in front of every block. Before he is drawn, build_sprite_mask ($9D18) makes a mask 4 bytes wide and 24 rows tall over the six screen places he covers, with every bit set. When the room was drawn, each place kept a list of up to four tiles with how near each is. For each place the routine finds the first tile nearer than Gerald and ANDs its mask, and the masks of the tiles queued after it, into his. These are the masks draw_picture uses to cut a hole for a tile's picture, so where a nearer block covers the place, the mask is cleared.

draw_object then ANDs each byte of his frame with the mask on its way into the sprite. Which rows and which three of the four bytes it reads depend on the fine part of his row and column, so the mask lines up with where he is drawn. The jewels and the lifts are cut the same way, by the same mask routine.

Nearer is a number. project gives Gerald a depth from his X and Y doubled plus his height, and each tile in a place's list carries one too, set when the room was drawn. A larger depth is nearer the viewer. sort_sprites ($A2F2) uses the same depths to choose which sprite the video chip draws in front when two overlap: it hands the lowest-numbered sprites, which the chip draws on top, to the deepest.
