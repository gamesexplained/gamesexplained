1989 · Taito America

Qix

Every sprite in the game, set by the game's own routines: the marker, the Sparx, the Spritz and the Fuse in a round's colours, and the four sprites that burst out of the marker when it dies and fly back in when a life starts.

01 · The marker and the enemies

The enemies change colour with the field

Each round draws its field's two colours at random from a table of 32 (qix_colours, $121B), and the enemies take their colours from the same byte (level_params, $90C0). The Sparx and the Spritz then cycle through three colour pairs as they animate.

Run on the site's 6502 over the game's memory: the round's colours by level_params, then a step of sprite_anim ($A4A9) every second frame, as the play interrupt runs it ($1568), at an American machine's 60 frames a second. A step moves each sprite's four-frame animation on and the colour pair round. The Sparx are multicolour sprites in black ($A1C8, $A241, $A320), their two shared colours from the round's byte; the Spritz are high-resolution sprites whose own colour cycles ($A4F4). Each pair of Sparx starts its frames out of step, so no two look the same at once.

The marker is one high-resolution sprite ($8CF9), white, and red while it draws a fast line ($8A8D). On the levels where pair B chases up your line, its two Sparx swap between yellow and green on every pass of the main loop instead of staying black (walker_pair_b, $A24E). The Spritz take the place of pair C from level 5, and of pair A as well from level 7.

One of the 32 shapes is never shown, as nothing in the code points a sprite at it: $20, below. Four more, $2C-$2F, are empty.

02 · The burst

Four sprites fly to the corners and back

When the marker dies, four sprites burst out of it and fly diagonally to the corners of the field, a pixel at a time, growing through eight frames each. When the next life starts, the same flight plays backwards and the four close in on the marker.

The game's own routines on the site's 6502, from the marker's starting place: marker_burst ($A774) for a death, and for a new life round_intro ($A6E8), which records the flight at full speed and then plays it backwards (burst_replay, $A709). A delay loop paces each move, not the frame, so the moves here are timed by the cycles they take at an American machine's 1,022,727 a second: about 9,600 a move out and 10,800 back, some 1 and 1.1 seconds in all. On the machine the flight takes about a tenth longer, as the video chip and the interrupt that runs every frame take some of the processor's time. A sprite that reaches its corner goes out ($A875), and one past the edge of the screen's window is hidden by the border, drawn grey here. Each pair of sprites steps its frame every ninth move ($A84E), $30-$37 and $38-$3F.
