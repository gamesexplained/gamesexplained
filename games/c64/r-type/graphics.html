Sprites

Eight sprites stretched to cover twenty

The Commodore 64 shows eight sprites at once. R-Type gives sprite 0 to the Force and sprites 1 and 2 to the ship, one above the other, and shares the other five among up to fifteen enemies in three bands down the screen, reusing each sprite in every band (attack_script, $4FA8; irq_band1_y, $0F6C). A boss takes over the same five sprites for up to seven rows of its own.

The shapes are 64 bytes each, in the last bank the video chip can see, so a sprite pointer p picks the shape at $C000 + 64 × p. They are multicoloured: every sprite shares dark grey and light grey, the colours the game puts in the shared registers, and each sprite has one colour of its own.

The sheets below draw every shape with white as that colour of its own, since that colour comes from the enemy’s type. Shapes are in pointer order, sixteen to a row. Each stage brings its own set in its third file, at $E000 and up, so these sheets are the full cast of each stage.

Characters

Shots, the beam and the lasers are characters

None of the player’s fire is a sprite. A shot, the beam, the reflecting and crawling lasers and the red wave are characters $CC to $ED of the play-area set, written into screen memory and moved a cell at a time (check_hits, $17FB; move_beam, $416C; move_crawlers, $4848). That leaves every sprite free for the ship, the Force and the enemies, and it lets the game find a hit by reading the characters in the cells under each enemy.

The scenery is characters too, in a set of its own for each stage. The Maps tab draws every stage from them.
