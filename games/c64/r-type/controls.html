The joystick

The stick flies, fire shoots and charges

Connect a joystick to port 2. The stick moves the ship in eight directions (stick_moves, $2CF7). A tap of fire sends a shot, and holding fire charges the beam, which flies when the button is let go (charge_beam, $40D1; release_beam, $40EC).

The keys

F7 pauses, and the music plays on

The title screen promises that F7 pauses the game, and on this copy it does. Once a frame, after the ship and the enemies have moved, the panel’s interrupt reads the top row of the keyboard; if F7 alone is down, and the stage is not already won, it sets the pause flag $024E ($0E85).

While the flag is set, the same interrupt skips the ship, the Force, the enemies and the stage’s own routine, and only checks the fire button. Fire clears the flag, and play resumes. The music driver is called before the pause is checked, so the tune keeps playing through the pause.

The C64-Wiki gives F7 a different job, turning the music on and off, with Shift Lock as the pause.1 The keyboard read in this copy tests F7 only, and for the pause.

The Force

Space sends the Force away and calls it back

The space bar works only while the ship has the Force. One press throws a docked Force forwards from the front of the ship, or backwards from the back, eight pixels a frame. A second press calls it home, three pixels a frame, and it docks at whichever end of the ship it reaches (force_control, $2A12; force_dock, $2953).

The crackers’ key

The left arrow wins the stage

The copy studied here is a crack by the group Oneway, who added a key of their own to the end of the bottom raster interrupt. Pressing ← sets the flags that say the stage is won and shortens the wait after it, so the next stage loads straight away (crack_skip_key, $6290). This key took the route through all eight stages that the Maps and the ending were checked on. The original tape has no such key.
