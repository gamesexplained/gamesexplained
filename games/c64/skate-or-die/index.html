Commodore 64 · 1987 · Electronic Arts

Skate or Die!

Five skateboarding events around a town square, each loaded from disk on its own, taken apart from the game's own code.

01 · The game

Rodney's shop, a town and five events

The game opens in Rodney's skate shop, where players sign in, practise or compete. Practice leads to a town square, and the skater rolls down its paths to the events: the Freestyle ramp, High Jump, the Downhill Race, the Downhill Jam and the Pool Joust.1 Each event is a separate program. Choosing one shows SKATING TO HIGH JUMP, or the like, while the game's own disk code loads it over most of the computer's memory ($F230), and each disk side holds its own events: High Jump comes from side 1, the Downhill Race and the Jam from side 2.

02 · The makers

From Epyx to Electronic Arts

The title screen credits Michael Kosaka, Stephen Landrum and David Bunch. Landrum had been at Epyx, the maker of Summer Games and Winter Games, and joined Electronic Arts in February 1987: "At Electronic Arts, I was the lead programmer on Skate or Die! for the C-64."2 The Retrogaming Times names Kosaka and Landrum among the Epyx team who "moved on to EA and began work on a skateboard-themed compilation of events".5

Rob Hubbard, the British composer, came to the game from across the Atlantic: "The first thing I did for EA was Skate or Die C64."3 Mark Lewis of EA had asked him "if I wanted to do a couple of months working in the US. That's when I did 'Skate or Die' C64 with the sampled guitars mixed in with the SID chipsounds. They then offered my a permanent job in California."4

03 · The reception

A Zzap!64 Sizzler

Zzap!64, the British Commodore 64 magazine, gave the game 92 % and its Sizzler award in its Christmas 1987 issue, as The Retrogaming Times recalls it.5

04 · What this site covers

Nine programs, every byte

Because each event replaces the last in memory, the game is studied as nine programs, each from a snapshot of it running: the loader, the title, the disk code that stays in memory, the shop and town, and the five events. Every byte each one loads is described in the Source code, which has a listing for each.

05 · Inside the game

Explore how it works

  • Loading and title: a one-sector loader that runs itself from the stack, a loader decrypted in layers ($0340), a disk check the drive does with its own code, and a title picture that moves only its colours.
  • The town: Rodney's shop as a menu, a square whose edges choose the event, and the table that loads each one ($F8A0).
  • Freestyle: a trick decided before the coping ($1C81) and a bonus for variety.
  • High Jump: speed from waggling the stick ($16E3), and why the height of the last pass is the one that counts.
  • Downhill Race: a course where every cell knows its ground, and a score that is mostly time ($0B14).
  • Downhill Jam: twelve attacks, rubbish worth points, and a time bonus that drains away ($23F8).
  • Pool Joust: first to three by two clear ($1B3C), and three computer skaters with their own habits.
  • Graphics: the half-pipe picture and its moving crowd, and all 158 poses of the skater drawn from the game's data.
  • Music and sound: Rob Hubbard's sampled guitars, and one sound driver that plays every event's music at random.
  • Discoveries: rules the manual leaves out, credits nobody sees, and code nothing calls.
