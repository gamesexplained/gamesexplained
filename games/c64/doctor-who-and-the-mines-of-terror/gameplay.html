Commodore 64 · 1986 · Micro Power

Doctor Who and the Mines of Terror

How the Doctor moves, what he carries, how Splinx is programmed, what kills him, and how the game marks his escape.

The quest

Stop the machine, take what matters, get out alive

Everything the Doctor does serves three aims. He halts the heatonite machine. He finds the CRYSTAL and the CAPSULE and keeps Splinx with him. And he leaves the mine, by the TARDIS or an escape pod. On the way, rock, clamped ladders, bad air, a security gate and a code lock stand between him and each of them, and the tools that clear them lie around the mine.

The mine is one map of 56 rows of 128 blocks ($E000). The Maps tab draws all of it, and the Solution tab takes the steps in order.

The Doctor

Walk, climb, jump, and don't fall too far

The joystick in port 2 moves the Doctor and makes him jump. Everything else is on the keyboard, and the keyboard can move him too. Press a move to see the frames the game shows for it.

The input routine (read_input, $8F24) runs once every pass of the game's main loop. It reads the joystick and six rows of the keyboard, and turns them into flags that the rest of the game reads: one for left or right, one for up or down, one for jump and one each for the keys.

The Doctor is two sprites. The lower one draws his coat and legs, and the upper one, eight pixels higher, his head and arms ($CB48). Walking, the game picks one of eight frames from his position, a new frame for every two pixels, and on a ladder one of four (doctor_pick_frames, $CC4C). Carrying something, he keeps one upper frame with his arms full ($CCB2).

A jump lasts 24 frames going up, 16 level and then a fall, and the direction is fixed at take-off (jump_arc_step). The game measures each fall from where it starts to where it ends, and a drop of 96 pixels or more kills the Doctor (fall_check, $CD62).

Pockets and items

Four pockets and four markers

What an item does depends on where the Doctor stands when he uses it (use_item_update). The PICK AXE digs at three places, three blows each, and the SPANNER undoes eight bolts. DETONATORs and EXPLOSIVES are set, and go off after a fuse; the blast destroys anything close, the Doctor included. The ACTIVATOR launches the escape pod. The PASS CARD matters only while it is in his hands ($BB15). The Maps tab shows where each one works.

Some items can be drawn into the scenery as well as flying as sprites. The game blends their shapes into 32 spare characters, pixel pair by pixel pair, so they sit on the rock without covering it (charobj_composite_char, $8652).

Splinx

A robot cat with a ten-step program

Splinx can follow the Doctor, stay put, or run a program of up to ten commands. Press S to open its programming screen.

1-4 GOTO MARKERwalks to one of the four markers the Doctor has dropped

D DROP OBJECTputs it down

On the programming screen the joystick moves between the commands and along the ten steps of the program, and fire writes the command into the step. The three choices on the right set Splinx's mode: shut down, follow the Doctor or execute the program (splinx_update, $B1A0).

The battery starts full at 24 units. It loses one every 1,024 frames, about 20 seconds, while Splinx follows the Doctor or runs a program, so a full charge lasts about eight minutes of work. Shut down, Splinx uses nothing. The gauge on the programming screen is a sprite the game redraws from the battery level ($B07D).

Splinx fetches items the Doctor cannot reach or carry. Markers are items too: the Doctor drops them with F3 where he wants Splinx to go.

Dangers

Five regenerations, and the ways to lose them

The Doctor starts with five lives. They are drawn as five figures in the status bar's item field, which is why the figures disappear when he picks something up ($94AC). After the last one, the game is over.

The fade is the TARDIS's own effect turned on the Doctor (doctor_draw, $CB6B). For the last 127 frames of the death, every fourth frame, the game copies his two frames afresh and clears more of their pixel pairs, six more each time, in the shuffled order the TARDIS landing uses (dissolve_sprite, $FEA9).

The air runs out only after the Doctor has breathed bad air. Walking into one of the four bad-air blocks high in the mine starts a countdown of 50 ($F1). It keeps running while he is high in the mine ($1C60 of 2 or more), except in open cavern, and he suffocates when it reaches 0 (doctor_tile_checks, $CA58). Holding the AIR MASK fills it again every time ($CAB1). A fifth bad-air block lies lower down, where the test is never made.

Who else is in the mine

Controllers follow fixed routes through the mine, 25 of them stored as lists of waypoints at $764A (controllers_update, $CD9A). Touching one is fatal. Other creatures patrol their own routes ($C404) or walk between waypoints (trio_update, $C28E).

The madrag guards two eggs. If an egg is moved, she fetches it and carries it back to its nest. She takes an egg from Splinx if it is carrying one, and bites the Doctor if she reaches him. Her code runs twice in every pass of the main loop, where other creatures' runs once, so she moves at double speed (madrag_update, $B680). If the Doctor goes near a free egg, a baby madrag hatches from it.

One creature, object $0C, goes after the CRYSTAL. It takes it from the Doctor's hands or even from his pockets, and carries it away ($B48A). The code does not say who it is.

At most eight objects appear at once, because the C64 has eight hardware sprites. Each pass, the game collects the visible ones and leaves out any ninth (sprite_list_add_object, $81DA). The Graphics tab shows every sprite image in the game.
