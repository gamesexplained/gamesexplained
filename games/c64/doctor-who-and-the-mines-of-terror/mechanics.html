Doctor Who and the Mines of Terror

How the Doctor moves and carries things, how Splinx is programmed, what kills him, how the game marks his escape, and how the screen is built.

The Doctor

Walk, climb, jump, and don't fall too far

The joystick in port 2 moves the Doctor and makes him jump. Everything else is on the keyboard, and the keyboard can move him too.

The input routine (read_input, $8F24) runs once every pass of the game's main loop. It reads the joystick and six rows of the keyboard, and turns them into flags that the rest of the game reads: one for left or right, one for up or down, one for jump and one each for the keys.

A jump lasts 24 frames going up, 16 level and then a fall, and the direction is fixed at take-off (jump_arc_step). The game measures each fall from where it starts to where it ends, and a drop of 96 pixels or more kills the Doctor (fall_check, $CD62).

Using an item depends on where the Doctor stands (use_item_update). The pick axe digs at three places, three blows each. The spanner works at eight points. Detonators and explosives are set, and go off after a fuse; the blast destroys anything close, the Doctor included. The activator starts the escape pod. The pass card matters only while it is in his hands: the game checks for it every pass ($16A0).

Some items can be drawn into the scenery as well as flying as sprites. The game blends their shapes into 32 spare characters, pixel pair by pixel pair, so they sit on the rock without covering it (charobj_composite_char, $8652).

Splinx

A robot cat with a ten-step program

Splinx can follow the Doctor, stay put, or run a program of up to ten commands. Press S to open its programming screen.

CommandWhat Splinx does
1-4 GOTO MARKERwalks to one of the four markers the Doctor has dropped
R RETURN TO DOCTORwalks back to him
W WAITstands still
P PICK UP OBJECTtakes the nearest free item, if one is within 16 by 8 pixels
D DROP OBJECTputs it down
C RECHARGEon a charging point, gains one unit of battery every 8 frames

On the programming screen the joystick moves between the commands and along the ten steps of the program, and fire writes the command into the step. The three choices on the right set Splinx's mode: shut down, follow the Doctor or execute the program (splinx_update, $B1A0).

The battery starts full at 24 units. It loses one every 1,024 frames, about 20 seconds, while Splinx follows the Doctor or runs a program, so a full charge lasts about eight minutes of work. Shut down, Splinx uses nothing.

Splinx is how the Doctor gets items he cannot reach or carry. Markers are items too: the Doctor drops them with F3 where he wants Splinx to go.

The Doctor starts with five lives. They are drawn as five figures in the status bar's item field, which is why the figures disappear when he picks something up ($94AC). After the last one, the game is over.

Air is a counter of 50 ($F1). It goes down once each pass while the Doctor is high in the mine, and on certain ground; the air mask fills it again ($CA90).

The cast

Controllers, a madrag and her eggs

Every sprite image in the game, drawn from the bytes at $5000-$73FF. Click one to see where it is stored.

Multicolour sprites in the colours the game gives them: the shared red and orange ($D025, $D026) and each object's own colour from $1850, for the object whose animation table or starting frame uses the image. Grey marks the few images no table names. The Doctor's own colour is black, which is why he shows only red and orange, as in the game.

Controllers follow fixed routes through the mine, 25 of them stored as lists of waypoints at $764A (controllers_update, $CD9A). Touching one is fatal. Other creatures patrol their own routes ($C404) or walk between waypoints (trio_update, $C28E).

The madrag guards two eggs. If an egg is moved, she fetches it and carries it back to its nest. She takes an egg from Splinx if he is carrying one, and bites the Doctor if she reaches him. Her code runs twice in every pass of the main loop, where other creatures' runs once ($B680). If the Doctor goes near a free egg, a baby madrag hatches from it.

One creature goes after the crystal. It takes it from the Doctor's hands or even from his pockets, and carries it away ($B48A). The code does not name it; it may be the Master.

At most eight objects appear at once, because the C64 has eight hardware sprites. Each pass, the game collects the visible ones and leaves out any ninth (sprite_list_add_object, $81DA).

Doing everything gives a bonus of 1,024 doubled five times, 32,768. With the crystal and Splinx and nothing else it is 4,096, the escape bonus a BBC Micro players' forum quotes for the same moment. The screen

One frame, rebuilt from memory

The first screen of the game, drawn by this page from the game's memory and the video chip's settings. Show the raster bands to see where the picture changes mode.

A reconstruction, not a screenshot: one frame recorded in the emulator, with every write to the video chip and the line it happened on, drawn line by line. It matches the emulator's own picture in all 104,448 pixels.

The picture is two screens in one. An interrupt at $8010 runs three times a frame and resets the video chip each time. From line 256 to line 78 of the next frame the chip shows the status bar, from memory bank 0 and screen $0400. From line 78 it switches to bank 1 and whichever of the two play screens is ready. The handler keeps its place in the frame in the offset of a branch at $8017, so each interrupt jumps straight to the next band's code.

The status bar

A font kept in the screen it draws

The status bar uses four rows of a 25-row screen. The other 21 rows are never shown: they hold the font the status bar is written in.

The game writes its own text in a private alphabet in which each letter is its ASCII code plus 155: A is $DC, Z is $F5, the digits are $F6-$FF and $94 is a space. The video chip finds a character's shape at eight times its code from the start of the font. In the status band the font starts at $0000, so the shapes for $94-$FF sit at $04A0-$07FF, in the unused rows of the status bar's own screen.

As the game starts, it copies the screen and its own shapes from $4B00, then copies A to Z and 0 to 9 from the Commodore's character ROM into place ($48F5). One shape does not fit. The shape for $FF would be the last eight bytes of the screen's memory, where the video chip reads the sprite pointers. So the digit 9 is drawn with $F5, the code for Z, and the digit table at $1730 sends 9 there. The status bar never needs a Z.

Sound

Music: five tunes, chosen by where you are

Two of the SID chip's three voices play music, both with a sawtooth wave (music_update, $74EA). The game picks one of five tunes from the Doctor's position in the mine, so the music changes as he moves between areas. The third voice plays the 24 sound effects, a lower-numbered effect cutting off a higher one (sound_start, $C7C7).
