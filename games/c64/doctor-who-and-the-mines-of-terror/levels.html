Doctor Who and the Mines of Terror

The whole mine, 4,096 by 1,792 pixels, drawn from the map the game keeps under its operating system.

The mine

One map, seven kilobytes

The game never loads a level. The whole mine is one map, and the screen is a window onto it that scrolls wherever the Doctor goes. Drag across the map to move the magnifier, and switch on the restart points to see where the Doctor comes back after losing a regeneration.

Yellow: the objects where a new game puts them (positions $1B70/$1BC0, $1C10/$1C60; names from $1770). Green: where an object does something, from the code that checks the Doctor's position. Red: blocks that kill or start the air running out. Drag the magnifier over a mark to read its label.

Every block drawn from the map at map_e000, the block shapes at $3000, the play-area characters at $4000 and each character's colour from $2F00. The orange cross is where the Doctor starts. Parts of the mine swap in their own shapes for sixteen characters (zone_graphics); this picture draws every part with the starting cavern's.

What the objects are for

Each use below is the code's own test: the game compares the Doctor's position with a table of points when he uses the object he holds (F5, use_item_update, $BACC).

The air runs out only after the Doctor has breathed bad air. Walking into one of the four bad-air blocks high in the mine starts a countdown of 50. It keeps running while he is high in the mine ($1C60 of 2 or more), except in open cavern, and he suffocates when it reaches 0 (doctor_tile_checks, $CA58). Holding the AIR MASK fills it again every time. The fifth bad-air block lies lower down, where the test is never made. Stalagmite blocks, sixteen of them, kill at a touch.

What the PASS CARD, MAT, PLATFORM, BOX, CHEMICALS and the eggs do is not traced here. The circuits count when they sit in their slots in the machinery (machinery_update, $B9B6), and the crystal and the TIRU capsule double the bonus at the end.

The map fills $E000-$FBFF, the memory the Commodore's operating system (the KERNAL ROM) normally occupies. The game switches the ROMs out as it starts ($01 = $35), which leaves the RAM underneath free. It holds 56 rows of 128 blocks, one byte a block. A block is four by four characters, sixteen bytes at $3000 + 16 × its number, so 256 block shapes build the whole mine. Row r starts at $FB80 − 128 × r. Row 0 is the bottom: the game measures heights upward from the floor of the mine.

Colour RAM, the Commodore's separate memory for the colour of each screen cell, is never stored for the map. As the game draws each new row or column onto the screen, it looks up the colour of every character in a 256-byte table (colour_from_chars, $8EB8). So a character is always the same colour, wherever it is.

Scrolling

Two screens, drawn in turn

The game keeps two copies of the play screen, at $7800 and $7C00. While one is shown, it builds the next position of the mine in the other, spread over three frames (coarse_scroll_step, $8C32), and then swaps them. Between those steps the video chip's fine scroll moves the picture a pixel at a time, so the view glides in all eight directions.

The mine is split into seven zones by the Doctor's position, checked every eighth frame (zone_classify, $AA3D). Entering a zone puts the creatures of other zones to sleep, wakes that zone's own and swaps in its shapes for sixteen characters (zone_graphics, $ABCF).

Restart points

Nine places to come back to

The mine has nine restart points, stored as four tables of nine bytes at $9059-$907C. When the Doctor comes close to one, it becomes the current point (check_restart_points, $907D). After he loses a regeneration, the game puts him back at the current point.
