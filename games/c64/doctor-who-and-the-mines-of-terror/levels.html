Doctor Who and the Mines of Terror

The whole mine, 4,096 by 1,792 pixels, drawn from the map the game keeps under its operating system.

The mine

One map, seven kilobytes

The game never loads a level. The whole mine is one map, and the screen is a window onto it that scrolls wherever the Doctor goes. Drag across the map to move the magnifier, or pick an object, a use or a danger from the list to go straight to it. Switch on the restart points to see where the Doctor comes back after losing a regeneration.

Yellow: the objects where a new game puts them (positions $1B70/$1BC0, $1C10/$1C60; names from $1770). Green: where an object does something, from the code that checks the Doctor's position. Red: blocks that kill or start the air running out. Drag the magnifier over a mark to read its label, or click a mark on the map to select it.

Every block drawn from the map at map_e000, the block shapes at $3000, the play-area characters at $4000 and each character's colour from $2F00. The orange cross is where the Doctor starts. Parts of the mine swap in their own shapes for sixteen characters (zone_graphics); this picture draws every part with the starting cavern's.

The air runs out only after the Doctor has breathed bad air. Walking into one of the four bad-air blocks high in the mine starts a countdown of 50. It keeps running while he is high in the mine ($1C60 of 2 or more), except in open cavern, and he suffocates when it reaches 0 (doctor_tile_checks, $CA58). Holding the AIR MASK fills it again every time. The fifth bad-air block lies lower down, where the test is never made. Stalagmite blocks, sixteen of them, kill at a touch.

What the PASS CARD, MAT, PLATFORM, BOX, CHEMICALS and the eggs do is not traced here. The circuits count when they sit in their slots in the machinery (machinery_update, $B9B6), and the crystal and the TIRU capsule double the bonus at the end.

The map fills $E000-$FBFF, the memory the Commodore's operating system (the KERNAL ROM) normally occupies. The game switches the ROMs out as it starts ($01 = $35), which leaves the RAM underneath free. It holds 56 rows of 128 blocks, one byte a block. A block is four by four characters, sixteen bytes at $3000 + 16 × its number, so 256 block shapes build the whole mine. Row r starts at $FB80 − 128 × r. Row 0 is the bottom: the game measures heights upward from the floor of the mine.

Colour RAM, the Commodore's separate memory for the colour of each screen cell, is never stored for the map. As the game draws each new row or column onto the screen, it looks up the colour of every character in a 256-byte table (colour_from_chars, $8EB8). So a character is always the same colour, wherever it is.

The code lock

A colour, a shape, a number, and the card in the box

Halfway up the mine is a panel that gives three clues, and below it three buttons that each show a shape. The answer is six shapes long, and the only way to find it is the code card that came with the game.

The Doctor works the lock with empty hands and F5 (code_lock_press, $BE29). Standing at the clue panel, each press shows the next clue: a number from 1 to 5, a shape, and a letter ($BE6F). The letters are W, R, B, G and Y, so we read them as white, red, blue, green and yellow, the colours of the lines on the card. Standing at the buttons, he presses the one showing the next shape of the answer. One button always shows it and the other two show other shapes (code_lock_deal_buttons, $BEEC). A wrong press deals a whole new code with new clues ($BED5), so guessing gives a one-in-three chance six times running.

The card is in the game too, as 186 bytes at $BD6F. Each byte is a coloured shape: eight times the colour plus the shape. To deal a code, the game takes three numbers from the frame counter (code_lock_new_sequence, $BF69), finds that colour and shape in the list, skips to the occurrence the number names, and reads six shapes from there ($BFC7). So the first shape of the answer is always the shape in the clue. The first empty-handed F5 of a game deals the first code ($BB49).

Choose the three clues, or deal one the way the game does. The answer is read from the list at code_lock_deck ($BD6F-$BE28), drawn here 31 to a row, with the six shapes of the answer boxed. The shapes are the game's own characters: $FD $FA $FE $FB $FC $FF for shapes 0-5 ($BD64), $F6 $F5 $F7 $F8 $F9 for the letters ($BD5F) and $A1-$A5 for the numbers ($BD6A), from the graphics set the hand-over image holds. The printed card runs the same list around its diamond bands: from the white triangle at the top corner, the outer band reads white triangle, red square, red circle, blue square, green triangle, red cross, white diamond, the first seven entries here (compared by eye with a photograph of the contributor's card; the rest of the card has not been read).

The clues never show the sixth shape, but answers can contain it: the frame counter is reduced to 0-4 for each clue, and the list uses all six shapes. Every colour and shape the clues can show turns up at least five times, so every clue has an answer.

Six right presses set the flag at $4E, play a sound and redraw one block beside the buttons (switch cell 13: block $C2 becomes $D5, $BF59). After that the buttons do nothing. The routine that sets the class of character $A0 tests the same flag, but the PASS CARD test straight after it overwrites the result every time ($BB05-$BB25), so the flag does not change that character.

The room around the lock, row 38 to row 47, columns 77 to 85, before (left) and after (right) the code is entered. The clue panel is the framed door at the top; the buttons are drawn onto the screen while the Doctor is there and are not part of the map.

Scrolling

Two screens, drawn in turn

The game keeps two copies of the play screen, at $7800 and $7C00. While one is shown, it builds the next position of the mine in the other, spread over three frames (coarse_scroll_step, $8C32), and then swaps them. Between those steps the video chip's fine scroll moves the picture a pixel at a time, so the view glides in all eight directions.

The mine is split into seven zones by the Doctor's position, checked every eighth frame (zone_classify, $AA3D). Entering a zone puts the creatures of other zones to sleep, wakes that zone's own and swaps in its shapes for sixteen characters (zone_graphics, $ABCF).

Restart points

Nine places to come back to

The mine has nine restart points, stored as four tables of nine bytes at $9059-$907C. When the Doctor comes close to one, it becomes the current point (check_restart_points, $907D). After he loses a regeneration, the game puts him back at the current point.
