Wizard

Forty rooms of ladders, spells and monsters. Treasure can also rewrite the room around you.

01 · The playfield

A room built from characters

The wizard and monsters are hardware sprites. The platforms, ropes and treasure are characters drawn from a custom alphabet. Changing one character can open a passage; changing its eight drawing bytes can alter every matching tile on the screen.

The reconstruction reads the actual screen, glyph and sprite bytes. All 104,448 visible pixels match the captured emulator frame. The palette shown here uses the site's C64 colors.

Screen cells begin at $C400, character shapes at $C800. Sprite 7 draws the wizard, sprite 6 handles spells and score effects, and six other slots hold the room's actors. Walking changes X by two pixels per movement call at $792E. Fire with left, right or up starts a jump; down and fire casts a spell.

02 · Forty levels

Treasure can change the rules

Collecting treasure applies a small terrain patch, then calls a program supplied by that level. Twenty of the forty supplied levels use that callback. A thief collecting the treasure calls it too.

Extracted terrain, with colors computed by the game's own drawing routines. W marks the wizard; numbers mark actor slots. The view uses the base alphabet and excludes later treasure changes. Level numbers here match the game's one-based display.

In Friend or Foe?, one treasure index moves the key along a row. Madhouse cycles directional arrows. Simon Says can set the death flag when the treasure's symbol fails its color-selected rule. The shared entry points are $8497 for the player and $7FA1 for the thief; the level callback excerpts show their separate programs.

03 · Rewards

Four treasures, seven rewards

A treasure's class and the difficulty together select its reward. The same diamond is worth more in a harder band. Crossing 10,000 points awards another life.

Calculation checked against the original routine at $8464 for all sixteen treasure/difficulty combinations and five boundary scores, including score wrap. $84D8 and $84DF hold the reward digits.

The bonus starts at 24 units. Finishing a room converts each remaining unit into 50 points, for a maximum of 1,200. Its countdown period comes from the level header, so “24” is a counter rather than a universal number of seconds. Completion accounting is in $3D96; the timer is at $9270.

Six players and their scores

This build accepts one through six players. Each has a separate life slot and three score bytes. All six accounts start with six lives. The ten persistent high scores store points in units of 50, with three initials for each entry and a sixteen-character name for the champion. See $349A, $36AE and $419A.

04 · Monster behavior

Appearance and behavior are separate

The construction editor chooses a monster's behavior independently of its sprite image. A familiar shape can therefore follow a different rule in a custom room.

The names come from the editor's DATA records. The shared controller dispatches them at $860E.

The cat is exempt from killing the wizard, and a cat–rat collision queues the rat for removal. A thief can take treasure and trigger its terrain changes. Cyan also has a mechanical role: frozen actors stop while a shared counter runs, and the collision handler compares actor color with the room's exempt color. See $7CE7, $7F7A and $862E.

05 · Magic

A key replenishes the spell counter

Each room supplies a spell and a charge count. Picking up its key copies that count into the HUD and enables the exit. SPACE or down and fire spends a charge; projectile direction follows the wizard's facing.

Key and exit logic: $7ACE. Casting: $765B. Projectile movement and queued hits: $8BDD. Longer effects return to the compiled game script.

Freeze turns its victim cyan; the actor controller restores its original color when the phase expires. Haste changes the main loop's delay. Slow changes actor horizontal speeds and also lengthens the loop delay. The special target-immunity differences advertised for the first four projectile names remain unverified.

06 · The SID

Sound: short effects, shared voices

Actions rewrite the SID's frequency, waveform and envelope registers. The treasure effect uses two triangle-based voices with ring modulation; falling-hazard impact combines triangle and pulse voices.

Sound controls require the built site and its shared SID player.

Five effect ports match the original routines' ordered register writes in sixty tests. These previews start from silent registers, use a fixed random value for the key effect and stop after 125 PAL frames. During play, effects can inherit oscillator settings and interrupt one another. The shared SID model approximates combined waveforms and analog output. Evidence: $852B, $987D, $97FB, $985A, $98B6.

07 · Construction

The editor shares the game engine

Construction loads a different program over the game's low-memory script. Both programs keep the same resident machine routines for drawing, loading levels and handling input.
