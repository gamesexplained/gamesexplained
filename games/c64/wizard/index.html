01 · The playfield

A room built from characters

The wizard and monsters are hardware sprites. The platforms, ropes and treasure are characters drawn from a custom alphabet. Changing one character can open a passage; changing its eight drawing bytes can alter every matching tile on the screen.

The reconstruction reads the actual screen, glyph and sprite bytes. All 104,448 visible pixels match the captured emulator frame. The palette shown here uses the site's C64 colors.

Screen cells begin at $C400, character shapes at $C800. Sprite 7 draws the wizard, sprite 6 handles spells and score effects, and six other slots hold the room's actors. Walking changes X by two pixels per movement call at $792E. Fire with left, right or up starts a jump; down and fire casts a spell.

02 · Movement

A jump can end on a rope

Walking off a platform can be fatal even when the gap is only one character wide. A jump follows a fixed sequence: it rises thirteen pixels in clear space, pauses at the top, then falls. A platform, rope or ladder can end that sequence early.

What the replay includes

Recorded outputs of the original player routine at $707B, using small prepared terrain examples and the original graphics. The launch position is fixed; these examples do not give a universal maximum gap width. Movement and collision state were compared with the emulator. The elevator example supplies a contact signal to isolate the ride rule; it does not simulate sprite collision geometry. Playback is slowed, and update counts are not video frames.

The ordinary fall check has only a short allowance to find support or reach a character-row boundary. It does not simulate a long accelerating fall. On a rope, holding up climbs one pixel per update. Elevator contact uses a separate rule: the wizard follows the platform from thirteen pixels above it.

03 · Forty levels

Treasure can change the rules

Collecting treasure applies a small terrain patch, then calls a program supplied by that level. Twenty of the forty supplied levels use that callback. A thief collecting the treasure calls it too.

In Friend or Foe?, a regenerating gold bar moves the key along a row. Repeated pearl pickups in Ladder Land erase three strips of terrain, row by row. 04 · Puzzle machinery

A hidden thief keeps the room changing

In Friend or Foe? and Ladder Land, a black thief sits over a treasure that reappears after collection. It keeps collecting while the wizard stands still. The thief calls the same treasure-patch and room rules as a player pickup.

Friend or Foe? · a moving key

The thief collects a gold bar; the room moves the key one cell right. Forty pickups bring it back around. The gold regenerates, so the cycle continues without joystick input.

05 · Simon Says

When the instruction disappears

In Simon Says, the words “Simon says…” determine which treasure is safe. When they are visible, take the requested kind. When they disappear into the black background, take a different kind. The instruction's color reverses the rule.

Choose a treasure to test the instruction.

Original character graphics and the level's pickup rule. The test agrees with the original callback in 4,096 cases; matching and mismatching pickups were also checked in the emulator. This example isolates the rule; movement and hazards are omitted. Level 08 map · Callback evidence

The wizard begins on a pearl. Collecting it turns on the instruction and initializes the puzzle before the first movement. Each later pickup also selects the next requested treasure from a table indexed by its position. It chooses the words' next color from black, white, cyan and light green. In the code, the level changes a single branch instruction: equal is safe with visible words, and not equal is safe with hidden words.

06 · Rewards

Four treasures, seven rewards

A treasure's class and the difficulty together select its reward. The same diamond is worth more in a harder band. Crossing 10,000 points awards another life.

Calculation checked against the original routine at $8464 for all sixteen treasure/difficulty combinations and five boundary scores, including score wrap. $84D8 and $84DF hold the reward digits.

The bonus starts at 24 units. Finishing a room converts each remaining unit into 50 points, for a maximum of 1,200. Its countdown period comes from the level header, so “24” is a counter rather than a universal number of seconds. Completion accounting is in $3D96; the timer is at $9270.

Clear the high scores at startup

Hold the Commodore key as loading finishes. The loader clears the score table in memory before entering the title screen. The check reads the key directly; it does not immediately rewrite the disk. See the loader’s score-clear branch.

Six players and their scores

This build accepts one through six players. Each has a separate life slot and three score bytes. All six accounts start with six lives. The ten persistent high scores store points in units of 50, with three initials for each entry and a sixteen-character name for the champion. See $349A, $36AE and $419A.

What the actor replay includes

Recorded from the original controller at $860E, movement at $7D82 and sprite animation at $78AD. These are isolated actor updates with a fixed sequence of random inputs, a controlled animation phase and a repositioned wizard marker. Other actors, collision interrupts and room timers are paused. Playback is slowed for inspection; update numbers are not video frames. The appearance comes from the named room.

The cat follows the wizard using the shared ground controller. It is safe to touch and removes rats on contact; it does not steer toward a rat. A thief can take treasure and trigger its terrain changes. Cyan also has a mechanical role: frozen actors stop while a shared counter runs, and the collision handler compares actor color with the room's exempt color. See $7CE7, $7F7A and $862E.

08 · Magic

A key replenishes the spell counter

Each room supplies a spell and a charge count. Picking up its key copies that count into the HUD and enables the exit. SPACE or down and fire spends a charge; projectile direction follows the wizard's facing.

Key and exit logic: $7ACE. Casting: $765B. Projectile movement and queued hits: $8BDD. Longer effects return to the compiled game script.

Fireball, Magic Missile, Disintegrate and Enchantment share the same hit rule: a queued projectile hit removes its target. Freeze is the exception. It turns the target cyan and pauses it until a shared countdown expires. Freezing another monster resets that countdown for every frozen monster. See $7D3A, $8C0F and $8C1C.

Invisibility spends protection on danger

Invisibility hides the wizard and gives it thirty-two units of protection. A safe movement update spends nothing. When the game reports a fatal event covered by the spell, it suppresses the event and spends one unit. Continuous contact can consume protection on successive updates.

The spell starts with 32 units of protection.

One “Fatal report” here is one protected event, not necessarily one enemy. Hazards that turn the wizard red bypass this protection.

Display changes and evidence

At sixteen units remaining, multicolor display returns. Below nine, the wizard changes color; at zero it returns to purple. These values come from the compiled script at $3091 and $30B3. The death gate at $2B6E checks red first, then protection. Safe updates, all thirty-two protected reports and the following death were checked in the emulator.

Spells that move the wizard

Feather Fall and Levitate keep moving the wizard two pixels per update until terrain ends the effect. Feather Fall also bypasses the ordinary fall check. Teleport exchanges the current position with a remembered point; the first cast uses the room's starting position. Each arrival gets a small horizontal nudge.

09 · The SID

Sound: short effects, shared voices

Actions rewrite the SID's frequency, waveform and envelope registers. The treasure effect uses two triangle-based voices with ring modulation; falling-hazard impact combines triangle and pulse voices.

Sound controls require the built site and its shared SID player.

Five effect ports match the original routines' ordered register writes in sixty tests. These previews start from silent registers, use a fixed random value for the key effect and stop after 125 PAL frames. During play, effects can inherit oscillator settings and interrupt one another. The shared SID model approximates combined waveforms and analog output. Evidence: $852B, $987D, $97FB, $985A, $98B6.

The editor shares the game engine

Saving resets elevator settings

The elevator controls let you enter motion parameters, but saving the room replaces them. Every packed velocity becomes $1F, the shared travel duration becomes 144 updates, and elevators start at Y=197. The save preparation performs the reset after title entry. A save and reload on a private test disk preserved those reset values.

The editor also protects the supplied game disk: finding its PPSS marker makes a save return “WRONG DISK”. A formatted custom disk without that marker is accepted. The disk check is at $8B98.
