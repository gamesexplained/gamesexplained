1989 · Origin Systems

Space Rogue

Space Rogue flies its ship in filled 3D and draws everything else with one routine from 16-by-16-pixel tiles: the star charts, the bases and the arcade game in the bases' cantinas. The whole Far Arm is nine 1.5 KB map files on the disk, and the planets in them go round their suns by the calendar.

01 · The game

A trader in the Far Arm

The story opens with the player's ship, the Princess Blue, answering a distress call from a derelict and being destroyed by the Manchi. The player is left with the derelict's scout ship, the Sunracer, and earns a living with it by trading and fighting while the plot comes out in conversation.

Flight happens in a cockpit with a filled-polygon view of ships, stations, planets and Malir gates, the wormholes between star systems. Pressing N leaves the cockpit for Navigation Control, a chart of the system the ship is in, where HELM flies to a chosen sector. Docking with a base leaves the ship altogether: the player walks around it in a top-down view, trades, talks to people and, in some cantinas, plays the arcade game HIVE!.

02 · The screens

Every screen is one bitmap, rebuilt here from memory

The game never uses a character screen in play. The cockpit, the chart and the bases are each one multicolour bitmap, with a frame of its own unpacked from the disk around the part that changes.

Each picture is a frame recorded in VICE, the memory and the video chip's registers at the start of a frame and every write to them during it, drawn by the site's renderer. All three are a bitmap at $C000 with its screen matrix at $E000. The bitmap runs under the I/O chips, so the engine keeps the processor port at $34, all RAM, and banks the chips in only around the code that needs them (bank_all_ram, $0E85). The engine draws the next picture into a second buffer at $4000 and flips the two (flip_buffers, $8ED3).

03 · Tiles

One chart drawer for space, the bases and HIVE!

Navigation, the bases and the HIVE! cabinet each load a tile set of 128 tiles to $4000 and a map to $5100, and the same four routines in the engine draw a window of 11 by 9 tiles from them.

Point at a tile to see its bytes.

The 128 tiles of each set, drawn by the page's port of draw_chart ($8658) from the bytes in the listings, white where a pixel takes the colour RAM, as in the game.

A tile set's 4 KB of pixels are laid out for the drawing loop rather than for a person. A tile is 16 pixel rows of two bytes, and tile t's row r is the byte at $4080 + $100 × r + t for its left half and $4000 + $100 × r + t for its right, so one index register reaches every tile in a page. The kinds at $5000 say what each tile does, and the colours at $5080 give each tile its two colours in the screen matrix.

04 · A system map

Karonus, with its planets placed for the date

A system map is 32 by 32 sectors. Its first kilobyte is the grid of tiles; after it come 32 entries for the planets, bases, gates, storms and notes, and a few bytes for the system as a whole.

Karonus as Q0 loads it, with the orbits placed for the day by the page's port of place_orbiting_objects ($2D60) and the chart drawn whole by the port of the four chart routines. Maps has every system and base.

After 128 days every planet is back where it started. The day count starts at 01 MAR 12, and the calendar has no leap years (month_days, $20AF).

05 · Sound

Sound: the engine hum restarts its wave every frame

There is no music in the game. The engine plays fourteen effects on the SID, and when the first voice has nothing to play it hums at a pitch set by the ship's speed.

The page runs a port of the engine's sound code, sound_play ($1091) and sound_step ($10D2), on the effect tables in the listing, through the site's model of the SID. A test, test_sound.js, runs every button on the engine's own code and compares every SID write of every frame. The hum is below 33 Hz, which laptop and phone speakers barely play: try headphones.

An effect is a voice, a starting frequency, a step added to it every frame, an envelope, a waveform, a length in frames, the frame on which the gate closes and a number of repeats ($11AD-$1238). Most effects use the third voice: the hits, the explosions, the alarm and the hazards, nearly all of them noise. The player's beam, the missiles and the beep use the second, and the door and HIVE!'s recall the first, which silences the hum while they play.

The hum lives in the first voice's idle frames ($114B). Every frame it sets and clears the test bit, which stops the oscillator and starts its wave again from zero, and then turns the gate on or off by turns with a triangle wave at the speed times 8 in the SID's units, so the hum is a triangle under 33 Hz cut into pieces 25 times a second. Going backwards it hums at the speed's size (slot_speed, $0415). In navigation and at a base the hum is off (hum_on, $1E), and while the disk turns every frequency is masked to zero (voice_mask, $1D).

06 · Pictures

Every picture is one run-length stream

The title, the story pictures, the ending and the frames around the cockpit, the chart, the bases and the HIVE! cabinet are all packed the same way, and all thirteen unpack with the same few lines.

Unpacked by the page's port of the intro's unpack_picture ($712D), which reads the same stream as the engine's ($1C1D). A test, test_unpack.js, runs both routines on every picture and on made-up streams and compares the 10 KB they write. The border is the colour the stream ends with.

A byte n below $80 repeats the byte after it n times; $80 + n copies the n bytes after it; a zero ends the picture, and the byte after it is the border's colour. The output is a bitmap with its screen matrix after it, at +$2000, and for the story pictures a page of colours at +$2400 that the intro and the ending copy into the colour RAM (colours_to_ram, $718B). The engine's frames have no colour page: the engine leaves the colour RAM white, which is why every frame's fourth colour is white.

The packing is plain run-length, so a picture's size on disk follows its detail. A story picture is a smaller scene on a black screen, and the text fills the rest. SPACE is the title's star field alone and ROGUE the logo on black; the title draws its 3D ship over them.

07 · Controls

Flying the Sunracer from the keyboard, or with two joysticks

The engine reads the keyboard itself, the C128's extra keys included, and looks each key up in a list of 37 commands. Click the table and press a key to find it.

The keys from key_cmds ($145C) and the routines from key_handlers ($1481), each address less one, as do_key ($135F) reaches them with RTS.

The keys that steer add a step to a turn, pitch or roll rate, and the rate keeps turning the ship until S stops it. Two joysticks fly the ship together: the one in port 2 steers and fires, and pushing the one in port 1 forward or back changes the speed (joystick_speed, $242C; Discoveries). F1 gives the code ESC, which pauses (key_escape, $1438), and F7 gives the code the list puts beside CTRL-E, so F7 switches between cruise and Newtonian flight too.

08 · Encounters

What the ship meets depends on the system and the player's rank

Each system's map carries a danger level and five weights, one for each class of ship. When an encounter comes, the weights choose the class and the player's rank chooses the ship within it.

The chance of each ship once one is drawn, worked out by the page's port of pick_ship ($2C8C) over every roll it can make, from ship_class_types ($2D24). A test, test_odds.js, runs the engine's own routine on every roll for every system and rank and counts the same.

A roll from 0 to 99 is spent across the weights from the last class to the first. A second roll from 0 to 15 picks one of the class's four entries directly if it comes out under 4; otherwise a roll from 0 to the Sunracer's rank value, halved and kept to 3 at most, picks it. The rank value is the player's rank plus 2 ($175B), and the rank rises from 0 to 5 with kills and surrenders ($1B2B), so a Dart becomes a Corsair, a Cruiser a Titan and a Wasp a Vulture more often as the player's repute grows.

Whether an encounter happens at all is decided before this, on each step of HELM travel, from the hours the step takes and the danger level (encounter_roll, $2C1E), and in flight every 256th pass with an eighth of the danger level (frame_update, $1239). Ja-Karn, the Manchi system, has danger 85 and sends nothing but Wasps and Vultures.

09 · Ships

Each model carries its own drawing order

The flight engine draws every ship, base and planet from fourteen model files and the two models built into MAIN, the Sunracer and the missile. A ship is seven to eleven flat faces, and the drawer never sorts them: each face names the plane that decides whether it is drawn before the others or after. Drag the ship to turn it.

Drawn by the page's port of draw_model ($7C61) at the engine's multicolour resolution: the points built from the model's axis vectors, the faces taken in the three passes of draw_polygons ($7ED6), and each filled with set_colour's patterns ($8F80). A test, test_ships.js, runs the engine's own draw_model on all 20 models from about 6,000 eyes and gets the same faces in the same order with the same colours, and runs set_colour on every colour code. The figures beside it are the type tables at $08D5-$0CB0; the Sunracer's are the fittings copy_ship_stats ($1737) copies in, as the listing's snapshot holds them, early in a game.

A model file opens with five instructions that put the address of its header where the drawer looks and jump to the drawer (draw_model, $7C61), so the engine draws an object by calling its model like a routine. The header holds five counts and the addresses of fifteen tables. Every point is either the model's centre or another point, plus or minus one of a short list of axis vectors, and the vectors are stored as logarithms. Scaling a model to its type's size is then one addition to each logarithm (prepare_model, $7C9B), which is how the planetoid, the planets and the station share one model file, PLA.

Instead of sorting, each face carries a plane entry that names one of the model's normals and a side of it. Before drawing, face_signs ($7E66) works out which side of each normal's plane the eye is on. The first pass draws the faces on the far side of their plane from the eye, the second the faces that name no plane, the third the faces on the eye's side. A face turned away from the eye takes its back colour, and a back colour of $FF leaves it out. Draw face by face to watch the order.

A colour is a fill pattern from $F1C8: a pattern for the even rows and one for the odd rows, making the four bit pairs solid or six checkerboards of two of them. Two of the bit pairs take their colour from the screen matrix, and the face rewrites one nybble of the matrix byte, or both, in every 4-by-8 cell it touches. A face drawn later can change the colour of an earlier face's pixels in the cells they share, and the drawer leaves those clashes on screen.

Beams and collisions never look at the model. trace_beam ($2189) tests each point along the beam against a box round the target's centre, its type's radius plus 2 units each way, and check_collisions ($6AB3) tests boxes of the two radii added. Show the hit box: the fighters fit inside theirs, but the Cruiser's nose, the Vulture's wings and most of the Titan stick out, so a beam can cross a Vulture's wing tip on the screen and do no damage.

10 · Combat

The computer pilots think on one pass in 17

Every computer ship flies by the same rules: find the ship its faction likes least, decide whether to fight it or run, pick one of ten tactics and steer to hold the target at that tactic's angle off the nose. This fight between two Imperial ships and two Manchi was run on the engine's own code. Drag to look round it.

The recording: the engine's main loop ($0DED) run for 2,000 passes in the kit's 6502 simulator, with the Sunracer sitting still, nothing pressed and the drawing skipped. The four ships were made with create_object ($1949) at the positions they start from here. A test, test_combat.js, runs the fight again and checks every position, heading, hit point count, tactic and target sampled every 8 passes, and every launch, hit, ram and kill. The models are drawn three times their size, so that the fighters show; the grid is the Sunracer's level, 100 units a square, and the lines drop each ship to it.

Choosing a target

Once in 17 passes ship_ai ($3689) runs for every computer ship; on every third of those runs a ship without a target looks for one and a ship with a target reconsiders it. The search (ai_find_enemy, $3811) takes the ship whose faction its own faction likes least, hostile before unfriendly, and a ship that finds no enemy takes the target of a ship its faction is allied to. How the four factions stand to one another is a table of sixteen bytes (faction_table, $38B1):

Toward the player each faction takes the player's standing with it, from $072E, which the player's kills move between −2 and 2. The Manchi's entry is the byte after the Imperium's, which is also the count of the beam weapon, $FE, so the Manchi are always hostile (Discoveries). In this fight the Wasp and the Vulture shoot at the Sunracer between their attacks on the Imperial ships.

A ship that is fighting and more than about 333 units from its target ($0861, a logarithm) pursues it; nearer, it changes tactic one time in ten that it reconsiders (ai_tactic, $375F): from cruising or pursuing to its type's own tactic, from any other to one of seven at random.

Ten tactics

A tactic is four numbers: how to steer, the angle to keep the target off the nose, a speed and a rule for the speed. The angle makes the moves: a ship closing in keeps its target 22 degrees off the nose, a circling ship 67.5 degrees, and an eluding one about 90.

steer_toward ($3D69) turns the target's direction into two angles in the ship's own frame, one across and one up or down, takes the tactic's angle off the first and divides each by the type's turning step. The quotients are how many steps the ship will turn and pitch, one step a pass on each, and the ship steers again only when it has stopped turning (ai_steer, $3CAB). Steering never rolls a computer ship. Its roll comes only from random spins: the swerve tactic, a hit, or one time in eight that it has nothing to chase (random_rates, $3CDC).

Firing

A ship that is fighting or fleeing fires when its target is under 400 units away ($08A4), the reach of a beam: 50 steps of 8 units in trace_beam. On each pass it thinks, it fires three times in ten, and three times in ten of those it launches a missile instead of firing the beam (ai_fire, $38C1). The fighters, the Wasp, the Dart and the Hunter, fire the beam only with the target within about 30 degrees of the nose; the larger ships, whose beam entries have the top bit set, fire it in any direction. The TAC prints ranges at four times these units, in metres (tac_range, $33F8), so a beam reaches 1,600 m.

The shields halve a hit once for each step of strength, the fore shield for a hit from in front and the aft shield for one from behind (shield_reduce, $6F88). A beam, and a plasma torpedo, gets through one step of them (beam_hit, $2222), so a fore shield of 3 halves a power-3 beam twice, to 5, and an SM-1 missile's 100 three times, to 12. A hit of 10 or more, after the shields, knocks out one of the ship's systems one time in five, and that hit takes nothing off the hit points (damage, $6EA0). Ships that touch ram each other: each takes eight times their relative speed, of which only the low byte counts, and they bounce apart as in an elastic collision (ram, $6BC3). The Cruiser and the Hunter, on the same side, collide seven times while both chase the Wasp.

11 · Flight

From the keyboard the Sunracer turns over eleven times faster than a fighter

Each press of a turn key adds a 1024th of a turn to the Sunracer's turn rate, up to 70, and the ship turns by its rates on every pass until S stops it. A computer ship turns by its type's fixed step, 2.11 degrees a pass for the nimblest.

test_combat.js runs turn_objects ($7634) on the engine with a rate of 1 and measures the turn: a 1024th of a turn for the Sunracer, and each type's step for the computer ships. A computer ship turns one step a pass on each of its axes while it has steps left (turn_ship, $76DD); the Sunracer turns its rates' worth on every pass (turn_by_rates, $9037).

Speed works two ways. The computer ships always fly in cruise, and so does the Sunracer until the player switches. In cruise a ship has a set speed, and cruise_thrust ($7540) moves its velocity toward the nose's direction at that speed by at most the type's thrust each pass: a Dart from a standstill reaches speed 70 in 224 passes, a Titan in 374. Speed 70 is 4.4 units a pass, and the readout shows 16 times the velocity's length (speed_of, $7C21). Newtonian flight, CTRL-E or F7, is the player's alone: the velocity changes only while + or − is held, by the thrust along the nose, so the ship keeps drifting the way it was going whichever way it turns, up to a speed of 70 (apply_thrust, $74D3). The Sunracer's thrust is its engine's, times 8 over 8 plus the cargo slots in use.

Space is a cube. Each object's position is three 24-bit numbers, a whole part of 16 bits and a fraction of 8, and its heading is three rows of three logarithms, the ship's axes as seen from outside (slot_orient, $044B). Steering uses all three axes: a ship pitches toward a target above it as readily as it turns toward one beside it. The planets, the stars and the black hole pull every ship toward them, the pull's strength from the type's scale, and the planetoids do not pull (gravity, $787B). A ship more than about 2,500 units from the Sunracer is removed on the next 256th pass (frame_update, $1239), which is how a fight that drifts away ends. In the fight above, the ships spread over a few hundred units on every axis.

13 · HIVE!

HIVE! pays only from the fifth drop

The cabinet in the cantinas is a game of its own, loaded over the conversations: a trooper drops onto an island, destroys a number of Manchi mounds and runs back to the drop ship.

From hive_game ($57BE) and hive_game_over ($57F8).

Drop n has 3 × n mounds to destroy, and there is no last drop: the game ends only when two Manchi stand next to the trooper at once (hive_caught, $5947). Caught on drop 5 or later, the trooper is paid (n − 4) × 8 credits; caught earlier, the trooper is torn in half and paid nothing. A credit buys a game. The island is loaded again before every game (hive_menu, $572F), and the mounds rise on it during play, up to five at once.

The Manchi chase the trooper with the routine the bases' guards use (station move_chase, $A785), and the screen is the chart drawer's window round the trooper. A reversed branch keeps the wait between mounds at its shortest from the first drop (Discoveries). Maps draws the island.

14 · Text

The game's 4,105 texts are Huffman-coded on the disk

The conversations, the QUERY notes and most of the engine's messages are not in the programs. They are read from side 2 a block at a time and decoded one character at a time with a Huffman tree of 136 bytes.

The codes are read from huffman_tree ($9D66) by the page, walking it as decode_char ($9CE0) does. A test, test_odds.js, feeds each code to the engine's routine and checks the character it gives. A character the tree leaves out costs the escape and 7 bits more.

The tree is a list of pairs, a 0 bit taking the left of a pair and a 1 bit the right. An entry below $80 is the distance on to the next pair, and an entry of $80 and up is a character. Space takes 3 bits, e takes 4, and capitals mostly 9 or more, which suits text that is mostly lower case.

A text is named by a number from $C000, where an address would be. resolve_text ($9BB7) takes a number from $C000 to $CFFF as a disk text, subtracts the counts in SPB's table at $BD00 to find the block of 512 bytes that holds it, reads the block from disk block $01AD on, skips the texts before it and decodes it into $BF00.

15 · Copy protection

Only a saved game has to answer the manual

The manual question comes only when the player loads an old game. A new game starts without it.

All 32 questions

The questions from manual_questions ($76C8), chosen as password_check ($74D2) chooses them.

The question is one of 32 records, each a page, paragraph, sentence and word of the Sunracer Owner's Guide and the word in capitals, picked by the frame counter AND 31. The check compares only the first four letters. A wrong answer shows ACCESS DENIED over the star field, which the typed answer has altered (Discoveries), and loads the title again (access_denied_key, $7965).
