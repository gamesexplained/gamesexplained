1986 · Hewson Consultants

Uridium

Each Super-Dreadnought is assembled from 148 map pieces when its level starts, and every collision in the game is worked out from the characters of that map; the video chip's collision registers are never read.

01 · The game

A strafing run over a Dreadnought

The player flies a Manta fighter low over the hull of an enemy Super-Dreadnought, one per planet, fifteen in all.

Each level is one Dreadnought, a long ship of walls, plating and targets that scrolls past sideways. The Manta flies over it in either direction. Up and down move it across the screen, and left and right speed it up or slow it down. Slowed below a crawl, it loops over and comes back facing the other way. Walls are fatal; anything that stands out from the plating can be shot.

Fighters come in waves. When the level's last wave has been launched, Land now! flashes at the top of the screen. The Manta lands on the runway at the Dreadnought's right-hand end, a bonus game decides how much the ship's destruction is worth, and the Manta takes off over the hull as it vaporises.

02 · The screen

One frame, rebuilt from memory

A frame of play drawn by this page from the memory and video registers recorded in the emulator.

Drawn by the site's renderer from a recorded frame: all 104,448 pixels match the emulator's picture. The status lines use one character set at $7000 and the playfield another at $7800.

Three raster interrupts split the screen. At line 82, $3F38 switches to the playfield's character set and multicolour mode and sets the fine scroll. At line 97, $3F73 clears a flag that lets the main loop start its frame. At line 252, $3F00 switches back for the bottom status line and the top of the next frame, and runs the sound driver.

The playfield's background colour is white. Space is a character filled with solid colour, drawn in black, so the white background shows only in the hull's plating. The stars are characters too. They stay still while the screen scrolls a character at a time because the game rewrites the star characters' pixels every frame with a one-pixel mask chosen by the fine scroll ($2ED7).

03 · The Dreadnought

Every Dreadnought is built from the same 148 pieces

Each Dreadnought is 512 characters long and 17 high, assembled at the start of the level from a list of pieces.

The map as build_map ($2CB2) builds it, from the level's list and the piece store at $E100, in the level's colours and tile set, 40 columns wide as the screen shows it. The port matches the game's own builder, run in a 6502 simulator, byte for byte on all fifteen levels and the title message (test_ports.py). The Levels tab shows every Dreadnought whole.

A level's list fills columns 64 to 511 with pieces side by side, and a second list lays more pieces over them at a given row and column, the targets, ports and markings among them. Columns 0 to 63 are open space where the Manta turns.

Every Dreadnought draws on the same store of 148 pieces at $E100. The routine that indexes the store ($2C66) never reads the level number: a level chooses only its two lists, its colours and one of three tile sets, which is copied into the characters $80 to $FF ($1A54). The same piece therefore looks different from one Dreadnought to the next.

Drawn in the tiles and colours of the Dreadnought chosen above.

Choose a piece to see where this Dreadnought uses it.

The 148 pieces in store order, numbered as $2C66 numbers them, each a set of columns stored bottom up. A faded piece is not used by the Dreadnought chosen above. Choose a piece to outline it on the map and move the map to it; the buttons step through its uses. Where each piece is laid was recorded by the same port, and replaying those placements rebuilds every map exactly.

The pieces range from piece 1, a single column holding one space that a 0 in the list repeats for open space, to sections of hull fifteen columns wide and the full seventeen rows high. Pieces 31 to 80 and 87 are the giant letters and digits of the title message; the letters it does not spell can still appear, since a new high score writes its initials into the message ($17F8) and the title builds the message again each time round ($2415). No level's list and no letter names pieces 18 and 27.

The characters decide what is solid. Codes $80 to $8F are walls, and the Manta is destroyed if one is under it ($27D5). Codes $90 to $9F are targets: a shot that reaches any character of one turns the whole target into wreckage, each character becoming the one $20 below it ($2AE9). The game never reads the video chip's collision registers; every hit is worked out from these codes.

04 · The Manta

The Manta's manoeuvres

The Manta has 46 frames: two cycles of a roll, one facing each way, and the frames of a half-loop seen end-on.

The animation tables at $31E2, played as $2713 plays them, one frame every other game frame. The shadow is the Manta's frame + $30, placed as $27BA places it: right by the height and down by half of it. The half-loops change the height by the table at $32F5.

Fire held for seven frames and then up or down rolls the Manta between level and edge-on ($2576). Level, it collides with the three characters on its row and those above and below its middle; edge-on, only with the three on its row, so the roll takes it through gaps one character high. Slowing below speed 2 starts a half-loop ($268C): the Manta climbs, turns end-on and comes out facing the other way with a push of speed. While its height is $14 or more, bullets and mines pass under it.

The shadow is sprite 7. Its frames are not stored: at every restart the game builds them from the Manta's frames at $5000, ORing each pair of pixels together so that the shape becomes a solid multicolour silhouette ($25E5), and stores them as frames $70 to $9D.

05 · Waves

Fighters on numbered paths

Each Dreadnought sends its fighters in a fixed order of formations, up to six fighters each.

The level's wave list ($C590 on), each wave's type from its record ($C200, 16 bytes a wave) and the type's fighter, drawn from $6800. The type tables at $36F3 set the rest.

A wave record names up to six paths, one per fighter, their start heights, the frames between fighters entering, the side they come from and the wave's type ($1A98). A path is a list of steps: accelerate one way or the other, climb, dive, stop, fire, or steer towards the Manta's height, each for a number of frames. The type picks the fighter's picture and sets its speed, its climb, its bullet, its score (100 to 1000) and its chance of firing, to which the game adds half the lives left, the level and the difficulty.

A new wave starts when the last has gone, checked every 64 frames. A wave shot down to the last fighter counts as a formation, worth 100 at the end of the level. While no wave is flying, a homing mine may materialise over one of the generator ports that flash on the hull ($1F7F).

06 · Landing

The landing test is one character and one attitude

After the last wave, the Manta lands only over one particular character, flying level and facing right.

Once the level's last wave has been launched, the Manta lands the moment the character under its middle is $6F, the end of the runway, while it flies level and facing right ($166D). Facing left, or rolled, the Manta passes over the same character without landing.

07 · The bonus game

The fuel rod chamber

The bonus for destroying the ship is a timing game against a countdown.

The chamber's rule from $114E: six steps from the bottom up, the countdown stepping every 256 - $310A[level] frames, each offer the countdown plus the bonus so far. The flashing here is paced by a timer, not the game's frame counts, and the lit side is random as in the game.

After landing, the fuel rod chamber offers a value and Quit in turn at each of six steps. The value is the countdown digit plus the bonus taken so far, and the bonus starts at the level number, so the first offer is 900 + 100 × level. Fire on the value takes it and moves up a step; fire on Quit leaves with the bonus. Letting the countdown reach zero costs a life ($15BD).

The bonuses are then counted up, and the Manta takes off over the hull while the Dreadnought vaporises behind it, one column at a time from a ragged random edge ($1413, $16D2). Its surface can still be strafed for points on the way.

08 · Quirks

Quirks

After Land now!, fighters keep coming. Once a level's list of waves is used up, the game picks one of four filler waves at random ($12 to $15), and their fighters score nothing and count no formation ($1E4B).

An extra Manta comes with every 10,000 points, at the carry out of the score's thousands byte ($19F5). The lives are kept in BCD and stop at 99.

After the fifteenth Dreadnought the game starts again at the first, with the difficulty raised by $10, up to three times. The difficulty adds to the fighters' chance of firing and shortens the chamber's countdown.

The disk protects the game with its timing. A stage of the loader sends the drive code that finds a mark on track 39 and measures the next ten sync marks; whether each of the last eight is at least as long as the second gives one bit of a key byte. The main file is decoded with that byte, and only the original disk's $97 gives the game.
