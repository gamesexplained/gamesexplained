1986 · Hewson Consultants

Uridium

Everything that moves in Uridium is one of eight hardware sprites: the Manta, its shadow, and six enemy sprites that take turns as fighters, bullets, homing mines and explosions. The Dreadnought itself is characters, 128 that every level shares and 128 that are the level's own.

01 · Everything that moves

Every sprite, moved by the game's own code

Each picture here is placed, coloured and stepped by the game's own routines, run on the kit's 6502 over the listing's memory a frame at a time, a fiftieth of a second, in the colours of the Dreadnought chosen below.

The sprites are drawn by the game's own code in the built site.

The Manta and its shadow

The Manta has 46 frames: a roll facing right ($40-$4F), a roll facing left ($50-$5F) and the end-on frames of a half-loop ($60-$6D). manta_animate plays its twelve manoeuvres from the tables at $31E2, a frame every other game frame; here they play in turn. The shadow is sprite 7, the Manta's frame plus $30 in dark grey, moved right by the Manta's height and down by half of it, so it drops away as a half-loop climbs.

The shadows' shapes are not in the game's file. thicken_shapes ($25E5) builds all 46 from the Manta's frames at every restart: any pair of pixels with one bit set gets both, which turns the multicolour Manta into a solid hires outline.

The launch

Before each life the Manta turns through its right-facing roll, one frame a frame, until fire is pressed or 65 frames have passed ($0CB4). Then launch_sequence ($2839) puts up the transporter, six multicolour pieces in two rows, with a door of bars at its front. The door opens a shape every three frames, then the Manta takes its place and slides out a pixel every two frames, and the transporter falls away to the left, faster as it goes.

The fighters

Sixteen types, each stored twice, facing each way ($A0-$AF and $B0-$BF). A wave takes its shape once, when it starts: $A0 or $B0 plus its type, by the sign of the Manta's speed at that moment ($1B49), and its fighters never change it, so they are not animated. Every fighter is multicolour, in the level's fighter colour with the Manta's two shared colours.

Bullets, mines and explosions

The fighters' bullets are six shapes, picked by the wave's type (type_bullet, $3723). A shot-down fighter, a bullet at the end of its life and a burst mine all become the same explosion, $14 to $1D a step every other frame (explosion_move, $1C37). A homing mine runs the same shapes backwards as it materialises, a step every fourth frame, and then flies as the cross $11 (mine_appear, mine_move). None of these sets a colour: sprite_put writes only the shape and the place, so each takes the colour its sprite last had, which in play is the fighter colour the wave gave it.

The Manta destroyed

manta_explodes ($245B) turns the Manta's sprite into a fireball, $30, in yellow with orange and black. Every three frames it throws one more of the eight sprites out from the fireball at a random offset, and steps every sprite on one shape until each has gone past $3A.

Every shape in the bank that holds a picture is drawn above. The twelve that are blank are ends: $13 is where a materialising mine turns into the cross, $1E and $3B are where the explosions switch their sprites off, and $1F, $3C-$3F, $6E and $6F with their shadows $9E and $9F fill out the blocks. How the sprites collide with the map is on How it works: Shots and bullets and Homing mines.

02 · The characters

Each Dreadnought is drawn from one of three character sets

The playfield is 17 rows of multicolour characters. Codes $00-$7F are the same on every level; $80-$FF are copied in for each level from one of three sets.

Point at a character to see its code.

The fixed half from $7800; the level's half copied to $7C00 by copy_tile_set ($1A54) from $D400, $D800 or $DC00, by the table at $E040; the colours from level_colours ($2F33), both run on the kit's 6502. Each cell is drawn in the level's map colour with its two shared colours, on the white background.

Walls are $80-$8F and targets $90-$9F, both in the level's own half, so each set gives its Dreadnoughts their own walls and targets. A target shot down becomes its wreck, the code $20 lower, $70-$7F in the shared half. The runway's end is $6F, and the stars are $42 and $43, rewritten every frame (Stars). The three sets are shared out over the fifteen Dreadnoughts: six use the set at $DC00, five the set at $D400 and four the set at $D800.

03 · The status lines

The status font is printed two rows tall with a gap in every other line

The score lines above the playfield use a font of their own, in hires, each character printed as a top half and a bottom half.

Point at a character to see its codes.

The font at $7000, as spread_font ($30A0) leaves it: code n is the top half and n + $80 the bottom half, drawn here one above the other in white on black.

The game's file holds the font eight rows to a character. spread_font runs once at start-up and spreads each character over two: the top four rows go to the top half at $7000 and the bottom four to the bottom half at $7400, each row followed by a blank one, which gives the status text its scan-line look. Among the 128 are the digits, the lower case, wide capitals in two halves, the joystick and colour icons and the URIDIUM logo.

04 · The fuel rod chamber

The bonus screen has a character set of its own

After a landing, the hull wipes away and the fuel rod chamber takes its place: a grey panel of paired slots joined by arrows, where fire at the right moment takes a bonus. It is drawn from a screen and a character set the rest of the game never uses, kept under the I/O chips at $D200.

The chamber rebuilt from memory. fuel_rod_chamber ($114E) runs on the kit's 6502 after the wipe (shift_rows_left, shift_rows_right): it copies the screen from $A600 and the characters from $D200 to $7C00, then fades the three backgrounds in to white, light grey and dark grey (chamber_fade, $38DE). The wipe leaves every panel cell at colour $F8, so the fourth multicolour is black — the slots. The countdown digit and the flashing bonus are drawn only once play reaches the panel, so they are blank here.

Point at a character to see its code.

The chamber's characters $80-$BF, copied to $7C00 from $D200, in the panel's colours: white, light grey and dark grey from the faded backgrounds, and black for the slot interiors. Codes $A0-$A3 spell “Quit”; the arrows, slot ends and digit box fill the rest. The shared characters $00-$7F are the same set the status lines use.
