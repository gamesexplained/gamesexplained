The title tune and the effects

Each button runs the game’s own code from its listing on the site’s model of the C64’s 6502 processor, heard through the site’s model of its SID sound chip. The tune is set up the way the title screen sets it up and played a frame at a time; each effect is started through the game’s own play_sound ($2509).

How this player was checked

The same calls were made in the kit’s model of the whole C64, with a raster interrupt calling the player once a frame as the game does, and every write to the sound chip compared, in order: 3,000 frames of the tune and 300 of each effect, all the same (test_music.js).

The tune

A music player with its maker’s name inside

The tune is played by a separate music player at $E000, whose text reads “MICROPEARL MUSIC PLAYER COPYRIGHT 1983 JOHN A. FITZPATRICK” ($E315). Nothing on screen shows it.

The title screen’s raster interrupt calls the player once a frame (irq_menu_bottom, $152C, calling $E006). It reads three streams of notes and commands, one for each voice of the SID ($E8D0, $E97D, $EA31): a byte with bit 7 clear is a note, and the commands that follow set the next notes’ length, the waveform, the envelope, an instrument, the pulse width, the filter, or the tempo (voice_next_note, $E578).

Before it starts the tune, the title screen writes a song list of song 0 followed by $FF, which sends the player back to the start of the list ($06CC-$06DA). The tune therefore repeats for as long as the title screen is up, every 1,154 frames, about 23 seconds. In play the game has no music, only effects.

The player’s song table has room for a second song, but song 1’s three voice addresses point into the sound-effect code at $EB00 and beyond (song_table, $E8C0). Nothing ever asks for it.

The effects

Three players, one per voice

The effects have a player of their own, copied three times, once for each voice of the SID ($EB03, $EF73, $F073), and all three run once a frame (frame_work, $15A8). An effect is a script of steps, each a command byte followed by a duration, a note, an envelope, a pulse width, or a waveform as the command needs (sfx_scripts, $EBE5).

play_sound ($2509) puts a new effect in the first free voice. When all three are busy it takes the voice playing the effect of lowest priority, as long as the new one’s priority is at least as high ($2572). There are fifteen scripts; nothing asks for effects 3 and 8.

The loading tune

Music: the cassette brings a tune of its own

The cassette plays a second tune while the game loads, over a screen of credits in white on black. It starts with the credits and stops when the last of the game is in, two and a half minutes or so later. The cassette's own code plays it, not the Micropearl player, and the Datasoft disk shows its credits in silence.

The player runs the cassette's own code and notes, as the cassette leaves them in memory, on the kit's 6502 simulator, once a frame as the cassette does. It was checked against the cassette loading in VICE: after each of the tune's first 6,200 frames, two minutes of it, the sound chip's registers are the player's.

The notes and everything else the tune does come from one list ($EF80), read a byte at a time: a note and how long it lasts, or a run of bytes to write into memory, the sound chip's registers among them. It plays at full volume on the pulse waveform alone, and never uses the filter. After its first 38 seconds it repeats every 1 minute 18 seconds.
