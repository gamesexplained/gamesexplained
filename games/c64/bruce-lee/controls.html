One joystick and fire do everything Bruce can do. In play the keyboard has just four keys, and a menu key turns the stick a quarter turn for a left-handed grip.

The joystick

Moves

Player 1’s Bruce reads the joystick in port 1. The C64-Wiki gives port 22, but on the disk studied port 2 never moved him: it is the second player’s, or a human Yamo’s ($169D, $16CF).

StickMove
left or rightwalk
fire while walkingflying kick, once he has walked for four passes ($1E70, $1EB1)
fire, standingpunch
upjump straight up, or climb a net
up-left or up-rightjump sideways
downlie flat, or climb down a net

The moves are chosen in choose_action ($1D77) when Bruce’s last move has finished.

In play the game looks at only those four keys. scan_play_keys ($1462) drives one row of the keyboard for each and reads one column, from a table of four pairs ($1488), instead of scanning the whole keyboard as the menu does (read_key, $139E).

Left-handed play

A quarter turn of the stick

A left-handed player can hold the joystick turned on its side. The game turns the readings back: pushed left reads as up, right as down, up as right, and down as left (lefthand_turn, $4A85). The setting is kept for each joystick ($1383, $1384), and the menu shows “JOYSTICK #1 IS IN LEFT HANDED MODE” when it changes.

Tested in the emulator: with joystick 1 set left-handed, the stick pushed left read $0E, up, in the game’s copy of the stick ($98); set right-handed, it read $0B, left.
