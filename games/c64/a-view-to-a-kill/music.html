Two tunes shared between four programs, a gunshot, and speech played by switching the volume on and off.

01 · The tunes

Two tunes, each kept in the memory under the operating system

The credits thank Tony Crowther for the arrangement of the James Bond theme, and name Duran Duran and John Barry for the title music.

The game has two tunes, each used by two programs. Both keep their notes at $E000 and up, in the memory hidden under the operating system's ROM. To read a note the player switches everything but RAM out for a moment (the intro $C773, Paris $7B73).

Which tune is the Bond theme and which is the title song was not checked by ear. In both drivers a note is three bytes, two for the frequency and one for its length, and the notes step after three frames and then two, in turn: 20 steps a second on a European machine. The intro's driver adds vibrato by adding 0, 4, 8 or 12 to the frequency each frame ($C798). One pass of the intro's tune is 5,106 steps, about 102 seconds.

Paris lets the player choose on the instruction page: the tune, the effects, or both ($4F30). In the mine the two effects, a beep for yes and a buzz for no, borrow the tune's second voice ($3B7D, $3B9C).

02 · The intro

A gunshot with the interrupts off, and the tune parked while it sounds

The gunshot is voice 3's noise at a fixed frequency, played with the interrupts off so nothing else runs ($CABC). Before it, the intro saves the music driver's whole state to $9F00 and puts it back afterwards, so the tune picks up where it stopped ($C1B0, $C1C0).

03 · Speech

Speech made of one-bit samples, played on the volume control

The intro ends with "Now, James, your mission begins" on screen, and Paris speaks when the chase is won or lost.

Both players work the same way. They switch the sound chip's master volume between 0 and 15, one bit of sample at a time, with the interrupts off (the intro $A000, Paris $1000). Each change of volume makes a click in the output, and a fast enough stream of clicks is heard as a voice.
