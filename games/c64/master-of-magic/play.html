The game, ported routine by routine from its 6502 code to JavaScript and checked against the original pass by pass. It starts where a new game starts, with Thelric's parting words.

Keys. Arrow keys or W, A, S, D are the joystick, space or Enter is fire, and M switches the music off and on. The screen takes the keys once it has been clicked. In a menu the stick moves the black cursor and fire picks. RUN walks until fire is pressed; every other verb takes its own time and then the menu comes back.

01 · The port

What runs here

The routines that run a game are all here, rewritten one for one: the main menu $11A2 and its chooser $1320, every verb the menu offers, the message window $2941, the line of sight $0B25, the player's movement $0D44, the creatures with their pursuit and attacks $2205, combat, the four spells, the potions, the picture strip $1515 with its DEAD labels, the clock and the bars, death and the win. The demonstration is the game's own: its recorded controls at $A543, read back by $179D wherever the game reads the joystick.

The port runs on a copy of the game's memory as it loads: the map, the character set, the tables, the texts and the pictures, 27,849 bytes, and none of the code. Each routine reads and writes that copy at the game's own addresses, so the tables that overlap in the original, and the pointers its routines share, behave as they do on the C64. The screen is drawn from the same memory the way the video chip draws it, in three bands. Set beside two screenshots from the emulator, the first menu and a moment of the demonstration, it matches them in every pixel but one, where a band changes. The music is the page's port of the driver, playing what the game asks for: the theme in play, one tune for a death and one for the win.

The first game uses the random numbers the loaded seed gives ($07, $AA). Later games carry on from where the last one stopped, as on the C64. The demonstration starts from the memory as loaded, with its own seed ($F8, $18), the way the first one after loading does, so it plays the same every time.

02 · The test

How it was checked

The built-in demonstration was recorded in the emulator at the start of every world pass $08CC, from its new game to the last pass before the player dies: 2,371 passes. The port was given nothing but "no key pressed" at every read of the controls and replayed the recording itself. At all 2,371 passes its memory equals the game's in every one of the 6,462 bytes the recording holds: the variables, the screen, the object and creature tables and the map. Six bytes are left out: the interrupt's frame counter $03A0 and band number $03B7, the wall latch it fills $038D, the move tick's copy of the frame counter $03A1, and the two registers the random routine saves $0385 and $0386.

The demonstration walks, bumps into walls, opens and closes doors, picks up, examines and casts spells, and its player is attacked and dies. The verbs it never uses were checked another way. Fourteen more recordings in the same format ran twice: through the game's own code in a 6502 simulator, and through the port. They cover the win, INVENTORY, WEAR, TAKE OFF, PUT IN, TAKE OUT, LOOK IN, SWAP, the weight limit, NO FREE HAND, ATTACK with each kind of weapon, every spell and UNCAST, the shield wearing off, all five potions, death, doors, the pool's edge and the menu cursor. At every pass and at the end they agree in all 16,121 bytes compared, colour memory, sprites and the picture strip included. The simulator runs no interrupt, so these runs leave the wall test out.

Walls are found by the video chip. Once a frame it reports whether the player's dot touches a wall pixel, and the game reads the report before each step. Which screens were on show in those frames depends on how long the game's code took, so the port keeps a clock: it adds up the cycles of each routine, counted from the listing, and tests the dot against the screen at every frame the clock passes. Tested only at the moment of reading, the demonstration goes wrong at pass 1,641. With the clock it matches to the end, and it still does with every count halved or raised by 40 %. The same clock paces the page. It is set to the 9.5 passes a second measured on the C64 while running, where the game's clock gains one second every 8 passes.

03 · Not ported

What is left out

  • The title screen, the story scroller and the instruction pages. After a game over, fire starts a new game here; on the C64 it goes back to the title.
  • The recorder behind CTRL and R on the title, and the footstep noise the game plays only with the music off.
  • The interrupt itself. Its timing is estimated from the cycle count, so in your own game the pace, and the frame in which a wall bump is seen, can differ a little from a C64's.
