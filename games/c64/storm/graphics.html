1986 · Mastertronic

Storm

Nothing in Storm is a sprite. Every figure is a block of characters that the game builds again, from its picture and the floor beneath it, each time it moves.

01 · Everything that moves

Every figure, moved by the game's own code

Storm and Agravain, the six kinds of monster, the generators they come out of, and the two shots. A player can take a step every other pass of the game's main loop, a monster takes one every ten passes, and a shot flies two columns a pass.

The figures are drawn by the game's own code in the built site.

Each tile is a machine of its own: the game's routines on the kit's 6502 in the page, over the listing's bytes, with the play area cleared. Each player is moved by players_update ($6871) on every other pass, as the main loop calls it, with his joystick held up, up and right in turn, so he walks a square. Each monster is moved by move_monster ($5FB6), which takes one of the ten monster slots a pass, toward a target that moves round the corners of a square whenever the monster reaches it. The shots are both players' fire held, with shots_update ($7B8A) run twice a pass. A pass here takes 2.6 frames, the rate counted in the emulator with six monsters out.

A player has a picture for each of the four ways he can face, and his step and his turn both go through redraw_player. The players' controls run every other pass, so a player walks at most a cell every two passes, about ten cells a second. A turn sets turn_delay to 2 and each run of the controls counts it down before it reads the joystick, so a held turn turns him once every four passes. Agravain's own colour is always red. Storm's colours come from a different row of colour_rows for each way he faces.

Each kind of monster has its own drawing routine, draw_kind1 to draw_kind6 ($6187-$62A3). Kinds 1, 3 and 6 have a picture for each way they face, and a monster faces the way it last stepped, across rather than up or down when it steps both ways at once. Kind 6 also takes the colours of the way it faces. Kinds 2 and 4 have two pictures and swap them at every step, using the top bit of the monster's kind byte to remember which is showing. Kind 5 has four and moves to the next at every step, counting in the kind byte's top four bits. The routine runs even when a monster has nowhere to go, so these three go on changing while they stand still.

A shot is one character, Agravain's $FE and Storm's $FF. At each step shot_step puts back what the shot covered and builds the character again over the next cell's floor from character 7, the shot's pattern. It stops at a wall, a player, an object or the edge of the play area, and hits a generator or a monster. The generators do not move: draw_generators draws each one once, in one of two looks, as the room is drawn.

The players', the monsters' and the generators' pictures are 1,998 bytes at $4E64-$5631, eight bytes to a character. A picture is a block of characters, read across and then down: a player's or a facing monster's is four characters by two rows, 64 bytes, and a kind 2 or kind 4 monster's or a generator's is three by two, 48 bytes. In multicolour each pair of bits is one wide pixel: black, the orange and yellow every character shares, or the character's own colour, which is what changes from row to row of colour_rows.

Nothing points at three pieces of the block. The 30 bytes at $4E64 sit between the last object's picture and Storm's, and are not a whole picture. The 128 bytes at $5142, after the generators, make two pictures four characters wide. The 16 bytes at $5422, between Agravain and kind 6, are two characters. The objects' 28 pictures are kept in a block of their own before these, and How it works draws each of them with what touching it does.

02 · Building a figure

A picture, cut by the floor's mask and laid over it

The floor's patterns stay on top of whoever stands on them. The game does it a character at a time, with a mask for every floor character.

Agravain facing

Room 1, drawn by the game's routines on the page's 6502, with Agravain put over the floor piece made of characters $2A-$31 by load_a, put_player and save_a, as the game puts him in a room. The mask is the eight bytes compose reads from floor_masks ($87CE) for each character under him: light where his picture shows, dark where the floor stays.

A figure owns its characters. Storm has $32-$39 and Agravain $3A-$41, the room's eight objects have eight each from $42, its five generators six each from $82, and the ten monster slots eight each from $A0. When a figure moves, restore_bg puts back the characters it saved from under it, after waiting for raster line $F0 so the change is not seen half done. compose then saves the characters at the new place and builds the figure's own: for every byte it ANDs the picture with the mask for the floor character beneath and ORs in that floor character, so floor decorations stay on top and the figure fills the gaps. put_actor writes the figure's characters to the screen with the colours of its row of colour_rows.

The mask for a character is found at (character − $11) × 8 into the table, counted in a single byte. For $31, the bottom right corner of this floor piece, that comes to $100, which wraps to 0: the mask for an empty cell, every bit set. Over that one character nothing is cut out of a figure, so where its pixels meet the floor's, the two patterns are ORed together and the colours mix. The mask that should have been used, eight bytes at $88CE, is never read.
