01 · Where it lives

The whole 64 KB, area by area

The bar is the machine's whole 64 KB, split into the areas the game uses. Pick a kind of area, or click one in the bar, to open it below: each subroutine and table in it, and the code that uses it. Click a subroutine to find it in the map of calls.

Point at a segment to name it. The engine runs from $0C55 to $2FB0, with the cracker's additions at its start; Rob Hubbard's music driver sits on its own at $A000.

02 · Who calls whom

The wiring between the subroutines

Each box is one subroutine, taller when it is longer, in a band for its job. Inside a band, the boxes run from the first reached to the last. Hover over a box to light up its connections, or click it to keep them lit and read what it does. A box marked ▶ shows that routine at work on its own: the game is held still and dimmed while only that routine runs, and a tune is drawn as its notes.

The lines come from the program's own JSR, JMP and branch instructions. Calls made through a pointer are not drawn: the raster interrupt game_irq, the music's music_irq, and the RESTORE restart are reached through vectors, and read_joystick through a call the trainer rewrites, so they start bands of their own. The unused tune's player, tune2_start to tune2_tick, has no callers at all.
