01 · Where it lives

A game in three slices of memory

The top bar is the machine's whole 64 KB. The bars below it zoom into the stretches that hold code, one segment for each subroutine or table.

Point at a segment to name it, and click a subroutine to find it in the map below. The engine runs from $0C55 to $2FB0, with the cracker's additions at its start; Rob Hubbard's music driver sits on its own at $A000.

02 · Who calls whom

The wiring between the subroutines

Each box is one subroutine, taller when it is longer, in a band for its job. Inside a band, the boxes run from the first reached to the last. Hover over a box to light up its connections, or click it to keep them lit and read what it does.

The lines come from the program's own JSR, JMP and branch instructions. Calls made through a pointer are not drawn: the raster interrupt game_irq, the music's music_irq, and the RESTORE restart are reached through vectors, and read_joystick through a call the trainer rewrites, so they start bands of their own. The unused tune's player, tune2_start to tune2_tick, has no callers at all.
