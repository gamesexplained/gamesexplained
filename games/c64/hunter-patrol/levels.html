Four levels over the same endless fields. What changes from one to the next is how many hits each kind of defence takes, and the target at the end.

01 · The four levels

The same landscape, a new target and twice the hits

The game has no map: the hills wrap round every 40 columns and the fields are stripes, so every level is flown over the same country. The level, kept in $44, picks the hit counts and the target.

Each level's target at its nearest size, three sprites side by side, drawn from the level's set of shapes on the Source tab in the ground band's colours. The figures beside it come from $0F60 and $0F40.

The four kinds come in the same order on every level: tanks, lorries, guns, buildings (Gameplay).

set_hits_needed ($0F60) doubles 5 once for each level after the first, and treats the fourth level as the third, so the count stops at 20. The distance bar along the bottom gives each kind a section 40 pixels wide, and bar_fill_bits ($0F40) scales the hits to it with the same doubling, so the bar fills at the same pace on every level however many hits it needs.

The target comes from the table at $8B80, which names $3000, $3800, $9800 and $9000 for the four levels. start_run copies 32 shapes from there into the sprite area at $7800 before each strafing run, and the Graphics tab shows each target at all ten sizes. After a target that took 16 hits or more the level goes up, and after the fourth it goes back to the first ($2EAD), with the lives carried over. A target that took fewer hits means the same level again from its first kind.
