1990 · Thalamus

Creatures

Every creature in the game is a few bytes of script: sprite frames with loops among them, which the engine steps every other frame. Below, all of them move as the game moves them. The three torture chambers have a tab of their own, Maps.

01 · The game

Clyde Radcliff and the Fuzzy Wuzzies

The Fuzzy Wuzzies are small furry aliens from the planet Blot. They crash-landed in the Pacific, swam ashore to an island and built a village there, and the island's demons, who hated the noise, invited them to a party, threw a net over them and carted them off to their torture chambers. The one Fuzzy they missed was Clyde Radcliff, who was being sick in the bushes. The manual says he woke the next morning with breath that could scorch a tree at six paces, and in the game that breath is his strongest weapon.

Each of the three levels is two stages of scrolling landscape, crossed from left to right against a timer. Some of the creatures in them are Magic Potion Creatures, which Clyde collects as money for the witch's shop, visited before every stage after the first, where they buy weapons and clues. After each level's second stage comes a torture chamber: one screen, one of Clyde's friends about to die, and a puzzle that saves him if Clyde solves it in time (Maps steps through each one). The ending follows the third chamber.

02 · The creatures

Every creature, moving as the game moves it

Each world's creatures, with the colours and speeds their levels give them. Pick one to see its frames and the script that plays them.

Speed

Every creature is stepped by the engine's own rules, ported (the cr-anim block in this page), over its script and colour list as the level file holds them, at 50 frames a second. test_gallery.js runs the engine's animate_objects and object_colours on a 6502 simulator beside the port for 3,000 frames of each script here and finds every frame and colour the same. The colours are those of most of the creature's spawn records. The background is the colour behind the top of the world's first stage, or black for a creature drawn in that colour. A few creatures move only when Clyde shoots them, and here they are shot twice a second.

A creature is an object in one of fifteen slots, and the level's spawn records fill the slots as the landscape scrolls in. A record names a type, and the type names an animation script and a speed in the level file's tables (anim_lo, anim_hi and anim_speed, from $8D5A in every level file). The engine's animate_objects reads one byte of the script at a time:

$02-$3Fa sprite frame: one of the engine's shapes, the 64-byte block at $4000 plus 64 times the byte $80-$FFa sprite frame: one of the level file's shapes, found the same way, from $6000 $40-$7Fa loop: the next byte is the distance back, and the body plays once more than the low six bits say $00start the script again $01remove the creature, the end of every death and burst

The engine animates the even slots on one frame and the odd slots on the next, by turning one branch at $C769 from BEQ to BNE and back, so each creature has its turn every other frame. A creature's speed counts those turns. At speed 1 a new byte comes every fourth frame, twelve and a half times a second, and at speed $7F one comes every 256 frames, which is how the still creatures are made. Colour works the same way, from a list of colours with loops of its own (object_colours), which is how the potion creatures change colour.

Some creatures move only when shot. A spawn record whose hit response has both low bits set leaves the animation off (object_spawn, at $20EE), and each shot gives the script one more turn (hit_response_3). The swelling balls of world 2 swell a frame with each shot until they are gone, and level 2.1's end creature, the crescent, changes only when it is hit.

Shapes are shared between scripts. The walking devils of world 1 have five scripts over the same thirteen frames, and one of the five holds a single frame still. A creature too big for one sprite is two or four objects that the level places side by side, like the dragon at the end of level 1.1, a head and a body. Two shorter scripts, types 41 and 42, move the dragon's shapes again, mostly in level 1.2.

03 · Music

Music: in a level the tune has one voice, and the effects two

The page runs the game's own music player: its code and every tune's bytes, from the listings, on a small 6502 core inside the cr-music block, once a frame, through the site's model of the SID. test_music.js runs the same bytes on the kit's 6502 simulator and compares every write to the SID, frame by frame, for every tune: to its end, or for 20,000 frames where it repeats. The level tunes play as they do in a level, on the third voice alone; the rest play on all three.

One player serves the whole game. The engine keeps it at $0334, and the title carries its own copy of the same code, assembled $CC bytes higher, because the title is a separate program. A tune is a header of two voice offsets and three voices of two-byte commands that call shared sections, and all eighteen tunes are in that form.

Before a level starts, music_hooks_ingame writes three bytes into the player's own instructions ($0456, $087C, $0887): where its voice loop stops, and which registers its copy to the chip starts and stops at. In a level the loop runs for the third voice alone, and the copy skips the registers of the first two, which are left to the sound effects. On the Get Ready screen, left and right on the joystick turn the level's music off and on.

04 · Clyde

Secret: Clyde can take one hit in every life

The manual says nothing about it, but each life has a spare hit. stage_start, after every Get Ready screen, sets $12A0 to 1. The first touch stops Clyde for 40 frames and leaves him untouchable for 127 more, flashing light red and black (player_hurt, player_dying_wait), and only the second touch kills him. Water kills at once, as do level 3.2's spikes, the timer running out, any touch while he rides level 3's broomstick, and every touch in a torture chamber.

05 · Weapons

Potion creatures buy the weapons, one grade at a time

Fire held and let go breathes the flame. The manual says to hold fire for a second; the charge counts 36 frames, about three quarters of one, and a released charge turns both of Clyde's shot sprites into the flame for 24 frames (player_fire). Down and fire opens the weapons tablet, which offers only what the witch has sold him. While the tablet is open, the engine keeps the flame's eleven sprite shapes aside and puts the tablet's in their blocks (tablet_swap_step).

The witch sells each weapon a grade at a time, up to the third, for Magic Potion Creatures of each kind (item_cost_1 to item_cost_3). A creature on a double-height sprite is worth five. Droopy comes at its first grade, so the shop sells it from the second.

Side A's MENU is the title. Fire there loads GAME, the engine, which stays in memory to the end and loads everything else from side B with its own fast loader. A file's name is a letter for what it is (L for a level, T for a chamber, C for the ending) and the stage number as a letter: LA, LB, TC, and on to CJ. The attract sequence and the witch's shop take turns in one block of memory at $E000. The shop arrives with level 1.2 over the attract code, so a game over reloads the attract block from a copy of its own on side B, a file called 1@.

Each world is one file loaded to $5800-$ABFF, with its character set, its sprites, its tunes and its own code at $A300. The second stage is a smaller file loaded over the first from $8700: a new map, new spawn records and new text over the same graphics and code. The engine calls a level's code through five hooks, which is how world 2 adds swimming, with an OXYGEN bar that empties in about 31 seconds, and world 3 a broomstick that flies in four directions for about as long. Each new stage adds a life.

07 · Hidden prizes

Secret: some creatures hide a prize until their group dies

A spawn record of type 55 shows the blank sprite, and no shot or touch reaches it. It belongs to a linked group, and killing any creature of the group kills the rest of it, the hidden one included, which then turns into its death type: a potion creature or an extra life (object_kill, object_kill_linked). Twenty-one such records hide ten potion creatures and nine extra lives across the six stages. Of the other two, one in level 1.1 hides only a puff of smoke, and one in level 3.1 belongs to no group, so nothing ever kills it.

08 · Scenery

Distant scenery scrolls by rotating its characters

The backgrounds move more slowly than the landscape, though the machine has one scrolling layer. While the screen scrolls, the level's frame routine rotates the bit patterns of the background's characters a pixel against the scroll, each strip at its own rate: in world 2 the green grass every fourth frame and the woods every other one, in level 3.1 the distant graves at three paces ($A8C3, $A95F).

09 · The cheat

Secret: the ending suggests the cheat to those who did not use it

Holding CTRL and 2 on the high-score table, once the title tune has ended, turns the table's fuzzies grey and writes three NOPs over the instruction that takes a life, DEC $B7BF at $1CA0 (title_cheat_input in the attract block). The cheat lasts one game, because the next title puts the DEC back. The ending reads the same three bytes, and when it finds the DEC still there, it adds "WHY NOT USE THE CHEAT MODE NEXT TIME" to its text ($7DE6).

The C64-Wiki's poke for the same effect, POKE 7328,208, writes a branch over the DEC. The branch is not taken, the two bytes after it run as other instructions, and at the first lost life the game hangs with the panel gone. POKE 7328,173 turns the DEC into an LDA instead and keeps the lives as they are.

10 · Resets

Secret: a reset erases the game

Every level, chamber and ending file starts at $8000 with the bytes the KERNAL looks for at a reset to start a cartridge, and both of its vectors point at memory_wipe ($AF6A), a loop that clears memory upward without end. A reset button pressed while a stage is in memory, to get at the game with a monitor, erases it instead. A VICE reset in chamber 1 stopped at $AF6A.

The same routine waits for anything else that looks like interference. The Get Ready screen and the title time each frame with CIA 2's timer B (timing_check), and anything but a PAL frame of 19,656 cycles, from an NTSC machine or a game frozen and restarted, goes to memory_wipe. RESTORE does nothing, because the NMI vector points at an RTI in every part.

11 · Left in the files

Secret: a picture and messages the game never shows

Side A holds a program called "uncle clyde" that the game never loads: a packed multicolour picture of Clyde pointing at the player, under the words CLYDE NEEDS YOUR HELP. It unpacks and waits when run on its own.

Level 1.1's file carries a message nothing prints, thanking Ablex "for their hospitality and support (and all the free food)". The title's NMI handler, a single RTI, is also the "@" in front of another message nothing prints, "FUZZIES RULE OK!", followed by a quote from Clyde. Unused sprite blocks in the title hold a message from Apex, the team the manual credits with the game: "THE APEX BOYZ ARE BACK!", with the follow-up to the shoot-'em-up Retrograde.
