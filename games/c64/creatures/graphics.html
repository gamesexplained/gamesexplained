1990 · Thalamus

Creatures

Clyde is three sprites: himself and his two shots, which also make his flame and the bubble he speaks in when he dies. Every creature is a few bytes of script, sprite frames with loops among them, which the engine steps every other frame. Below, all of them move as the game moves them. The stages and the three torture chambers are drawn on Maps.

01 · Clyde

Clyde, his shots and the weapons tablet

Clyde is sprite 0, and his shots are sprites 1 and 2. Each scene here is the engine's own routines, run a frame at a time, fifty times a second, over the sky of world 1.

The page runs the engine's own code for each scene, from the listings, on the kit's 6502 simulator (the cr-clyde block): player_animate chooses Clyde's frame, player_fire throws, charges and breathes the flame, weapon_setup gives each weapon its path and colour, player_dead plays his death, and weapons_tablet, tablet_open and tablet_close work the tablet. The page holds the joystick for them and draws the sprites where the game's sprite tables put them.

Clyde's three sprites are set from a few bytes each frame (sprites_player): a frame, a place and a colour for each, his own colour light red, and all three multicoloured with dark grey and yellow. A shot not in the air has the frame $0F, which leaves the sprite blank. The flame takes both shot sprites, which is why Clyde cannot breathe it while a shot is still flying.

The weapons tablet is drawn in the score panel's own band of the screen: while it is open, the band's eight sprites take the tablet's places, frames and colours instead of the panel's (panel_sprites_tablet). Its eleven shapes are kept at $B100, outside the 16 KB the video chip can see, and opening it swaps them, a shape a frame, with the flame's in blocks $54-$5E (tablet_swap_step); closing swaps them back. The tablet's sprites stay off while the shapes are on the move.

02 · The creatures

Every creature, moving as the game moves it

Each world's creatures, with the colours and speeds their levels give them. Pick one to see its frames and the script that plays them.

Speed

Every creature is stepped by the engine's own rules, ported (the cr-anim block in this page), over its script and colour list as the level file holds them, at 50 frames a second. test_gallery.js runs the engine's animate_objects and object_colours on a 6502 simulator beside the port for 3,000 frames of each script here and finds every frame and colour the same. The colours are those of most of the creature's spawn records. The background is the colour behind the top of the world's first stage, or black for a creature drawn in that colour. A few creatures move only when Clyde shoots them, and here they are shot twice a second.

A creature is an object in one of fifteen slots, and the level's spawn records fill the slots as the landscape scrolls in. A record names a type, and the type names an animation script and a speed in the level file's tables (anim_lo, anim_hi and anim_speed, from $8D5A in every level file). The engine's animate_objects reads one byte of the script at a time:

$02-$3Fa sprite frame: one of the engine's shapes, the 64-byte block at $4000 plus 64 times the byte $80-$FFa sprite frame: one of the level file's shapes, found the same way, from $6000 $40-$7Fa loop: the next byte is the distance back, and the body plays once more than the low six bits say $00start the script again $01remove the creature, the end of every death and burst

The engine animates the even slots on one frame and the odd slots on the next, by turning one branch at $C769 from BEQ to BNE and back, so each creature has its turn every other frame. A creature's speed counts those turns. At speed 1 a new byte comes every fourth frame, twelve and a half times a second, and at speed $7F one comes every 256 frames, which is how the still creatures are made. Colour works the same way, from a list of colours with loops of its own (object_colours), which is how the potion creatures change colour.

Some creatures move only when shot. A spawn record whose hit response has both low bits set leaves the animation off (object_spawn, at $20EE), and each shot gives the script one more turn (hit_response_3). The swelling balls of world 2 swell a frame with each shot until they are gone, and level 2.1's end creature, the crescent, changes only when it is hit.

Shapes are shared between scripts. The walking devils of world 1 have five scripts over the same thirteen frames, and one of the five holds a single frame still. A creature too big for one sprite is two or four objects that the level places side by side, like the dragon at the end of level 1.1, a head and a body. Two shorter scripts, types 41 and 42, move the dragon's shapes again, mostly in level 1.2.

03 · The loading picture

The name, spelled down the side

The 1990 cassette first shows its loader's banner, CYBERLOAD NOW LOADING FLUFFY THANGS. Then, 53 seconds in, it puts up its picture whole: CREATURES on a slant across rainbow stripes, and beside it a list whose first letters, picked out in cyan, spell the game's name: Clyde Radcliffe Exterminates All The Unfriendly Repulsive Earth-ridden Slime. It stays up, in silence, for nearly three minutes.

The cassette's own screen, recorded in VICE and drawn as the video chip draws it. The page carries only the 37 characters the screen shows, 296 bytes of Commodore's character ROM 901225-01.

Every part of it is the C64's own character set in ROM, with no picture of its own: the slanted letters and the stripes are built from the set's triangle and block pieces.
