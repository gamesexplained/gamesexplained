Every cave is a recipe: random dirt from a seed, then walls drawn from a short list of records.

01 · Every cave

Twenty caves, five levels each

Choose a cave and a level to see its map as the game builds it. Sixteen caves are the playing caves A to P, and four are the short bonus caves that follow D, H, L and P.

    Each map is built by the page from the cave's own bytes ($582E) with the game's own recipe, then drawn with the game's character shapes. Every object is named beside its picture, because the colours alone do not tell them apart. Animated objects are shown in their first frame.

    02 · How a map is made

    Random dirt first, then drawn walls

    A cave is not stored as a picture. The game fills rows 1 to 21 of the 40 by 22 cave with random objects, then draws the cave's fixed features, the steel border first, on top. The fill takes each cell's random byte from a small sixteen-bit generator ($7085) and compares it with four thresholds in the cave's header: below the first threshold the cell becomes the first of four header codes, below the second the second code, and so on, with the last threshold that matches winning. A cell that matches none is dirt ($8029).

    The generator starts from one of five seed bytes in the header, one for each level. The cave is therefore the same every time that cave and level are played, and levels differ in where the boulders and diamonds fall, not in the walls ($8226). The random-byte routine at $6E68 is a different one, driven by the timers. It serves the amoeba, Rockford's push and idle animation, the cave's opening and closing, the glisten, and the sound; the fireflies and butterflies make no random choices.

    The fixed features are a list of records from $2420 in the cave header copy: a single object, a line, a filled rectangle with a different inner object, or an outline rectangle. The top two bits of the first byte choose which, and the low six bits are the object ($8518). The list ends with $FF. The steel border is drawn first ($85A7), so a record can overwrite it.

    To check the recipe, the page's builder was run for cave A, level 1, and compared with the 880 map bytes in the emulator right after play began. All but 33 cells matched, and the 33 differences are Rockford himself and boulders that had already fallen in the seconds of play before the snapshot.

    03 · The header

    Thirty-two bytes that set the cave’s rules

    ByteHolds
    1Seconds for the amoeba to start growing fast and for the magic wall to stay active ($7478, $7E9F)
    2 and 3Points for each diamond before and after the quota is met ($74F6)
    4 to 8The random seed for levels 1 to 5
    9 to 13Diamonds needed on levels 1 to 5
    14 to 18Seconds allowed on levels 1 to 5 ($8274)
    19, 20 and 21The two extra multicolour colours and the colour of the cave ($8226)
    24 to 27 and 28 to 31The four fill codes and their thresholds

    Bytes 0, 22 and 23 are not explained. Byte 0 repeats the cave number, and the use of bytes 22 and 23 was not found.
