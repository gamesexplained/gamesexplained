The cave is built from characters, and the creatures move by rewriting their own characters.

01 · The creatures

Four animated pictures, eight phases each

The firefly, butterfly, amoeba and diamond move by changing the shape of their own characters, not by moving a picture around.

The 16 by 16 picture is a tile of four characters. Each phase of the frame tables holds 16 bytes for the upper row and 16 for the lower, and the routine at $7C61 copies the current phase into the character set at $3100-$33A8 once for each step of the animation ($7E8F). The colours are cave A's.

A cave only pays for the pictures it uses. The mask byte for the cave ($8740) says whether fireflies, butterflies or the amoeba are present, and the routine rebuilds those sets only when their bit is set; the diamond and Rockford are always rebuilt. This keeps the work of each animation step small.

02 · Character sets

Two sets, copied into place at start

The program keeps two character sets in its file and copies them to where the VIC chip reads them when it starts ($8D11). The text set of 118 characters holds the digits, letters and the title's pieces. The tile set of 128 characters holds the cave's tiles. Characters of the tile set from $20 up that are animated hold only a first frame in the file, and the frame routine overwrites them as the game runs.

Text set, characters $00 to $75 in rows of 16 (drawn black and white)

Tile set, characters $00 to $7F in rows of 16 (cave A's colours)

03 · Colour

Four colours a cave, three from its header

The cave tiles use the VIC chip's multicolour character mode, in which each pair of pixels picks one of four colours. One is the background, black. Two come from the cave's header (bytes 19 and 20, written to $D022 and $D023 at $8226), and the fourth is the colour held in colour memory, which the header's byte 21 also sets. This is why each cave looks different with the same shapes: cave A pairs orange and dark grey with white, and the bonus caves choose others.

Two screen regions with different settings share the display: the status line at the top and the cave below it. The raster interrupt ($8A0C) switches the VIC registers between them by writing from two small tables ($899C for play, $89A4 for the title).

04 · Scrolling

A window on an 80 by 44 character buffer

The cave is 40 by 22 cells of 2 by 2 characters, which is larger than the screen shows at once. The game draws all of it into a character buffer 80 characters wide and 44 high at $4000 and shows a window of it, scrolling it one fine pixel step at a time with the VIC's scroll registers and moving the window's start by a character when the fine position wraps ($7878, $78F1). Two screens at $0C00 and $2C00 are copied to in turn, by routines copied into $9E00 and $9F00 at start.

The window follows Rockford: the scroll speed is set from the row and column of the window where he stands ($79E2), and stops at the cave's edges ($796D).
