1990 · Codemasters · Commodore 64

Fantasy World Dizzy

An Australia where only Dizzy turns upside down, a lever that can make the game impossible to finish, a bottle of whisky that never wears off, and a backwards word that opens a room browser.

01 · Australia

Bug: in Australia only Dizzy turns upside down

Fall far enough down the bottomless well and Dizzy lands in Australia, three rooms underneath the rest of the world. On the Spectrum and the Amstrad these rooms are drawn upside down. On the C64 they are drawn the right way up, and only Dizzy is turned over.

Hold a button to push the joystick that way. Dizzy goes the other way, on his head, with his picture turned the way the game turns it.

In each of the three Australian rooms the game turns Dizzy’s picture through half a circle and swaps the joystick’s left and right. Push left and he walks right. His walking picture is turned with him, so he also faces the wrong way as he goes: he walks backwards, on his head, across a floor that is the right way up. Every few seconds he jumps or takes a step by himself as well.

The game has no way to turn a room over. The routine that draws rooms can flip a picture left to right, but it has no way to draw one upside down, so the C64’s Australia keeps the turned-over Dizzy and the swapped joystick of the other versions without the turned-over world that explained them.

02 · Daisy’s prison

Lowering Daisy’s cage too soon locks a coin away for good

One of the thirty coins lies under the table in Daisy’s prison. Pull the lever that lowers her cage before fetching it, and the coin can never be reached again. Daisy wants all thirty, so the game can no longer be finished.

The prison as the game draws it. The dotted line is Dizzy’s only way to the coin: off the end of the table, down to the floor and left under the table.

From the left, the daggers on the floor kill Dizzy, and even with the rug thrown over them he can’t get under the table from that side. The way in is from the right, through the gap between the end of the table and the wall.

The lever works only once. It lowers the cage until its floor is level with the table, filling that gap, and the game never raises it again, not even after Daisy is rescued. Lowering the cage is how Dizzy reaches her, so the coin has to come first. Hint files for the game warn players about this coin, and the C64 version keeps the trap.

03 · The whisky

The whisky can only be drunk, and Dizzy stays drunk for good

A bottle of whisky waits beside the snap-happy gator. It is not needed for anything in the game. Try to drop it and Dizzy drinks it instead, and from then on the joystick misbehaves for the rest of the game.

Once the bottle is gone, the game swaps the joystick’s left and right for about three seconds in every six. With the stick left alone, he staggers off to the left by himself. The bottle never comes back, so nothing can sober him up. Drunk in Australia, the two swaps cancel out, so there the joystick works normally for half the time.

04 · A cheat

Typing ESPILCE opens a room browser

Type ESPILCE during play, one key after another. It is ECLIPSE spelt backwards.

The browser as the game runs it: left and right move one room along, up and down one row of the world.

Dizzy vanishes, his lives go back to three, and the joystick now flips through the world. Left and right step to the next room along. Up and down jump a whole row, to the room above or below. Pressing fire drops Dizzy into the room on the screen, which makes the browser a way to skip anywhere in the game.

The browser doesn’t stop at the edge of the world: it lets the room number run well past the last room, into numbers that have nothing to draw. The Maps tab lays the rooms out in the same rows.
