1990 · Codemasters · Commodore 64

Fantasy World Dizzy

How every room is stamped together from a few small pictures, where the thirty coins hide, and the game’s music and sound.

01 · The game

Escape the dungeon and rescue Daisy

Dizzy is an egg with boxing gloves, locked in the King’s dungeon. Objects are the way out: a jug of water, a loaf of bread and the apple he starts with each have a use. The adventure leads through a village, down a well and up to the wizard’s cloud castle, where Daisy is held prisoner.

Rescuing Daisy is only half the job. She runs home and asks for thirty gold coins, so that the pair can buy a cottage. Dizzy has three lives, and fire, water and some of the creatures each take one.

02 · The rooms

Every room is stamped together from small pictures

A room is stored as a short list. Each line names one of the game’s small pictures, where to put it and what colour to draw it in, and the game stamps the pictures onto the screen one after another, the way you might print a scene with rubber stamps. A wall is one brick stamped again and again, and a picture can be stamped back to front to make its mirror image.

The 49 rooms of the world are built from 2,714 stamps of just 90 different pictures. The things Dizzy can pick up are stamped last, from a separate list of where each one is, so carrying an object only changes where that list says it is. The Maps tab lays every room out as a map.

03 · The coins

Thirty coins, and where they hide

Twenty-two coins lie in plain sight. Seven are hidden under things Dizzy can pick up, and the last only turns up after he finds a troll hiding in the mines.

The hidden coins sit under a piece of railing, a clump of leaves or a window frame. Each of those is an object Dizzy can carry, and picking one up puts its coin in the room at the same spot.

The last coin appears in the dungeon where the game began, but only once Dizzy has walked into the deserted mines and met the troll hiding there: “Oh no! Not you again! Go away! I’m hiding, and it’s all your fault.” The game sends you back to its very first room for it.

When Dizzy brings the coins home, Daisy checks only that the tens digit of his count is three. With exactly thirty coins in the world, every one is needed, and one of them can be locked away for good: see Discoveries.

04 · Music

Music: the title and the adventure

Two tunes: one for the title screen and one that plays while Dizzy explores. A small music program in the game plays them on the C64’s sound chip, which has three voices, each reading its own list of notes. Pressing M during play swaps the adventure tune for the sound of Dizzy’s footsteps.

05 · Sound

Sound: a coin, a vanishing egg and footsteps

The same music program plays two sound effects: one when Dizzy picks up a coin, and one when he vanishes in a burst of sparks after losing a life. The vanishing sound plays again when Daisy is rescued and the screen fills with random letters.

Footsteps come from a routine of their own. While the music is off, every other step Dizzy takes clicks a short burst of noise on the first voice, alternating between a higher and a lower pitch.
