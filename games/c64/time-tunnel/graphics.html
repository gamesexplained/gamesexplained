1985 · U.S. Gold

Time Tunnel

Everything that moves is an object with a sprite, the gnome included, and each of its pictures is copied into the sprite from the object's table. Most change at the pace the table gives; many at a pace their room's own code sets; and a few are drawn while the game runs: the time machine's lights, Medusa's shot and the gnome breaking up.

01 · Everything that moves

All 109 objects, drawn by the game's own code

Each picture is copied into a sprite by the game's own sprite_draw, run on the kit's 6502 in this page over the listing's memory, and changes when the game would change it. A pass of the game is a frame, a fiftieth of a second.

The objects are drawn by the game's own code in the built site.

The gnome

The gnome is objects 0 to 3: from behind, from the front, and from the side each way. The last is made at start-up by sprites_build, which mirrors the side view into memory at $0400. He takes a step on every other pass and his picture steps on every other step, a frame every four passes. The bolt he fires is one object, its frame set by the way he faces. On a trip in time he spins, shown every fourth pass as the next of objects 0 to 3 on a new random background. While the time machine runs, gnome_scramble clears random bits in his sprite every pass, so that he breaks up.

Gnome Mansion, today

The fire changes its picture every four passes. In the time machine's room the ladder is shown at double height. The machine's two lights have their pictures in memory the game writes: each pass of the countdown mansion_pass fills them with random bits under a mask, and the lights are copied into their sprites the same pass.

California gold rush, 1849

The horse is two objects, $22 facing right and $19 facing left, whose pictures sprites_build mirrors from the other's at start-up. While it follows the food it takes a step every fourth pass, and its picture steps with each step. Once the horse is on the treadle the three wheels turn a frame every eight passes (gold_wheels). A bat shot becomes the dead bat, the bat's first frame. The torch is unlit at frame 0; lit, play_torch steps it through frames 1 to 3, one every fourth pass. The time portal, which opens in nine rooms across the eras, takes a random colour every pass; as it opens it grows a frame every 64 passes, and it stays open for 16 counts of 64 passes and shut for 61.

The Black Hole, 9999

The staff is shown at double height wherever it has a sprite, and play_state moves its colour on by one every pass.

Magical Persia, 893 BC

Here the rooms' own code holds most pictures back. The snake rises from its basket a frame every 16 passes, and the risen snake changes its picture every 64. The woman on the flying carpet changes hers every 32 passes and the carpet every 16.

Stone Age, 9600 BC

Mythological Greece, 86 BC

Medusa's two halves step a frame together every 16 passes (greece_medusa), and she sways a step every 64, her x from medusa_sway. Her shot is eight bytes at $0676 that the same routine redraws every pass with random bits inside a round outline, so it sparkles. The sword changes colour on three passes in four, from sword_colours.

Intergalactic spaceship, 3456

Every other pass ship_common gives the round light and the pyramid key a random colour, so that they twinkle. Each lever is two objects, one off and one on, and the room's code shows the one its state says.

Colonial Salem, 1692

The flying broom is three objects side by side, 12 steps apart. Smoke rises a frame every seven passes, from the lamp in Persia, the dynamite and the cauldron, and goes as it reaches its seventh frame.

The throne room

The king on his throne is six objects, two across and three down, with the staff in his hand at double height, changing colour every pass.

Pictures the game never shows

Drawn faint. Objects $46 and $47 have a place in every table but start nowhere, and no code names them. Medusa's halves have a fourth frame after their three, and the burning dynamite a fifth after its four, which their frame counts never reach. The basket's lid has a third row that its size leaves out. More on these is on Discoveries.

Every picture is sprite_draw ($A185) run on the 6502 after the start-up's sprites_build ($0ED3), each frame from obj_gfx_lo and obj_gfx_hi ($A451, $A4BE), in the colour, width and mode obj_flags ($A230) gives, with the two shared colours orange and white that sprites_build sets. A frame lasts the passes obj_anim ($A29D) gives, as sprite_update ($A15E) counts them, except where the code named above sets the pace. The lights are mansion_pass's fill at $10BA, Medusa's shot greece_medusa's at $26D1, the breaking up gnome_scramble ($10CF), and every random number rnd ($3D78), all run on the 6502. Objects made of several are placed as obj_start_table ($0909) and their rooms' code place them. Each is drawn on the grey or black that shows it best; under each, every frame it shows, the one showing marked.

02 · Where each one starts

Each object in the room where it starts

Pick an object to see it where the game first puts it, in its room as the game's own code draws it.

The object in the room where it starts, three times over: the game's own room_enter ($3A47) draws the room and gives the object its sprite, and sprite_draw ($A185) draws each frame into it, from the listing, on the site's copy of the C64's processor. The frames change as sprite_update ($A15E) steps them: each lasts as many frames of play as the object's table says, and a frame of play is a fiftieth of a second. Where a room's own code sets another pace, the pictures above show it. An object that starts nowhere is drawn on the plain colour that shows it best. Where the object starts comes from obj_start_table ($0909).

A sprite's picture is 24 pixels wide and 21 high, three bytes to a row. A frame of an object is copied into it in rows of one, two or three bytes, so a narrow object's frames take less memory. The object's flags give its colour, the width of its rows, whether the sprite is stretched to double width, and whether it is multicolour. A second table gives how many frames it has and how many frames of play each one lasts, and whether the gnome can pick it up. How the room behind the objects is drawn, and a whole frame of play rebuilt, are on How it works.
