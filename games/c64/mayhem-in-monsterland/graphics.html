1993 · Apex Computer Productions

Mayhem in Monsterland

Each land brings two character sets and 120 sprite images in its file; Mayhem's 56 images stay in the engine. Everything here is drawn from those bytes and coloured the way the game's code colours it, and what moves in the game moves here at its pace.

01 · Characters

Every character, in the colours the game gives it

Each land's file carries two character sets, sad and happy, each character coloured by its code. Pick a land, then sad or happy.

Point at a character, or click one.

All 256 characters of the chosen set, 16 to a row, from the land's file as it loads: the sad set at $7800 and the happy set at $A900, each character's colour from $8900 (sad) or $A800 (happy), and the background and the two shared multicolours from $9093, $908F and $9091, one byte for each mood. Checked: Jellyland's sad set, drawn this way and laid out as the screen memory of the recorded frame of play names the characters, matches that frame pixel for pixel in all 960 cells of the playfield (the frame drawn without its sprites, leaving out the 38-column border and the panel's mode change on raster line 242), and the colour memory holds the table's colour in every cell. No happy land was recorded, so the happy sets and colours rest on the code. In play, the happy land's star characters $FB-$FE are overwritten with the spinning star's frames.

The playfield uses multicolour character mode, with each character's colour fixed by its code. A colour byte with bit 3 set draws the character four double-width pixels to a row, in the background, the two colours the whole land shares, and the byte's own low three bits. Without bit 3 the character has eight pixels to a row in a single colour.

The character code also decides what Mayhem can stand on. Codes below the land's $9021 are walls, solid even while he jumps up through a platform. Codes from there up to $9020 are platforms, solid only while he walks or falls: each pass, $2AC3-$2AD1 sets the threshold the collision tests compare with to one or the other. The checkbox above marks the two ranges.

02 · Mayhem

Mayhem is two sprites laid over each other

The 56 images at $4C00-$59FF, the engine's sprite blocks $30-$67, paired as $E4A6 pairs them and drawn as $E426 sets the two sprites up: the same position, sprite 6 single-colour in black, and sprite 7 multicolour in light red ($1BE8) with yellow from the shared sprite multicolour $D025. Checked: the standing pair, $46 over $47, drawn this way at the position and with the colours of the recorded frame of play, matches that frame pixel for pixel, and the play snapshots of all five lands hold the same colours and multicolour bits. The images come from the engine's play snapshot and equal the file as loaded byte for byte. The other images were not seen on screen, and the flicker after a hit ($EF45) is not shown.

A multicolour sprite has only twelve pixels across, each two wide, which leaves its edges coarse. Mayhem's body is a multicolour sprite, and a second, single-colour sprite at the same position draws his outline at the full 24 pixels. The outline, sprite 6, lies in front of the body, sprite 7, because the video chip always puts the lower-numbered sprite on top. The body uses only two of its three colours: no image of his has a pixel in the third, $D026, which the monsters' bands change down the screen.

Every frame, $EE9E picks the outline image as a base for his facing ($30 right, $4A left, $EF86) plus a step for what he is doing, and $E4A6 shows the next image as the body. Running uses the eight walking images, stepped through faster. A charge is the one exception to the pairing: three outlines take turns over a single body image, $43 or $5D. Two images are edited during play. $EF93 writes one of three versions of three bytes into the jumping outline, for rising, the top of the jump and falling, and $E27B one of two versions into the standing outline. The buttons beside a jumping or standing image show them. The walk and the charge play here as the game steps them, one call of $EE9E a frame: the walk at the speed the buttons beside it choose, the charge's three outlines one a frame.

03 · Monsters

Every monster, moving as the game moves it

One card for each look: the records that make the same picture in the chosen mood are one entry, and ×N says how many times the land's list holds it. A card puts a group's sprites where the spawner puts them (the follower offsets from $940F, the Y from each record's byte 6), the lower sprite number in front, each in its own colour and mode: the colour from $903A, the record or a colour list as $EB11-$EBAA chooses, multicolour with $D025 and its band's $D026, and double height from byte 5. An object sent out by another is drawn to its right. The buttons choose the script $CA8B would pick. Play runs the scripts at 50 passes a second: a slot has a turn every second pass ($C8A7), reads its next byte every (delay + 1) turns, and steps a colour list the same way ($B645). Defeated shows the script byte 11 names, sized by byte 4, then the item it leaves, if any. Click a card for its records, colours and scripts. Checked against 79 recorded frames of play, 15 or 16 in each land, all sad; 68 of them show play, the rest a stage card. In those 68 frames the multiplexers placed 237 objects. For every one, the sprite registers equal the slot tables in the frame's RAM (one image had moved on a step between the start of the frame and the band's write), and the model of its record predicts them: the image is one of its scripts' images, and the colour, multicolour, priority and both expansion bits are the ones the record gives, for living, defeated and item objects alike; the 7 followers sit at their $940F offset, and the 2 objects after a portal are white. $D025 and the 193 bands' $D026 equal the colours the level start's fade leaves. Drawn with this page's data and the site's sprite drawing, the 235 visible objects of those frames match C64.renderFrame's drawing of the same frames pixel for pixel: 34,932 pixels, none different, and none of the 28,623 monster pixels the chip drew is missing. No happy land was recorded, so the happy colours, colour sets, flashing and scripts rest on the code.

Each object is a record of 15 bytes in the land's list at $A000, and the spawner $E75E turns a record into one of 15 object slots as its column scrolls into view. The slot number is the low four bits of byte 0. A slot is also a sprite: the slots come in three bands of five, and each band is shown on sprites 0-4 by its own multiplexer, $C0DC, $C01C or $C07C, which copies the slot's image, colour and position into the video chip and sets the band's multicolour, priority and expansion bits from bytes the spawner filled. Byte 8 bit 0 makes the sprite multicolour, byte 8 bit 1 puts it behind the scenery and byte 5 bit 4 doubles its height. Nothing doubles a living monster's width: the spawner clears that bit for the slot.

A monster bigger than one sprite is several records in a row with the same group number, byte 8 bits 4-6, which the spawner brings in together ($EC72). The record whose byte 3 has bits 6-7 clear is the leader. Each follower names the leader's slot in byte 14 and starts at the leader's X plus an offset from the land's table at $940F, chosen by the low bits of bytes 1 and 2 ($E970-$E981); its Y is its own byte 6. From then on every sprite follows its own record's movement paths. Most followers have their leader's paths, so the group moves as one. The lower-numbered sprite is drawn in front, which lets a group lay a single-colour outline over a multicolour body, the way Mayhem is built. A record whose byte 12 has bit 7 set is a second kind of companion: it waits hidden, is sent out from the position of the slot that byte 12 names, and is sent out again each time its own path starts over ($C56D, $C30D).

Each record's type, byte 3 while sad and byte 7 while happy, selects six animation scripts from the land's tables at $956E: moving up, moving down, moving left, moving right, standing facing left, and standing. $CA8B picks one from the direction of the object's last move, and byte 5 bits 6-7 limit the choice to the vertical pair, the horizontal four, or one script named directly. A defeated monster switches to the script that byte 11 names, and byte 4 bits 5-7 decide how it looks while that runs: double width, double height, and whether it stays multicolour ($C842-$C89A).

A sad land's monsters are all one grey because the spawner never looks at an object's own colour while the land is sad: it takes the land's $903A instead ($EB4C-$EB54). Three kinds of object escape that rule even while sad. A portal takes its colour from its record. The object spawned straight after a portal in the same group comes out white, because the counter at $ECF0 that the portal raised gives the next object colour 1 ($EB2B-$EB37). An object sent out by another, with byte 12 bit 7, steps through a colour list of the land's.
