1987 · Firebird

I, Ball

Iball is sprite 0 and the aliens are sprites 1 to 7. Twice more each frame the same eight sprites are moved into the panels, where they are the icons, the timer, the score and the lives. Here every picture moves as the game moves it: one step for each pass of its logic, which comes every frame while the screen is quiet and about every other frame with seven aliens about.

01 · Iball

Iball rolls through three pictures and back

Each pass of the logic, animate_iball ($4ABD) gives sprite 0 the next of eight pointers: three pictures and back, each held for two passes. One set faces right and one faces left. He rolls all the time, standing still too; the stick only decides which way he faces, and when he turns the count carries on, so he turns mid-roll.

Drawn from the sprite shapes in the listing, in grey ($0C) with the play area's shared light and dark grey ($0F, $0B), at one step a pass, 50 a second. The tables are roll_right ($4ADF) and roll_left ($4AE7). On a pass that draws a new row of the map, the step is skipped.

02 · Losing a life

A lost life breaks Iball into eight pieces, and eight pieces bring him back

When Iball is killed, the game says "OH NO!" first, and while it speaks nothing moves. Then death_step ($5061) runs once a frame. Every sprite jumps to where he was and turns multicolour. For 13 frames the background flashes through 13 colours (death_colours, $50F2) while the sprites still show the pictures they had, aliens included; only sprite 7 already shows the piece, because the loop meant to give all eight their piece stops after one. Then all eight become the piece ($9E), Iball's in white, and fly off in eight directions at 10 pixels a frame, slowing by one every two frames.

Run by this page from the game's own code, on the site's 6502 over the bytes on the Source tab, one call a frame as irq_top ($4432) makes it: materialise_step while Iball materialises, else death_step while he dies, else animate_iball. It starts from a moment of play: the eight sprites as they were at a death in a run of the game on the kit's model of the C64. The pause for the speech is 40 frames, the length of the sample ($AC00-$B6FF, two volumes a byte) at the pitch the game gives it ($8C). The search for a clear place finds one at once here, with no walls.

When the last piece has left, materialise_step ($4E98) takes over. It waits at least 51 frames, scrolling the map back while there is a wall where Iball starts, then puts eight pieces 70 pixels out around the start, in colours 1 to 8, and brings them in, a pixel a frame faster each frame up to five. When they meet, sprite 0 is Iball again, grey, at $AE, $A0.

03 · Aliens

Each alien flickers into being in one colour, then turns in two greys and its own

A new alien takes a kind at random from kind_choices ($568B), where kinds 2 and 3 are listed twice; kind 6 is kept for the bonus discs. For 49 passes it is a single-colour sprite stepping through forming_colours ($5777), and cannot hurt Iball. Then it goes multicolour in one of 16 colours (alien_colours, $5650). All the while it turns through its kind's pictures (alien_frames, $53C4), one step a pass. The count belongs to the kind, not the alien (anim_counts, $5452), so two aliens of a kind always show the same picture. Shot, it is single-colour again (alien_shot, $5C49) and steps through the explosion (explode_frames, $5CF8), each picture held two passes, and is gone after the fifteenth.

Each panel plays one alien's life at the game's pace: 49 passes forming, three seconds formed, the 15 passes of its explosion, then a gap, and again in another of the 16 colours. In the game an alien lives until it is shot or until Iball dies.

04 · The panels

The panels move the same eight sprites twice more each frame

Above line $36 and again below line $F8, an interrupt gives the eight sprites new places, pointers and colours (panel_sprites_top, $46C2; panel_sprites_bottom, $470E). The ten extras' icons are multicolour with their own colour black until the extra is given, so only the two greys show. The timer is two single-colour digits from ten pictures ($8A-$93). The score is drawn into two sprites ($9A, $9B) from the text set's digits by draw_score ($54BC). The Electro Shield and the Hyper Brakes share one meter ($9C), whose colour steps every pass.

Run by this page from the game's own code: each pass it calls timer_tick ($5594), which takes a second off every 50 passes, and shield_meters ($659B), which clears one more byte of the meter every seventh pass, 63 bytes in about 441 passes. When the meter is empty the page gives the other extra, through the game's own routines for the Electro Shield ($640A) and the Hyper Brakes ($6483). The score is draw_score's picture of 000000.

The lives sprite is $E5 plus the lives left, for 0 to 3 (show_lives, $5F0D), multicolour in grey with the icons' two greys. With no lives left it is $9D, which is empty.

05 · Friends

Glow Ball glows on the title screen

The title screen shows Iball twice, facing right and facing left ($94, $97), and the four friends to rescue below ($F1-$F4), each with the two greys and a colour of its own (title_sprites, $6ED7). Every frame the title's interrupt adds one to Glow Ball's colour ($6E6D), so it cycles through all 16 colours three times a second. After levels 4, 8, 12 and 16 the friend rescued stands beside Iball (show_friend, $5AC5), in the same colour as on the title, but for Glow Ball, which is white there.

No table or routine points a sprite at block $BF, between the bonus disc's pictures and the playfield's characters. $F5-$FF are empty.
