1989 · Melbourne House

Aaargh!

Each monster is a body and a pair of legs, and the city sends hornets, a white flyer and a cannon against it. Here every picture they wear moves as the game moves it, from the game's own tables, at its pace: a pass of play takes four frames, and most pictures change on a pass.

01 · The monsters

Seventeen moves for each monster, each of four frames

Pick a monster and a move. It plays at the game's pace, and a move that ends holds its last frame for a moment and starts again.

The body is a sprite pointer from $80EF (the lizard) or $8177 (the ogre), the legs one of 98 shapes at $0BB8 by $8133 or $81BB, both at the move's action plus its frame ($8215, $8213), and the legs are drawn 21 lines below the body (monster_sprites, $9925). Walking steps the frame on every pass. A punch runs its timer for 8 passes, a flame and eating for 6, and while a timer runs the frame steps on each even count ($9136): frames 0, 1 and 2 for two passes each, then 3. A flame with the button held starts again at once. Dying counts the monster's state down a pass at a time from 31 ($8AA9): frame 0 at 30, and the next frame each time the count is a multiple of 8; at 0 the monster is gone. The lizard is light green, green in the castle; the ogre light red; both with red and black ($D025, $D026).

A punch to the left or the right that has punching power and hits no building also shows a burst, a third sprite 25 pixels to that side, in yellow, while the punch's timer runs. Its picture is read from $9C09 to the right or $9C00 to the left by what is left of the timer, so the burst grows as the punch goes out. A punch that hits a building shows none: the building gets a hole instead. Up and down punches have no burst.

On the title the two monsters run through the first 64 frames of their tables in order, every move but dying, one frame each four frames of the screen (title_loop, $B09C). The last move in the list plays that.

02 · Above them

Two hornets and the white flyer

The first band of sprites, at the top of the screen. Each changes its picture on every pass.

The hornets are sprites 0 and 1, yellow; the white flyer is sprite 2. The interrupt on line 0 ($8810) gives each its pointer from flyer_frames ($81FF) at its base plus the step $8212, which $A446 counts round 0 to 3 on each pass: base 4 for a hornet flying left, $0C for one flying right, 8 for the white flyer. A hornet or the flyer burnt by a flame counts on a pass at a time ($823D-$823F), and the interrupt adds the count to $D8 for its pointer: the burst grows for five passes, and it is taken off.

03 · The cannon

The cannon turns to fire, and turns again when it burns

The cannon is two sprites side by side, 21 pixels apart. Its frame picks both pictures, and it has five.

The frame $8240 indexes the pointers at $AB96 and $AB9D. Rolling right is frame 2 and rolling left frame 4 ($AA2B, $AA33). To fire it takes the shell's way as its frame ($AA64): 1 up, 2 right, 3 down, 4 left, so the shell leaves the way the barrel points, and it keeps that frame until it rolls again. Each flame that burns it takes one from its health and moves the frame on, 1 to 4 and round ($8D8C). Below 7 health it shows frame 5, breaking up, for a pass each step down, and at 1 it is gone ($A9BC). Brown, with red and black.

The tables hold a sixth frame, $BB beside $BC, which the code never picks: the breaking-up branch sets 6 only for a health below 2, and a health of 1 has already been caught. $BC is the first A of the title's logo.

04 · In the city

Fire, falling buildings and the things on the ground are characters

These are tiles of the screen, not sprites. Thirteen of them are redrawn on every pass by the game's own animate_chars ($B463), run here on the kit's 6502. Pick a place for its colours.

A punch knocks a hole in a building, $A9 over $AA in red (draw_hole, $B44A), and the hole is where the fire starts: at each stage of a building's fire, every cell of it holding one of the six characters from $A9 to $AE ($82B0) is moved on to the next (building_stage, $93F0). As a building falls, its top row is $94 between $93 and $95, in black. These two sets change in four phases, from $B4BF and $B547; $97 to $9A change in two, from $B507. A person is $96 on the floor's colour and steps a cell on every other pass. A pickup is four characters, each with the colour its code writes ($9693). The cannon's shell ($9F) and the white flyer's dropping ($9E) are made each time they move from the character they cover, with a square cut out of the middle and filled ($ABA7, $ABAF); here they cover the place's floor.

05 · The rest

The title's logo, and the pictures nothing shows

The logo is seven sprites that never change. Below it are the pictures in the sprite memory that no table or routine points at.

The title's interrupt on line 0 ($B1CE) puts eight sprites at the places at $B353 with the pointers at $B375, doubled in width and height, light blue with purple and blue. The eighth, $C3, goes to the corner, under the border, where it cannot be seen.

Two bodies with their legs ($4F over leg shape 24, and $8F over 87), a lump of legs (48), the cannon's sixth frame and the logo's hidden eighth sprite. No frame table holds those bodies or legs, and the title and the fight in the cave read the same tables.
