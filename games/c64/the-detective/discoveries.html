1986 · Argus Press Software

The Detective

The answer to the safe is written on an object in the house, the winning move is made long before the accusation, and the time limit ignores a test meant to spare a player outside.

Every section on this tab gives away part of the mystery.

01 · Secret

Secret: the arrest is won by swapping the bullets

Accusing the right person is not enough. At the accusation, Dingle leads Snide to the cellar at gunpoint and fires. What his gun holds was decided earlier: the last line of Dingle's murder script takes the ammunition from one place in the house, place $18 in the object table, where the bullets start ($B1E6). The arrest runs the same routine again as he draws ($BC5E). Whatever is in that place when he gets there is what he loads: the real bullets (object $33), or the blank bullets (object 1), if the player has swapped them in.

$BC61 lda $1DAD ; the blank bullets' place $BC64 cmp #$F7 ; $F7: Dingle's $BC66 beq $BC6B ; blanks: "PULLS THE TRIGGER, BUT NOTHING HAPPENS.." $BC68 jmp $2042 ; real bullets: the game is over

With the blanks, play goes on with Dingle in the cellar, and Snide can use the gun on him: "DON/T SHOOT! I/LL COME QUIETLY.." and a promotion ($BCAF). The player who waits too long sees "DINGLE HAS ESCAPED..NEVER TO BE SEEN AGAIN" ($B159). Nothing in the game hints that the bullets matter.

02 · Secret

Secret: the safe's combination is inscribed on an object

Behind the picture in the drawing room is a safe. "TYPE IN THE COMBINATION:" waits for six characters, and the game compares them with six bytes at $8AFB: "210319" ($8A62). One object in the house, examined, says "IT/S INSCRIBED: TO ANGUS MCFUNGUS, BORN 21 MARCH 1919" ($9B90). The day, the month and the year are the combination.

Sam Manthorpe has said the safe codes were part of the original's copy protection. In this release, the clue to the code is part of the game.

03 · Secret

Secret: the first victim is the murderer

Mr Dingle goes up to his room, locks the door and, once he is alone, lies down as if dead: a few lines of 6502 code called from his script swap his sprites for a body and add "DINGLE/S BODY!" to the room ($B1CF). The Doctor's script then waits until Snide is in the same room and announces "INSPECTOR..MR. DINGLE/S BEEN STRANGLED!" (opcode 23, $A8D2). After "POOR FELLOW..TERRIBLE", the Doctor's script rewrites Dingle's script pointer (opcode 25), and from then on Dingle's script is the murderer's ($B09C). The Doctor is the second to die, and Dingle explains why at the end: "I PAID THE DOCTOR TO CERTIFY ME DEAD, AND THEN TOOK CARE OF HIM".

04 · Secret

Secret: secret passages keep the border still

The border flashes when Snide first reaches something he can investigate, a door included ($3E32). One kind of door is left out: a door of type 8, the secret passage, gives no flash while it is closed. Closed, it is also invisible, because closing it puts back the picture behind it ($3B8B). Examining the wall in front of one says "A SECRET PASSAGE!" and opens it ($892A). Professor Bull's notebooks are "PLANS OF THE CASTLE AND SHOW SECRET PASSAGES IN THE KITCHEN AND IN THE BEDROOMS".

05 · Bug

Bug: the time limit's test for the room reads the wrong byte

When the clock reaches 11:03, an unseen assassin shoots Snide ($2592). Before it checks the time, the routine skips the shot when Snide is on the stairs, and when the room's type is 3. Type 3 is the type of rooms 26 and 28, outside the house. But the instruction reads $1BC0 without an index: the type of room 0, the drawing room, which is 1 and which nothing in the game ever changes. The test never holds, so the assassin's shot finds Snide outside as well. The intended test was probably LDA $1BC0,X with the player's room in X, as the room builder reads the same table ($38B2).

06 · Quirk

Five things in Snide's hands, not six

Zzap!64's review says Snide can carry six items. The code counts what he holds and refuses the next one at five: "YOU CAN/T CARRY ANY MORE!" ($30CF, counting through $883B). Carrying more means putting things inside other things: a briefcase, a jacket or a bag holds five more ($8856), and the evidence envelopes hold ten.

07 · Quirk

Ten script pointers from a table of nine

At the start of a game, init_scripts ($A7E6) copies each character's script start from the table at $A7FB. The loop counts from 9 down to 0, which is ten entries for nine characters. The tenth start is read from the two bytes after the table, the first instruction of the next routine ($A80D: LDY #$01), as the address $01A0. Its low byte lands in character 0's pointer high byte, which the loop's last pass then overwrites, and its high byte, 1, in character 0's chosen door ($1E7F), which the Reverend's script replaces before he first uses a door.

08 · Quirk

Another game's text in the gaps

This release was packed by crackers, and their unpacker left memory it did not clear. Between $0800 and $0BFF the snapshot holds text from a different program, in German: "TOWN RESCUE", "KASSETTE OHNE HIGHSCORE", "SAVE SCORES", "HERO LIST". No instruction of The Detective reads it. Inside the game's own data are leftovers of its development too: the icon characters at $4E00 hold two older copies of the word list, one spelling the vicar's title "THE REVERAND", which the first menu overwrites.

09 · Quirk

"GUEST ERROR.."

When a script asks a character to walk to a room that no door leads to from where they stand, or to use a door it never chose, the screen flashes red and the game prints "GUEST ERROR.." in that character's voice ($A618). It is a check the author left in for his scripts.
