1986 · Argus Press Software

The Detective

The McFungus house is thirty rooms drawn from seventeen packed pictures, each stretched, recoloured and given its own characters as Snide walks in.

01 · Every room

The house, room by room

Pick a room to draw it from the game's own bytes. Wide rooms scroll in the game; here they are drawn whole.

Drawn in the built site.

Each room is a picture record from the list at $C100, chosen by the room's type in $1BC0. build_room ($388D) clears a map 256 columns wide and 15 rows deep at $0C00, unpacks the record into it ($3BD7), and copies a 40-column window of the map to the screen as the player walks ($35E8). The same record serves several rooms: the corridors upstairs are one picture, and the bedrooms another, told apart by their colours ($1B80, $1BA0) and by which of three sets of characters the room asks for ($1EE0).

Doors are not part of the pictures. The game draws them over the map afterwards, from the door table at $1F00, which is why a closed secret passage looks like plain wall here and in the game. The cast and the objects are drawn by other routines again.

02 · The pictures

Seventeen records, packed a bit at a time

A record is the address of the next record, the picture's width in columns, and then the picture as a stream of bits, read lowest bit first ($3D56). The stream is a series of runs. Each run is a count byte and one bit: with the bit clear, each character of the run is a whole byte; with it set, a 4-bit high nibble follows and each character of the run takes only 4 bits of its own ($3C3D). A count of zero ends the picture. Rooms are mostly walls and floors drawn from a few neighbouring character codes, so most runs cost half a byte a character.

Records 10 and 11 are not any room's own picture: they are laid over the landing and the hall, from the left edge, once two of the cast are dead ($3D0A). Record 16, the last, is used by no room.
