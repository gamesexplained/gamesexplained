1988 · Elite Systems

Mike Read's Computer Pop Quiz

A round of the television show that was written and never reached, credits nobody sees, a question in every block that is never asked, and a question picker that repeats itself sooner than it means to.

01 · Bug

Bug: the question picker starts repeating questions after about two in three of a subject

Each subject has 42 or 43 questions in a block, and the game keeps a bit for each to avoid asking one twice. The counter that is meant to say when the subject is used up runs out early.

The game's own pick_question ($2C72) run on the kit's 6502 over the listing, for block 1's Pop questions, 200 times from 200 seeds of the game's random generator, with the group sizes, masks and counters set as the block loader sets them ($2AE8-$2B19).

pick_question draws a random question of the subject and, if its bit in asked_questions ($1DC1) is set, draws again. To stop it from looking for ever once every question is used, it counts down subject_left ($0F09), set to the subject's size when a block loads, and stops checking the bits when it reaches 0. But it counts every draw ($2C96), the rejected ones too, so the count reaches 0 after 43 draws, not after 43 different questions. From then on any question can come up, asked or not.

The second row onwards comes from a second slip: nothing ever clears asked_questions. When the next block loads, the counters start again but the bits stay set, so the picker treats the new block's questions as asked wherever their numbers were asked in the last block, spends its draws rejecting them, and reaches 0 sooner.

02 · Secret

Secret: a "What happened next" round is in the code, and the show never runs it

The game holds the heading and the answer buffers of a round called "What happened next", with a routine to fill them. No step of the show reaches any of it.

The text at wnh_text ($25F8), as the printer would show it: the heading, then four answers read from buffers at $B600, $B655, $B6AA and $7F40. A routine to fill those buffers from a built question, copy_to_wnh_buffer ($2640), is there as well.

A search of all three parts finds no instruction or pointer that reaches either the text or the routine, and the show's table of 32 steps ($338E) has no step for the round. The buffers stay at zero in every snapshot taken during play.

03 · Secret

Secret: unused credits give the visuals to Steve Beverley

The menu's texts include nine lines of credits for the text panel that the game never prints. They name a different artist from the credits the player sees.

Texts 12 to 20 of the panel's table ($CC8E-$CD0A), drawn here one under another with the panel's own font. Every call of print_text ($194C) in the game loads a text number other than 12 to 20.

The credits the game does show are in Mike Read's speech bubble ($3E82): designed by Ian Upton and Nick Acton, graphics by Dave Percival, music and sound effects by Mark Cooksey, programmed by Richard Underhill. The unused lines say "Visuals by Steve Beverley", name Ian Upton alone for the design, and have no heading for Richard Underhill's name. Which of the two was written last, the files do not say.

04 · Secret

Secret: the last question of every block is never asked

Each block holds 256 question records, numbered 0 to 255. The header that divides them into subjects ends the last subject at 255, so question 255 belongs to none and is never asked.

Built by the game's own build_question ($3416) and write_answer ($3C3A) on the kit's 6502, from each block as the game loads it. The header's last byte, $B80A, is 255 in all five blocks.

The question picker adds a random number below a subject's size to the subject's first question ($2C88), and Mystery, the last subject, runs from 213 up to, but not including, 255. The record is not wasted: its answer is borrowed as a wrong answer by other questions, so Neil Diamond turns up on the answer list without ever being the question. The question builder on the How it works tab shows block 1's question 255 built.

05 · Secret

Secret: four of the eleven sound effects are never played

Effects 0, 7, 9 and 10 of the effects player that every block loads have no caller: each of the game's nine calls of fx_start ($4FCB) loads another effect's number. Three of them are finished sounds. All four have buttons on the How it works tab, played by the game's own effects code.

06 · Quirk

Each question block carries a copy of the game's own pictures, which it never reads

A block's three question files are cut from one stretch of memory, $B800 to $FEFF, and the parts the questions do not fill were saved as they were. In block 1 the stretches $DF09-$F1FF and $FB4F-$FEFF are not read, and much of them is the game's own graphics, byte for byte, 26,624 bytes ($6800) above where the game keeps them: $E800-$F13F is the game's $8000-$893F (the Jukebox ball, the subject icons, the numbers and the first pictures), and $FC00-$FDFF its $9400-$95FF (clock tiles, the star frames and the start of the bubble font). So the questions were saved from a machine that held the game's graphics there. Nothing reads these bytes.

07 · Quirk

The bubble's font has no "/", and points into the other font instead

The speech bubble's table of glyph addresses gives the code for "/" the address $AA39 ($A82D/$A87B, entry 76), which is inside the text panel's font. A "/" in the bubble would draw those bytes as a glyph; the Graphics tab shows it, the jumble in the font's last row. No text the bubble shows uses "/".
