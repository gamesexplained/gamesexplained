Hammerfist

A hidden room shortcut, a safer final recharge, and a sound-register error.

01 · room shortcut

Secret: YCY turns F3 into a room shortcut

Entering YCY in a qualifying high-score row changes the routine that handles F3. F3 normally selects the ordinary fist. The initials check changes a branch, an instruction that chooses where execution goes next. F3 then advances the route number and sends the player through the room-entry routine.

Native instruction traces at $8325 changed $98C1 from $F0 (branch if equal) to $D0 (branch if unequal) for YCY. YAY left the instruction unchanged. The normal callback at $98BF reached the fist indicator; the patched callback reached $AFF1 with the incremented route index. These tests enter the initials-check routine directly; they do not record a high-score playthrough.

02 · safe final charge

Secret: the final room forgives an overcharge

Ordinary recharge overflow destroys the active body. The fourth load treats room 7 differently: it supplies energy 224 and switches bodies. It also skips the score subtraction on that room's recharge path.

Try the recharge comparison →

Native instruction steps in fourth-load rooms 6 and 7 started with energy 224. Room 6 ended with zero; room 7 retained 224. Both switched character. The relevant branches are $913C and $9162. This establishes the room-specific code behavior without claiming a played route through the finale.

03 · effect register

Bug: an effect writes the wrong voice register

The SID sound chip has three independent sound channels, called voices. One effect-retrigger path clears a voice and restores its envelope settings, the numbers that control how the sound rises and fades. The last write, for sustain and release, uses the voice number as its offset. The other writes use seven times the voice number, the spacing between the voices’ control registers.

VICE single-stepped $FE13 through $FE25 with X=0/1/2 and Y=0/7/14. Test value $A7 reached the three addresses shown. The wrong destinations are established; their audible effect depends on the effect and the other voices.

04 · retained source

The payload retains a piece of assembler source

The first disk load contains readable assembler text, a notation for processor instructions, beginning “MULTISETUP LDX #$07”. The text describes transfers between sprite fields. During play, saved game state and actor records reuse the same memory, so parts of the text disappear.

The original unpacked payload and native load-entry snapshot both contain the text at $1200 through $13DD. The shared listing preserves these authored bytes alongside the routines that later use the allocation.

05 · extra layouts

The docking load stores two extra backgrounds

The docking map allocation contains ten background records. Its route entries select rooms 0 through 7. Records 8 and 9 remain in the payload immediately after those rooms and decode with the same tile format.

The map records begin at $3C37. The first glyph plane bounds the room allocation. The route table and room callbacks were checked separately; the page calls these extra stored backgrounds rather than claiming a player can never reach them.
