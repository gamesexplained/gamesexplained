1985 · Elite Systems

Commando

Everything that moves in Commando is a sprite: 112 shapes at $E400, shared out among sixteen slots on the video chip's eight. Here each figure steps through its shapes as the game's code does, a step for each pass of its logic: one a frame, 50 a second, while the screen is still. While it scrolls, one frame in eight goes to drawing the next row of the map, and everything waits.

01 · Super Joe

Four shapes for each of eight directions, shared with every soldier

Super Joe and the enemy soldiers walk with the same 32 shapes. Only the colour tells them apart.

The walk takes its four shapes for each heading from the table at $3C82, a new one every four frames: Super Joe's counted in $04E6 while he moves ($3B8E), the soldiers' from the game's frame counter ($040A), so they all step together. Each diagonal walk borrows one of its shapes from the end of the set ($DF-$E2). Every figure on this page is drawn from the game's bytes on the Source tab, in its colour, with the light grey and black the game shares among all its sprites, on the playfield's brown.

02 · Bullets, grenades and bombs

A grenade grows as it rises and shrinks as it falls

The battlefield is seen from above, so a grenade's height cannot be shown by where it is. Its shape grows as it rises and shrinks as it comes down.

Each one shows its shapes in turn, holding each for as many frames as the game does, then starts again. The bullet's spark ($36F6) and the explosion ($37AC) are the same for Super Joe and the enemy; the enemy's explosion is red, his orange.

03 · Soldiers

Each kind of soldier has its own moves

The soldiers that only walk and shoot use the walk above. The others have shapes of their own, stepped on by their behaviour's timer.

04 · Everything else

Prisoners, vehicles, and shapes that never move

A vehicle is two sprites side by side, 24 pixels apart, moved together. Shapes the game never changes are shown still.

The title's logo is seven sprites, each doubled in width and height, in orange with black and yellow as the shared colours ($0F48). The two doors have handlers but are in no area's list of what to put on the map; they are drawn here in the colour their handler gives them.
