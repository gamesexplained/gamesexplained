1986 · Epyx

World Games

The lifter, the diver, the skier, the lumberjacks, the wrestlers, the bull and the caber are all drawn in poses: a short list of sprites, each with its place and the address of its shape. Each event has its own poses and its own copy of the code that places them.

01 · Everything that moves

Every figure of every event, played from the event's own lists

Each event is a program of its own, loaded over the last, with its own poses and its own lists of them: a pose and how long to show it, step after step. Here each list plays as its event plays it, every pose placed by the event's own routine and drawn in its colours.

The figures are drawn by the game's own code in the built site.

Weightlifting

The lifter in 97 poses, eight sprites each. A lift is a script of poses, each held for so many frames, with windows in which the stick must push the way the bar names. Here every window is pushed in its last frame, except the last of each lift, which is held through: the lifter rocks under the weight until it ends. The bar's plates are drawn into the screen's bitmap rather than as sprites, so they are not here.

Barrel jumping

The skater and the barrels in 120 poses. Each list is a pose a step, to $FF, shown for so many turns of a delay loop: about two frames a pose for the take-off, eight for the opening. The stride's eight poses are the stride's states, which the stick moves on, left from the third and right from the seventh.

Cliff diving

The diver, the splashes, the pelican and the gull in 175 poses. Every list is a pose and its frames, ending on a pose held or going back to play again. The pelican on its rock takes one of three poses by how far the diver is above or below it, and in the air the stick chooses the body position, from tucked to straight. Three sets of shapes take turns at $7000, so the dive, the water and the pelican's extra turns each have room for their own.

Slalom

The skier's frame comes from the turn, up to five steps either way, and from the stance: the stick up, neither, or fire held. A step of the turn waits until the stick has stayed put long enough, longer when skiing slowly. The push-off, the falls and the stops are sequences of frames, each with its time; the falls' shapes are swapped in over the push-off's once the skier is away.

Caber toss

The thrower plays a list for each state, its poses and their frames side by side. The run steps through six poses over and over. The caber has lists of its own, each step a pose (or $FE to keep it), a move across and down, and its frames, and they move it here as they do in the event. The walk off is drawn from a second set of shapes swapped in after the toss.

Bull riding

The bull and its rider are drawn together, in 52 frames stored facing left; a frame number with bit 7 set is the same frame turned round, mirrored as its shapes are copied. A ride's moves step on every 7 frames, and the chute and the endings every 11.

Log rolling

Each lumberjack's picture comes from its pose: the run is poses 0 to 5, round and round as the feet go, and an arm pose runs on to 16. A fall is played in stages from a set of 100 pictures unpacked for it, one set for each lumberjack's fall: the fall and the splash, then the two lumberjacks in turn, the one in the water and the one left on the log.

Sumo

Every pose names the next one forward and the next one back, and the wrestler's speed steps it on, so the poses fall into loops of their own. A throw runs back through its poses, and the wrestler thrown runs forward through a fall. These are the right wrestler's poses, in red; the left wrestler is the same poses turned round, in white.

Poses nothing shows

Three of barrel jumping's poses point their sprites at the character set at $4000, and no list in the event shows them.

Every pose is made from the listing's bytes by the event's own code, run on the kit's 6502 in this page, with the video chip's registers caught as the code writes them. The routines are wl_pose_shapes ($1E3A) and wl_pose_place ($1E8B) in weightlifting, bj_draw_skater ($2394), cd_draw_diver ($21C8), sl_draw_frame ($8EC6), lr_build_sprites ($2445), ct_draw_thrower ($12F6), br_draw_bull ($1E16) and su_pose_right ($1DB6). Where an event changes its shapes during play, the page runs its own routine for that first: cd_sprite_set ($10B3), sl_swap_shapes ($C25E), lr_unpack_set ($2992) and ct_swap_shapes ($1EB2). The lists are read from the listing as the events read them: wl_scripts ($2000), bj_play_poses ($229C), cd_diver_list ($2441), sl_next_pose ($C203) and sl_pose_frames ($8F86), ct_thrower_list ($1DC3) and ct_caber_moves ($1EE7), br_next_frame ($130A), lr_poses ($1EED) and lr_fall_step ($29E4) and su_next_pose ($251F). Where a pose's time hangs on the player, the speed or a delay loop, it is shown for a fixed number of frames.

02 · One pose

A pose is a list of sprites

A pose names each sprite's place against the pose's own and the shape it shows. Choose a pose to see its sprites one by one.

The places are counted from the place the page gives the event's routine, which is where the pose stands in play. Each sprite is boxed at its size, numbered as the video chip numbers it; sprite 0 is drawn in front.

In six of the eight events, weightlifting, barrel jumping, cliff diving, slalom, the caber toss and bull riding, a pose is a count of sprites and then, for each, its place from the pose's and the address of its shape. Placing a pose is a loop over them that writes each sprite's place to the video chip and points the sprite at its shape. A shape is 63 bytes, 21 rows of three, and the same six keep the rest of what a sprite needs in the 64th byte, which the chip never reads: the colour in its low four bits, multicolour in bit 7, double width in bit 6 and double height in bit 5 ($1E8B, $23C9). Sumo makes every sprite multicolour and gives each wrestler one colour of its own.

The video chip finds a sprite's shape through a pointer of one byte, so it can only show the 256 shapes in the 16 kilobytes it is looking at, and the screen and its pictures take some of those. The events have more, and each makes room its own way. Weightlifting copies a pose's eight shapes to $4000 each time it shows one, from 206 kept higher up. Bull riding copies its eight to $5400, and turns each row round on the way when the bull faces right, so its frames are stored facing left only. Cliff diving keeps three sets of shapes and copies one to $7000: the dive's, the water's once the diver is in, and the pelican's extra turns. Slalom swaps the falls' shapes in over the push-off's, and the caber toss a second set for walking off. Log rolling keeps its pictures packed and unpacks a set at a time over the last, the standing lumberjacks' to start and a fall's when someone falls.

Sumo and log rolling need more than eight sprites. The two wrestlers are eight sprites each, and the left one is the right one's poses turned round, each shape mirrored as it is copied ($119B); a multiplexer moves sprites down the screen as the beam passes the ones above ($1581). Log rolling sorts its sprites by height ($25D2) and reloads those past the eighth into sprites that have finished, in up to three bands down the screen ($26DF). How one frame of the caber toss is put together, scenery and sprites, is on How it works.
