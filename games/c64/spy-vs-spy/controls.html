1984 · First Star Software

Spy vs Spy

Each spy has a joystick, and the game reads both, with three keys, on every raster interrupt. The keyboard and the black spy's port share their wiring, so five keys can play the black spy, and a held black stick switches off the pause, music and restart keys.

01 · What the game reads

One routine reads both sticks and three keys, twice a frame

Hold sticks and keys below to see what the game reads and what it does about it.

Port 2: the white spy

Port 1: the black spy

Keys

A port of read_controls ($94A1), run once each time a stick or key changes, as the game runs it on every raster interrupt. Checked against the game's own routine in a 6502 simulator over 46,141 combinations of sticks, keys, demo and held-S states, from the bytes of the listing, with the keyboard and the ports modelled as the C64 wires them: every byte agrees. Not tested on a real machine or in the emulator.

Twice a frame, at each of its two raster interrupts, the game calls read_controls. The routine copies joystick port 2 into $0285 for the white spy and port 1 into $0286 for the black, then looks at three keys: F5 goes back to the options screen, RUN/STOP pauses the game and stops both clocks until it is pressed again, and S switches the music off and on. A stick reads 0 in the bit for each direction held: up, down, left, right and fire, from bit 0 to bit 4. In the attract demo the game ignores the sticks' directions and fire on either stick ends the demo.

In a one-player game the computer plays the black spy, and its moves come from the computer player's own byte rather than from port 1 (computer_player $9A67). Both sticks still work on the options screen, so either player can set up the game.

03 · The keyboard

Secret: five keys play the black spy

In a two-player game, 1, ←, CTRL, 2 and SPACE move the black spy up, down, left and right, and fire.

The C64's keyboard and its port-1 joystick share wires. The keyboard is eight rows of eight keys: the computer selects rows by writing to $DC00 and reads the columns at $DC01, and joystick port 1 pulls down the same five column lines as the first five columns. read_controls finishes every call by leaving row 7 selected, and the next call reads port 1 before selecting anything else. So the black spy's byte is the stick and row 7's first five keys together: 1 is up, ← is down, CTRL is left, 2 is right and SPACE is fire. Press them in the panel above.

The same wiring makes SPACE work as fire wherever the game takes fire from either stick: it starts a game from the options screen and ends the attract demo, though the options screen's own text asks for the fire button.

04 · The three keys

Holding the black spy's stick switches off F5, RUN/STOP and S

KeyRow, columnThe row must readDoes
F50, 6$BFback to the options screen ($0282 bit 7)

S1, 5$DFmusic off or on, once per press ($0284); on again, the tune starts from the top

The routine checks each key by comparing its whole row with one value, the row with that key alone held. Any other key held in the same row changes the value, and so does the black spy's stick, which pulls down columns of every row. So S does nothing while left SHIFT is held, RUN/STOP does nothing while SPACE is held, and none of the three keys work while the black player holds the stick in any direction or holds fire. The white player's stick, on the other port, does not get in the way. Try it in the panel: hold S, then push the black spy's stick.
