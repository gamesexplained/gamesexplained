1984 · First Star Software

Spy vs Spy

Every picture the spies are drawn with, moving as the game moves them: walking, fighting, caught by the traps and knocked out, and the sprites for what they carry and the plane they escape in.

01 · Walking

A spy walks in three pictures, there and back, one each time the game serves it

The spies are not sprites. Each frame is a stack of strips drawn into the bitmap, and a spy's shapes are kept facing one way and turned round when it turns.

From spy_anim_start ($6D92): the sequence for the facing ($B602) names a list of six frames ($B6B0), the black spy's six lists further on ($B55A), and the next frame is drawn each time the main loop serves the spy (store_position, $7022). The loop serves the two spies by turns (next_spy, $674E), so a walking spy steps about 18 times a second; every card on this page keeps that pace. A frame ($B710) is a stack of strips, each a shape from $AB76 on (draw_spy_shape, $77FC). The shapes are stored facing one way ($BAF0) and turned round in memory when a frame needs them the other way (mirror_shape, $865B). Drawn in the colours of a room's cells, white, light red and black, on one of the rooms' backgrounds.

02 · The club

The stick holds the club in five places, and a swing knocks the other spy down

With fire held in a shared room, a spy turns to face the other and the stick moves the club. Swinging it from one side to the other, up to down or forward to back, hits the other spy if it is in reach. Ten hits knock a spy out.

From spy_move ($6BEA): the stick gives a move ($B594) and the move a frame for the spy and the side the other spy is on (move_frames, $B5A4), shown here facing right; facing left the game uses the same frames mirrored, 47 to 60. A swing in reach (club_in_reach, $6CF1) shows the other spy knocked down for six of its turns, a frame by the swing (swing_frames, $B5B8). Thrown by the spring or the guard at the exit door, a spy shows a frame by its facing ($B564): facing right or left, the club's.

03 · Frozen and knocked out

A spy flinches while it is frozen and rises as an angel when it is knocked out

When a trap goes off, the other spy freezes for 40 of its turns, and so does the winner of a fight. A spy caught by a trap or the guard, or beaten in a fight, rises out of the room as an angel.

The frozen spy from spy_walk ($6B3D): its flinch frame ($B562) plus bit 1 of the count of turns left ($021F), so the frame changes every second turn. The angel from spy_sinking ($6959): the frame ($B560) plus 1 on every other turn, and a line higher every second turn, until it is more than 14 lines above the room's floor line. Then its monitor is turned off, and the spy lies stunned for 144 of its turns.

04 · The traps

A trap that goes off is eight shapes drawn over the spy

The spy stops where it stands and becomes part of the room's picture. Then the trap's shapes are drawn over it, a step at a time, and the spy rises as an angel.

From spy_action_start and spy_action_step ($69C2, $6A4E): the spy is copied into the room behind it (bake_spy, $7ABA), and after a wait of 13 turns each of eight steps draws an object shape (action_frames, $0973) in its own box, over the room as it stood (draw_shape, $7C7E), three turns a step. What a step's box leaves out stays on the screen. The bomb also shakes the screen. The gun on its string jumps, at offsets for each step ($09A3, $09AB). The water bucket ends in a nine-step sequence of four turns a step ($B582): the skeleton, with the room flashing white ($B570).

05 · What the spies carry

What a spy holds is a sprite beside it

The sprites are kept for small things: what a spy carries, the four lights for the items on its trapulator, and the arrow that picks a trap.

From build_spy_sprite and place_spy_sprite ($92AC, $90A4): each held object picks a class ($14A0), and the class a packed shape ($1576 on, class plus 5) and a colour ($14F4 on). The four items share one sprite. While an action runs, the spy holds a marker of its own, the last two. The shared multicolours are white and black (reset_play_state, $8EBF). The trapulator's lights are sprites 0-3 in their colours ($149A), and its arrow is drawn in yellow (place_arrow, $9142).

06 · The airport

The plane is six sprites in the winner's colour

The spy who reaches the exit with the full briefcase walks to the plane, climbs in, and flies off.

From airport_scene and place_plane_sprites ($8722, $91BC): six sprites ($21DA on) at offsets ($21C5, $21CC) whose first multicolour is the winner's, white or grey ($21C3), and the winner in sprite 0. plane_takeoff ($885D) revs the engine for about two seconds, then moves the plane 160 steps, two pixels a step, climbing a line every fourth step once it is past column $4B, each step a wait that shortens as it speeds up.

07 · Never shown

Two frames are never drawn

Nothing in the code picks frames 63 and 64, one for each spy. Frame 0, a copy of the white spy standing towards you, is drawn once, on the first turn of a game, before the white spy has a frame of its own.
