Five stored tunes and six sound effects, played by a driver that runs seven times in six video frames.

The player

Play the stored sounds

The page runs the game's own sound driver, the bytes at $8000-$8DFF, on the kit's 6502 simulator, and plays what it writes through a model of the SID chip with the filter modelled as one 6581's. A test (tests/music.js) runs the game's own interrupt routine on the same bytes and compares every SID register after every frame of every sound below; they match. Low notes are quiet on laptop and phone speakers.

The driver

Three voices, seven bytes of state each

The raster handler at line 230 calls the driver once a frame (play_music, $2AA6). Each call runs two ticks (music_play, $8003), and the phase counter $C0, which adds $33 a frame, carries on every sixth frame and makes the driver run a second time. The driver therefore runs seven times in six frames. A tick counts down a delay and then steps each of the three voices through seven bytes of state: its pattern pointer, its note, and the slide, vibrato, and pulse sweep (music_tick, $8083).

Each tune is a list of patterns for each voice, stored as pointers at $8530 (five tunes, three voices each). A pattern is read by music_next_note ($8191), which also starts a pending effect on the second voice and a tune change at the next pattern end. A list ends with $FF, and music_end_of_tune ($83D1) then restarts the tune and looks the next one up in the follow-on table at $8D6C. Notes use the frequency tables at $843C and $8486.

Sound effects are requested by number through $B5 and play on the second voice. The game requests six: $1E from the spray, $1F from pickups and rescued people, $20 when a shot lands, $21 when the firefighter is hit, $22 from the movement code, and $24 when a fire goes out.
