1986 · MicroProse

Gunship

Everything that moves in the flight. The gun's tracer, the missiles and the explosions are sprites over the 3D view, moved by the flight's own raster interrupt; the marks on the map are sprites too. The tanks, bunkers, bases and hills of the 3D world are wireframe shapes, all of which turn below.

01 · The window's sprites

Sprites for the gun, the missiles and the explosions

The 3D view is drawn line by line into a bitmap, but what flies through it is sprites: the sight, the target's box, the tracer, missiles leaving the wings and in flight, explosions and the enemy's gun flashes. Each tile here runs the flight's own interrupt code, a frame at a time, fifty times a second.

The page runs the flight's own interrupt, the sprite part of irq_bottom from $92F9 to $944D, on the kit's 6502 simulator over the flight's listing (the gs-sprites block). It gives each sprite its frame, and the launches and the enemy's flashes their places and colours; the page sets what the flight's main loop would: the gun fired and a hit, a launch, missiles in their slots, an explosion's start, the enemy firing. The window's sprites are multicoloured but for the sight, with the two shared colours the interrupt sets.

The interrupt runs once a frame at line $FB, below the window, and the window's sprite registers are its own instructions: the main loop writes each sprite's place, colour and frame into a table at $8154, and sprites_copy writes them into the interrupt's LDA instructions, which store them into the video chip. So the main loop can take as long as its 3D picture needs, and the sprites still move every frame.

02 · The panel and the map

The marks on the panel and the map

A second interrupt, at line $9A where the window ends, gives the panel its own eight sprites: the pitch ladder, the slip and waypoint marks, the four needles and the helicopter's mark on the CRT. On the big map the cross and the helicopter are sprites again.

The cross and the helicopter are the big map's own interrupt (irq_map) and heading_sprite run on the kit's 6502 simulator, the page turning the heading. The stills are the shapes as the listing holds them, in the colours the panel gives them.

03 · The wireframe shapes

Every wireframe shape in the 3D world, turning

The tanks, missile launchers, bunkers, bases and hills in the 3D view are all wireframes. Each is one record of a few dozen bytes in the theatre's terrain file.

-

Drawn from the bytes of Western Europe's terrain file, as they sit in memory during a flight. A number written with a $ is in hexadecimal, base 16, and most such numbers on this page are addresses in the C64's 64 KB of memory. Each point is a combination of one x value, one y value and one height, picked by the tables at $1C37, $1CA8 and $1D19, or it is a fraction of another point. Every edge the shape lists is drawn here. The game leaves out an edge when both of the faces it joins turn away from the eye. Pick a shape from the grid or the list, and drag the large picture to turn it.

A shape's record starts with how many points it has and their numbers ($1E9F on). Then come its faces, five bytes each, a colour number and its edges. An edge names two faces and two points, or a single point drawn as a dot. A flat shape has no faces, so all its edges are always drawn.

How the world puts the shapes on its map, near and far, and how a hill's faces crash the helicopter, is on How it works.
