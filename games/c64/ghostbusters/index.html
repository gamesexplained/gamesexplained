David Crane’s adaptation of Ghostbusters puts you in charge of a ghost-catching business. Buying equipment and earning back your investment matter alongside the proton streams, while the film’s theme becomes a bouncing-ball sing-along.

01 · The game

A franchise to keep afloat

The bank lends you $10,000. Before catching a ghost, you choose a car and load it with equipment, steering a forklift across the shop to carry each purchase aboard. A cheaper car leaves more money for tools, but its limited carrying space restricts what you can take.1

Once equipped, you move the Ghostbusters logo through the city’s streets, drawing a route to a haunted building. The journey becomes an overhead driving scene before you arrive outside to set a trap. Two men use their proton streams to herd a Slimer above it (how the catch works). Bring the streams too close together and the backpacks short out, sending the team back to headquarters.

Successful catches earn fees. Trips to headquarters empty the traps and restore the team, but the city keeps getting more dangerous. Its PK energy meter measures supernatural activity, and wandering ghosts raise it when they reach the Temple of Zuul.

The Marshmallow Man threatens both the city and your finances. Diverting him with bait earns a reward from the mayor; letting him flatten a building brings a damage bill. When the Keymaster and the Gatekeeper meet at Zuul, your balance decides whether the bank forecloses or the team gets a final attempt to close the portal.

02 · The development

The driving game behind Ghostbusters

Crane, who had previously created Pitfall! for the Atari 2600, already had a driving game called Car Wars in development. That unfinished project supplied Ghostbusters with its economy, car customisation, and driving sequence, according to his recollection quoted by Games That Weren’t.7

Those inherited ideas give the film adaptation its shape. Choosing a vehicle affects how much equipment you can carry. Buying that equipment reduces the balance you must rebuild through ghost-catching fees. The business continues across games through an account number that lets you bring your money back.

Retro Gamer described Ghostbusters as having been turned around in six weeks. The economy, the car choice, and the driving scene came from Car Wars, not from the film. The ghost-catching scenes were what the film added.6

03 · The makers

The people behind the presentation

The title screen credits David Crane alone. Later sources identify more contributors: MobyGames lists Adam Bellin for additional programming and Hilary Mills for graphics.3 C64-Wiki and Wikipedia attribute the arrangement of Ray Parker Jr.’s theme song to Russell Lieblich.45

The sing-along has its own production story. Wikipedia, citing Retro Gamer, says Crane passed the bouncing-ball sequence to Garry Kitchen and his game designers, who added it at the last minute.4

Crane remembered writing an audio digitiser and playback program for the Commodore 64, although he could not clearly recall who supplied the voice. That uncertainty remains part of the credits.6 The speech itself can be heard on the Music and speech page, including a shorter version of the title call stored in the game but never used during play.

04 · Reception

What the reviewers heard

Writing in Personal Computer Games in January 1985, Chris Anderson praised the presentation, particularly the music and speech. His judgment also carried a qualification: he thought the game’s success depended heavily on how well it recreated the film’s atmosphere.8

The logo, the theme, and the spoken title call all arrive before the equipment shopping begins, which is where the film’s atmosphere is set.

Your Commodore also praised the programming and highlighted speech produced without extra hardware. The magazine attributed the effect to the sound chip’s filters; the program actually produces speech by rapidly changing its volume.9 The speech player shows how.

The Commodore 64 version came first, followed by adaptations for other computers and consoles. In Britain, Ghostbusters topped the chart from January to March 1985.4

05 · Inside the game

What the account number remembers

Ghostbusters lets a business survive the end of a game without saving a file. Its account number contains the balance, rounded down to hundreds of dollars, together with a check calculated from the player’s name. Entering the name and number brings that spending power into another franchise.

The check also has weaknesses. Names made from the same letters share accounts, and the number reader accepts some unexpected characters. The account-number demonstration lets you make a number and see what the game accepts.

Two details contradict common advice. A PK meter at 9,999 does not end the city phase: the Keymaster and Gatekeeper must meet at Zuul. The bank also accepts a balance equal to your starting funds, despite the ending’s claim that you made more money.
