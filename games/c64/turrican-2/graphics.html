1991 · Rainbow Arts · Commodore 64

Turrican II: The Final Fight

Every stored sprite piece, the character shapes, and the pictures that frame the game.

01 · player

The player uses three overlapping sprites

Two multicolour pieces draw the upper and lower body. A third piece has its height doubled and supplies the outline. Seven shape numbers make each walking cycle.

The shape changes when the controller’s divider expires, every four calls. The preview schedules one call per PAL frame. It runs the isolated animation rule, without moving a player through a level. Sources: $08D3, $08DA, $12D5, and $0F33.

02 · pieces

The pieces used to draw moving things

These are the stored pieces from which characters, enemies, weapons, and large bosses are assembled. The colour control lets a piece be seen in its different actor colours. Code and known control-table slots are omitted. The generated scene banks are drawn separately from the bytes that preceded initialization. Character codes that become weapons or effects remain visible in their font sheets.

03 · pictures

The story is told with sixteen pictures

The story pictures use one colour pair for each image. The title and ending use four-colour cells. Each choice draws the decoded bitmap bytes, including the smaller ending inset.

04 · loading picture

Eight sprites make the loading picture

The loading display uses shapes $A29B through $A49A, copied to bitmap RAM under the I/O area. The preview arranges the eight pieces by their stored X coordinates. The game moves the group vertically while the next files load.

05 · ending text

The ending builds its text from two fonts

Each row chooses a colour, horizontal shift, and starting column. Font commands can change type within a row. The game puts those glyphs into an eight-sprite text field and scrolls the field. All 195 row and control records are available here. The fonts start at $906C and $93DC.

06 · playfield

The player shares the screen with character terrain

The terrain uses eight-by-eight character shapes, while the player and larger enemies use overlapping sprite pieces. Raster interrupts change the display between the playfield, sprite bands, and the status panel.
