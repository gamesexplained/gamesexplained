1991 · Rainbow Arts · Commodore 64

Turrican II: The Final Fight

Manfred Trenz’s last game for the Commodore 64. Its bosses are a handful of sprites redrawn down the screen, and its disks still carry a developer’s save of level 1-2, taken in the middle of a game.

01 · game

Bren McGuire fights his way back

Bren McGuire survives the attack on the starship Avalon 1. Wearing a Turrican fighting suit, he crosses five worlds to reach the Machine. The game tells how it happened before its title screen appears, in a story of sixteen pictures. Most levels are explored on foot. The third world puts him in a ship for three scrolling flying stages, and each of those loads its own player, scrolling, and weapons over the platform game.

On foot the suit fires a spread shot, a laser, or a bouncing shot, each made stronger by pickups. Holding fire grows a long beam of light from the suit. The space bar sends a power line across the screen, and down with space turns the suit into a spinning wheel that drops bombs.

Level 1-1: the first world opens in a rocky valley.

02 · music

Music: the soundtrack is saved for the flying stages and the final fight

The levels on foot load no music player at all, so all three of the sound chip’s voices are free for the guns and the explosions. The flying stages bring their own player: world 3-1’s keeps two voices for music and leaves one for the guns. Worlds 3-1 and 3-2 change to a second tune when their boss arrives, and the final level plays the tune of world 3-3 again.

The story at the start has a player of its own, and its tune keeps playing while the game starts to load. Markus Siebold wrote the music. The title screen credits the music routine to Dave Hogendorf and Edwin Determann.

03 · effects

Sound: each weapon has a sound of its own

The sound effects are numbered patterns in one shared player. Each gun, the beam, the power line, and the wheel’s bombs ask for a number of their own. Explosions share one, and several bosses have a sound saved for their destruction.

04 · scores

Fifty diamonds buy a continue

Diamonds collect on the panel, and every fifty of them buy another continue. When the Machine falls, the ending turns whatever is left into points.
