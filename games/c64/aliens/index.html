Activision's game of James Cameron's film is six different games in a row, joined by the film's story told in pages of text and speech balloons. Each level arrives from the disk as a file of its own and runs on a shared engine.

Six missions from the film

The game follows the film from Ripley's hearing on Gateway Station to her fight with the alien Queen. Between the levels it tells the story in pages of text and in scenes of speech balloons, which RETURN moves on.1 Each of the six levels plays differently.

The marines first fly the drop-ship down to the planet through a pipe of rings. Next they walk four lost marines back to the APC, one at a time, through a complex of rooms. A marine with a flame thrower then holds the operations room while a cutter burns through a door. After that the squad crawls through the air ducts, which cost a marine for every grenade. Ripley goes back alone to find Newt before the station blows. Last, in the power loader, she fights the Queen.

The marines alive at the end of a level go with the player to the next. A status code after each level holds them, so a game can start again from there.

The people behind the game

The title credits the design to Steve Cartwright, Glyn Anderson, Peter Kaminski, and Gene Smith, the music and sound effects to Russell Lieblich, and production to Brad Fregger. The manual adds Hilary Mills and Jennifer Diane Reitz for additional graphics, two names the game itself never shows (see Discoveries).1

Lieblich's three tunes and sixteen effects play on the Music tab, run by the game's own sound code. In Europe the game came out as Aliens: US Version, because Activision's British label, Electric Dreams, had already published a different game called Aliens.2

Creepy aliens, and every level its own game

The American magazine Info reviewed the Commodore 64 version in its issue of January and February 1987 and gave it four stars out of five. Its reviewers praised both the monsters and the variety: "The aliens are appropriately creepy, and each sequence is well done & plays quite differently from the others."3

That variety runs through the code as well. Each level is its own file, loaded over the engine when it begins, with its own sprites, its own pictures, and often its own way of using the machine. The drop-ship draws its nearest ring by moving two sprites on every line of the screen, and the operations room shows ten aliens with four sprites.

What's hidden in it

One of the game's sixteen sound effects is never played. The level skip the manual describes withholds the ending, and the cracked disk studied here carries a second skip that does not. Level 4 finds where aliens come out with a test that reads the wrong byte, and the APC rescue keeps a block of rooms free of marines.

Explore the complex and the air ducts on the Maps tab, watch every level's sprites move, or find the keys the manual leaves out.
