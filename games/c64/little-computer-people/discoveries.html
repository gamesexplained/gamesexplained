Commodore 64 · 1985 · Activision

Little Computer People

Little Computer People scores your manners, and "please" is worth four points on its own. A sentence its parser cannot understand repeats your last request.

01 · Manners

Secret: "please" is worth four points

PLEASE is a rule on its own, worth four points on top of whatever the request scores. For most requests that is the difference between now and later, and for some between later and never.

How he scores what you type is on How it works.

02 · Nonsense

Bug: nonsense puts your last request back in the queue

The parser never clears its list of actions ($C00B), so a sentence that matches no rule puts the previous request in the queue again. In the emulator three nonsense sentences after "hello" left three more greetings waiting.

03 · Words he cannot read

Bug: some words can never be understood

WHAT'S has an apostrophe, which ends a word. START, LIKE, APATHETIC and IS are listed twice, and only the first entry is ever found, so the rule that needs the second can never fire.

04 · Food

Secret: CTRL F does nothing until he has eaten

The food delivery is refused while all four places in the cupboard are full ($2B5C), and the cupboard starts full. Once he has eaten, the key works.

The other CTRL keys are on How it works.

05 · More from the code

Things the manual never told you

The time you type sets only the wall clock

The house keeps its own hour, which starts at 15 whatever you type ($0379). The am/pm byte is counted up and never read.

His random numbers start from "Zeke"

The four bytes of the random number generator's state ($0414) arrive holding the letters Z, e, k, e. Just before them, "Billy Bob Binkle" ($03FC) arrives too, and nothing in the program reads it.

The games are only in the dictionary

CARDS, POKER, WAR, ANAGRAMS and HANGMAN are words he knows, and they lead to activity 126, which is the chooser. No routine, table or image in the program belongs to a card game.

The sound driver writes to voice 3 every frame

Its tick begins by recomputing two pointers of the piano piece in the RAM under the I/O chips ($B4FB). The interrupt has the chips switched in, so it reads a write-only SID register and stores 4 and 8 more than it read into voice 3's frequency and pulse width, $D40E and $D410. While a sound plays voice 3 the driver's own writes cover them.

Leave him alone and the screen goes dark

After ten periods of 65,536 frames without a key, about 3.6 hours, the screen blanks ($0589), and illness cannot start. The counter is 8-bit: after 93 hours it wraps and both come back.

The water tank never fills its top line

It has twelve lines, and CTRL W stops adding water at eleven ($2B54).
