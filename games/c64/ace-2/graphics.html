1987 · Cascade Games

ACE 2

The enemy jet, the missiles and the targets on the ground are not sprites. They are pictures the game builds into fresh characters of the view's own character set, a few bytes at a time, on every pass of its main loop. Only the explosions are sprites.

01 · Everything that moves

Every moving shape, a picture a pass

The game draws each pilot's view once a pass of its main loop, about 16 passes a second. Here each thing moves at that rate, chosen and built by the game's own code, in the colours of plane one's view over the sea.

The enemy's pictures are chosen by the game's own code from enemy_in_view ($2C86), for its heading against ours turning a full circle, with both planes' roll and our pitch level, then built by shape_build ($1581) and shape_shift ($15DB), all run on the kit's 6502 over the listing. The distant dots and the missile are dot_shape ($5FD4); the targets' pictures are chosen by our roll through target_picture_by_roll ($7570); the explosion steps through explosion_sequence ($4DD1) as step_explosion ($214A) counts. The colours are the view's: black, light blue ($D022), white ($D023) and the cells' own colour, blue over the sea and yellow over land.

The enemy has four looks by its distance. At range 6 or more its cell is left empty. At 2 to 5 it is a single dot, or two rows of one when it comes to range 2. Nearer, it is a medium picture of a single cell's eight bytes, drawn into two cells by two so it can move a pixel at a time, and nearest, within $78 across and $13 in height, a large picture of two columns of 16 rows, spread over three by three cells. The large picture is chosen through four tables from the enemy's pitch, roll and heading against ours, and our own roll and pitch (large_view_step_one to large_view_pictures), and comes out as one of fifteen pictures in one of four frames.

Every picture is drawn in the fourth colour of a multicolour cell, the colour held for the cell itself. The game sets that colour for the whole view when the ground under the plane changes, blue over the sea and yellow over land (map_square_check, $14AF), so the enemy jet turns yellow when its pilot's view is over land. A dot in the view's light blue ($55) is lost against the sky, which is why the targets' far dots are drawn in it, over the ground only, and the enemy's in the cell colour ($FF). A missile's dot takes one or the other by the screen being drawn ($59), which changes every pass, so a missile against the sky flickers.

The fifteen large pictures fill only 480 bytes at $FE00-$FFDF, the top of memory, under the KERNAL ROM. Each is drawn in one of four frames, as stored, mirrored left to right, upside down or turned half round: shape_build reads the picture's bytes in one of four orders (shape_orders, $7241) and, for the mirrored frames, turns each byte round through mirror_table ($9000). That makes sixty drawings, and the tables pick 39 of them for the angles the enemy can be seen from. The spy ship and the radar station each have eight pictures in two sizes, chosen by our own roll alone and turned half round for the half of the roll that turns the view over. The explosion's twelve pictures are sprites, windows of 63 bytes on one run of bytes from $F026, so some share bytes, and its sequence never goes past picture 10, so picture 11 is never seen.

02 · How a picture is placed

A picture moves inside its cells two pixels and one row at a time

The enemy's large picture at each fine position. Move it across and down to see it shifted inside the three by three cells it is drawn into.

shape_shift ($15DB) run on the kit's 6502 over the picture shape_build made, with the fine position in the top bits of $05 (down) and $06 (across), as project_to_view ($3948) leaves them. The lines mark the character cells.

The view is characters, so a picture can only go where there are characters to draw it in. project_to_view gives each object a cell and a fine position inside it. shape_shift moves the picture down by the fine row, 0 to 7, and right by 0 to 3 double-width pixels, one bit pair at a time, through the cells to its right. To do it fast for the large size it swaps the 72-byte picture into zero page and back. shape_draw ($16A7) then gives each cell the picture touches a character of its own, from $E5 up for the enemy: the eight bytes of the view character already in that cell, with the picture laid over them through a mask ($9F00), so the sky or the horizon shows round the jet. The finished view is drawn into the character set that is not on show, and the interrupt swaps the two (flip_screens, $4C5C), so the picture is never seen half built.
