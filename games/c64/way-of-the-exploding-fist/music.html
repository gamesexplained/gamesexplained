01 · Music

Music: four tunes, tuned for an American machine

The attract mode, the one-player game, the two-player game and the high-score table each have a tune, played on three voices by a driver called once a frame from the raster interrupt at line 255.

The game's own driver ($09A5) running on the kit's 6502 simulator in this page, over the music bytes as the game loads them. Played through the site's model of the SID, filter on (the cutoff is one 6581's). Each voice shows the note the tune asks for and, under Frequency, the note a PAL machine actually plays.

Each voice follows its own order list of patterns, with commands to play a pattern, jump and repeat ($0D31). There are 25 instruments, with pulse-width and filter sweeps driven by three slow oscillators and by voice 3's output read back from the chip ($0DFC).

The note table ($0D14) gives each semitone's frequency for the clock of an NTSC machine: at 1,022,727 Hz every note is slightly flat, by at most 5.4 cents. A PAL Commodore 64 runs at 985,248 Hz, so on this PAL release every note sounds 65 to 70 cents flat, two-thirds of a semitone.

02 · Sound

Sound: the shouts are played by switching the volume on and off

The grunts and cries of the fight are recordings, played while the music runs. The sound chip has no way to play a sample, so the game flips its master volume between full and silent thousands of times a second.

The volume register also sets the music's loudness, so the samples ride on top of the tune and the tune briefly disappears while the volume is at zero. Each sample byte is one half of a wave: the volume stays where it is for 9 cycles per unit of the table entry the byte picks, plus about 108 for the interrupt itself (measured in the emulator), then flips. The upper strip plots that time for every byte of the sample, so a stretch of short flips is a high, buzzing sound and a run of long ones a low one; the lower strip is the wave itself, 30 milliseconds of it at the playhead. DEL in the attract mode turns the samples off ($CF); the menu calls them sound effects.

03 · Sound

Sound: the loader shouts as its picture comes up

The cassette and the disk each play one shout while the game loads, as the loading picture comes up (Graphics). Loading stops while it plays, for two and a quarter seconds.

The shout is drawn and played by the site's own scripts.

The loader plays it the way the game plays its shouts, by switching the volume between full and silent. Each of the shout's 5,591 bytes picks the time to the next switch from a table. A timer counts down in steps of 9 cycles, so a byte that picks a count of n gives 9 × (n + 1) cycles. The switches come on average every 394 cycles, a wave of about 1,250 hertz, and the whole shout takes about two and a quarter seconds.

On the disk the shout and its table are two files of their own, M.TSOUND and M.SPCHTBL, and neither is in the game's memory once it runs. The game's own shouts above are four other samples.
