The picture the cassette and the disk show while they load, from the release's own bytes.

01 · The loading picture

The picture comes up with a shout

Melbourne House's 1985 cassette first shows two screens of text: one that says what the loader's border colours mean, and one that lists the game's keys. Then, 138 seconds in, it puts up its picture whole: MELBOURNE HOUSE PRESENTS over a pagoda, Mount Fuji, a gateway and a garden in blossom, with The way of the exploding fist across the foot. As the picture comes up, the loader stops for two seconds and plays a shout. Then the picture stays, in silence, for three minutes while the game loads.

The cassette's own bytes, recorded in VICE with the picture up and drawn as the video chip draws them, with the border as the loader painted it in that one frame.

The shout is played the way the game plays its own, by switching the volume on and off, and you can hear it on Music and speech.

The disk does the same once 1 is pressed at its menu: the picture and the shout are files of their own on it, M.PICTURE, M.TSOUND and M.SPCHTBL, byte for byte the bytes the cassette loads. The stripes are the cassette loader's. While the tape plays, it moves the border on to the next of the C64's sixteen colours again and again, faster than the screen is drawn, so the border fills with bands of every colour.
