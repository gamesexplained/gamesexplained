A joystick each, or a block of nine keys each on one keyboard: sixteen stick positions make the moves, and the function keys set up the game.

01 · The stick

Sixteen stick positions, 21 moves

Every move but the two blocks comes from the stick, with or without fire. The blocks happen on their own when a fighter pulls back as a blow comes in reach.

Each fighter has 21 moves, numbered as the game numbers them. Nineteen come from the stick, with or without fire: down-forward gives one punch from standing and another from a crouch, and back with fire gives the turn round and then, held, the spinning kick.

Sixteen stick positions give sixteen moves, in the table at $286C. Down-forward from standing is the middle punch (move $18), and from a crouch the low punch (move 7). Fire with back from standing becomes the turn round ($12), and only a fighter still holding it there gets the spinning kick ($11, $29B4).

Pulling back while the opponent's blow is in reach turns into the block that stops it ($2AF2), so a player defends by retreating.

Each card is the move's key frame, built from the game's tables like the step cards on the Graphics tab: the frame a blow is tested on ($1204), or a typical pose for the other moves. The stick is shown for a fighter facing right, so → is towards the opponent; facing left swaps left and right ($2857). Points are for a whole point, in the game's own table ($12A5). The game stores no names, so the names describe what each move's frames show. The block each blow meets confirms its height ($118F): the high block answers the high punch and the high, flying, back and spinning kicks, and the low block answers the low and middle punches and the low and middle kicks. A pull-back never blocks a sweep ($2AF2); the computer opponent jumps it instead. The other tabs refer to the moves by these numbers. Click a card to see its animation step by step on the Graphics tab.

02 · The keyboard

Two joysticks, or two halves of the keyboard

The white fighter reads joystick port 2 and the red fighter port 1 ($2851). F7 in the attract mode swaps both to the keyboard, with a block of nine keys for each player.

Each block is the stick's eight directions around its fire key, S or :, and the first direction held wins. The function keys work only between games ($1E64): F1 or fire starts one, F3 switches between one and two players, F7 between joystick and keyboard, DEL switches the shouts off, and F5 abandons a game at any time.
