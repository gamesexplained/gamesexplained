1982 · Commodore

Clowns

Everything that moves in Clowns is three sprites and a font. The flying clown is sprite 0, given one of eleven pictures each tick; the see-saw is two sprites, with a second clown standing on it as part of its picture; the balloons are characters, each drawn eight times. Here the title screen plays itself, run by the game's own code.

01 · The title screen

While it waits for F1, the game catches its own clowns

Between games the see-saw steers itself, 2 pixels a tick towards the spot that puts its raised end under the clown. It is quick enough most of the time. Now and then a clown comes down too far away and falls; press Make it miss to see that sooner.

Run by this page from the cartridge's own code, on the site's 6502 over the bytes on the Source tab: from the reset vector into the title screen's loop ($ED4E), at the game's rate of about 95 ticks a second. Each tick that loop moves the see-saw ($F1A7), moves the clown and draws it ($E63C), scrolls the balloons ($F1E7) and blinks GAME OVER, then waits for the next tick at $F711, where the page stops it until the tick is due. The screen and the sprites are drawn from what the code has written to the screen, the colour memory and the video chip. Make it miss puts the see-saw at the far side of the screen each tick until the clown is down.

The clown has eleven pictures, each 16 pixels square, kept one after another in the cartridge ($FCBF). Every tick, the routine that draws the clown ($F034) copies the one it needs into sprite 0's 63 bytes of RAM at $03C0. The same copy, shifted to line up with the character grid, is what the game tests for collisions against the font (How it works, "Collisions").

The see-saw is sprites 1 and 2, side by side, and it has only two pictures: left end down, from sprite shapes $EF and $F0, and right end down, from $F1 and $F2. The video chip reads both straight from the cartridge ($FBC0). The clown standing on the lower end is part of each picture. When the flying clown lands on the raised end, the see-saw changes to the other picture, which shows a clown standing where it landed, and sprite 0 moves to the end that has just gone up: the clown that was standing there is now the one that flies.

The balloons are not sprites. Each row is 36 cells of the screen, redrawn every third tick from eight copies of the balloon in the font, each a pixel further right than the last (How it works, "The balloons"). The top and bottom rows travel right and the middle row left, one pixel every three ticks.
