Commodore 64 · 1984 · Epyx

Impossible Mission

Protection that keeps checking during play, a phone that dials real touch tones, an ending timed separately for Europe, the claims the code contradicts, and the questions still open.

01 · Copy protection

Secret: two protection checks keep running during play

Most protection runs once, while the game loads. This game carries two more checks into the game itself, and each would stop a copy that slipped past the loader.

CheckWhat it doesEvidence
The diskThe program on the disk is broken in three bytes. The loader asks the drive about the disk, and only a good answer writes the correct bytes in. A copy that fails crashes once loading finishes.$C300; the loader is described on the About tab
A chip register as an instructionThe start-up writes $60 into CIA 2's data direction register B at $DD03. $60 is the opcode of rts, and the game's delay routine calls $DD03 as a subroutine on every pass.$7F6D; tested: 47,272 calls in 120 frames, and a crash when the register holds anything else
A colour the loader leftThe loader's last stage sets a background colour register, $D024, to $0A. Every frame the interrupt checks it, and if it has changed it jumps into the middle of an instruction where the byte $02 halts the processor.$84FA; tested: the machine froze at $799A within 30 frames
wait_ticks   clc
$7F6E        adc $E0        ; the frame counter
$7F70        jsr $DD03      ; CIA 2's register, holding $60: rts
$7F73        cmp $E0
$7F75        bne $7F70

The first instructions that set up $DD03 no longer exist by the time the game is running. The start-up clears its own first 17 bytes in the same loop that clears the sprite registers, so a memory dump taken during play shows zeros where the setting-up was.

02 · The phone

Secret: the phone dials real touch tones

The pocket computer's phone key calls for help. The game dials one of two numbers, plays real touch-tone pairs, rings the line and answers.

Each key plays the two frequencies from the table at $7E6E. Sound plays in your browser through Web Audio.

The table holds two SID frequency values per key, one for voice 2 and one for voice 3. Converted at the American NTSC clock they come out as the touch-tone standard (DTMF): rows at 697, 770, 852 and 941 Hz, columns at 1209, 1336 and 1477 Hz. A PAL machine runs its SID 3.7% slower, so a European C64 dials slightly flat. The numbers are in $7E36; 555 is the prefix American films and television use for fictional numbers.

One number turns the two pieces in the memory window the right way up, and the other says whether the leftmost piece's puzzle can be finished (How it works).

03 · The ending

Secret: the ending is the only thing timed for PAL

Win, and the screen switches to a full-screen picture of Professor Elvin Atombender, who talks while the lights of his control room twinkle.

Drawn from the bytes the game keeps under the I/O area: the glyphs at $D000, the screen at $DC00 and the colours at $F800, in multicolour with the three colours the game sets at $AADF. The mouth is 13 cells; the game swaps them between the glyphs at $AB93 and $ABA0. The emulator's own picture of the same moment is here.

The picture lives where the program cannot easily read it. At start-up the game switches every ROM and chip out of the way and moves three blocks into the RAM beneath them ($71C0): 2 KB of glyphs to $D000, the screen to $DC00 and its colours to $F800. The ending points the video chip at the last 16 KB of memory, where it can see that RAM, and copies the screen and colours into place ($AA40).

The mouth moves in seven steps whose lengths come from a table, and there are two tables. On a PAL machine, the start-up patches three instructions so that the ending reads $AB6E instead of $AB67 ($3FB9). The PAL durations are five sixths of the NTSC ones, because the PAL picture runs at 50 frames a second, not 60, while the speech plays from a timer that counts processor cycles, and the two machines run their processors at almost the same speed.

Nothing else is adjusted. The game clock still counts 60 frames to the second, so on a PAL machine the six hours last 7 hours 12 minutes (How it works).

04 · Checked against the descriptions

Where the code and the descriptions disagree

Descriptions of the game, read in web search summaries on 1 October 2026, make claims that the code settles.

The claimWhat the code does
The pieces of a puzzle overlap, so three can fit before you find you must start again.The four pieces divide the picture exactly, and only their common frame may overlap ($B180, $8037, How it works).
There are six speech lines.The data holds eight, and the game plays seven of them ($BED9, Sound and speech).
The stronghold has 31 rooms, or 32.32 room records, two of them the code rooms ($0E70, The stronghold).
A cheat site: POKE 26831,169 for a cheat mode, and four pokes for no opponents.In this copy 26831 and 27028 are inside the agent's sprites, 31005 is in the routine that flips a piece, and 21006 is in the room character set. The pokes must be for another version.

05 · Open questions

What these pages have not settled

  • The words of the eight voice lines: the samples were not listened to.
  • The format of the headers in the speech data is not fully understood.
  • The code rooms' puzzle was read in the code but not played.
