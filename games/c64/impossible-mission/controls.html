Commodore 64 · 1984 · Epyx

Impossible Mission

One joystick in port 2 plays the whole game: the agent, the lift, the pocket computer and the terminals. The keyboard is read only when the hall of fame asks for a name.

01 · The stick

Left and right walk, fire somersaults, and up searches only while he stands still

Press the stick and fire below, or click the picture and use the arrow keys and the space bar. The agent answers the way the game's own routines would, a tick at a time on a PAL machine.

The game reads the stick in port 2 straight from the chip, at $DC00: five bits, each 0 while its direction or the button is held. Three routines share the work. The room's loop ($7249) runs every tick; it counts each new press of fire ($51, with $DE set while the button stays down, so holding it counts once) and sends up to the search when the agent is standing still. Every third tick the agent's update reads left and right ($87ED), moves him ($8859) and picks his next frame ($8817).

Left and right set the facing and a step of five pixels. With neither held the agent drops straight to his standing frame and stops: there is no slowing down. A counted fire press turns any walking or standing frame into the first frame of the somersault, and the somersault then runs its twelve frames with the stick ignored. Its steps come from tables ($8D07-$8D42), not from the walking speed, so from the moment he leaves the ground a standing somersault carries him as far as a running one. On the last frame the stick is read again: he lands running if left or right is held, and standing if not.

Up wins over everything else while the agent stands. When up is held and he is on his standing frame, the room's loop calls the search ($92A0) instead of the agent's update, so pushing the stick up and to one side leaves him standing where he is. Down does nothing in a room unless he stands on a lifting platform.

03 · The keyboard

Five characters for the hall of fame, and three keys that delete

Type a name, by clicking the keys or with your own keyboard while the picture of the keyboard has the focus. Each key shows the character the game's table gives it; the faded keys give nothing.

The game scans the keyboard itself ($BE2D) rather than through the machine's ROM, which it has switched out. It drives the eight rows of the keyboard one at a time through $DC00, from the row of RUN/STOP and SPACE down to the row of DEL and RETURN, and reads the columns at $DC01. The first key it finds is the one it keeps, and a 64-byte table ($BE78) turns it into a character. A key counts once, until every key has been let go ($DF).

The table gives every letter and digit, and a few signs: the full stop, the asterisk, and the equals sign, which the minus key types as well. SPACE gives character $FD and the colon $98, neither of them the blank or the colon of the game's own texts. Three keys delete: DEL, the left arrow at the top left, and cursor right. RETURN ends the name early; a fifth character ends it at once. The shift keys, the function keys and the rest give nothing.

The name is taken from the screen, five characters from where it was typed ($BD61), and kept with the score at $CF00 until the machine is switched off. While the name is typed, RUN/STOP is ignored ($BD24). After it, the game waits for RUN/STOP alone and then starts a new game ($B89D).

04 · RESTORE

RESTORE starts a new game from anywhere, except while a voice line plays

The RESTORE key is wired to the processor's non-maskable interrupt, not to the keyboard's rows. The game points that interrupt at $388C, the part of its start-up that sets up a new game ($BECD), so pressing RESTORE at any time abandons the mission and starts again in the lift. The hall of fame's prompt, HIT RESTORE OR RUN/STOP FOR NEW GAME, names both ways.

While a voice line plays, the speech driver uses the same interrupt for its samples, nearly ten thousand a second, and points the vector at its own handlers in page $08. RESTORE then lands in the speech driver and starts nothing. The start-up, a death and the end of a line point the vector back at the new game ($BEC2). The speech is on Sound.
