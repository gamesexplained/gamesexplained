# Load 26 — verified technical facts

The resident draw loop at `$6D41–$6D63` ran 200 iterations in the first 200 frames after this forced load returned. Its four indexed reads use `$81CF`, `$82CF`, `$83CF` and `$84CF` to place a metatile's four character codes in a two-by-two block. The resident movement checks use the indexed attribute table at `$80CF`; the exact meanings of all its bit combinations remain open. This observation follows a forced transition and does not establish a route by ordinary play.

The entry snapshot has 15 row pointers in split tables at `$85CF` and `$8620`. They point to rows beginning at `$8671` and spaced 128 bytes apart; the address immediately after that storage is `$8DF1`, the character-data pointer recorded at `$7F41`. The downward scroll check uses `$7F4C` as its vertical boundary. This does not establish that every stored cell is encountered during play.

The captured sound pointers select voice lists at $A422, $A428, $A42E; their records name 6 distinct event patterns. In the captured selector-1 loop, the resident sequencer selected each pattern and reached its documented zero marker in the listing-based simulator. `test_stage_sound_patterns.js` checks the pointers, pattern boundaries and those visits. This observation does not establish when ordinary play selects the sound.

The captured descriptor names 12 six-byte trigger records at `$8FE1` and 2 sixteen-byte stage object records at `$9029`. `$7979` copies the trigger fields; `$4655` returns before reading the object pointer if its count is zero, otherwise it copies the fields and calls the map updater. `test_stage_records.js` checks the copies and descriptions, stubbing the map updater for the sixteen-byte records. These captured records do not establish that ordinary play activates them.

The loaded pointer at `$7F3F/$7F40` targets `$8FA1`, inside the eight-page character-copy source at `$8DF1`. The entry was observed in the 200-frame forced-play probe and monitor collection. The code and graphics uses overlap; `codemap.json` records only observed instruction starts in that source whose bytes still matched the load-entry snapshot.

The update entry at `$8FA1` sets `$4DCB,X` and updates the horizontal-motion gate `$E1`. If both `$4DB6` and `$4DB3` are zero, it takes one value from the six-byte pattern at `$8FD0` and advances the mutable index at `$8FCF`; otherwise it writes `$FF` and leaves the index unchanged. The two resident directional speed-update paths return early on a zero gate. Afterward this load advances through `$0732` only when `$6F3C` is zero. Controlled tests cover these decisions; the pattern’s timing and reachability in ordinary play remain open.

The seven entity-state handlers and their shared update are relocated copies of load 16. `test_entity_code_copies.py` compares all 212 dispatcher and handler instruction encodings, accounting for internal address relocation, and checks the seven split-table targets and eight animation offsets. This does not establish their natural reachability in this load.

The supplied side B file with index $1A supplies two compressed streams. Executing the game’s decoder on those bytes in the 6502 simulator consumed 2098 and 5487 bytes and wrote $EB40–$FE3F and $7EDF–$ACB1, respectively. All written output bytes match the forced VICE load-entry snapshot. This establishes these stream writes for the supplied image, not natural reachability or ownership of repeated data across loads.

Controlled record-copy, frame-restore and display-routine simulations selected 10 additional bitmap pointers in this load. Slot assignment, position, effect state and one colour were forced. The bitmaps match the load-entry and play captures; no instruction entered their ranges during a 200-frame probe. These examples do not establish natural reachability.

Listing tests and nine controlled VICE calls check these branches across four loads with callbacks stubbed and decimal mode clear; natural reachability remains open.
