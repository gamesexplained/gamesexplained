# Load 21 — verified technical facts

The resident draw loop at `$6D41–$6D63` ran 200 iterations in the first 200 frames after this forced load returned. Its four indexed reads use `$81CF`, `$82CF`, `$83CF` and `$84CF` to place a metatile's four character codes in a two-by-two block. The resident movement checks use the indexed attribute table at `$80CF`; the exact meanings of all its bit combinations remain open. This observation follows a forced transition and does not establish a route by ordinary play.

The entry snapshot has 13 row pointers in split tables at `$85CF` and `$8620`. They point to rows beginning at `$8671` and spaced 99 bytes apart; the address immediately after that storage is `$8B78`, the character-data pointer recorded at `$7F41`. The downward scroll check uses `$7F4C` as its vertical boundary. This does not establish that every stored cell is encountered during play.

The captured sound pointers select voice lists at $919B, $91A1, $91A7; their records name 6 distinct event patterns. In the captured selector-1 loop, the resident sequencer selected each pattern and reached its documented zero marker in the listing-based simulator. `test_stage_sound_patterns.js` checks the pointers, pattern boundaries and those visits. This observation does not establish when ordinary play selects the sound.

The captured descriptor names 7 six-byte trigger records at `$916D` and 0 sixteen-byte stage object records at `$9197`. `$7979` copies the trigger fields; `$4655` returns before reading the object pointer if its count is zero, otherwise it copies the fields and calls the map updater. `test_stage_records.js` checks the copies and descriptions, stubbing the map updater for the sixteen-byte records. These captured records do not establish that ordinary play activates them.

In the forced play capture, 2 stable 63-byte sprite bitmaps from this load were named by active display-slot entries in VIC bank 3. Their entry and play bytes match; non-stopping checkpoints saw no instruction entry into those bitmap ranges during the following 200 frames. The observation covers those captured states and does not establish all animation or ordinary-play behavior.

The loaded pointer at `$7F3F/$7F40` targets `$9158`, inside the eight-page character-copy source at `$8B78`. The entry was observed in the 200-frame forced-play probe and monitor collection. The code and graphics uses overlap; `codemap.json` records only observed instruction starts in that source whose bytes still matched the load-entry snapshot.

The supplied side B file with index $15 supplies two compressed streams. Executing the game’s decoder on those bytes in the 6502 simulator consumed 638 and 2762 bytes and wrote $EB40–$F4BF and $7EDF–$973D, respectively. All written output bytes match the forced VICE load-entry snapshot. The extracted file has 156 further bytes after the two streams; their role is not established. This establishes these stream writes for the supplied image, not natural reachability or ownership of repeated data across loads.

Controlled record-copy, frame-restore and display-routine simulations selected 2 additional bitmap pointers in this load. Slot assignment, position, effect state and one colour were forced. The bitmaps match the load-entry and play captures; no instruction entered their ranges during a 200-frame probe. These examples do not establish natural reachability.

The load’s type-$09 entity vector selects a dispatcher at $952E. Its four captured state-table pairs point to $9548, $9581, $9679, $969D; the state byte is not locally bounded. The listing test checks all four dispatches in each of the six loads. Controlled VICE calls checked all four targets in loads 5 and 24 and one each in loads 7 and 22. The state index and ordinary-play reachability remain open.

The six-load listing test checks representative sign and wrapping cases with both callees stubbed; five VICE calls checked loads 5, 13, 22 and 24 using the same stubs. Natural contexts remain open.

A six-load listing test checks the ordinary, countdown and wrap cases; five VICE calls checked loads 5, 22 and 24. Natural reachability remains open.
