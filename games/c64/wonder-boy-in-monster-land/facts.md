# Super Wonder Boy in Monster Land — verified technical facts

The contributor supplied the Nostalgia disk release. Its trainer was left at `NO` for the captures. The route and snapshot names are in [orientation.md](orientation.md).

## Input and rewards

Joystick port 2 is sampled at `$3C4C`. The code retains the preceding left and right samples and uses a reversal at an entity's creation to set up the 65–69 gold reward. The arithmetic and controlled tests are in [the resident facts](parts/resident/facts.md).

The check at `$5C0C` runs only on the pass that creates a bag or coin, and it skips the reversal while bit 7 of `$4DB3` is set, which is while the hero is rising in a jump. In play from a snapshot of round 1 reached without forcing, the first bag (load 1, column 11, row 13) paid 14 gold when walked onto and 68 gold with a left-to-right change of the stick as it appeared. A TASVideos forum thread (topic 25336, April 2024) traces the same `$FF` amount and 65-gold payout in the arcade game.

Each load's trigger records (six bytes each, from the pointer at `$7F52` and the count at `$7F54`; matched against the hero's tile at `$48BF`) name the hidden items through four callbacks: `$5BE1` makes a money bag (entity type `$FE`), `$5C2A` a coin (`$FD`), `$5CCB` a heart (`$FC`) and `$5CFE` an hourglass (`$FB`). On the maps the 31 loads hold 24 bags, 24 coins, 3 hearts and 4 hourglasses, on 19 maps; eleven of the money spots start with a count of two or three. Two records lie beyond the right edge of their maps and can never match: a coin in load 16 at column 113 of a 21-column map (the record `$71 $07 $01 $03 $2A $5C` at `$8D01`, the same six bytes as load 8's first record) and a key check in load 2 at column 253 of a 115-column map. The other callbacks are messages, the key check at `$7960` (it clears the key flag, bit 6 of `$4DE2`, and `$5284`), the hazard at `$72AB` (it adds one back to its own count, so it never runs out, and starts a recoil and a temporary entity of type `$2B` when `$527D` is at least `$0B`) and the load-9 spot below.

The wing-boots flag and counter are also in the resident engine. The counter starts at `$FF` and is decremented only on a qualifying visit while up is pressed. The same resident facts give the conditions and the VICE tests.

## The hourglass

The status panel’s hourglass is the routine at `$4A43`, called once in each pass of the main loop (`$0437`) and of the alternate loop (`$07B2`). It decrements the count at `$4B0D`; when the count reaches zero the forward step at `$4A50` reloads it with 150 and moves the picture number at `$4DD4` on by one. On reaching picture 6 the count is set to 3. The step after picture 6 calls `$77BC` with A=8, which takes 8 from the selected player’s meter, and goes back to picture 0. The meter starts at 40 (`$6BF3`), and the panel code at `$3800` divides the limit by 8 for its hearts, so 8 is one heart. `$4A90` draws the picture: twenty screen codes from one of the seven blocks at `$5127`–`$51B2`, in five rows of four at columns 36 to 39 of the top five screen rows.

The tile check at `$613B`, in the tile-interaction routine the main loop calls at `$0423`, compares the first sampled map value at `$61D6`, the cell at the view tile’s column and row, with the load’s byte at `$7F76` and, on a match, decrements `$4B0D` once more while it is at least 2. Loads 2, 8, 9, 11, 15, 17, 22, 25 and 28 hold a tile number there (`$42`, `$20`, `$20`, `$20`, `$42`, `$42`, `$20`, `$35` and `$83`); the other 22 loads hold `$FF`. Drawn from each load’s map data, that tile is the top row of the load’s pools.

The contact handler at `$7646` belongs to entity type `$FB`, the hidden hourglass that the trigger callback `$5CFE` makes in loads 1, 2, 4 and 6. It adds to the score and writes 1 to the flag at `$4DD3` and to the count. While the flag is set the backward step at `$4A74` moves the picture number down by one every 8 calls, and clears the flag at picture 0. The area setup at `$6C73` adds 8 to `$4DCD`, sets the count to 1 and the picture to 6, so the first tick takes the 8 back and draws picture 0. `$4DCD` is the first player's meter, not indexed by the selected player, while the tick takes from the selected player's; in the one-player game the two are the same. At the start of round 1, in play, the main loop ran 167 times in 500 frames, so one heart lasts about 54 seconds there; the hourglass steps themselves are readings of the listing.

## Maps and display

The renderer at `$6D41–$6D63` expands a map byte into four character codes from the tables at `$81CF`, `$82CF`, `$83CF` and `$84CF`. A character-to-colour table begins at `$7FCF`. Each captured load supplies its own tables and map. The pointer and dimensions were checked against the entry snapshot of each part; the part's `facts.md` gives the observed row count and stride.

The [Maps](levels.html) viewer draws the 31 captured maps from their tile and character data. The numbers identify forced load captures, and do not establish the order or reachability of natural gameplay. The viewer omits sprites and later runtime changes.

The visual-resource selector at $7119 builds screens from per-load pointers to compressed metatiles, colours and character shapes. ## Goods, damage and the healing counter

The shop price table in load 2 is at `$B184`: 24 entries, of which entries 5, 12 and 18 repeat the one before them (Winged Boots, A Helmet, A Gauntlet), so it names 21 goods. `test_shop_widget.js` checks every name and price in 17 loads' copies and 2,040 payment cases against the code, and the price table on How it works against those names and prices.

Buying the Helmet runs `$76F3`: the charge count at `$4DDE` is set to 5 and bit 7 of the player's flags. The damage routine `$5D7A` returns at once while the cooldown `$4DE1` is nonzero. Otherwise, with the Helmet's bit set, it decrements the count and halves the damage (`LSR` of the patched operand at `$5DAC`); at zero it clears the bit and shows message 10, “Helmet is broken.” (`$550F`), and the fifth hit is still halved. It then subtracts the damage through `$77BC` and sets `$4DE1` to 40. The main loop decrements `$4DE1` once a pass (`$03F4`), and `$1469` hides the hero's sprites on odd values, so he flashes while it runs. The terrain damage paths at `$617A` and `$61AD` call the same routine and then set `$4DE1` to 80 or 60. `test_gauntlet_widget.js` runs 40 Helmet hits and 55 Gauntlet blows through the game's code against the How it works bars.

Loads 4, 9, 19, 20 and 30 each hold a copy of a healing counter: in load 4 the setup is at `$A48F`, the price check at `$A4DA`, the payment at `$A58F` and the decline at `$A573`. Each copy carries a price list of seven BCD tens, 2, 4, 7, 10, 11, 12 and 13 (20 to 130 gold; `$A679` in load 4), and a per-player visit count at `$528C`, which the payment increments (`$A5B1`). Each of the three reads of the list loads the count into Y and then reads `LDA $A679,X` or `CMP $A679,X` with X the selected player, so the first player always pays 20 gold and the second 40, and nothing else reads `$528C`. `test_counter_price.js` runs the payment path of all five copies for both players and seven visit counts. The payment calls `$6097`, the routine of the end-of-area tally, which turns the meter into score and fills it back to its limit, and sets the hourglass to its first picture. The messages are “Next, Please.” or “Wow, how nice guy!” (chosen by bit 1 of the stage-record field `$4753,X`), “You are OK.” with the meter full, “Sorry, but you short of money.”, “Take care.” after paying, and on declining “Please come again tomorrow.” or “Shit!”.

The continue count `$0698` is set to 3 at `$032F`, after the title's Fire. On the continue screen (`$05E0`), choosing to continue with a nonzero count spends one (`$0628`), fills the meter to its limit and clears the three score bytes; with a zero count `$063F` shows “Add credits and select again.” from `$0678` and returns to the choice. The other choice returns to the title (`$0620`). These are readings of the listing; `test_continue_menu.js` covers the choice.

## Secrets and bugs

The load-9 trigger record at `$92D9` puts the callback `$92F7` at column 43, row 6, with 255 firings. While its countdown at `$932D` is nonzero it writes it to the border colour and decrements it; at zero, unless `$7227` already holds `$18`, it writes `CLC` and `RTS` to `$7227`, the start of the routine that tests the hero against an entity, sets the selected player's meter and limit to `$50` (ten hearts), sets flag `$10` (the revival medicine, which `$04B2` uses) and shows “Something wonderful has happened...”. Forced VICE calls and `test_stage09_trigger.js` check the paths. In play the spot was not reached: the hero's highest jump near it ends at row 7.

The “DANGER! KEEP OUT” callback at `$950A` in load 23 saves X to `$9514`, the opcode of its own `LDX #$FF`, where the nine other message callbacks of the same shape save it to the operand. In play from the start of load 23, walking one tile left changed `$9514` from `$A2` to `$01` (`ORA ($FF,X)`).

The two-player setting `$4DBC` is written only at `$0378`, with 0. Set by hand in round 1, it made the right-hand score show the second player's (00) under the label HIGH, removed the PLAYER 1 label, and after the first player's end-of-area tally the game started the second player's turn (`$0716`); `$129B` picks the hero's colours by player.

## Entity records

Each later load supplies an entity-record pointer at `$7F4F` and an initial slot limit at `$7F51`. `$369E` copies ten bytes from a record into the resident entity arrays. A zero first byte ends the record sequence; the slot-reuse path can select records beyond the initial limit. Controlled VICE tests checked all ten copied fields for load 2's first record and load 19's twelfth record. `test_entity_records.js` checks the copies for the later loads' 179 described records against the resident instructions. A field's later use depends on the entity's handler.

## Text

The `$C800` character set contains digits at codes `$01–$0A`, upper-case letters at `$41–$5A` and lower-case letters at `$61–$7A`. The message-copy routine converts stored spaces to the blank glyph. The punctuation and message-record format are documented in [the resident facts](parts/resident/facts.md); the first load's opening messages are in [its facts](parts/stage-01/facts.md).

Each of the 30 later-load entry snapshots supplies an update target through `$7F3F/$7F40`, and every target begins inside that load’s eight-page character-copy source. The resident indirect jump at `$0450` dispatches through it. During a 200-frame forced-play probe plus monitor collection, the entry was observed in loads 2–28 and not observed in loads 29–31. The code maps record observed instruction starts within the character source. All bytes matched the load entry except two documented patched operands in load 16. The entries in loads 25–27 also cycle a six-byte horizontal-input gate when two motion-state bytes are zero; load 26 additionally tests the map column before choosing its jump. The gate’s timing and natural reachability remain open. In load 16, the two instruction starts whose operands changed are a patched jump and a patched comparison, documented in that part’s facts. `test_stage_updates.js` exercises the 27 simpler entries at 140,616 controlled input combinations and checks the three gate entries; representative branches in loads 2, 6, 12, 16 and 24 also matched VICE. These tests do not establish natural routes to their conditions.

## Listing error rate

The independent sample with seed 261110 drew 60 comments from a population of 29,177 across all parts. The checker used gpt-6-astra and marked 59 right and one wrong-detail. The finding held: the comment at resident $70C0 described the redundant branch at $70C2, while $70C0 follows DEC $7063. The sampled error was corrected, and the adjacent branch now has its own description. The measured error rate was 1/60 (1.7%), with a 95% Wilson interval of 0.3%–8.9%. A full audit is recommended. This sample does not constitute the maintainer check required for this imported analysis. The verbatim draw and verdicts remain in ignored working reports.

The final sample (seed 261111) drew 80 of 32,315 comments: 60 of 21,215 written comments and 20 of 11,100 generated comments. An independent checker found one wrong detail: $7165 loads a table value, while $7168 stores it to $6F. The same wording was corrected in 29 loads. The plain observed error rate is 1.25% (Wilson 95% interval 0.2%–6.7%); weighting by stratum size gives 1.7%. This measures a sample, not a full audit. A maintainer must perform the imported-analysis check before the tier can be Silver.

The maintainer's sample (seed 51117, by air, checker claude-fable-5-2) drew 80 comments: 60 of 22,776 written and 20 of 9,543 generated. It found 17 wrong, and each finding held: 21.2% plain (Wilson 95% interval 13.7%–31.4%), 23% weighted by stratum. Fifteen described bytes as unexplained characters of an eight-page character copy when they are a load's compressed screen, tile definitions, sound pattern, record or message. Running the resource loader at `$7130` in the kit's 6502 for every load and selector gave the ranges it reads; with the structure comments they identified 5,060 such comments, all rewritten. One "Referenced at" list stopped at four entries, and 80 such lists were completed; one window check was off by one, corrected in all five copies of its routine. The sample is over the mark, so Silver needs a full audit of every comment and of this file on proven models, and a new sample after it (`kit/CHECKING.md`).

The three samples above were drawn from the listing as it stood before 18 November 2026. That day its 32,319 notes were folded to 14,310, one for each structure: each run of a copy's characters is one note that names the characters, where the copy puts them and which screen or map tiles draw them; a sprite's 63 bytes and its 64th byte are one note; bytes that a copy sweeps up are described once, by the note on the record, screen or routine they belong to. Sentences that said only that a use stayed open, or how a capture had checked the bytes, were removed. Every counted byte still has a description, and the game's tests check the folded notes against the loader and the sprite routines.

## Coverage and remaining uncertainty

The exported ledger describes all 490,491 counted bytes across the resident engine and 31 load parts. Eight loaded bytes retain an explicitly unknown semantic role; 200-frame VICE probes recorded no CPU access to them while a control interrupt checkpoint recorded 200 hits. Full coverage means each byte has a description, including bounded unknowns. It does not establish every possible path through the game. No loaded-data range is left outside the ledger.

The imported analysis requires the maintainer check in `kit/CHECKING.md` before the tier can be recorded as Silver. The maintainer's sample is over the mark, so that check is now a full audit followed by a new sample.
