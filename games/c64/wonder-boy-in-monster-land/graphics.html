One character, two sprites

The game draws a character as a pair of hardware sprites laid one over the other: a one-colour outline and a multicolour fill. This character from load 2 alternates between two such pairs. The player below shows the two layers apart.

Monochrome layer

Multicolour layer

The two primary display slots take the frame and the adjacent frame at $1CAB–$1CEC. This character changes pair every six frames. Its outline is black; its fill uses white with the two shared colours, red and green. Other characters have frames and timing of their own.

Each slot is 64 bytes apart in the VIC bank. The first 63 bytes make a 24-by-21 bitmap; the game chooses the bank through CIA2. The slot bytes for this example are at $F1C0, $F200, $F240 and $F280.

Status-panel lettering

These 24-by-21 shapes stay in memory throughout the game. The game shows them as one-colour sprites over the status panel; the digits beside them change separately.

The hero in two sprites

The hero is two sprites as well, an outline and a fill. The game stores one pair of shapes and mirrors it to draw the hero facing the other way, shown here.

Combined

The VIC selects the mirrored multicolour bytes through sprite pointer $75 at $DD40–$DD7E. The fill is yellow with the shared red and green. The two stored shapes begin at $ACC7 and $AD07 in the first load. This is one frame of the hero’s animation.
