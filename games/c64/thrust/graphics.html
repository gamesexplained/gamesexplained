1986 · Firebird

Thrust

Every shape the game draws with: the ship in 32 directions, the objects in each level's colours, and the five-line font the status bar and messages are written in.

01 · The ship

The ship in 32 directions

The ship has one sprite for each direction, drawn as a single-colour outline. Turning swaps the sprite.

Pointers 0-$1F, at $4000-$47FF, drawn from the listing's bytes in yellow, the ship's colour. The ship turns one direction on three passes of the main loop in four, and the loop ran about 26 passes a second in the emulator, so the animation turns about 20 directions a second. With the shield up, the sprite is the circle at pointer $20, in the level's shield colour, shown on two frames in four.

02 · The objects

Each level colours its objects

The guns, fuel tanks, reactor and pod stand are one or two sprites each. Their colours come from the level's tables.

Pointers $21-$30, at $4840-$4C3F, in the colours object_sprites ($889B) gives them: the guns, the pod's stand and the reactor's dome from $A945, the pod from $A939, the word FUEL from $A94B, the rest yellow. Two sprites drawn at one place make one object.

A fuel tank is drawn one of two ways, chosen by whether its place in the level's list is odd or even: as a tank with a separate FUEL sign, two sprites, or as one sprite with the word on it. The tractor beam is two sprites the game shows under the ship on alternate passes while it drains a tank. The rod is drawn afresh each pass into a pair of sprite buffers at $5300 and $5380, as the How it works page explains.

03 · The font

A five-line font for every word on screen

The game writes its words with its own font, five lines high, into the bitmap and into the status bar's characters.

status_font ($8E52): 37 characters (a space, A to Z, 0 to 9), one table of 37 bytes for each of the five lines. print_char ($8F4E) draws upper and lower case with the same capitals.

The status bar is a picture held in 80 characters, one for each of its cells (status_bar_chars, $BB96). The frame, the lines and the words FUEL, LIVES and SCORE are drawn in it, and the font writes the numbers into it. The last digit of the score is part of the picture: it is always 0.

The 80 characters at $BB96, two rows of 40, drawn as plain pixels in white. In the game, the first row is multicolour and takes its colours from the status bar's settings.

04 · The loading picture

Firebird's picture, before the crackers signed it

Firebird's 1986 cassette first shows a black screen that says LOADING and counts the blocks of the tape as they come in, in hexadecimal. Then, 113 seconds in, it puts up its picture whole: THRUST in pink letters, the ship towing the pod on its rod past a planet, and Bob signed small in the corner. It stays up, in silence, for nearly two and a half minutes while the game loads.

The cassette's own bytes, recorded in VICE with the picture up and drawn as the video chip draws them, at the tape's pace, with the border taking each new colour at the moment it did on the recorded load. Skip goes to the end of the load.

The border keeps the count. Each time another block of the tape comes in, the loader moves the border on to the next of the C64's sixteen colours. While the picture is up, the border changes 91 times, more than five times round all sixteen colours, before the game starts.

Firebird's disk shows the same picture, byte for byte, while it loads. The copy these pages describe is a crack, and it shows the picture with TCI added under the title and the signature redrawn in a blue box (How it works).
