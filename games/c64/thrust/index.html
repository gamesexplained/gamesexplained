1986 · Firebird

Thrust

A ship that falls, a pod on a rigid rod, and caves stored as nothing more than two lists of slopes. This copy also carries the BBC Micro original's key page, and the hooks for a music player it does not have.

01 · The game

Steal the pod and fly it out

You fly a small ship down into a planet's caves to steal a pod, the Klystron pod, and tow it back out into space.

The ship turns and thrusts against gravity, and it keeps moving until something slows it. Guns on the cave walls fire at it, a reactor powers them, and fuel tanks stand on ledges. The pod waits on a stand. The tractor beam picks it up, and from then on the ship and the pod swing together at the two ends of a rod. Leaving the planet with the pod completes the mission. Shoot the reactor enough and it goes critical: the planet blows up a few seconds later, and a ship still inside goes with it.

02 · One frame, rebuilt from memory

Two screens in one frame

The status bar at the top is a text screen, and the cave below it is a multicolour bitmap. The video chip switches between them twice in every frame.

A frame of level 1, drawn by this page from the memory and the register writes recorded in the emulator (VICE). Of its 104,448 pixels, 104,446 match the emulator's own picture of the same frame. The two that differ are on line 191, where a sprite is moved in the middle of the line.

The frame begins with the status bar. raster_handler_0 ($89C4) runs at the top of each frame, sets the video chip to text mode, and points it at the screen at $5C00 and the character set at $5800. The status bar is two rows of 40 characters, and each of its 80 cells holds a character of its own, codes 0 to 79. The character set is therefore a tiny bitmap. The game writes the fuel, lives and score into it with its own five-line font ($8E52).

At lines $41-$43 raster_handler_3 ($8A4D) turns on the bitmap at $6000, in multicolour, and the rest of the frame is the cave. Below that, raster_handler_1 ($8AA1) moves sprites 0-5 down the screen band by band. The guns, fuel tanks, reactor, pod stand and rod all go through those six sprites. The ship is sprite 7, and the towed pod is sprite 6.

03 · The ship

32 directions and a falling ship

The ship has 32 directions. Each one has a thrust vector, stored as a pair of signed numbers in a table.

The ship's sprite for each direction, from $4000 on, and its vector from direction_tables ($B8E7), drawn to scale. The sideways number is half the scale of the up-and-down one, because a column of the cave is twice as wide as a row is high.

A and S turn the ship one direction at a time, on three passes of the main loop in four (rotate_ship, $9E09). A pass is not a frame: the main loop waits for three ticks of a timer (wait_pass, $B44C). Measured in the emulator, it ran 131 times in 250 frames, about 26 times a second.

Gravity, thrust and friction all happen in ship_physics ($9588), and only on 6 passes in 16. On those passes the level's gravity is added to the falling speed. Shift adds a sixteenth of the direction's vector to the speeds, or a thirty-second while the pod is towed, and burns one unit of fuel. Then friction takes a 64th of the sideways speed and a 256th of the falling speed. Gravity rises from 5 on level 1 to 13 on level 6 (Levels).

04 · The pod on its rod

The pod pulls the ship round

Once the pod is attached, the game no longer moves the ship. It moves the point halfway along the rod, and turns the rod about it.

With Space held, the shield goes up and the tractor beam comes on. pod_pickup ($9926) waits for the ship to come within a short distance of the pod and then move away again. As the gap opens past a longer distance, the pod is attached. That moment matches what a player sees: the beam draws in, and then the rod goes taut.

From then on the physics moves the centre of the pair, $07-$0C. The ship is drawn at the centre plus the rod's vector, and the pod at the centre minus it (ship_position, $9E77; pod_position, $B377). Thrusting in a direction across the rod gives the rod a spin ($1E-$20), so the ship and pod swing round each other. Thrust is also halved while towing. The rod itself is a line drawn every pass into a pair of sprites (draw_rod, $AB26), alternately at $5300 and $5380, so the one on screen is never the one being drawn.

05 · The cave walls

A cave stored as runs of slopes

Each wall of a level is a list of runs: so many rows, each moving the wall a few steps left or right. The game builds the walls row by row as the camera moves.

RunRowsSlope a row

Level 1's walls, drawn by this page with a port of the game's generators, from the run lists at $A03C. Press a row of the table to pick out its run. The port matches the game's own code on every row of all six levels (test_levels.js).

Each wall has two generators (walls_step, $841C). Each generator keeps an x position, the rows left in its run and its place in the lists. Every row it adds the run's slope to x, and when the run is used up it reads the next. The results go into two rings of 256 rows at $0400 and $0500, one per wall. draw_walls ($828B) then fills the rock from the edge of the screen to each wall. It does that on every second pixel line, which gives the rock its stripes. It draws only the pixels that changed since the last pass, by exclusive-or (EOR).

The first runs of every level are long and flat: open sky, with the left wall at 0 and the right wall at 255. The cave starts a few hundred rows down. All six levels are drawn whole on the Levels tab.

06 · Guns, fuel and the reactor

Everything is an object with a type

Each level lists its objects in five lists: x, row (low and high), type and flags. Object 0 is always the pod on its stand and object 1 the reactor.

TypeWhatHit boxPoints

Sprites from $4880 on, in level 1's colours; hit boxes from hit_width and hit_height ($84F1, $84FA), in columns and rows; points from hit_points ($8508), as the status bar shows them.

update_objects ($8571) goes through the level's objects every pass. It tests the player's four shots against each object on screen. A gun hit once is destroyed. Each hit on the reactor silences the guns for a random time (guns_silent, $3D) and adds the same random amount, 0 to 31, to its damage ($3E). Damage starts at 50 on each level, and when the total passes 255 the countdown starts at 10 (countdown, $6A). It goes down one every 33 passes, beeping, and is shown twice on the status bar. At 0 the planet blows up.

A gun fires on a pass when a random byte comes out below gun_rate ($A3). The rate is 1 for the first two missions, then rises by one each mission up to 35, with 8 more for the mission after a planet was destroyed without the pod. Each gun's flags set which way it aims and how widely it scatters its shots.

A fuel tank can be shot for 150 points, or drained with the tractor beam: 11 units of fuel a pass for 26 passes, then the tank goes, for 300 points.

07 · Scoring and the end of a mission

Every score ends in a painted zero

The score is stored in binary-coded decimal (BCD) at $0180-$0182, two digits to a byte. The last "0" on the status bar is drawn into the status bar's picture and never changes.

The status bar's character set is copied at start-up from status_bar_chars ($BB96), and the cell after the score's six digits already holds a "0" (at $5A51 in the copy). So every stored value is shown ten times over: a gun adds 75 to the score and the player sees 750. An extra life comes each time the score's ten-thousands digit changes (add_score, $92A2).

Rising above row $120 leaves the planet (above_planet, $B3C7). With the pod, mission_complete ($B608) counts up a bonus of 400 for each of (level number + 5) steps. A planet destroyed on the way out adds 5 more steps. Without the pod the mission is "Incomplete" and the level is played again. If the planet was destroyed and the pod left behind, the mission has "Failed", with "No Bonus", and play moves on to the next level.

08 · This copy

What the crackers changed

This is TCI's cracked copy, packed into one file. A few things in it differ from what reviews of Firebird's release describe.

The start-up code ends with a jump to crack_start ($AF82), which starts a game at once, with the player in control. The high score table and the demo pilot appear only once that game is over. In the emulator, with no key pressed, the ship fell and was lost three times. After about 35 seconds the table came up.

This copy has no music. raster_handler_0 calls $3000 once a frame, where a music player would be called, and the start and end of every life call music_hook ($A951) with a tune number. Both reach an RTS. No instruction in memory writes to the sound chip (SID) at $D400. The game's own sound effects write it through its mirror at $D500 instead (sound_tick, $9B58). The odds and ends the crackers left are on the Discoveries tab.
