Choplifter

There is one world, it is 4,888 bitmap bits wide, and every coordinate in it is written into the code.

The whole world

Four sheds at one end, the base at the other

Choplifter has no level table. The four sheds stand at world X $037D, $047D, $057D and $067D, written as immediate operands in $B8AE; the base is at $1254 in $BA3F; the camera is clamped between $02B0 and $11D8 in $BD9C. Nothing reads any of those from a table, and the second and third sorties use the same numbers as the first.

Rows count upward from the bottom of the play area, which is how the bitmap row tables at $0A00 are indexed. Row 26 is where the background register turns grey, so anything below row 26 is standing on the ground.

The only thing that changes

Where the eight loose hostages stand

Every sortie starts the same way. Sheds 1 to 3 are shut with 16 people inside each, shed 4 is already open with 8 left inside, and 8 are standing outside. That is 64. $BE52 sets all of it. The one number it does not hard-code is the gap between the eight who are already outside: $20 plus a sample of CIA1 timer A masked with $7E, so an even gap somewhere between 32 and 158 units.

Eight figures, laid out from the same starting point with the gap the sample produced. The spread between the tightest and the loosest sortie is about 880 units, roughly two and three quarter screens.
