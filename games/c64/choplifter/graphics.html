Commodore 64 · 1982 · Brøderbund Software

Choplifter

Nothing here is a sprite. Each picture is one of the 75 shapes in the table at $8009, ORed into the bitmap by the game's own routines, and each one below moves the way the game moves it.

The game draws the next picture as soon as it has finished the last one, so it runs faster where there is less to draw: about 20 pictures a second with the helicopter flying over empty country, and 12 beside the base. Everything below steps at the first rate, apart from the base, which steps at the second. Anything else on the screen slows the game down further.

01 · The helicopter

Turn the helicopter and tilt it

Side on, the helicopter is one of eleven drawings, one for each step of tilt, and facing left is the same drawing flipped. Turned towards you it is one of three narrow drawings, and those are leaned as they are drawn: the blitter at $90D2 plots one bit at a time and drops a row every few columns. The main rotor is three shapes and the tail rotor four, and each moves on one shape every time the game draws the screen.

lean left lean right

The helicopter is placed by its middle, not by a pixel, so it can land on either bit of a pixel pair. Each pair of bits is one wide pixel, and the same shape takes different colours when it moves by one bit. The game does nothing about it, and in play the helicopter changes colour as it flies. Bump the skids shows the frame drawn on the one pass the skids touch the ground, shape 14 side on and 15 facing you: one row shorter, redrawn rather than cut. Lean is $5A and facing is $59.

02 · The hostages

The people walk, wave and climb in

A person is nine bits wide and eleven rows tall, in one of nine drawings. $9B7D moves every person one step and draws them on each pass: a walker takes shapes 55 to 58 and two bits of ground a step, flipped when walking left; someone standing waves through 59, 60, 61 and 60; someone at the door takes 62 twice and 63 twice and is gone.

03 · Explosions and the mine

The cloud and the mine

Everything that blows up becomes the same actor, $B106, which draws shapes 51, 52, 53 and 54 on four passes and frees itself. A cloud on the ground is lifted four rows so that it sits on the ground instead of half in it. The mine that homes on you from the third sortie is one drawing, shape 50, which $B73D draws flipped on every other pass, so it seems to spin.

04 · The tank

The tank's tracks are one drawing, flipped

A tank is three shapes: the hull, the turret on top, and one of five barrels. Nothing animates the tracks. $A0BB draws the hull as stored when bit 2 of the tank's X is set and flipped when it is clear, and a moving tank goes four units a pass, so the hull flips on every pass it moves. The barrel is picked by the bearing to the helicopter: drag the slider.

bearing to the helicopter

The gun has five positions. $A249 holds the bearing at which each one starts and $A244 how far along the hull its barrel is drawn. The rule that turns the gun and fires it is on How it works.

05 · The jet

Four canned manoeuvres, one record a pass

The jet cruises on shape 23. Each manoeuvre is a list of four-byte records, one a pass: which drawing, how far along, and two steps down the screen. The wide turn ends facing the other way, and from then on the jet is drawn flipped.

06 · The base and the sheds

A flag that flaps every pass

The base is built from five shapes on a blue block. The sloping end is one shape drawn twice, once flipped, and so is the bunker. The flag is two drawings, and bit 0 of the pass counter $89 picks between them, so it changes on every pass. A shed is one of two drawings on a bar of ground, and once it has been blown open the people in its doorway are one shape, drawn flipped on every other pass.

07 · The rest

Shots, a mine and the scenery

These never change their drawing. The shots only move, and are drawn flipped when they fly left, and the hills and the blobs in front of the base slide past at their own rates as the view scrolls.
