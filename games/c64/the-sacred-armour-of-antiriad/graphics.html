1986 · Palace Software

The Sacred Armour of Antiriad

Everything that moves is a sprite running an animation: a wait, a colour and a short list of pictures, stepped once a frame. Tal and the suit are two sprites each, one above the other.

01 · Everything that moves

All 95 animations, running as the game runs them

Each sprite slot holds an animation number, and once a frame the game's interrupt moves it on. Choose any of them to see where its pictures come from.

Shared multicolours

Every animation in anim_ptrs ($FCA6), from the listing's bytes, stepped 50 times a second as step_animation ($9E39) steps it. Where an animation ends, the game switches the sprite off; where it goes on to another, it runs that one. Here, to keep each tile moving, one that ended starts again half a second later, and one that went on to another starts again after half a second of it. The panel's sprites are set each frame by panel_sprites ($AC56) and drawn in its colours.

An animation is a header and a list. The header gives the number of frames each picture lasts and a byte for the colour and the multicolour bit (its bits for double width and height are never set). The list holds picture numbers, $10 and up, and ends in a command: 0 goes back to the first picture, 2 and a number jumps to that picture, 1 ends the animation, and any other value is followed by the animation to go on to. That is how Tal crouches and stays crouched, throws a stone and lets it go, collapses and lies dead: each is a short animation chained to the next.

The creatures in multicolour share two of their colours with Tal, because a C64 has one pair of sprite multicolours for all eight sprites. The game sets them to 8 and 9 while Tal is on foot and to 14 and 11 once he is inside the suit, so the four creatures drawn in multicolour and the bomb-thrower change two of their colours when he puts it on. Tal on foot is drawn with the first pair, and the worn suit with the second.

Tal is sprites 0 and 1, and a stone or a shot is sprite 2. The empty armour takes 3 and 4, and a room's creatures and things fill the slots from 7 down to 3 (place_room_sprites). When the beam reaches the panel, the game reuses sprites 5, 6 and 7 for the radiation meter and the picture window, all in a single colour.

The sprite pictures are 64 bytes each at $4400-$6FFF, numbered $10 to $BF, and every one of them is shown by something: an animation, or the panel. Many pictures serve several animations. The two creatures in cyan and light green share $A0-$A2 with different colours in their headers, the three drops share four pictures and their splashes three more, and the machine gunner's bullet uses the pictures of the Pulsar Beam's shot to the right, in red. Tal's collapse starts from the picture of him facing the player, and getting up runs the collapse's pictures backwards.

02 · Dying and appearing

Nine masks eat a picture away

A creature that dies, and Tal as he gets into the suit, dissolve: the picture is ANDed with nine masks in turn. Anything that appears does the reverse.

The masks are dissolve_masks, nine of 64 bytes at $0800, one byte for each byte of a picture. Left, the first picture of the animation as dissolve_step ($9F60) and materialise_step ($A0EF) draw it, a step every four frames; right, the picture under each mask.

Every fourth frame dissolve_step ANDs the sprite's picture, where it is kept at $4000 + 64 times its number, with the next mask. The masks keep 252, 235, 220, 195, 143, 79, 43, 19 and then none of a picture's 252 pairs of pixels, so it thins slowly at first and then quickly. After the ninth the sprite is switched off and the picture put back from a copy saved at the start. Appearing, materialise_step draws the saved picture ANDed with the inverse of each mask, so the pixels come back in the order a dissolve took them. The animation stands still while either runs.
