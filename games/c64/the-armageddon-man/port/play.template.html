01 · Strategy

Rules of thumb

No sure way through the year, but these follow from how the game decides what happens.

  • Only the survivors count. At the end of week 52 the rating is 100, less six or seven points for every country destroyed. Confidence, resources and the weekly communiques do not enter it, so spend them freely to keep everyone alive.
  • Watch Libya first. Its hostilities start out wrapping round to the top of the scale against about seven countries a game, and its only rung above 234 is the Total Missile Attack, so it is often the first to fire, sometimes inside the first week.
  • Park the U.N.N. Force where the trouble is. A Total Missile Attack from or on the country the force sits in is stopped, and that country obeys your letters whatever it thinks of you. Moving it takes up to three weeks, so move it early.
  • Never answer Neutral to terror. For an assassinated diplomat, a mobbed embassy, a car bomb or aid to a fanatical opposition, Neutral recalls you for incompetence half the time, and the game is over.
  • Give in to demands. When a country demands food or technology from another, Yes hands it over and cancels the event the demand came before. No lets that event happen, and Ignore makes both sides as hostile as the next harsher event and runs that one instead.
  • Never agree to be replaced. When a country writes that it has asked the U.N.N. Council to replace you, Agree ends the game on the spot. Ignore costs nothing, Disagree a little of that country's confidence.
  • Put the SDI satellites over the likely targets. Each one catches a missile flying under it by a 1 in 4 chance every frame. Israel falls after 16 hits, Japan after 32 and Pakistan after 48, against 240 or more for China and the Eastern Block.
  • Mind the radiation. Every hit that does not finish a country raises it by a 1 in 4 chance. One big exchange of a hundred missiles adds about 25, and at 255 the world ends at the next week's end.
  • Read the IN tray every week. Mail still unread when the week ends runs its follow-up, as if you had ignored it.
  • Use the pauses. The clock stops whenever a window is open, so a report, the radio or the satellite panel can stay up while you think.
