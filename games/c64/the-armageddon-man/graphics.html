1987 · Martech

The Armageddon Man: graphics

The picture the cassette shows while it loads, from the tape's own bytes.

01 · The loading picture

The picture the disk never shows

Martech's 1987 cassette puts its picture up whole, 81 seconds into the tape: THE ARMAGEDDON MAN in striped letters under Martech's name, a mushroom cloud, two missiles, a man in a hood holding a glowing globe and a satellite firing a beam, with 1987 MARTECH GAMES LTD down the right-hand edge. It stays up, in silence, for two and a half minutes while the game loads.

The cassette's own bytes, recorded in VICE with the picture up and drawn as the video chip draws them, with the border as the loader painted it in that one frame.

The disk version loads the same picture, byte for byte, into the same memory, and never puts it on the screen (Discoveries). It is the Spectrum version's loading screen, redrawn square by square, which is why it fills only the Spectrum's 256 by 192 pixels, set four character columns in from the left.

The stripes are the loader's. While the tape plays, it moves the border on to the next of the C64's sixteen colours again and again, faster than the screen is drawn, so the border fills with bands of every colour.
