1991 · Imageworks (Mirrorsoft)

Speedball 2

Everything that moves in a match is a multicolour sprite: the players, the ball, the arcs over the players under control, the pickups and the flashes on the walls. Most of the match's frames are made just before kick-off, by copying frames and turning right-facing ones into left-facing mirror images.

01 · Everything that moves

Every move a player makes, at the game's speed

The game runs at 50 frames a second, and looks at each player once every four frames, a quarter of them each frame. That is when a player's frame moves on: a running step, the next part of a throw, the next frame of a fall.

Team

The sprites are drawn from the game's bytes in the built site.

Every frame here is made from the listing's bytes by the game's own code, run on the kit's 6502 in this page: match_setup ($2657) from its first unpack to its last mirror image, and the second unpack after the run-out ($29B6-$29DE). The frame numbers and the steps follow the game's code: players_contact ($3B2C) for the players and the keepers, ball_frame ($2B12), control_markers ($2A9D), pickups_spin ($306E) and the run-out at $298F. Each is drawn in the colours the game gives it, with the two colours every sprite shares in a match, black and light grey.

A player's frame is his move plus the way he faces, one of eight, counted round from up. Standing is frame $40 plus his facing, sliding $48 plus it, and a jump $88 plus it; each of those is one frame, held for four looks (16 frames) for a slide or a jump. Running is four steps for each facing, from $50, and a throw three parts, from $70. A player knocked down falls through $98-$9E and lies on the last until his time is up, which is longer the more tired the player who tackled him; an injured player lies on the stretcher, $9F. A player who scores celebrates with $A1-$A4, rocking between the last two.

Brutal Deluxe are always red. The other team's colour comes from its number in the league: grey, green, yellow or blue (team_2_colours, $1D34). The player with the ball turns purple, or white or cyan on the other side (pick_up, $2D59).

The ball's frame is its height: $28 plus the height divided by 32. A lob starts at a height of 20, rising 19 the first frame and one less each frame after (throw_lob, $41E4), so it peaks at 210 and comes down on exactly 0. Frame $2F, for heights from 224, is never reached; it is a copy of $2E.

The run-out uses the first ten frames twice over. match_setup unpacks the arena's frames to $C000, then the ten frames of a door opening over the first ten of them. Once the players have run out, a grey sprite plays the door's ten frames, a frame every four ($298F); then the game unpacks the arena's frames again ($29B6), and frames $00-$05 are the arcs over the players again.

Frames $26, $27, $90-$97, $B8-$BB and $BF are empty. Two of them have a use: the ball is given $BF before the kick-off (match_setup) and $B8 when it goes back to the centre at the end of a half (match_end_frame, $223D), so it is still a sprite, but one that shows nothing.

Six frames that match_setup makes are never shown. It copies pictures into $A0, $A8, $A9 and $AB, and mirrors two of them into $AA and $AC, but no code in the game puts any of the six in an object's frame. Nor does any throw reach $2F, the ball's frame for heights from 224, which is a copy of $2E.

02 · Where the frames come from

Half the match's frames are made just before kick-off

The game keeps its sprite frames packed, and in columns. Before each match it unpacks them, turns them into the rows the video chip reads, copies some into new places and mirrors others.

Each packed frame is stored as its three columns of 21 bytes, which the game's unpacker squeezes better than the chip's rows of three bytes. sprite_rows ($4D8C) turns each one round. Three packed sets make the match: match_sprites ($A04F) for frames $80-$BF, arena_sprites ($A1D0) for $00-$2F, and door_sprites ($A5BB) for the run-out. Frames $40-$7F are not packed at all: they sit in rows at $D000-$DFFF, in the RAM under the video and sound chips, for the whole game.

Of the 64 frames at $80-$BF, only seven are in the packed data, and one of those, $AE, is covered before it is used. The rest are made. sprite_copy ($4E5E) copies 23 frames from $40-$7F into the jumps, the fall, the stretcher, the celebration and the keepers' stances (copy_pairs, $2A6F). Then sprite_mirror ($4E18) makes 45 left-facing frames from right-facing ones (mirror_pairs, $2A0B), reversing each row and each pair of bits in it. The pictures copied out of $40-$7F were in the places of left-facing frames, so the mirroring writes over them; between matches those places hold the copied pictures again, which the game copies back after each match ($74CE). The coin's spin is made the same way: five of its twelve steps are mirror images of the others ($29DC).

The pitch is drawn with characters at $F800, on two screens at $CC00 and $F400 that the game swaps as it scrolls, and the score panel below it is a third screen at $F000, all in the same 16 KB as the sprites. How the video chip changes from the pitch to the panel part way down, and how eight sprites become 26, is on the How it works tab.
