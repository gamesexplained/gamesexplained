Speedball

Four bugs in the rules of the C64 Speedball, each shown here by running the game's own code: a knockout cup that hands you the wrong opponents, tackles measured between the wrong players, a skill figure that works backwards, and leagues that run eleven weeks long.

01 · The knockout

Bug: the knockout's opponents play with another team's figures

The KNOCKOUT ROUND screen names your opponent and shows a card of figures. The figures belong to a different team in eight or ten rounds of ten, and they get weaker as the cup goes on.

The game's own knockout_init ($3F05) and the knockout's part of match_restart ($31DF-$31E6, up to the prematch screen) run on the site's 6502 from the listing, from a new random seed at each press. The figures are the computer side's stamina, power and skill as opponent_load leaves them; the template is the one that holds them.

A new knockout fills the eleven team records from the templates (knockout_init, $3F05). Record 0 is yours. Templates 1 to 10 go into the other records in a shuffled order: template 1 into a random record, template 2 three records on, and so on round the ten. Each record notes its template in team_template ($1CD1), and the KNOCKOUT ROUND screen draws round n's opponent from record n, its face and name from that template.

Before each leg, match_restart ($31C2) loads the computer's side. It reads record n's template number and then loads the record with that number:

ldx round · inx · ldy team_template,x · jsr opponent_load

opponent_load ($3F81) takes Y as a record number, so the template number is used as an index into the records a second time. The figures loaded are those of whatever template sits in that other record. Applied twice, the shuffle steps backwards through the templates one at a time, so from a random start the opponents' figures fall a template a round, and wrap once from the weakest team, Mira, to the strongest, Persus. The manual promises opponents that get harder as the rounds go on. The card under the named team's face shows the figures the computer really plays with, since attributes_text_set ($ACE6) reads them from the side, not the record. A league uses the records in template order, so its opponents are who they say they are.

02 · Tackles

Bug: thirteen pairs of players can never meet

Each pass the game measures how far apart all 45 pairs of players are, and files each measurement in a cell it looks up thirteen bytes too early. Thirteen pairs, nine of them opponents, are never seen to touch, and when green's keeper touches a team-mate the game acts on players far away.

Each square is a pair of players, its row the first and its column the second: 0 to 4 are green (0 the keeper), 5 to 9 red (5 the keeper). For each square the page sets that pair touching and the rest apart, then runs the game's own pair_distances ($B038) and tackles_check ($8DD6) on the site's simulator. Green squares are seen to touch, amber ones are seen and set off other pairs as well, and red ones are never seen. Press a square to see what the game does.

pair_distances works out how far apart the two players of each pair are, from pair 44 down to pair 0, and writes the answer twice into a table of 10 by 10 at $1E49: in the cell it finds through $F290, and in the mirror cell from pair_cell_mirror ($F2CA). tackles_check then looks each pair up through $F290 again. The right list of cells starts thirteen bytes later, at $F29D, and nothing reads it.

From pair 13 on, each pair writes and reads the cell meant for the pair thirteen before it. No other pair uses that cell, so these pairs work. The first thirteen pairs take their cells from the last thirteen bytes of pair_second, player numbers 6 to 9, so all of them land in four cells of row 0, which pairs 18 to 21 use as well. In each of those cells the pair measured last wins, and that is one of pairs 0 to 3: green's keeper with a team-mate.

So green's keeper never meets a red player, green's player 1 can walk through red's 5, and green's player 2 through red's 5, 6 and 7. When green's keeper and player 1 touch, the game also sends green 0 and red 5, green 0 and red 8, and green 2 and red 5 to the tackle routine, wherever they are on the pitch.

03 · Skill

Bug: skill makes your players easier to bring down

The SKILL on a team's card is 255 less a tackle figure. In play the figure is read only when a player slides into one of the team's players, and a higher skill makes that slide more likely to work.

The chance that a slide brings down a player of each team, from the test in tackle_resolve ($8EC2, and $9057 the other way): the slide works when a random byte is at least the victim's tackle figure. Run on the site's simulator 384 times each, slides into a side with skill 224 worked 88 % of the time and into one with skill 48 20 %, whatever the slider's own skill.

The shop sells extra skill, which takes 10 off your own tackle figure (shop_extra_skill, $AE59), and reduce skill, which adds 26 to the computer's for the next match (shop_reduce_skill, $AE6F). Both work against the buyer: extra skill makes your players easier to slide down, and reduce skill makes the computer's harder. The figure's one other use in a match is the computer's aiming, which tests only whether it is above 127 (ai_side1_target, $A898). Between other teams in a league the figure works the way the card suggests: the random results of the week's other matches favour the team with the lower figure (league_week_end, $8519).

04 · The league

Bug: a ten-week league lasts twenty-two weeks

The menu offers leagues of TEN WEEKS to ONE HUNDRED WEEKS. Each lasts eleven weeks more than it says, and the shortest more than twice as long.

The end of the game's own match_over ($3339-$336D) run once a week on the site's 6502 from the listing, from the state league_init ($3EEF) leaves, until it reaches the league's last screen (league_over_screen, $825A). The week's other matches (league_week_end, $8519) stand in by their one effect on the count: after week 10 they play week 11 at once.

A league's weeks run from 1 to 11. Your team plays in weeks 1 to 10 and rests in week 11, so each run of eleven weeks gives you ten matches. The length you choose goes into $1EB0 as a number of runs, 1 for TEN WEEKS up to 10 for ONE HUNDRED. match_over counts down $1EDB at the end of each run and moves the round counter on when it reaches 0, and the league ends when the round counter reaches the length. league_init sets $1EDB to 2, not 1, so the first round takes two runs: a ten-week league is 22 weeks and 20 matches, and a hundred-week one 121 weeks. The two player league counts its weeks separately and ends on time (twoplayer_week_end, $81E4).

05 · Tiles

The manual's electron tiles never appear

The manual lists ten power-up tiles. The C64 game chooses from eight of them.

The tile table at $BB21 holds a token and the types F, R, P, S, D, G, W three times, and J; it never holds 7 or 9, the electron bolt E and the eight-way burst M. Their entries in the table of effects (tile_effect_lo, $BBBF) point at the same routine as W, the ball that tackles whoever it touches (tile_ball_tackles, $9A9F), and the game has no sprite for a bolt. The How it works page rolls the game's own tile_choose a thousand times at a press.

06 · The disk

Two saved games ship on the original disk

The disk's directory lists two files the loader never reads, called 0 and 1. They are saved games.

SAVE GAME writes the game's state, the 1,024 bytes from $1C00 to $1FFF, through the KERNAL's own save routine, as file 0 for a league, 1 for a knockout and 2 for a two player game (game_save, $BD50; the digit from $2293). The disk's 0 and 1 are 1,024 bytes each and load at $1C00. In 0 the game type $1EAF is $FF, a league, its length 10 runs (ONE HUNDRED WEEKS), at week 1 with nothing played; its team records are in a knockout's shuffled order. In 1 the game type is 1, a knockout, at round 0. LOAD LEAGUE and LOAD KNOCKOUT therefore work on the original disk with no game saved, and start these two.
