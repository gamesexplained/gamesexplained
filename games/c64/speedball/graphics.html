1989 · Image Works

Speedball

Every player is a multicolour sprite whose shape comes from a frame list, one for each move and each of the eight ways he can face. The ball launcher opens a hatch drawn in characters, and the team choice screen moves its pictures by copying bytes into the bitmap.

01 · Everything that moves

Every move a player makes, at the game's pace

In a match the game moves everything once every two frames, a pass, 25 times a second. The players here are moved by the game's own animation code, run a pass at a time on the kit's 6502 in this page, and so is the team choice screen.

Side

The players are action_set_now ($A0C7), which starts a move from the tables at $BB30-$BBE2, then each pass player_frame_set ($8B0B) for the shape and the step at $A02E for the next frame, in that order, as player_dir_step ($9F98) runs them in a match. Their colours are player_colours ($370F), run each pass too. Sprites share black and white as their two common colours (play_sprites_init, $9123), and are drawn over the pitch's light blue. The team choice is team_select ($3D4C) and the part of team_choose from $3E4D that plays a team's animation. When a player's move or the launcher's spin would wait on a joystick or a random number, the page decides.

A move, which the game calls an action, is a frame list for each of the eight ways a player faces, the number of frames, and how many passes each frame is held. Running holds each of its eight frames for two passes, and loops; a slide is one frame held for thirteen passes; a fall plays fourteen frames, the last nine all the same. A move that ends hands the player to the action in action_next ($BB57): standing, after a jump, a throw, a slide, a fall, a tackle won or a dive, while running and the keepers' punches go round again. Standing is the middle one of the run's five shapes, so it needs no shapes of its own.

Some moves share their shapes. A tackle won is a throw without its first frame, and both keepers stand, between punches, on the field players' standing frames for up and down. A throw starts from the standing frame in seven of the eight directions; facing down, it starts from $66, a running frame, instead of $67.

The player under your control flashes light green, or light red on the other side, for four passes in every eight, while bit 2 of the pass counter $62 is clear. The ball has four sizes. A player's throw keeps it on the ground, at the smallest size, unless fire is held for five passes after it; then, as after every keeper's throw, the ball goes up, a size larger every eight passes, stays at the largest for twenty and shrinks again on the way down (ball_move, $380E).

The launcher is two sets of shapes. Its body is six characters by six in the pitch map, and the four characters that fill its middle are a hatch. At a kick-off launcher_step ($B2B9) opens it a line a frame, sliding its top half up and its bottom half down over a black hole, and after the shot closes it again a line a pass. While it is open, four sprites make a frame round the ball, and the ball's own sprite shows an arrow that turns anticlockwise a step every six frames, seventeen to forty-nine times, before the ball is shot out the way it points (launcher_sequence, $3739). While it turns, the game sets the sprites' shared black to dark grey at line $6E of the screen and back to black at line $9E.

Three of the menus' shapes are never shown: two corner pieces, $08 and $09, and a block of upright stripes, $0B. Nor are the last ten, $96-$9F, which are alternately filled and empty. The launcher's table at $2D21-$2D58 has room for two sets of four parts, but launcher_sequence only ever sets up the first; the second would be four light red copies of the second-smallest ball, $36.

The menus' highlight bar and the shop's cursor are each a row of sprites, a left end, middles and a right end, loaded with every other setting of the video chip from the sprite blocks at $2D72 and $2D9A (sprite_block_load, $8917). Moving one is writing a new height into every other byte of its block.

02 · Where the shapes live

The match's sprites move into the menus' colour screen

Shapes $00-$6F stay at $4000-$5BFF for good. The other forty-eight share $5C00-$67FF with the menus' bitmap, and are kept between matches where the pitch's map is built during one.

On the menus, $5C00 is the colour screen of the bitmap at $6000, and the shapes $70-$9F wait at $F300-$FEFF. Before a match pitch_setup ($90C8) copies them down to $5C00 (play_sprites_in, $916A) and then builds the pitch's map where they were (pitch_map_build, $917D). After the match play_sprites_out ($9147) copies them back up over the map, and the menus draw their colours over $5C00 again.

In a match the pitch is two character screens at $6C00 and $7400, with its characters at $7800, all in the same bank as the sprites. How the chip's eight sprites are shared out among the players, the ball and the launcher is on the How it works tab, with a frame of a match rebuilt from memory.

03 · The loading pictures

A fist that bursts out of a spark

The 1989 cassette shows two pictures while it loads. The first, about 83 seconds in, names the game's makers: Pantheon Software, Image Works and The Bitmap Brothers, beside a hand. The second comes up about 41 seconds later: SPEEDBALL over a grey wall, with an empty space below. A second and a third later a spark of light flares there and opens out into a fist in steel.

The first picture, as the cassette's own bytes draw it, with the border as the loader painted it in one frame: it flips the border between two colours as it reads the tape, so each stripe is a stretch of the signal.

The second picture, at the cassette's pace from the moment it comes up: the fist arrives in five steps, four frames apart, each drawn in two halves a frame apart.

As the fist lands the cassette starts the game's own menu tune, and plays it for two and a half minutes while the rest of the game loads (How it works).
