Commodore 64 · 1986 · Beam Software

Fist II: The Legend Continues

The sequel to The Way of the Exploding Fist left the dojo: a karate fighter crosses a scrolling world of 123 screens, fights whoever he meets, and hunts for eight scrolls.

01 · The adventure

Eight scrolls and a Warlord

A Warlord rules the land from a volcano. The masters of the Exploding Fist wrote their secrets on scrolls, hid them around the realm, and built meditation chambers where a warrior can grow stronger. The player walks through jungles, temples, caves and mountains, fights the Warlord's men one to one with the punches and kicks of the first game, finds the eight scrolls and takes each to its own chamber.

There is no dojo and no referee. The fighter carries one energy bar from screen to screen; it refills slowly between fights, and meditating fills it and raises its limit. Each scroll brought home adds a life, and after a defeat the fighter starts again in the last chamber he visited.

02 · The release

Beam Software's sequel

Beam Software made the game in 1986. Melbourne House published it in Britain as Fist II: The Legend Continues, and Mindscape in the United States as Fist: The Legend Continues. The credits given online are programming by Gregg Barnett, music by Neil Brennan, and graphics by Russel Comte and Greg Holland. The Commodore release has two programs: the adventure, and a combat practice mode for learning the moves.

03 · The copy studied

A Mindscape disk, and the Adventure load

The copy is a Mindscape disk. It shows the Mindscape logo and asks for Training or Adventure; each has its own set of files. This site covers the Adventure. Its loader reads six files and moves some of them under the Commodore's input and output chips and its operating system ROM, until the game fills memory from $0400 to $FFF8.

04 · What the code shows

A game that runs as two programs at once

The scrolling is done by a second task with its own stack, which the raster interrupt switches to and from. The fighters are 229 packed sprite images stored facing one way, and mirrored as they are unpacked. The input routine can read a second joystick for the other fighter, though nothing in the game switches it on. These are on the Discoveries tab.
