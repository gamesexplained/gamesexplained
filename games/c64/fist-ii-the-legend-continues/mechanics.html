Commodore 64 · 1986 · Beam Software

Fist II: The Legend Continues

How a blow is judged to land, how the scrolls are recorded, and how the rooms are joined.

Fights

How a blow is judged to land

Whether a blow lands is worked out from the move and the frame of its animation, not from the pictures touching.

For each move and each frame, a table gives either $80, no strike in this frame, or a value the game compares with the distance between the two fighters ($379C, tables at $7CA6 and $7E53). There are two tables, one for fighters facing each other and one for both facing the same way, so a kick behind you is judged by its own numbers. The hardware's sprite collision register is read as well ($3328). A blow that lands scores 100 points times the move's value ($051D).

A move is a script of four-byte steps: how long to hold, which pose, and how far to move across and up ($AF46, read by $1AF0). There are 44 scripts. Opponents fight with attack combinations, short scripts of moves chosen from a jump table ($3288) and a random number from an 8-bit shift register ($3141). The energy, lives and enemy rules a player meets are on the Gameplay tab.

Scrolls

Eight flags, and the code that reads each

The eight temples are rooms $44 to $4B, one for each scroll. Eight bytes ($0405-$040C) record each scroll: 1 when it is found, $80 once it is delivered to its temple. Delivering one adds a life ($2CCD). Each power is a test of one of these bytes somewhere in the code: $0A59 for scroll 1, $36DE for 2, $0649 for 3, $0A44 for 4, $27AD for 5, $064C for 6, $10E5 for 7 and $2CE4 for 8. What each does is on the Gameplay tab.

The places where opponents, hazards and scrolls wait are 54 three-byte records ($E609): flags, room, position. Bit 7 of the flags marks a record done, bit 6 a scroll. A done opponent comes back only after the hero has beaten 30 ($2820).

The world

123 screens joined by exit lists

The world is 123 screens, each belonging to one of 48 areas ($4A71). The game starts on screen 94, in area 28; the first opponent is waiting on screen 100, in area 30. There is no map in memory: each screen has a list of exits ($4AEC), four bytes each, giving how the exit is taken (a ladder or stairs up or down, an edge, a hole or a drop), the column it is at, the screen it leads to and the column the hero arrives at. Walls in the screen's item list ($501E) stop the hero and the view. The area's zone (areas 0-7, 8-29 or 30-47) also picks the tune, the border colour and the colours of the scenery ($11DA).

How a room is drawn is on the Graphics tab, and every room, joined up, is on the map.
