1985 · Epyx

Winter Games

The skiers, the skaters and the sled are each eight sprites in four pairs: a multicolour sprite for the colours, and a sprite of one colour at the same place for the fine edges. Each event carries its own pictures and the code that draws them, and most keep their frames packed until the moment they are shown.

01 · Everything that moves

Every frame of every event, drawn by the event's own code

Each event is a program of its own, loaded over the menu, with its own pictures. Here are all of them, unpacked by each event's own routine and drawn in its colours. Point at a frame for what it is.

The frames are drawn by the game's own code in the built site.

The opening ceremony

Seven animations, each a list of frames and how long to show each one, playing here at the game's rate of fifty steps a second. The flags are one sprite each, the flame two side by side, and the torch bearer eight, his head and body over his legs.

Hot Dog Aerials

The skier's 58 frames: the stunts, the flips, the awkward poses and the four falls.

Biathlon

The skier's 16 frames, then the rifle's sight, a sprite of its own.

Speed Skating

A frame for each state of the stride, drawn in each skater's colour, blue and then red. Speed Skating is the one event that keeps its frames whole rather than packed.

Figure and Free Skating

The skater's 60 frames. The sprites in front are white like the ice, solid but for a hole the skater shows through. On the ice only their edge can be seen: pick white behind them to see her as the game shows her.

Ski Jump

The small skier on the hill is a single black sprite, with 24 shapes; three of them no frame uses, drawn faint here. From the take-off, a close-up in the corner of the screen shows the jumper large, for the 26 frames from 5 to 30.

Bobsled

The sled's 13 frames, in the order of its place across the chute: from high on the left wall, through the middle, to high on the right, then the crashes over each wall.

Every frame is made from the listing's bytes by the event's own code, run on the kit's 6502 in this page. The packed frames are unpacked by each event's draw_frame: $1632 in Hot Dog, $2EF1 in Biathlon, $1BBB in the skating, and build_sled ($4E45) in Bobsled. Speed Skating's frames are read where they lie, from frame_bases ($A154). Ski Jump's close-up is drawn by its draw_frame ($1973) over the landing slope's picture, which unpack_list ($1273) unpacks. The colours are each event's own, from the tables its start-up writes to the video chip; the opening's come from its raster interrupts, which set them three times down the screen ($AB3F, irq_flame and irq_bearer).

02 · Two sprites at every place

Eight sprites, four pairs

A multicolour sprite's dots are two wide. Winter Games puts a sprite of one colour, with dots one wide, at the same place in front of each, and the pair draws finer than either could alone.

Drawn over light blue, a colour none of the events' sprites use. The sprite of one colour is drawn in front because its number is the lower: the video chip puts sprite 0 in front of sprite 1, and the events give the even sprites the single colour and the odd ones the multicolour.

Each event shows its skier, skater or sled as a square of four places, two across and two down, with two sprites at each place, one over the other. The one behind is multicolour: each of its dots, two pixels wide, is one of three colours, two that every multicolour sprite on the screen shares and one of its own. The one in front has one colour and dots a pixel wide. Hot Dog, Biathlon, Speed Skating and Bobsled draw outlines and fine lines with it: black round the Hot Dog skier, grey in Biathlon, dark grey in Speed Skating and orange in Bobsled.

Figure and Free Skating turn it round. The sprite in front is white like the ice and solid, but for a hole in the skater's shape. Its edge trims the multicolour dots where they stick out, so the skater's outline is finer than a multicolour sprite can draw alone. The skating's moves on How it works have a switch to take the white sprites away.

A frame is eight sprite shapes, 512 bytes. Every event but Speed Skating keeps its frames packed: a frame is 64 cells of eight bytes, and each cell starts with a mask byte whose set bits stand for the bytes that follow. The bytes without a bit are filled with 0, or, in the skating's white sprites, with $FF, which is what makes them solid. Each draw_frame unpacks a frame into the set of eight shapes the video chip is not showing, then points the sprites at it, two sets in turn, so that a frame never changes while it is being drawn on the screen. Speed Skating keeps its twelve frames whole, 6,144 bytes, and only points the sprites at them.

Ski Jump's close-up is drawn another way: 30 cells of the bitmap and four black sprites over them, each eight-byte cell stored whole, or as the single byte $33 where it is plain. How the bobsled screen puts the sled over its bitmap, one frame rebuilt from memory, is on How it works.
