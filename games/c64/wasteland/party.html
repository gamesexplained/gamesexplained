Fourteen people in the maps can join the party, and every one of them is as obedient as the next. A new Ranger’s attributes are meant to be the best three of five dice, and a slip in the sort loses the best dice instead.

01 · Characters

The NPCs, record by record

Fourteen people in the 42 maps can join the party. Each is a 256-byte character record kept in the map that holds them, laid out like a Ranger's and copied whole when they join ($A42C). Most of it shows on the character screen once they have joined. Two things never do: a skill outside the 35 that the maps test to know who is in the party, and the rule that decides whether they join at all.

Every record in the 42 maps' lists of characters who can join, read from the maps' listings, with the address of each in its map. Joins is record byte +$31: 0 joins without dice, and 2 or 3 throws for it (below). Hidden skill is the record's last skill pair, +$BA and +$BB, at level 255. Each map opens on the Maps tab.

The hidden skills are numbers 36 to 48, one to a character. The skills page lists a skill only when its number is below 36 ($11A7), and so does a Use, so nobody playing sees them. A check square that asks for one finds it all the same: the search for a skill reads all 30 pairs ($1392), and at difficulty 0 knowing a skill is enough, with no dice ($8EAE). Felicia, Jackie, Ralf, and Christina carry hidden skills that no square asks for.

Dan Citrine's skill, 37, turns up far beyond the courthouse. Four squares there ask for it, among them the cell where his father waits: He sees Danny and says, "Thanks for rescuing me, son. Here's the money. I must run now." Another 83 check squares in 27 other maps test skill 37 and nothing else, and 60 of them take CON when they fail. No Ranger can have it: the Ranger Center sells skills 1 to 35 ($8502), and no library in the 42 maps teaches one above 35. So for anyone else those checks always fail. Dan has the skill at level 255, so he passes them, at once at difficulty 0 and on any throw of 5 or more above it. Per Jorner's guide lists this among its cheats as a recurring error in the game's "scripting": with Dan in the first party slot he'll mess up or nullify many hazards in the game, ranging from humble cactuses to lethal traps (page 81). The first slot matters where the first conscious member is tested for the whole party. On map 1's cactus every member is tested, and on the kit's 6502 simulator the game's check code ($8E14) ran it 1,000 times for a party of a Ranger and Dan. The Ranger was hurt every time: You've walked into a cactus. The spines poke you and hurt a lot. Dan was hurt 107 times, each time on a throw under 5.

Nine of the fourteen join as soon as they are asked while the party has room. The other five, Mad Dog Fargo, Metal Maniac, Ralf, Mort, and Dr. Mike Scot, throw the open 2d6 first ($A435). Their side is (CHR + IQ) / 2, plus their rank, plus record byte +$31. The hirer's side is (CHR + IQ) / 2 plus CHR plus rank, plus the throw, and a throw under 5 fails whatever the sums. The manual says only that charisma affects how an NPC will react to you when you want to hire him or trade equipment (page 4). Per Jorner writes in his guide that he has never seen an NPC resist more than one recruiting attempt (page 49). On joining, +$31 becomes the hirer's CHR ($A492), and nothing else writes it. The next section is about what that number is for.

In a fight an NPC takes no orders ($B500). It picks a group in reach at random, and its own fire mode. With a gun that fires bursts, it fires a single shot when fewer than 3 rounds are left and a burst at exactly 3. With more, a number from 1 to 10 against a table decides between a burst and automatic fire ($B4F8), and the table is read by how many characters are not conscious: never automatic while all are, then 2 in 10 with one down, 4 with two, 6 with three, 8 with four, and always with five, six, or seven. The manual says only that NPCs don't always do as told in combat (page 12). Per Jorner's guide passes on a report that Christina likes to fire full auto a lot (page 50). The code gives her no choice of her own: the gun and the characters who are down decide, the same for every NPC.

02 · Promotion

Nobody is promoted past rank 181, Supreme Jerk

A Ranger is promoted at the radio once the experience reaches 512 × k × (k − 1) for rank k. Experience is a 24-bit number, and from rank 182 on that threshold needs 25 bits, so the climb stops at 181, whose name is Supreme Jerk.

The radio tests each conscious Ranger in turn ($7F1B). Each rank costs 1,024 more experience than the one before: 1,024 to reach rank 2, 3,072 for rank 3, and 6,144 for rank 4. After a promotion the test runs again ($7FFF), so one call can promote a Ranger several times, with two points to spend each time. The threshold is worked out in three bytes, and one too big for them means no promotion ($7F46, $7F56). Rank 181 needs 16,680,960. Rank 182 would need 16,866,304, past the 16,777,215 that three bytes hold, where experience itself stops growing ($0825).

Every rank a Ranger can reach, with the name the radio's table gives it ($81D8) and the experience at which the radio promotes into it. A new Ranger starts at rank 1. The five names that break the run of General Argents are marked.

The table of names has an entry for every rank from 0 to 255. Ranks 2 to 11 have a name each, the names after that last two ranks, then three, then four, and from 131 every rank is General Argent but five: Imperial Scarscalp at 141, 1st Class Fargo at 151, Photon Stud at 161, Revenant Argent at 171, and Supreme Jerk at 181. Past 181 the five come round again every ten ranks to 231, and once more at 251 to 255, out of reach. When a promotion keeps the old name, the radio says the Ranger is still a Sergeant or a Captain ($7FBB).

The fans counted the same ladder. Rangers had told the HQ-Grid's Underground for years that there were 183 ranks, until K. Reimer showed (11 September 2010) that there are 181, as the C64's code has it. The site's list matches the C64 rank for rank, but for ranks 30 to 41: the C64 runs 2nd Lieutenant, 1st Lieutenant, Lieutenant, then Fireteam Lieutenant, and the list Lieutenant, 1st Lieutenant, Fireteam Lieutenant, then 2nd Lieutenant. K. Ballou (14 May 1998) found the cost of the climb with the Night Terror's 64,000, which will give each char several promotions the first time, but it gets to a point where you only get one promotion per 64000 exp. On the C64 the first such kill takes a new Ranger from rank 1 to 11, and from rank 63 on a kill no longer pays for even one promotion every time.
