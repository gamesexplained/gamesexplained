Fourteen people in the maps can join the party, and every one of them is as obedient as the next. A new Ranger’s attributes are meant to be the best three of five dice, and a slip in the sort loses the best dice instead.

01 · Characters

The NPCs, record by record

Fourteen people in the 42 maps can join the party. Each is a 256-byte character record kept in the map that holds them, laid out like a Ranger's and copied whole when they join ($A42C). Most of it shows on the character screen once they have joined. Two things never do: a skill outside the 35 that the maps test to know who is in the party, and the rule that decides whether they join at all.

Every record in the 42 maps' lists of characters who can join, read from the maps' listings, with the address of each in its map. Joins is record byte +$31: 0 joins without dice, and 2 or 3 throws for it (below). Hidden skill is the record's last skill pair, +$BA and +$BB, at level 255. Each map opens on the Maps tab.

The hidden skills are numbers 36 to 48, one to a character. The skills page lists a skill only when its number is below 36 ($11A7), and so does a Use, so nobody playing sees them. A check square that asks for one finds it all the same: the search for a skill reads all 30 pairs ($1392), and at difficulty 0 knowing a skill is enough, with no dice ($8EAE). Felicia, Jackie, Ralf, and Christina carry hidden skills that no square asks for.

Dan Citrine's skill, 37, turns up far beyond the courthouse. Four squares there ask for it, among them the cell where his father waits: He sees Danny and says, "Thanks for rescuing me, son. Here's the money. I must run now." Another 83 check squares in 27 other maps test skill 37 and nothing else, and 60 of them take CON when they fail. No Ranger can have it: the Ranger Center sells skills 1 to 35 ($8502), and no library in the 42 maps teaches one above 35. So for anyone else those checks always fail. Dan has the skill at level 255, so he passes them, at once at difficulty 0 and on any throw of 5 or more above it. Per Jorner's guide lists this among its cheats as a recurring error in the game's "scripting": with Dan in the first party slot he'll mess up or nullify many hazards in the game, ranging from humble cactuses to lethal traps (page 81). The first slot matters where the first conscious member is tested for the whole party. On map 1's cactus every member is tested, and on the kit's 6502 simulator the game's check code ($8E14) ran it 1,000 times for a party of a Ranger and Dan. The Ranger was hurt every time: You've walked into a cactus. The spines poke you and hurt a lot. Dan was hurt 107 times, each time on a throw under 5.

Nine of the fourteen join as soon as they are asked while the party has room. The other five, Mad Dog Fargo, Metal Maniac, Ralf, Mort, and Dr. Mike Scot, throw the open 2d6 first ($A435). Their side is (CHR + IQ) / 2, plus their rank, plus record byte +$31. The hirer's side is (CHR + IQ) / 2 plus CHR plus rank, plus the throw, and a throw under 5 fails whatever the sums. The manual says only that charisma affects how an NPC will react to you when you want to hire him or trade equipment (page 4). Per Jorner writes in his guide that he has never seen an NPC resist more than one recruiting attempt (page 49). On joining, +$31 becomes the hirer's CHR ($A492), and nothing else writes it. The next section is about what that number is for.

In a fight an NPC takes no orders ($B500). It picks a group in reach at random, and its own fire mode. With a gun that fires bursts, it fires a single shot when fewer than 3 rounds are left and a burst at exactly 3. With more, a number from 1 to 10 against a table decides between a burst and automatic fire ($B4F8), and the table is read by how many characters are not conscious: never automatic while all are, then 2 in 10 with one down, 4 with two, 6 with three, 8 with four, and always with five, six, or seven. The manual says only that NPCs don't always do as told in combat (page 12). Per Jorner's guide passes on a report that Christina likes to fire full auto a lot (page 50). The code gives her no choice of her own: the gun and the characters who are down decide, the same for every NPC.

02 · Promotion

Nobody is promoted past rank 181, Supreme Jerk

A Ranger is promoted at the radio once the experience reaches 512 × k × (k − 1) for rank k. Experience is a 24-bit number, and from rank 182 on that threshold needs 25 bits, so the climb stops at 181, whose name is Supreme Jerk.

The radio tests each conscious Ranger in turn ($7F1B). Each rank costs 1,024 more experience than the one before: 1,024 to reach rank 2, 3,072 for rank 3, and 6,144 for rank 4. After a promotion the test runs again ($7FFF), so one call can promote a Ranger several times, with two points to spend each time. The threshold is worked out in three bytes, and one too big for them means no promotion ($7F46, $7F56). Rank 181 needs 16,680,960. Rank 182 would need 16,866,304, past the 16,777,215 that three bytes hold, where experience itself stops growing ($0825).

The table of names has an entry for every rank from 0 to 255. Ranks 2 to 11 have a name each, the names after that last two ranks, then three, then four, and from 131 every rank is General Argent but five: Imperial Scarscalp at 141, 1st Class Fargo at 151, Photon Stud at 161, Revenant Argent at 171, and Supreme Jerk at 181. The fans counted the same ladder. Rangers had told the HQ-Grid's Underground for years that there were 183 ranks, until K. Reimer showed (11 September 2010) that there are 181, as the C64's code has it. The site's list matches the C64 rank for rank, but for ranks 30 to 41: the C64 runs 2nd Lieutenant, 1st Lieutenant, Lieutenant, then Fireteam Lieutenant, and the list Lieutenant, 1st Lieutenant, Fireteam Lieutenant, then 2nd Lieutenant. K. Ballou (14 May 1998) found the cost of the climb with the Night Terror's 64,000, which will give each char several promotions the first time, but it gets to a point where you only get one promotion per 64000 exp. On the C64 the first such kill takes a new Ranger from rank 1 to 11, and from rank 63 on a kill no longer pays for even one promotion every time.

03 · Skills

Secret: Combat shooting is on the skill list, and there is nowhere in the game to learn it

The game has names for 35 skills, and the seventeenth is Combat shooting. A Ranger buys skills at the Ranger Center and in the libraries, and neither of them sells this one.

Every skill has one byte that holds the IQ it needs and the price of its first level. The Ranger Center and the library each carry a copy of the table ($8502, $CC6C). Combat shooting's byte is $FF, every bit set, which reads as IQ 31 and 7 skill points. The dearest skill on sale, Cyborg tech, needs IQ 24 and costs 3. The Ranger Center lists only the skills that a new Ranger's IQ allows, and a new Ranger's IQ is 18 at most. A library lists only the skills in its own record, and skill 17 is in none of the eight records (below). Promotions can raise IQ to 31 in time, and the Ranger would still find no list to pick Combat shooting from.

A Ranger who had the skill would find nothing to do with it. A weapon's record names the skill that goes with the weapon, and Combat shooting is missing from all 95 item records. It is missing from every test on the maps and from the fourteen NPCs' records too. The engine did give it an attribute. Each skill adds one attribute to its throw ($0DD8), and Combat shooting is the only skill that adds Speed.

04 · Skills

What the maps ask of a Ranger, skill by skill

A square can test a skill, an attribute, an item, the party's cash, or the party's size. Counting every test in the 42 maps shows what each skill is worth outside a fight. Perception is asked for 122 times on 33 maps, and six of the weapon skills are never asked for.

A test of a skill starts by looking for the skill. A Ranger who does not have it fails without a throw ($8EA5), so one level of Perception opens 122 tests that are shut to a Ranger without it. With the skill, the test is the throw on the Dice and combat tab, against 15 + 5 × the difficulty. 05 · Squares

Secret: some squares check whether a Ranger is a man or a woman, and one checks rank

A test of an attribute names a byte of the character record, and a few squares name bytes that are not attributes. Three of them read the Ranger's sex, and a square outside the walls of the Guardian Citadel reads the rank.

The party

Set the party and pick a square. The game's text appears here.

The four Rangers stand on the square on the game's own code, with the sex and rank set here written into their records, and the page shows what the game prints. At Leroy's the first Ranger goes alone with $200. The men's door, the lady, and the Citadel's square run when the party is on the square ($ACC1), and the women's door when the party tries to step onto it ($8DEC).

The sex byte is 0 for a man and 1 for a woman, and a test of it passes when the byte equals the test's number ($0D0A). Scott's Bar in Quartz has two doors with a picture on each. The men's door lets anyone through, and when any Ranger is a woman a man walking out gives the females among you a funny look ($3C35). The women's door tests every conscious Ranger, and one man is enough for the bouncer to turn the whole party away ($3C42).

The third square is the three-legged lady at Leroy's in Needles, who asks for $50. She takes one visitor at a time, so the record first tests for a party of exactly one. Then it tests the visitor's sex, and a woman is told Look, I just don't DO ladies. ($3C2D). A man with $50 pays and leaves with Wasteland Herpes, which is one of only two ways to catch it (below).

The rank test passes when the rank is at least the test's number. On a square outside the Guardian Citadel the number is 10, the rank of Grenadier Major. A party with nobody of that rank is told Leave, wasteland scum, you are not old enough to die here. With one such Ranger the voice takes the party seriously: You would be a fool to dream of assaulting the Citadel. ($3BC0). Only the words change.

06 · Diseases

Secret: Sewer rot and Wasteland Herpes never cost a hit point

A Ranger can catch six diseases, and the game names them only in a doctor's exam. Two of the six, Desert dust and Rabies, take hit points as time passes. The other four take none. What all six do is stop the Ranger from healing, and for a Ranger who is knocked out that is as deadly.

The party

Give the Rangers a disease each and walk. Their CON appears here.

Four Rangers walk the streets of Quartz on the game's own code, half a minute to a step ($AF32). Each starts at 20 CON of 30, knocked out at −5, or badly hurt at −15. The page reads their CON from the records after each walk.

Each disease is one bit of a byte in the character record. On most maps the game's clock runs a health check on every Ranger every four minutes of game time ($B795). A healthy Ranger gains a point from time to time, and a Ranger who is unconscious, with CON from −1 to −10, gains one at every check until they wake. With any bit of the disease byte set, the Ranger gains nothing ($B854). With one of the four high bits set, a conscious Ranger loses a point every 16 minutes and a Ranger who is down loses two ($B816). The last point is never taken from a Ranger who is awake: CON goes from 1 to −1, and the Ranger falls unconscious. From there the loss goes on until CON passes −50, where the Ranger dies. A Ranger with 20 CON and Desert dust falls after 5 hours and 20 minutes and is dead after 12 hours.

The game also counts an unconscious Ranger with a disease as lost. The game goes on while one character is awake or will wake unaided, and a Ranger with a disease will not ($7EE6).

07 · Diseases

Bug: Rabies gives a badly wounded Ranger twice as long to live

A Ranger whose CON has fallen to −11 or below is dying. That Ranger loses a point at every health check and dies when CON passes −50. A dying Ranger who also has Rabies or Desert dust loses the points half as fast.

The health check looks at the disease byte first. When one of the four high bits is set, the check takes the disease's branch and never reaches the code for a dying Ranger ($B7A2). The disease's branch acts only on every fourth check and takes two points from a Ranger who is down ($B834). The code it skipped takes one point at every check ($B83C), which is four points in the same time. From −15, a Ranger with no disease is dead after 2 hours and 24 minutes, and a Ranger with Rabies after 4 hours and 48 minutes. The plate above shows both when the Rangers start badly hurt.

08 · Diseases

Secret: the doctor can name two more diseases, D6 and D7, that nothing in the game gives

The disease byte has eight bits, and the doctor's exam has a name for each. The names of the last two are D6 and D7 ($CCC2).

Press the button. The doctor's list appears here.

The doctor's own routine prints the list for a disease byte of $FF ($CC04). The game cannot set the last two bits, so a doctor in play never prints lines 7 and 8.

The health check treats the two spare bits as it treats Desert dust and Rabies, because it tests the four high bits together ($B7A2). The doctor would list either one in an exam and cure it at the usual price. Every routine in the 42 maps that gives a disease takes the number of the disease from its record, and the records hold only the numbers 1 to 5. Radiation sets bit 0. So a Ranger never has D6 or D7, and the plate above is the one place to see what they would do.

11 · The Ranger Center

Secret: the Ranger Center has a key that hands back every skill point, and the C64 keyboard has no way to press it

A new Ranger spends skill points on a list of skills, and a point spent is spent. The program holds a way to start the list again. It wipes every skill bought and gives the points back, and it waits for a key that the C64 version cannot read.

The engine's lists take the digit of a line, RETURN, the left arrow, and one extra key that each list sets for itself. On the skills page in play the extra key is R, for reordering the skills. The Ranger Center sets the extra key of its skill list to the code $92 ($83FE). When that key comes in, the program clears all 30 of the Ranger's skill slots, sets the skill points back to the Ranger's IQ, and shows the list again ($80B5).

The game's key codes are the ones an Apple II keyboard sends, where $92 is the Control key held with R. The C64 version turns each key of its own keyboard into one of those codes through a table of 64 entries, one for each key ($C953). In that table R alone gives $D2, the Control key gives nothing, and none of the 64 entries is $92. A player on the C64 who buys the wrong skill can only delete the Ranger and roll a new one.

The same list has a message that can never be shown. You are not smart enough! answers a pick of a skill that needs more IQ than the Ranger has ($8179). The list shows only the skills that the Ranger's IQ allows, and the engine takes only the digit of a line that is on the screen, so the pick that the message answers cannot be made.
