A Ranger who is down cannot be hurt by any monster, and a party left behind heals twice as fast as the party being led. Every check and every shot comes down to two dice that are thrown again on a double, and Wasteland’s dice come up doubles one throw in five.

01 · Rangers who are down

Secret: a Ranger who is down cannot be hurt by any monster

A Ranger whose CON has fallen to 0 or below is out of every monster's reach. A shot that lands on a Ranger who is down is turned into a miss, and a monster that fights hand to hand only picks Rangers who are on their feet. Bleeding, disease, and the ground can still kill a fallen Ranger.

The party

Against

Six Guardians with guns, 10 and 14 feet away.

The game's text for the round appears here.

A room in the Guardian Citadel's Outer Sanctum where six Guardians with guns wait (encounters 17 and 18 of map 36), with four Rangers on the square beside them. Press a Ranger to put them down or stand them up. Fight a round runs the game's own round from its code ($B867), with random bytes from this browser where the C64 reads its raster line and a timer. Every press starts from the same state. The Rangers on their feet evade, and have 250 CON each so that they last. In 400 such rounds with Hell Razor and Angela Deth down, 1,847 blows were aimed at those two and cost them no CON, and 1,864 were aimed at the other two and cost them 73,640.

When a monster's shot is on target, the game picks one of the party at random to take it, and a Ranger who is down can be picked ($9DEC). The routine that hands out the damage then looks at the victim. If the victim is already down and the attacker's weapon is a gun, the routine prints the Ranger's name and but misses., and leaves the CON alone ($A889-$A8AD).

The same routine has a rule for a claw or a club landing on a Ranger who is down: two dice, thrown again on a double, have to come in under the monster's number of damage dice ($A899). That rule never runs. A monster in melee picks its victim from the Rangers still on their feet, and makes no attack when there are none ($81B7). In the emulator, two Mutants on the world map fought twelve rounds against a party with one Ranger unconscious, and all 25 of their blows went to Rangers on their feet.

So an unconscious Ranger is the safest member of the party. From −1 to −10 CON a Ranger gets a point back after every round, and wakes with half the CON they had before the blow ($B7BA-$B7DB). From −11 down the Ranger loses a point after every round and dies below −50 ($B842). That count is the only danger in a fight, and a monster can do nothing to hurry it.

02 · The patrols under the Temple of Blood

Bug: the Guards under the Temple of Blood cannot fire while one of their Bloodbeasts is alive

Ten patrols guard the tunnels under the Temple of Blood, each made of Guards with guns and Bloodbeasts. As long as one Bloodbeast of a patrol is alive, its Guards never fire. They start to shoot in the round after the last Bloodbeast dies.

The game's text for the round appears here.

One patrol (encounter 19 of map 28) with four Rangers of 60 CON on the square beside it, 14 feet away. The fight runs on the game's own code from round to round, and a new patrol comes when it ends. The switch moves one branch ($9CAC) so that a line without guns goes where an empty line goes ($9DB9). Over 30 patrols of up to 14 rounds with the Rangers attacking, the Guards fired no shot in the 217 rounds a Bloodbeast was alive and 126 shots in the 125 rounds after. With the Rangers evading, they fired 516 shots in 384 rounds with the switch on, and none in 407 rounds with the switch off.

When the monsters shoot, the game goes through a group's three lines from the last to the first ($9C65). An empty line is skipped: the code jumps on to the next line ($9CA0). A line of monsters without guns takes a different jump, to the end of the whole group ($9CAE), which is where a peaceful group is sent. Every line still to come is passed over with it.

A patrol's record lists the Guards first and the Bloodbeasts second, so the Bloodbeasts are looked at first and close the patrol's turn before the Guards are reached. The order in the record is all that decides it. With the two lines swapped in the record, the same patrols fired 616 shots in 420 rounds.

One encounter outside the tunnels is laid out the same way. In the Guardian Citadel's Outer Sanctum, Master Ciro and Adept Kate carry guns in the first two lines of their group, and Brother Nuris fights hand to hand in the third. While Brother Nuris lives, neither of the other two fires. These eleven are the only ones among the 583 encounters with fixed monsters that list a gun before a monster without one.

03 · Running away

Bug: a lone gunman runs away easily, and a line of ten gunmen never runs

Before each round, a line of monsters with guns weighs itself against the party and runs if it is the weaker. The code sets out to make the test depend on the kind of monster. Through a slip it depends on how many of them there are, and every extra monster in the line halves the CON the party is credited with.

The patrol of the section above, met again and again by four Rangers with the CON set here. The game's own code places the patrol, rolls its hit points, and plans its move ($936E, $83D7). In 420 meetings at seven levels of CON from 20 to 250, every plan followed the count of the Guards: a lone Guard ran when his hit points were under half the party's average CON, and a pair when their average was under a quarter of it.

The test compares the line's average hit points with the party's average CON ($869F). The party's figure is to be halved first for cyborgs and halved twice for animals, mutants, and humanoids. Robots skip the test and never run ($847F-$849E).

The number of halvings waits in a spare byte while the two averages are worked out. The routine that averages the hit points uses the same byte, and leaves the number of monsters in the line there ($8719). When the halvings are read back ($86A8), the count of monsters comes out in their place.

The line runs when its average hit points are below the party's average CON after the division. Ten halvings leave 0 of any CON under 1,024, so a full line of ten never runs from a party a player is likely to have.

A lone Guard is a humanoid, so the party's CON should be quartered for him and is only halved: he runs from a party half as tough as the one he was meant to run from. Two Guards get the quartering by chance. The count is of the monsters still alive, so the survivors of a line that is being shot down grow more willing to run.

The whole group follows its first line. When the Guards run, their Bloodbeasts go with them, and a group that is on the move makes no attack that round ($83D7).

04 · Healing

Secret: a party left behind heals twice as fast as the party you lead

CON comes back with time, a point at a time. A wounded Ranger in the party being led gets a point every 128 ticks of the game's clock, and a Ranger in any other party gets one every 64. Leaving the wounded where they stand and walking the others about heals them at double speed.

Walk runs the game's own clock for each step and fills the table with the Ranger's CON after it.

One Ranger with 30 MAXCON, once as a member of the party that walks and once as a member of a party left elsewhere. Each step runs the game's clock routine on the map chosen ($AF32), which runs the health routine whenever it is due ($B795).

The clock that counts is a byte that goes up with every step, by an amount the map sets: 16 on the world map, 2 in a town such as Quartz, and 1 inside most buildings ($AF32). Each time the byte comes to a multiple of 16 the game looks at every Ranger in every party ($B795). A Ranger who is hurt and on their feet gets a point when the lowest six bits of the byte are all 0, which is every 64 ticks. For a Ranger in the party being led the test takes seven bits, which is every 128 ($B7FD-$B810).

On the world map that is a point every four steps for a party left behind and a point every eight for the party that walks. In the emulator, 64 steps on the world map took a party left behind from 6 CON to 22 and the walkers from 5 to 13.

A Ranger who is down does not wait for those ticks. An unconscious Ranger, from −1 to −10, gets a point every time the game looks, in whichever party, and a Ranger who is worse off loses one. The game looks at every step on the world map, so a Ranger at −25 is dead 26 steps later. Inside the Citadel it looks every 16 steps, and the same Ranger lasts 416. It also looks after every round of a fight.

None of the healing reaches a Ranger who has stood in radiation. A mark in the Ranger's record stops every point coming back, awake or unconscious, until a doctor clears it ($B854).

05 · The Chopter

Bug: fists, knives, and chainsaws can never hit the Chopters outside Base Cochise

The Chopters that guard the outside of Base Cochise cannot be struck in melee. Every blow with a fist or a hand weapon misses them, whatever the Ranger's Brawling and AGL. Guns hit them as they hit anything else.

The Rangers strike at

The game's text for the round appears here.

Chopters and VTOL Auto-fire Robots in one group outside Base Cochise (encounter 11 of map 16), 10 feet from four Rangers who fight with their fists and have 900 CON each so that they last. The round is the game's own, and every press starts from the same state. In 300 rounds the Rangers struck 1,800 blows at the Chopters and landed none. In 300 more they struck 1,800 at the robots beside them and landed 573.

A blow lands when a throw from 1 to 100 comes in under the Ranger's chance. The chance starts at 50, or at 60 when the monster's group is on the move, and gains 4 for each level of Brawling and a bonus for AGL ($8256). The monster's skill byte is then taken off. For a monster that carries a gun, the code first adds 5.

The Chopter's skill byte is 0, the only 0 among the 398 monster records in the maps. To take the byte off, the code turns it into a negative number of 16 bits, and it gets 0 wrong: the low half stays 0 and the high half is set to $FF regardless, which makes −256 ($828A-$8292). The chance falls far below 0 and is held at 0.

The same byte is the Chopter's aim, added to its dice when it fires ($9C65). The other machines with guns outside the base have 30 or 40 there. So the worst shot on the map is the one enemy a chainsaw cannot touch. The Chopters of Las Vegas are a different record, with a skill of 2 and no gun, and a blow can land on them.

06 · A round that never ends

Bug: a single shot that kills the last Ranger outright can freeze the game

When one shot takes the last living Ranger from their feet to dead, and another monster's shot is on target in the same volley, the game looks for somebody alive to take the second shot and never stops looking. The text stops in the middle of a sentence and the game stops answering the keys.

The game's text for the round appears here.

The Outer Sanctum's room again, with one Ranger left at 1 CON, evading, in front of Master Paz, Brother Jackson, and Brother Nobel (encounter 18 of map 36; the room's other group is already wiped out). The first button plays a round with a fixed run of random bytes, one of three in 3,000 that froze. The others use this browser's random bytes. A round is called frozen here when the game's code has run 1,500,000 instructions without ending it, five times what a round takes.

A monster's shot that is on target picks a Ranger at random. If that Ranger is dead it picks again, and it goes on picking until it finds one alive ($9DFD-$9E26). The game checks that somebody is alive before the monsters start to fire, and again after each burst or spray, but not after a single shot ($9C65).

A blow kills outright when it takes a Ranger to −50 or below, so the shot has to do the Ranger's CON and 50 more. Five monsters with single-shot guns roll enough dice for that: the Barkeep of Spade's Casino, and Brother Jackson, Brother Nobel, Redhawk, and Sister Faith in the Outer Sanctum. The Barkeep fights alone in his encounter, so no second shot follows his. The room staged above has two of the others side by side.

The other Rangers must be dead for this, not merely down: a Ranger who is down counts as alive, takes the shot, and comes to no harm.

07 · The round

A round is fought in four passes, and every gun fires before any blade

Orders are taken for the whole party, and then the round runs in a fixed order: the Rangers' guns, the monsters' guns, the melee, and everything else. Speed decides who goes first only inside the melee.

The four passes of a battle round ($B867). Rangers who run and parties that move go after the fourth pass ($A276), then the monsters' groups make their moves ($872D), and the clock and the health routine run once.

In the melee, a Ranger's initiative is two dice plus SP plus 4 for each level of Brawling, and a monster's is two dice plus 8 times its skill byte ($7F64). The highest goes first, and a tie goes to the Ranger ($8057). On their turn a Ranger strikes once, and once more for every two levels of Brawling. A monster attacks as many times as its skill byte.

That one byte of a monster's record does four jobs. It is added to the monster's dice when it shoots, it is the number of its attacks in melee, it sets its initiative, and it comes off a Ranger's chance to hit the monster or to avoid its blow, four times over when the monster has a melee weapon ($8256).

A Ranger's order changes how hard they are to hit in melee. The chance to avoid a monster's blow starts at 60 for a Ranger who is evading, 50 for one who is attacking, and 40 for anyone else, such as a Ranger who is loading a gun or using an item ($8177). Evading also escapes any shot that would have hit by a margin under 6 ($9DEC).

08 · Checks and shots

Two dice that keep rolling

Every skill check, attribute check, and party member's shot comes down to one throw: two six-sided dice, added up, and thrown again whenever they match. The repeats add on, so a pair of sixes followed by a 3 and a 4 makes 19 ($0845). Each repeat is a one-in-six chance, so high totals are rare but possible.

A check has a target of 15 + 5 × its difficulty ($0867). For a skill check, the throw plus the attribute that goes with the skill plus 4 for each level of the skill must reach it ($0C6A). The table at $0DD8 pairs each skill with its attribute, such as Climb with AGL, Picklock with DEX, and Medic with IQ. An attribute check adds the attribute alone ($0D0A). A throw under 5 fails whatever the bonus, which is one throw in nine.

Every throw of 5 or more is added to the character's experience, pass or fail ($0C41). A pass can also raise the skill a level, but only while the level is below the character's rank and below the difficulty. Then a d10 no higher than half of (difficulty − level), rounded down, plus 1 raises it ($0CA2). A new character has rank 1 ($7FDD), so no skill improves by use before the first promotion.

Players levelled whole parties on this rule. On the Wasteland Ranger HQ-Grid's Underground, D. Sinner (4 May 1998) played the Desert Nomads' slot machine and then three card monty in Las Vegas, reckoned on about ten levels from a session, and guessed that you get one xp for every $ you make. The money plays no part. A pull of the slot machine tests the player's Gamble and LK by turns, up to ten times and hardest first, until one passes ($3B9C), and a hand of monty tests LK and Gamble ($3BC9). The experience is the dice of each of those throws that came to 5 or more, won or lost.

– chance to pass

The chance is exact for fair dice: every way the throw can reach each total is counted, doubles and all. The chart is the throw alone; red totals fail at once. Roll runs the game's own check, ported from the engine ($0C29, $0D0A) and tested against the original code on a 6502 simulator: 27,324 skill checks over every skill, levels 0 to 10 and difficulties 0 to 31, and 3,072 attribute checks, with the same random bytes, gave the same results and used the same bytes.

A shot uses the same throw ($A584). To it are added DEX, 4 for each level of the weapon's skill, and the fire mode: 4 for a burst, the rounds left in the gun for automatic fire, and 10 for an explosive ($A5D6). A full Uzi on automatic adds 40. The total must reach a target that grows with the distance, rounded up to 5 feet, at one of three rates ($A793, $BD48). Pistols, thrown weapons, and explosives start at 0 and add 5 for every 5 feet. SMGs, carbines, the Flamethrower, the Ion beamer, the Mangler, and the Sabot rocket start at 10 and add 2. Every other gun, from the rifles to the Meson cannon, the LAW, and the RPG-7, starts at 20 and adds 1, so beyond 25 feet a rifle is an easier shot than a pistol, and beyond 50 feet than an SMG.

Nothing in that total can turn against the shooter. It is kept in 16 bits and stops at 65,535 ($079D), each level of skill adds 4 however high the level goes, and the skill never enters a hit's damage. So every level of Energy weapon makes a shot likelier to hit. J. Webb wrote on the HQ-Grid's Underground, in an undated note, that an Energy Weapon skill level above 8 seems detrimental. The C64's code has no such turn.

The target is 7 higher when the monster the code looks at has no gun ($A76E). The line it looks at is the one numbered like the target's group, which is not always the line being shot at ($A74B, $A766).

A throw of 3 is not a miss by itself. One more die is thrown, and a 1 spoils the shot however easy it was: rockets and explosives fail to detonate, and any other gun jams and loses its rounds ($A58D). That is one shot in 108.

A burst that hits lands 1 to 3 hits ($A0B3), and automatic fire one d4 of hits for every 4 rounds ($A98C). A burst spends 3 rounds and automatic fire all of them, hit or miss ($A02B, $A220).

Each hit rolls the weapon's dice, adds the attacker's luck bonus, and takes off the monster's armour roll, stopping at 0 ($A7DE, $A9AD). Anti-tank weapons roll 2 × their dice − the armour when the armour is no more than their dice, and the armour roll is not taken off ($A818, $A677). An explosive rolls its damage once, without luck, and every monster in the group takes it less its own armour roll ($A054, $AA90). The luck bonus has no floor: a hit it takes below 0 kills, as the throwing knife shows on Discoveries.

09 · Experience

A kill in melee earns double experience

A monster killed with fists or a hand weapon (a knife, a club, an ax, a chainsaw, or a proton ax) gives its killer twice the experience a shot gives for the same kill. The melee round adds the kill's worth twice, one call after the other. Guns, rockets, and explosives add it once.

The manual never spells this out. Its section on hand-to-hand combat (page 16) says the Rangers have an advantage over the citizens in hand-to-hand combat, and nothing about experience. Its section on promotion (page 11) says You're awarded experience points each time you accomplish a difficult task or win a difficult battle, and nothing about how a kill is made.

The two places the game pays for a kill, from the Source tab: the end of a melee attack and the end of a shot. Step runs one line of each. The kill is worth 40 here, as each Shambler Ghoul was in the emulator. There, three kills in melee each took their killer's experience from 0 to 80, 40 at each of the two calls.

The kill's worth comes from $A9E8: the base value in the monster's record, added once for each of its armour dice and once more. $03DD adds that number to the experience of the character whose turn it is, and leaves the number in place. The melee code calls it at $8163 and again at $8166, with nothing between that could change the number or the character. A shot calls it once ($A719), and so does an explosion for each monster it kills ($A08A).

The game prints the kill and not the experience, so nothing on the screen shows the second payment. Experience brings promotion, the first at 1,024 for a new Ranger ($7F1B). Each promotion gives two points to spend on attributes and a higher rank, and a skill improves with use only while its level is below the rank. Every kill made in melee counts twice toward the next promotion.

Players knew of the double payment. The Wasteland Ranger HQ-Grid's Underground says the Night Terror in Finster's mind maze gives the most experience in the game, and of course twice that if you beat it with melee weapons, and K. Ballou wrote there (14 May 1998) that killing it with a proton axe paid 64,000. On the C64 its record has a base of 32,000 and no armour dice ($42C1), so the kill is worth 32,000, more than seven times the next monster's, and a proton ax pays it twice. For a new Ranger, 64,000 is ten promotions at the next radio call; the ranks show why each later kill brings fewer.

10 · Learning

No fight can teach a weapon skill past level 7

A fight teaches as it goes: after an attack, the attacker may learn a level of the weapon's skill. How far a fight can teach depends on the target's armour, and no monster in the 42 maps has the armour to teach beyond level 7.

The lesson needs no check to pass ($AA35). It is the roll a passed check makes, at a difficulty of half the target's armour dice, rounded down, plus 1 ($9ED7, $0CA2). The level must be at least 1, below the attacker's rank, and below that difficulty, and then a d10 decides, as it does after a check. A blow in melee that misses teaches nothing ($8103-$810C), and neither does an empty gun.

The 26 that teach to level 7 are in the Guardian Citadel (21) and Base Cochise (5). Armour of 0 or 1 makes the difficulty 1, which teaches nothing, since the level must be at least 1 and below it.

Players found the ceiling by grinding against it. L. Stegner told the HQ-Grid's Underground (7 November 1999) that Brother Goliath, in the Guardian Citadel, can raise your guys up to level 7 in most fighting skills. His record has 13 armour dice, the most any monster has, so his lessons stop at 7 ($428E). Zoster (1 July 1999) took weapon skills up to 7 by firing on the Scorpitron in Las Vegas. On the C64 the Scorpitron has 10 armour dice, so its lessons stop at 6 ($531E).
