The ending of Wasteland needs three places: Las Vegas, the Guardian Citadel, and Base Cochise. The walk on this page reaches it in 1,748 moves on the game's own code, and it never sees Highpool, Quartz, Needles, or Darwin. On the way, Base Cochise is not on the map until a robot is rebuilt, a rope gets a party in without a pass and the door then keeps it in, and one party cannot set the self-destruct. 01 · The walk

The ending needs three places: Las Vegas, the Guardian Citadel, and Base Cochise

Four Rangers leave the Ranger Center, put a robot together under Las Vegas, take four keys from the Guardian Citadel, and set Base Cochise to destroy itself. The walk below does all of it in 66 legs and 1,748 moves: 1,732 steps, and 16 turns spent standing on a square to search a bag or work a station. Everything the party uses comes out of a bag on the way, through the bag's own prompts.

The walk, leg by leg

    Pick a leg, or walk it from the start. The path is drawn in yellow, from the white dot to the red one.

    The game's text appears here.

    The walk runs in this page on the game's own code. A step is the game's move routine ($ADD1), a Use is its Use routine ($8BEF), and a bag is searched, and the party split with Disband ($99EE), with the keys a player would press. The map is drawn as each leg leaves it, so a blown door stays open. Three things are not played. No wandering monsters turn up, a fight on the way is taken as won, and a throw of the dice is taken as passed. Lines in grey are the page's own notes.

    The ending is a program that the game loads when a countdown runs out. The countdown starts when four buttons are pressed in Base Cochise's reactor, and each button's station first wants a key of its own. All four keys are in the Guardian Citadel. Base Cochise itself is not on the map until Max, a robot that lies in pieces under Las Vegas, has been put together. So the game asks for three places, and the walk visits them in that order.

    The party is the four Rangers the game starts with, as they are: Hell Razor, Angela Deth, Thrasher, and KIT, with no skill added and no level gained. They carry what a new party carries. Each has a rope and a crowbar, and the walk uses all four ropes. It finds the rest: the Sonic key, the nine parts of Max, the four keys, a plasma coupler, and 12 charges of explosive (3 of plastic explosive, 5 of TNT, and 4 LAW rockets).

    It is a short way through the game's locks. It is not a way a new party could play, because the walk skips the fights and the dice. What it shows is what the game's code checks before it will run the ending, and what it never checks.

    02 · Max

    Base Cochise is not on the map until Max is rebuilt

    The square of desert where Base Cochise stands is an ordinary square at the start of the game. It becomes a way in when the party leaves the sewers by the door that Max opens. The only other road is the Guardians' helicopter, and the skill to fly it is taught in a place that the same door puts on the map.

    Base Cochise is at column 2, row 3 of the world map. As the map is stored, that square has a record that draws desert and does nothing ($4D4D). Two squares of the world map change it into the trail to the base, You come upon a little used trail which leads you to a place that you never knew existed before. Both are in the bottom-left corner, at (0,63) and (1,63), walled in by squares that nothing can enter, so no party can walk to them. The first is where the party lands when it leaves the lab under Las Vegas by the exit that appears when Max is complete ($4F21). The second is where the Guardian Citadel's helicopter sets down ($4F35).

    The helicopter is no way round Max. Flying it takes the Helicopter pilot skill, twice. None of the 14 people who can join the party has that skill, and one place teaches it: the Chopper Sim on the third level of the Sleeper Base, to a Ranger with an IQ of 19. The Sleeper Base is not on the map at the start either. The same square that reveals Base Cochise reveals it, at column 59, row 7. So every road to the ending goes through Max.

    Max is built at four stations in a lab on the lower level of the sewers. They take two servo motors for the legs, one for the arms, a fusion cell, a power converter, a servo motor, and a Rom board for the body, and an android head and a second Rom board for the head. All nine parts lie in the sewers, each one under a fight. The lab's door takes the Sonic key and nothing else, and so do the two hidden doors of the Temple of the Mushroom Cloud, which is the way down. The walk wins its Sonic key in a fight on the Las Vegas street. The temple's inner doors open to a name, and the game takes EINSTEIN or OPPENHEIMER.

    The sewers charge the party twice. Their doorways hold deadfall traps that take 4d6 of CON from every Ranger in the party, and the record asks for a skill that no Ranger can learn, so nothing avoids one. A trap is sprung once, though, and the walk has Thrasher disband and go through alone. The river of sewage is the short way down to the lab, and wading in it gives every Ranger Sewer rot ($3DD4). A Ranger with a disease does not heal with time. All four Rangers end the walk with it.

    03 · Base Cochise

    A rope and one explosive get a party into Base Cochise without a pass, and the door then keeps it in

    The front of Base Cochise is a gate and then a door that opens to Secpass B alone. The walk never has that pass. It goes over the dome wall on a rope, blows the cover off a weapons pod, and lets itself down inside. The door is as shut from the inside as from the outside.

    The dome wall takes a throw of Climb or a rope, and a slip costs 6d6 of CON on the wall's barbs. Inside the wall are the base's weapons pods. The weapons pod cover is jammed on the broken robots, so it cannot retract fully. Any explosive but a Mangler opens it, and a second rope lowers the party into the dome. From there a wall panel that sounds a bit hollow gives way to a crowbar, and behind it is the robot assembly level. The panel is a way past the base's computer, which the walk never meets.

    The game has two Secpass B cards, one in the Guardian Citadel's Inner Sanctum and one in Finster's mind maze, and the walk enters neither. Without a pass there is no leaving by the door. The inner door's record holds one key, the pass, and the door is that one record whichever side the party stands on ($3B2F). A party that came in over the wall leaves the way it came: up the rope it left hanging under the pod, which is a throw of Agility or Climb, and over the wall with its last rope.

    Below the assembly level are four tests in a row: oil pipes that one Ranger must cross alone, a mine field, a weather room, and a combat simulator. Each ends at a control panel that opens a vault door to the next, and the fourth door opens the way down to the reactor. The reactor's doors are the hardest locks on the walk. Six of them open to plastic explosive, a LAW rocket, a Sabot rocket, or an RPG-7, and otherwise to a throw of Strength at difficulty 30, a target of 165.

    04 · The self-destruct

    One party cannot set the self-destruct

    The reactor has a station in each corner, and each takes one key. When all four keys are in, pressing a station's button shuts a panel behind whoever pressed it. The buttons must be pressed red, yellow, green, blue, so the game needs a Ranger standing at each one.

    The red station takes the Blackstar key, the blue one the Nova key, the yellow one the Pulsar key, and the green one the Quasar key. A party that then steps onto a station is told Press button to activate safety procedure #1342-666. Any key but RETURN does it: You hear a hissing sound followed by a loud slam. You turn around and notice that the panel behind you has been sealed shut. All four rooms are shut at once. One party of four, shut in at one station, has no way to the other three or to the escape pod.

    The walk uses the game's Disband command. As each key goes in, three Rangers, then two, then one disband and step off the station, and the Ranger left behind stays on it. The last key leaves a Ranger at every corner. Four presses follow, and the fourth brings ALERT! Self destruction sequence activated. All personnel evacuate the base.

    Two parties are enough if one of them stays outside. A console in the south corridor, the Cylinder Closing System ($3E97), opens the panels again without undoing the buttons already pressed, so one Ranger can walk from station to station while the others work the console. It takes a throw of Electronics, Luck, or IQ. Run that way on the game's code, one Ranger pressed all four buttons and armed the base.

    The countdown is 240 counts ($82B5), and on the reactor level a step is one count. Each Ranger walks back to the escape pod in the middle of the level, where they join into one party again. The last arrives at count 151. The pod lands inside the dome, the rope and the wall take the party outside by count 165, and it is off the base's grounds at 195. A step on the world map is 16 counts, so the ending loads three steps later with all four Rangers out.

    05 · The map

    Most of the wasteland is not on the way

    The walk enters 12 of the game's 42 maps. No lock between the Ranger Center and the ending asks for anything from the others.

    The 12 are the world map, the Las Vegas street, the Temple of the Mushroom Cloud, the two levels of the sewers, the Guardian Citadel and its Outer Sanctum, and the five maps of Base Cochise. The walk never enters Highpool, the Agricultural Center, Quartz, Needles, Darwin Village, the Sleeper Base, or the Citadel's Inner Sanctum, and in Las Vegas it passes Spade's Casino and Faran Brygo's hideout by. It never goes near Darwin, so Finster is still in his base when Base Cochise blows up.

    Those places are where a real party grows strong enough for the last three. They hold the fights that raise a Ranger's rank, the libraries that sell skills, and the armour and guns that a new party lacks. The game does not check for any of it. It checks for one Sonic key, nine robot parts, four keys, a plasma coupler, four ropes, a crowbar, and 12 charges of explosive.

    06 · The dice

    The walk plays no fights and takes every throw as passed

    The walk wins 21 fights without fighting them, and where a square throws the dice it takes the throw as made. This is what the four starting Rangers would face on those squares with the game's real dice.

    A check has a difficulty, and its target is 15 plus five times the difficulty. The Ranger throws two dice, throws again on a double, and adds an attribute and four times the level of a skill ($0C29, $0D0A). A total on the dice under 5 always fails. The chances below come from the engine's own dice, thrown 200,000 times, and they are for one square.

    Every throw the walk leaned on. A walk that could go round a throw went round it, and a square that an item answers, such as the desert heat with a canteen, is left out.

    The tests under Base Cochise are where a new party would die. The oil pipes are 26 squares, each a throw that KIT passes about once in eight, and each slip costs 5d6 of CON. The weather room is crossed twice, and on its 12 squares each Ranger throws Luck against lightning for 3d6 of CON, wind for 1d6, and cold for 2d6. The mine field is kinder to a reader of this page than to a player. Its throws only show where the mines are, and the walk steps round them without being shown. A mine that goes off takes 12d6 of CON from every Ranger.

    The explosives depend on dice of another kind. The walk blows 12 doors and covers, and the count in most of its bags is thrown when the bag is opened. The six bags that hold explosives are sure to hold 8 charges between them. On this walk they held 22. A party that finds too few has a sure supply inside Base Cochise: a bag on the robot assembly level holds 10 LAW rockets and 10 RPG-7 rockets, with no dice in its count ($3CB9). It is 107 steps from where the hollow panel lets the party in, behind a door that takes Picklock, Strength, or an explosive.

    Two deadfall traps are the only things that hurt the party on the walk. The four Rangers finish with 24, 23, 17, and 19 CON out of 28, 27, 34, and 23, and with Sewer rot.

    07 · The locks

    Every lock on the walk, with every key its record takes

    The game keeps a lock as a record on a square: a list of skills, attributes, and items, and any one of them opens it. A skill or an attribute is a throw of the dice at the difficulty shown, and an item is sure. This is each record as it stood when the walk reached it.

The deadfall traps ask for skill 37, which no Ranger can have: it is the mark the game puts on Dan Citrine of Quartz, and the Party tab has the rest of that story.
