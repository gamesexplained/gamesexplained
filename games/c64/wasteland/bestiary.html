Digging silver in the mine shaft wakes three animals that are met no other way, and the eleven light plates in Base Cochise's reactor are its eleven kinds of wandering robot. Below them is every monster in the game, with the numbers the game keeps and never shows.

01 · The mine shaft

Secret: digging silver in the mine shaft wakes three animals that are met no other way

The mine shaft has sixteen kinds of wandering monster and picks from the first thirteen. Each time the party chips silver out of the vein in the north-west corner, the game adds the next kind to the choice: a Gecko, then a Skink, then a Shell Cougar. These three stand nowhere on the map, so a party that leaves that vein alone never sees them.

What can wander into the party

    The mine shaft (map 43) on the game's own code. A dig runs the routine the map runs when a Ranger has chipped silver out of a vein: $3E39 for the north-west vein and $3E2A for the east one. Walk takes 1,000 steps and makes the game's roll for a wandering group after each ($B015), with random bytes from this browser where the C64 reads its raster line and a timer. Each group is taken off the map again before the next step.

    The mine has two veins. One is in the north-west corner of the map and the other is on its east side, and both squares say There is a vein of silver here! Someone with a pickaxe could chip out a lot of money. A Ranger who passes a Metallurgy check and then uses a pick ax reads You get to work and chip out quite a bit of silver., and finds a bag holding 1 to 10 dollars. The map then fills the bag again ($3E48), so a vein can be dug for as long as the party likes.

    Every dig has a second effect that the game says nothing about. The mine's record has a count of the kinds of monster that a wandering group is picked from, and it starts at 13. A dig at the north-west vein adds 1 to the count, up to 16 ($3E39). Kinds 14, 15, and 16 are the Gecko, the Skink, and the Shell Cougar, and none of the groups that stand on the map are made of them. The Shell Cougar is the strongest animal in the mine, with 38 to 187 hit points and more armour than anything else there.

    A dig at the east vein makes every kind come more often. The mine rolls for a wandering group after each step, and a group appears on 1 roll in 70. Each dig at the east vein takes 2 off the 70, down to 2 ($3E2A). After 34 digs a group appears on every second step, on average.

    Neither change wears off. The game writes a map back to the disk when the party leaves it ($2856), and the two counts are part of the map. A mine that has been dug stays dangerous for the rest of the game.

    02 · Base Cochise's reactor

    Secret: the eleven light plates in Base Cochise's reactor are its eleven kinds of wandering robot

    A panel in the reactor level of Base Cochise has eleven light plates, and the game says only that several are lit. Each plate is one kind of robot that can wander into the party, and the level starts with six. A Ranger who passes a check at the panel turns a plate off and takes the strongest of those kinds away. A Ranger who fails turns another plate on, and a stronger robot joins the patrols.

    The eleven light plates

      The game's text appears here.

      The reactor level (map 20) on the game's own code. The two buttons run the routines that the panel's check leads to, $405C when it is passed and $404D when it is failed, and the text is the game's. Walk takes 1,000 steps with the game's roll for a wandering group after each ($B015).

      The panel is in the south of the level and reads There are eleven light plates on the panel and several are lit. A Ranger can try Alarm Disarm, Electronics, luck, or IQ on it. Success prints One of the light panels turned off. and failure prints Another light panel has turned on. The game never says what a plate is for.

      Each plate is one kind of robot. The reactor's record has the same count as the mine's, the number of kinds a wandering group is picked from, and it starts at 6: the Laser Pod Blaster, the Titanium Clawer, the Silver Strangler, the Silicon Sniper, the Hunter, and the Killer. A failure adds 1 to the count, up to 11 ($404D), and a success takes 1 off, down to 1 ($405C). The kinds come in a fixed order, so the plates that failures light are the VTOL Auto-fire Robot, the Threshing Crawler, the Steel Reaver, the Xenon Laser Cannon, and the Fusion Octotron.

      The order matters because the list runs from weak to strong. The Fusion Octotron has 354 to 487 hit points, and a kill is worth more experience than any other in the game except the Night Terror's. A party that wants to meet one on its walks has to fail at the panel five times. A party that wants a quiet reactor can pass five times and meet nothing on its walks but the Laser Pod Blaster.

      The level has a twelfth robot, the Particle Beam Gattlin, and the panel cannot reach it. Four of them stand guard at fixed places, and the count stops at 11.

      03 · Base Cochise's robot assembly floor

      Secret: wrecking a security monitor on the robot assembly floor brings more robots

      The walls of Base Cochise's robot assembly floor carry security monitor panels, and a Ranger with a crowbar can smash one. Every panel that is smashed makes the floor worse: more groups of robots at once, robots more often, or a stronger kind of robot. The cure is in the cages. Wrecking a command console behind a cage door undoes one panel's harm.

        04 · The Security Check Interval screen

        Bug: the reactor's screen says Interval Reduced when it has made the robots come less often

        A second panel in the reactor sets how often a wandering group of robots appears. Its two messages are the wrong way round. A Ranger who passes the check reads Interval Reduced., and the robots come less often than before. A Ranger who fails reads Interval Increased., and the robots come more often.

        The game's text appears here.

        The screen's two routines on the game's own code: $403C after a pass and $402B after a failure. This plate and the light plates above share one reactor, so a change made here shows in the walks there.

        The screen reads A message on a screen reads Security Check Interval. and takes the same four checks as the light plates. An interval is the time between two things, so a reduced interval ought to mean robots more often. The code does the opposite. After a pass the game adds 2 to the reactor's 1 in 70, which makes a group rarer, and prints The screen reads Interval Reduced. After a failure the game takes 2 off, and prints The screen reads Interval Increased.

        The result of each check is the fair one, and only the words are swapped. A pass helps the party, and a failure hurts it. The number can rise to 98 and fall to 2.

        05 · The roster

        Every monster in the wasteland, with the numbers the game never shows

        The game tells the player a monster's name and how many there are. Each monster also has eight bytes that set its hit points, its attack, its armour, and what a kill is worth. The table holds every one of them. Press a row for the monster's portrait and the maps where the party meets it.

        MonsterKindHit pointsAttackArmourExperienceMaps

        Every monster record of the game's 42 maps that has a number in it. A monster that has the same name and the same eight bytes on several maps is one row, and a monster whose numbers differ from map to map has a row for each set. Hit points are the lowest and the highest the game can roll. Attack is the damage of one blow or one shot. The portraits are drawn and moved by the code of the Pictures and text tab.

        The table shows a few things at a glance when it is sorted. By hit points, the Night Terror of Finster's mind stands alone at the top with up to 40,000, and the Finster Leviathan beside it has 1,500. By experience, the list under the Night Terror is led by the robots of Base Cochise. By the hardest blow, the same robots lead, with one surprise among them: the barkeep of Spade's Casino, of whom the game says Gus, the happy barkeep, wouldn't hurt a fly.

        06 · Walking away

        Secret: a monster that the party walks away from gets new hit points when they come back

        The game does not remember how hurt a monster is once the party is out of its reach. When the party comes back, every monster that is still alive gets a fresh roll of hit points. A wounded monster is whole again, and a monster that rolled high the first time may roll low the second. The dead stay dead.

        The Pit Ghoul's hit points

        not met

          The game's text appears here.

          The Pit Ghoul of the waste pit in Needles (map 31) and four Rangers with 250 CON each, on the game's own code. Walking up runs the scan that finds the monster and the routine that gives it hit points ($94CC, $936E). A round is the game's battle round with every Ranger attacking ($B867). Walking away puts the party at the far end of the map and runs the scan again. The game never shows a monster's hit points; the plate reads them from the game's memory.

          A group of monsters lives in two places. The map holds the lasting part: where the group stands, what its monsters are, and how many of each are left. The hit points are kept apart, in a table that has room for the groups the party is dealing with at the moment ($EE00).

          The table is cleared of a group as soon as the party cannot reach it. Before every round the game scans the squares around the party for groups ($94CC). A group counts when the party can see its square and is within the distance written in the group's record. A group in the table that the scan did not find is dropped, and its place is free for another.

          When the scan finds the same group later, the game treats it as new. The routine that fills the table rolls hit points for every monster the map says is alive ($936E). The number of monsters was written back to the map after each round of the fight ($B9F2), so the ones the party killed are not rolled again.

          For most monsters the fresh roll changes little. For a monster with a wide range it changes the fight. The Pit Ghoul rolls anything from 64 to 318, and a party that has met a strong one can walk off and meet a weak one in the same pit.

          07 · Monsters that start out running

          Secret: a lone brawler often runs away before anyone has touched it

          A monster that fights hand to hand runs when its hit points fall below half of a number in its record. The roll that gives a monster its hit points can come out below that half. So a monster that is met alone starts one fight in every four or five already beaten, and spends it running. Animals are the exception: they never run.

          This Pit Ghoul

          not met

          The game's text appears here.

          The same Pit Ghoul, met afresh each time. The party walks up, the game rolls the Ghoul's hit points and plans its move ($83D7), and the Rangers stand and evade for a round. A Pit Ghoul runs when it has fewer than 127 hit points, half of the 255 in its record.

          The number in the record does two jobs. A monster's hit points are a quarter of the number plus a roll from 1 up to the whole of it ($9483). The Pit Ghoul's number is 255, so a Ghoul has 63 hit points plus a roll of 1 to 255. The same number is the mark for running. Before each round the game takes the average hit points of a line of monsters, and when the average is below half the number, the line runs ($8448).

          The two jobs overlap. A roll in the lowest quarter of its range gives fewer hit points than half the number, and the monster runs from the first round. The Pit Ghoul runs on a roll of 1 to 63, which is 24.7% of its rolls. A monster that is running makes no attack. It moves away from the party, and the game prints The Pit Ghoul runs away.

          A group is safer from this than a monster alone, because the game looks at the average of a line. In a line of ten, the weak rolls and the strong ones even out, and the average of a fresh line almost never falls below half.

          Animals are left out of the test altogether ($8448). A rat or a lizard fights until it dies, whatever it rolled. Monsters with guns follow a rule of their own, which is on the Dice and combat tab.

          08 · The Night Terror

          Bug: a Night Terror with more than 32,767 hit points runs away as if it had none

          The Night Terror of Finster's mind has more hit points than anything else in the game: from 8,256 to 40,000. The strongest ones run away. The game's sum for a monster's nerve cannot hold a number above 32,767, and counts a Night Terror that has more as having 0. With the weak rolls that run under the usual rule, nearly half of all Night Terrors turn and run once the fight starts.

          This Night Terror

          not met

          The game's text appears here.

          The Night Terror of Finster's mind (map 23) and four Rangers, on the game's own code. Attack 100 meets a fresh Night Terror each time, has the Rangers attack it for one round, and reads the plan the game makes for the next round ($83D7).

          The Night Terror's number is 32,000, far above any other monster's. Its hit points are a quarter of that, 8,000, plus 256 times a roll of 1 to 125. That gives 125 different totals from 8,256 to 40,000, in steps of 256.

          The Night Terror runs under the usual rule when it has fewer than 16,000 hit points, which is 31 of the 125 rolls. The other rule is the bug. To take a line's average, the game adds up its monsters' hit points with a routine made for adding and subtracting ($86E9). That routine reads a number above 32,767 as a negative one, and holds a sum that would go below 0 at 0 ($079D). So a Night Terror with 32,768 hit points or more has an average of 0, and 0 is below 16,000. That is another 29 of the 125 rolls. Together 60 rolls in 125 make a Night Terror that runs: 48%.

          The Night Terror is also peaceful until it is attacked. Its meeting is marked as peaceful in the map, and the game plans no move for a peaceful group. A party that only waits beside it is never struck. The first Ranger to attack makes it hostile, and from the next round the Night Terror either fights or runs.

          A kill is worth 32,000 experience, and twice that in melee. A Night Terror with 9,000 hit points pays the same as one with 32,000.

          09 · Never met

          Monsters and meetings that are on the disk and never reach the player

          The maps hold more than a player can find. Some monsters have a full record and stand nowhere. Some meetings are written out, with their monsters and their opening line, on squares that the map never makes. Two portraits belong to no monster at all.

          10 · A monster's record

          How eight bytes make a monster

          Every map carries its own list of monsters, and a monster is eight bytes and a name. The same name can have different numbers on different maps, which is why the Threshing Crawler of the reactor is a harder fight than the one on the assembly floor.

          The eight bytes of a monster's record, as the game reads them ($9EA4 and the routines after it), with the Pit Ghoul's as an example: 64 to 318 hit points, three attacks a round of 13 to 38 damage, and 1,020 experience.

          A name is stored once and bent into its plural by the game. The text holds the part that both forms share, then the singular ending, then the plural one: Gunm, a, e, and n make Gunman and Gunmen. Most plurals just add an s, and a few do more: one Policeman and two Police, one Juvenile and two Juvies, one Rodento Grosso and two Rodento Grossi. The Woman in the Las Vegas jail has the plural Womans, which the game never prints, because she is one of the monsters with no meeting.

          The hit point roll explains two oddities in the table. A monster's lowest and highest totals are far apart, because the roll runs up to the whole number and the fixed part is only a quarter of it. And the reactor's Threshing Crawler has 322 to 325 hit points, a range of four. Its number is 260, which is a high byte of 1 and a low byte of 4. The assembly floor gives the same robot the number 235, and 59 to 293 hit points.
