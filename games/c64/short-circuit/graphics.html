01 · The loading picture

Number 5 comes alive a row of cells at a time

While it loads, the cassette shows the picture the game later uses as its title: lightning striking Number 5 on a hilltop at sunset, under the words "Something wonderful has happened... No.5 is alive." On a white screen it fills in a cell at a time, left to right along each row of cells and down the screen, over 34 seconds, while Martin Galway's Ocean loading tune plays (Music).

The game's own bytes, from the listing: the bitmap at $6000-$7F3F, its screen colours at $5C00-$5FE7 and the colour RAM that the title copies from $8548, all three the same byte for byte as the cassette loads them. The bitmap appears at the tape's own pace, where it had got to every fifth of a second, measured in VICE.

The screen colours and the colour RAM are in place when the screen comes on, so each cell comes in finished. The picture is a multicolour bitmap, 8,000 bytes, read in the order it lies in memory: the eight rows of an 8 by 8 cell together, forty cells across, then the next row of cells down. It arrives at about 230 bytes a second, from 68 seconds into the tape to 102.

The game keeps the picture. In the attract mode it comes round between the credits and the high-score table (How it works).
