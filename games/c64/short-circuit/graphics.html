01 · The loading picture

Number 5 comes alive a row of cells at a time

While it loads, the cassette shows the picture the game later uses as its title: lightning striking Number 5 on a hilltop at sunset, under the words "Something wonderful has happened... No.5 is alive." On a white screen it fills in a cell at a time, left to right along each row of cells and down the screen, over 34 seconds, while Martin Galway's Ocean loading tune plays (Music).

The game's own bytes, from the listing: the bitmap at $6000-$7F3F, its screen colours at $5C00-$5FE7 and the colour RAM that the title copies from $8548, all three the same byte for byte as the cassette loads them. The bitmap appears at the tape's own pace, where it had got to every fifth of a second, measured in VICE.

The screen colours and the colour RAM are in place when the screen comes on, so each cell comes in finished. The picture is a multicolour bitmap, 8,000 bytes, read in the order it lies in memory: the eight rows of an 8 by 8 cell together, forty cells across, then the next row of cells down. It arrives at about 230 bytes a second, from 68 seconds into the tape to 102.

The game keeps the picture. In the attract mode it comes round between the credits and the high-score table (How it works).

02 · The credits screen

Chrome letters from one colour register

The attract mode opens on the credits: the SHORT CIRCUIT logo and three lines of names, in letters that shine like chrome. The screen is text, not a picture. It is a screen of characters at $8000 in video bank 2, drawn from a character set at $8800 whose characters $80 to $F1 ($8C00-$8F8F) are the logo's and the names' pieces. The shine comes from the raster interrupt: on 24 lines between 93 and 145 it writes a new colour into $D023, the third shared colour of multicolour text, so every letter is striped by the same bands as the beam goes down.

One frame recorded in VICE with every write to the video chip, and drawn here by replaying them line by line; the drawing matched VICE's own picture in all 104,448 pixels. $9637 sets the screen up: multicolour text ($D016 = $18), colour RAM filled with 9 and a black border. The background register is light blue, but none of it shows: every empty cell holds character $F1, whose eight bytes are all $55, every pixel the first shared colour, $D022, which is black. The band colours are the table at $8439-$8447, which the interrupt steps through and rotates as the screen stays up, so the stripes run down the letters.

03 · Number 5

Two sprites, eight facings

Number 5 is two multicolour sprites, one above the other: an upper half with the head and arms, and a lower half with the body and the tracks. The game has a pair for each of eight facings, and Number 5 turns through them one step at a time towards where the stick points, a step every fourth frame. While he drives, the upper half stays still and the lower half swaps between two shapes, so the tracks roll.

Drawn from the listing. The eight pairs are the table at $1C60, which $1AF0 writes into the sprite pointers while he turns. The walk uses one four-byte table a facing, at $1C70-$1C8F: two upper halves, which are always the same shape, and two lower halves, swapped every fourth frame by $1B32. Colours as the game sets them: cyan (colour 3), with dark grey and black shared by every sprite ($D025 and $D026, set once at $956C).

Part two draws Number 5 from other shapes. He jumps with $A0 and $A1 facing right and $A2 and $A3 facing left ($2C01), ducks with the low upper halves $F5 and $F7 over the lower halves in $2DBE, and walks with the pairs $AE/$AF and $B0/$B1 ($3ABD). When the ground swallows him, $3950 copies his two shapes into the empty slots $B2 and $B3 and moves them down a row at a time, so he sinks inside his own outline.

Left to right: the two jumps, the duck (both upper halves over the first lower half), and the two walking pairs.

04 · Guards, a robot and a truck

Part one's other sprites

The guards in the laboratory are two sprites as well: a body with a gun, which only changes with the way the guard faces, over legs that walk through four shapes. A guard walking left uses $F0 over $F1-$F4, one walking right $E1 over $E2-$E5 ($23F8). The enemy robot is Number 5 again: $2151 takes its shapes from the same table at $1C60.

Left: the two walking guards, animated, and the pair $EC/$ED that $2312 sets just before a capture, unless the guard's sprite stands from 0 to 15 lines below Number 5's. Right: the eight sprites as $2498 sets them up when part one starts, at their positions in $24B9, with their shapes and colours: the truck, four sprites ($C8-$CB), a guard standing ($E6/$E7) and Number 5 ($C2/$C6). Under it, the wheel $D9 that $257E gives sprite 3. Guards in colour 8, orange.

05 · Part two

Fourteen things in the way

Part two's map gives each of its 256 columns an event number ($896E). When the scroll reaches a column, $8936 starts that event's creature through the table at $8A6E. Fifteen numbers have a routine; number 10 has none, and no column asks for it. Each creature runs through its own short list of shapes in $8F9A-$8FC7, and where one is hit, a flash plays: the nine frames at $8F91-$8F99, one every fourth pass, its colour flipping between yellow and red ($3BF1).

Each creature as the game draws it, in its own colour. The shapes and colours were taken from each event started on the kit's model of the machine, from a snapshot of part two with the scroll column set to a column of that event.

06 · The whole bank

All 128 shapes

Every sprite shape is in one place: $E000-$FFFF, 64 bytes each, sprite pointers $80 to $FF in video bank 3. Hover or tap a shape to see who uses it. For five of them ($EB, $EE, $EF, $FA and $FB) no instruction or table that sets a sprite pointer was found to name them; two are clouds of dots and two are pieces of a snake. The last slot, $FF, is no picture: its 64 bytes, $FFC0-$FFFF, run into the processor's vectors at $FFFA, and nothing points a sprite at it.

Shapes drawn in cyan with the game's shared dark grey and black.
