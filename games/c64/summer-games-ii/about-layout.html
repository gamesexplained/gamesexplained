About this page

The copy studied

The pages describe US Gold's release for European machines on two disk sides, as G64 images. Side 1 holds the boot file, the fast loader, the menu, the title, both ceremonies, and four events; side 2 holds the other four events, copies of the menu and the closing ceremony, and the first Summer Games' two swimming events. Each of these fourteen parts loads on its own through the game's loader, and each has its own listing on the Source tab, built from memory saved as the part started, or for the swimming events during a race. The world records saved on the copy studied were set by its earlier owners.

The eight other images of the game in the contributor's set were compared. Epyx's American disk and a second copy of it keep their events outside the disk's directory, where the kit's disk reader does not reach them; their boot file is the same, byte for byte, as US Gold's. A three-disk US Gold set stores the events unpacked under other file names. Every page here describes the two-disk set.

No game image is hosted here.

Original game credits

The title picture names Chuck Sommerville, Jon Leupp, Kevin Norman, Michael Kosaka, Larry Clague, and Scott Nelson, with © EPYX INC. 1985. Wikipedia credits the six as designers, and the music to Randy Glover and Larry Clague.1 Epyx published the game in North America and US Gold in Europe.

Memory footprint

Every byte of the C64's 64 KB, one pixel each, 512 to a row. Lit pixels are code, graphics, sound, text, tables and variables; dim areas are screen and working memory.
