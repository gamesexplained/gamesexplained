Commodore 64 · 1988 · Elite Systems

Ikari Warriors: gameplay

How the road moves, what fire does when tapped and when held, and what each token, tank, and mine does, read from the game's code.

01 · The road

The screen moves only when you do

The road scrolls up one pixel a frame while every living player is in the top four fifths of the screen, above raster line 188 ($BC, checked in lower_panel_irq). Hang back near the bottom and the scrolling stops.

The soldiers come from a list of 369 entries at route_list, each a position along the road and the group of soldiers that appears there. spawn_from_route starts a group only once the scroll has passed its position, so while nobody moves forward, no new soldiers arrive. The same list places the mines, and a list of twelve at tank_list places the empty tanks.

The road itself is drawn from a script of 1,891 rows, which the Maps page runs from start to finish.

02 · Fire

Tap to shoot, hold to throw

player_fire reads the fire button every second frame. The moment it goes down, a bullet leaves at once, one of the 99 in the panel. Keep holding, and at the ninth count, eighteen frames after the press, a player on foot throws a grenade. The same long hold does two other jobs: beside an empty tank it climbs in, and inside a tank it climbs out.

A hold of 16 frames fires one bullet only; holds of 20 and 30 frames fire a bullet and throw a grenade.6 A grenade flies a set distance for the way you face, from the table at grenade_range, and goes off where it lands. A wall stops a grenade, which falls where it meets it, unless the cyan token lets shots through; a grenade thrown with that token kills the first soldier it meets outright and flies on (update_grenade_hits). The test reads player one's token for both players, a slip in the code.

03 · Two players

Your partner's bullets hit you too

In a two-player game every bullet is tested against both players, and only the one who fired it is spared (update_bullet_hits). A grenade's blast kills every player on foot inside it except the one who threw it (blast_hits). Two players have to keep out of each other's line of fire.

04 · Tokens

Seven tokens, given in turn

Every fourth soldier spawn_from_route starts carries a token. Which one comes next is fixed: the list at item_cycle hands them out in the same order every game. A carrier you kill drops its token where it falls (drop_item), and walking over it calls pick_up_item.

OrderTokenWhat it does

Each token's colour comes from item_colours, and the swatches here are those colours. A token is drawn in multicolour, so only the low three bits of each colour show. The C64-Wiki lists the same colours.2 Every power-up is taken away when you lose a life (init_player).

An eighth token hides in pick_up_item: a purple one worth 800 points that the list never hands out. Discoveries has the details.

05 · Tanks

Tanks: proof against bullets, not against grenades

Twelve empty tanks wait along the road, each with a flashing IN above it. Hold fire beside one to climb in. A player in a tank cannot be hit by bullets (update_bullet_hits), runs soldiers over for 100 points each (check_trampling), and fires shells instead of bullets: a shell flies like a grenade and blows up where it lands, so it destroys what bullets cannot. Water stops a tank (move_player).

Fuel drains every six frames (update_fuel). When it runs out, or when a grenade hits the tank, a countdown of 100 frames starts, two seconds with a warning sound, and then the tank blows up with whoever is inside. Hold fire to climb out in time. A black token fills the tank again.

06 · Mines

Mines that show themselves just in time

A mine is invisible. update_mines looks at each one every eight frames, and when a player comes within 16 pixels across and 32 down it appears, flashing between the two colours at mine_flash_colours and beeping. The mine goes off when a player is within 5 pixels across and 12 down, so there is just time to step around it.

07 · Water

Water

A character whose colour has a low nibble of $E is water (char_colours). A player on foot with both feet in it is slowed every second frame (move_player). A tank cannot enter it at all. A soldier in water dives under, drawn with frame $6F, and bullets pass over him (update_bullet_hits).

08 · Enemies

Soldiers, guns and helicopters

One bullet kills a soldier, for 100 points. A player on foot who walks into a soldier dies (check_trampling); a player in a tank runs him over. A soldier in water dives under it, and a bullet passes over him. Enemy tanks and other vehicles, and the helicopters, ignore bullets: only a grenade's blast, or a shell's, destroys them (blast_hits).

The guns along the road come in two kinds (update_guns). A turret, drawn in characters, turns to face the nearer player below it, left, down, or right, redrawing its barrel in the character set as it turns (turret_left_gfx, turret_right_gfx), and fires single bullets. A mortar pit checks every 30 frames for a player below it, within 90 pixels, and fires three shells in a spread. A helicopter flies down the screen when the road's script calls for one (map_special), and is worth 1,000 points brought down.

09 · Score

Points and extra lives

The score is kept in hundreds of points, in decimal digits (add_score). Each player starts with five lives, and the same routine gives an extra one at 20,000 points, at 50,000, and at every 50,000 after that.

10 · The end

The end of the road

Twelve rows before the road ends, its script puts eight figures on the screen (ending_setup). At the last row it hands over to route_finished, which gives each player with lives left 100,000 points, in twenty steps of 5,000, and prints the message at ending_text: "Congratulations, you have rescved Colonel Cook and received 100,000 pts. Continue to fight hard and bring freedom to the people you protect." The spelling is the game's own (Discoveries).

A top-ten score at the end of a game is entered in the high-score table with a name of nine letters (enter_high_score).
