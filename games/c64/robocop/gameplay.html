Commodore 64 · 1988 · Data East

RoboCop: gameplay

Nine stages, each against its own clock, with one bar of power to last the whole game.

01 · The stages

Nine stages in three loads

The game loads from disk three times. Each load is a level of the game, and holds the code, maps, and pictures of its stages. Six stages are walked, with RoboCop shooting his way to the end; three use a crosshair or the photofit.

The clocks are set by $AC8B in level one, and by tables in each of the other loads. The end of each stage is checked in its main loop.

Level one starts from the title screen; levels two and three load when the level before them ends, after a screen that asks for the disk and a press of fire. The score goes with the player from one load to the next, kept in three bytes at $0100, below the stack, while the rest of memory is loaded over.

02 · Power

One power bar for the whole game

RoboCop has no lives. His power is a number from 0 to 24, shown in the status panel as a bar of six characters, each of four units. Damage does not take effect at once. A hit adds to a store of damage waiting, and the game deals out one unit of it each time its slower jobs run, which is one frame in six ($ACD1). Baby food goes into a store of its own. While there is food in store, a unit of damage takes a unit of food instead of a unit of power, and the store also gives power back one unit at a time, up to the full 24 ($ACFB). So the bar drains and fills a character at a time. At 0 RoboCop dies and the game is over.

Walking into an enemy in levels two and three costs six units at once. On stage nine, a shot that hits the hostage puts 24 units of damage in the store, which is all the power there is.

03 · The clock

A second on the clock is four-fifths of a second

Each stage has a clock in minutes and seconds. RoboCop dies when it reaches 0:00, except on the hostage stage, where at 0:00 the gunman kills the hostage instead, and on the photofit, where it is the end of the stage. The clock counts frames: it takes a second off every 48 frames ($ABFC). On a European machine 48 frames would be just under a second. This disk is the American version, and an American machine shows about 60 frames a second, so each second on the clock lasts 0.8 of a real second, and stage one's 2:31 is about two minutes.

When a stage ends, the time left is turned into points: a second every four frames. Level one pays 100 points a second ($ADB9). Levels two and three pay 300 ($B7AD), a difference the published guides, which give 100, do not mention.

05 · Pickups

Every fifth kill leaves a pickup

A run of kills is counted, and every fifth enemy killed drops a pickup instead of disappearing. It stays for a while and then vanishes. What it gives is chosen by a table of routines ($9CEB in level one): baby food, which adds 8 units to the store; three-way shots, which fire three bullets in a spread; and better bullets for 25 shots, some of which pass through enemies. A better bullet does not replace one that is better still, but the 25 shots are given either way.

In level three an armoured carrier leaves the Cobra cannon ($A688). It fires bullet type 6, of strength 51, which goes through enemies, and has eight shots.

06 · Hostage stages

Shooting past a hostage

On stages two and nine RoboCop does not move. The joystick moves a crosshair, and fire shoots where it points. On stage two a gunman walks back and forth behind his hostage, following a list of moves, and needs 20 hits; if the clock runs out first, the hostage dies, RoboCop loses 14 units of power, and the game goes on to stage three.

On stage nine Dick Jones walks with OCP's president. Which of the two a shot hits depends on the crosshair's row and column against tables of their shapes ($AA98). Eight hits on Jones end the game; one on the president ends RoboCop.

07 · The photofit

Building a face

Stage four is a puzzle against a 40-second clock. The player puts together a face from six features, each with several choices, to match the suspect's. A wrong answer, MISMATCH, turns the background red. A match gives the name of Murphy's killer; at 0:00 the game says UNSUCCESSFUL MATCH, puts eight units of damage in RoboCop's store, and goes on to stage five anyway.

08 · Stairs and ED-209

Stairs, and a robot that can only be punched

Stages five and seven have stairs. RoboCop climbs them by pushing the joystick diagonally when he stands at the right place at the foot or the top of a flight, and on the stairs fire and down shoots downward. Level three carries a copy of level two's stair code, but nothing in level three calls it, so stage eight has no stairs (Discoveries).

On stage six RoboCop meets ED-209 and Dick Jones. His programming will not let him fire at either, so fire and down throws a punch instead. Six punches on ED-209 destroy it. In level three an ED-209 returns as an enemy that can be shot: it has 50 hit points, scores 5,000, and while it is in view the street does not scroll when RoboCop walks left.
