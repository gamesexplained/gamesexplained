1989 · Activision · Maps / levels

Power Drift

Five courses of five stages. Each stage is a few bytes in a course record: a list of track pieces, a list of roadside objects, two colours, two horizon pictures, and whether the track starts high off the ground.

01 · Every stage

A stage is a list of pieces and a list of scenery

Each of the 25 stages, drawn from its course record: its horizon, the pieces of one lap, and the objects along the road.

‹›

The horizon

The stage's two horizon pictures, the whole 80 columns of each, as draw_horizon_rows copies them at the start of a stage: the upper one with its bottom row on screen row 9, the lower one on row 15, in the stage's sky and ground colours and its view multicolour. A bend scrolls them sideways a column a step, and a hill moves them up and down. Their characters $F1-$FE are the set setup_stage copies in for the stage: from $B785 when the lower picture is the one at $A571, otherwise from $B685.

One lap

The stage's piece list, each piece as long as the number of steps its routine runs for, with the bend above it: 9 notches either way from straight, left upward. A lap is about 400 steps; the list starts again from the top at its end.

Along the road

Every kind of object the stage's scenery list names, in the order it first appears, drawn at distance 6 with the block of character shapes the list has loaded by then, in the stage's colours.

A course record (course_a and the four after it) holds two bytes for each stage in six groups: the piece list, the scenery list, a raised-track count with the view's multicolour, the sky and ground colours, and the two horizon pictures. setup_stage reads them at the start. The pieces are described on the How it works tab; the scenery list is a run of streams, each four records of an object on the left, one over the road and one on the right, with now and then a block of character shapes to copy into the race set before the next.

A raised-track count above 0 at the start means the stage begins high off the ground, where a kart that leaves the road falls. Climbs add to the count and the pieces that level out take from it.
