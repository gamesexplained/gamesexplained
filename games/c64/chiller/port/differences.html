• The loading. There is none: the page starts where the loader hands over, at the title.
  • The KERNAL. Chiller keeps the C64's ROM switched in and uses three pieces of it: the 50 Hz timer interrupt's own work (the clock and the keyboard scan), the routine that prints a character, which draws the cards between levels, and the one that places the cursor. The page does not carry the ROM. It has those three written out in JavaScript from what they do to memory, and they were checked against the game's run with the real ROM.
  • The screen. The page draws the picture once a frame from the memory and the video chip's registers as they stand. Chiller changes nothing in the middle of a frame, so this is the picture the C64 shows.
  • Sprite collisions. The game learns that a ghost has touched you, or that you have walked into a wall, from the video chip's collision registers, which the chip sets as it draws the line where two things overlap. The page works them out line by line on the same clock, from the sprites and characters in memory.
  • The sound. The game's writes to the sound chip go to the site's model of the SID, with the filter the game sets on the 6581's curve as the reSID emulator measured it from one chip. Real 6581s differ from one another, and they distort, which the model does not.
  • The interrupt. On the C64 the music and the keyboard scan can interrupt the game between any two instructions; on the page they run between the port's steps. The game reads three bytes the interrupt keeps: the clock, the key held and the SHIFT keys. Where the two runs could read different values, the check gave the port what the game read (section 01). In play, a key or a clock tick can be seen a few hundred cycles later than on a C64, which no one can tell.
  • Start on. The level buttons are the page's own. They start a new game and finish each level before the one chosen, in the game's own code, as if the crosses had all been taken, so the scores, lives and energy are those of a new game.
  • The cheats. They are the page's own, not the game's. Running out of energy is the only way Chiller ends, so with Endless energy on the game goes on for ever. The toadstools still poison you with Ghosts don't hurt on; with both on, they only flash the border.
  • The keys. The arrows or Z and C walk and up or Space jumps, as the joystick in port 2 does. Shift jumps and ? or / swaps the boy and the girl, as it does on the C64, and fire or any key starts a game. On a C64, SHIFT and C= pressed together switch the character set and scramble the picture, because the game leaves the KERNAL's switch unlocked; the page has no C= key, so that cannot happen.
