Before Codemasters, the Darling brothers made a £1.99 C64 game whose "Thriller" tune was replaced after a legal dispute.

01 · The journey

To the haunted house and home again

In Chiller, a boy crosses a forest, a cinema, a ghetto and a graveyard to reach his girlfriend in the haunted house. He collects five blue crosses on each screen. On the way home the girl joins him, and together they must collect the blue and red crosses through the same places in reverse.

The return journey remembers what happened on the way out. Mushrooms eaten and ledges broken are still gone. Take the final cross and the game starts again in the forest with your score intact. There is no ending screen.

02 · The makers

A £1.99 Mastertronic game

Mastertronic published Chiller in 1984 as a £1.99 cassette game. The game's own title screen says it was programmed by David and Richard Darling. Games That Weren't attributes the code to Richard, graphics to David and sound to David Dunn. Those more specific roles come from that archive, rather than from the title screen.

The Darling brothers later established Codemasters. Its company history dates the founding to 1986.

03 · Reception

A budget game reviewers noticed

Price was part of the story from the start. In Personal Computer News, 15 September 1984, Bryan Skinner called the £1.99 game "a winner". He argued that if Mastertronic kept making games of this standard, publishers charging £6 to £10 would face pressure.

Fraser Marshall was more measured in Personal Computer Games, December 1984, p. 72. He compared the cross collecting with Manic Miner, but judged Chiller to be "different enough from that classic to provide many hours of enjoyment". He also found the graphics occasionally indistinct and admitted he never got past the cinema.

The Mastertronic Collectors Archive's sales table lists 121,874 copies for the C64 release, placing it fourth among the original games in its title-by-format table. The archive does not separate copies with the first tune from those with its replacement.

04 · The two releases

The tune that had to change

The first release played David Dunn's rendition of Michael Jackson's Thriller during the game. Reports from November 1984 describe a dispute over the music. Mastertronic issued a later version with a different in-play tune.

The copy examined for this site is the first version: its music data matches the documented first-version transcription byte for byte. That establishes which version the analysis covers. On the Music tab, you can listen to both tunes through a port of the game's own sound driver and inspect the small change to the driver itself.

05 · Related games

A namesake and later Chiller games

Chiller is also the title of Exidy's 1986 arcade light-gun game, later released for the NES. It is a different game from Mastertronic's 1984 C64 platformer covered here.

A related game is Chiller 2, an unofficial C64 sequel by aNdy with twelve single-screen levels. The creator says one of the Darling brothers gave permission for it. The download includes the sequel's 6502 source code.

In comments on the Chiller 2 page, aNdy says he is working on a further sequel called CHILL3R. As of 30 September 2026, it is not listed for download on his itch.io page; we have not verified a public release elsewhere.

06 · What the code keeps

A game with traces of other ideas

Normal play gives you one energy bar and no way to kill enemies. The code still contains routines for lives, enemy deaths and points for kills. Some unused score data calls the pickups SILVER CROSSES, and a GAME OVER menu prompt is read but never drawn.
