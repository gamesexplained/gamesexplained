A boy crosses five haunted levels, collecting magic crosses, to rescue his girlfriend, then the two of them play the same five levels back home. Finish the way home and the game starts again in the forest: there is no ending.

01 · The screen

Let's play: every screen, rebuilt from its own 128 bytes

Each of Chiller's ten screens is set up from its own 128 bytes of settings: where to find the play area, character set and colours, and where to put the two players, the five enemies and the ten crosses. The picture below is drawn by this page from those bytes. Pick a screen, and switch on the tiles the boy can stand on, the ledges that crumble and the pick-ups.

The ten screens' settings sit at $7000-$757F. setup_screen ($5E19) reads them field by field, in the order below. The first five levels keep their graphics at $8000-$B1FF, one $A00 block each, and the way back borrows them.

02 · The crosses

Blue for him, red for her

Every cross is the same character, tile $57. The only thing that makes it the boy's or the girl's is its colour. Pick a character and a cross colour to see what cross_touch does.

touches a

cross_touch ($7F50) reads the colour under the cross and tests one bit. Light blue is 14 (%1110) and light red is 10 (%1010), so bit 2 tells them apart. A match replaces the cross with a space and counts it off the MAGIC CROSSES counter (cross_count, $5B5E). The crosses flash because every 40th pass colour_cycle ($7F80) takes 8 off each cross's colour and then puts it back.

Going out, only the boy plays, and each screen needs its five blue crosses. On the way back the girl comes too, and all ten are needed. Fire, or the ? key, swaps who you control. switch_allowed ($7280) refuses before the first way-back screen, as the inlay says. The border shows who is playing: blue for the boy, light red for the girl.

A swap does not move anyone. player_swap exchanges the two sprites, positions and all, so whoever you control is always sprite 0. The one left behind stands still, and still loses energy when an enemy touches it (girl_touch, $CD9D).

03 · The end

Get the boy and the girl home, and the game starts again in the forest

Take the last cross on the way home and Chiller loads the forest again, with its five crosses back and your score kept. There is no ending: no screen, no message, and nothing in the code that counts how many times round you have been.

When a level's last cross is taken, crosses_left ($7F00) reads the next level's number from the level's settings and passes it to next_screen ($7680). After the last level, the forest on the way home, the number is $12, and the game swaps it for $FE, which next_screen takes as the forest going out. Just before that are three NOPs ($7F06-$7F08), the size of one subroutine call. That hints at something taken out, perhaps an ending, but nothing in the image says what.

A commenter on Lemon64 remembers Chiller as impossible to finish because one cross was out of reach, and says a later crack fixed it. In this release every cross can be taken. The lowest three, in the graveyard, the ghetto and the cinema on the way home, sit in the bottom two rows, and the boy takes each of them by walking left along the ground. This was checked on the game's own code. The boy can never stand lower than row 22 (move_sprite stops him at Y $E3), but the game looks for a cross in the cell below or beside him, not the one he is in (probe_offset, $5145), so from row 22 it still reaches rows 23 and 24.

04 · The world

To the haunted house and home again

The boy works through five haunted levels, the forest, the cinema, the ghetto, the graveyard and the haunted house, to reach his girlfriend. He cannot leave a level until he has collected its five blue crosses. After the haunted house the two of them head home through the same five levels in reverse. The game reuses each level exactly as he left it: a mushroom he ate, a cross he took and a ledge that crumbled under him are all still gone.

The way home has no levels of its own: it reuses the first five. When the boy has taken a level's crosses and moves on, next_screen ($7680) saves screen rows 1 to 24, and on the way home the game reads that copy back. The forest goes to $7800. The other four have no room left in ordinary memory, so they go into the RAM hidden under the KERNAL ROM, at $E000, $E400, $E800 and $EC00. A write to those addresses lands in the RAM even with the ROM switched in. Reading it back needs the ROM switched out, and that is all copy_under_kernal ($72A4) does: it sets the processor port $01 to $34, copies, and puts it back.

05 · Music

The tune that got the game withdrawn

This copy is the first release, the one Mastertronic withdrew because its in-play tune is a rendition of Michael Jackson's Thriller. The re-release replaced it with a new tune. Both are played here by the game's own driver, ported line by line. Play the in-play tune and switch the release to hear the other.

Release:

Every sound-chip write the port makes matches the game's own driver, in the same order. The game sets the sound chip's filter once and keeps it on voice 1, the bass, for the whole tune (music_init_filter, $60A0). The Filter switch plays it three ways, even while it plays. Off leaves the filter out. 6581, the chip in every C64 in 1984, darkens voice 1 as the game meant, on the curve the reSID emulator measured from one chip. 8580, the chip of later C64s, barely changes it. Real 6581s differ from one another and they distort, which this model does not. It plays at PAL speed, so the notes are flat, as they were on PAL machines.

The re-release changed only the in-play tune and one byte of the driver. Its tune data comes from the steward's transcription in unorig/Chiller; this copy matches the other file there, Version 1, byte for byte. The level-start and game-over tunes are the same in both. The driver change is the compare at $60B6: this copy cuts each note off three interrupts before the next step, and the re-release cuts it one before, so its notes ring on longer.

Both tunes are tuned for an NTSC C64. Of the first release's in-play tune's 478 frequency values, 464 land within two cents of a note on an NTSC machine, the kind sold in North America. A PAL machine, the kind sold in Europe, runs about 4 % slower, so there every note plays about a third of a semitone flat.

The driver runs on the system interrupt, 60 times a second, on two voices. Each command is one byte, and the byte is the low half of its handler's address in page $61. music_next_command ($6100) writes it into its own JMP at $610B and jumps, so the tune data is a list of jump targets.

The count command is how the music talks to the game. The card at the start of each level stays up until the level-start tune counts (card_wait, $7610), and GAME OVER stays up until the game-over tune does ($5DA9). The in-play tunes never count. The first release's loops after about 69 seconds.

06 · The cast

A boy, a girl and seventeen kinds of enemy

The boy and the girl share one set of moves: four frames walking each way, two in the air and two standing. Every level brings five enemies, drawn from seventeen different animations that the levels share between them. Flip through each level's five.

The boy's frames at $3600 (pointers $D8-$E7), the girl's at $3A00 ($E8-$F7), in the order the game uses them: walking left, walking right, jumping, standing.

Four frames per enemy, in the order the game steps through them. Colours are the ones the game sets on arrival ($D029-$D02D); multicolour sprites share $D025 and $D026.

The girl's frames sit exactly 16 pointers after the boy's, so one set of routines can draw either. player_swap ($576B) adds or takes away that 16 in the three places the frame numbers are kept.

The enemies live on sprites 2 to 6, and a sixth on sprite 7 is thrown in from one of them now and then. Their shapes are named in the screen's settings. Most are stored at $2000-$29FF, and seven of the ten screens also use a second store at $0B00-$0FFE.

07 · The enemies

You can't kill them, only keep out of the way

An enemy that runs into the character you control costs energy and nothing else. It carries on along its path, and it cannot be killed.

The game has no hit test of its own: sprite_touch ($CE87) reads the hardware's sprite collision register, $D01E, and when your sprite and exactly one enemy sprite overlap, two steps of poison go on the bar. Stand still at the start of the forest and the bar holds for 16 seconds, until an enemy wanders onto him and takes 69 steps in a single visit.

The enemies follow scripts of direction bytes (0 up, 1 down, 2 left, 3 right), each ended by $FF, at path_scripts ($4A00-$4A8F). path_step ($CC12) moves an enemy one pixel every few passes and reads the next byte every 8th move. Nine scripts serve all fifty slots. Three walk down and back up, four hold one direction for ever, and three are long runs down with one stray byte that matches none of the four directions, so it does nothing. Step through any enemy's script on the Maps / levels tab.

An enemy that walks off an edge comes back later: respawn_timer ($2FB2) gives an empty slot a chance out of 128 ($45E2) to return.

08 · Energy

No lives, and every step costs

Chiller has no lives. You have one energy bar to manage, and when it empties the game is over. Enemies and toadstools drain it and mushrooms fill it. Unusually, walking and jumping cost energy too. Try each on the bar below, which uses the game's own step sizes.

Drawn with the game's own bar characters, $A1-$A9, in the forest's colours: 33 cells of eight steps. An empty bar ends the game.

An empty bar goes straight to GAME OVER (out_of_energy, $5A70).

09 · Controls

Joystick or keyboard

As the title screen says, there are two ways to play: a joystick in control port 2, or the keys Z, C, ? and SHIFT. The game reads the joystick port itself, and leaves the keyboard to the Commodore's built-in routines.

10 · The jump

Jump to go up, fall as far as you like

The jump is 24 one-pixel steps up, each a little slower than the last, and the same steps back down in reverse. However far the boy falls afterwards, he lands unhurt.

One jump up and to the right in the forest, from the game's own code (the port on the Play tab), three times the C64's pixels. It shows the take-off, the top and the landing, with a dot for each frame at his feet. He rises 24 pixels in 24 frames, hangs at the top while the slowest steps run, and lands 28 pixels to the right 70 frames, or 1.4 seconds, after he took off.

Up on the stick, or SHIFT, starts a jump (start_jump, $5418) unless he is already falling. In the air the game switches the controls off by writing an RTS over the first byte of read_controls ($C84D), so he keeps the direction he took off in until he lands. He climbs by jumping. All ten screens switch off up and down as walking directions ($4500-$4503), so he has no climb of his own and gets up the screen one jump at a time, from ledge to ledge.

Some ledges crumble. Tiles $4D-$53 are one ledge in seven stages. Every eighth step on it moves it on a stage, and the last stage is empty space (tile_touch_a, $5A9A). Stand on a crumbling ledge too long and it drops you.

11 · Secret

Secret: enemies could once be killed, for points

In Chiller you cannot hurt an enemy, and there are no lives and no scrolling. Its code has all three, and a second sound player whose every write goes nowhere. None of it can be reached in play.

We first read the kill code as part of a shooting game, which is why the first routine is named for a shot. The code does not say how an enemy was hit. unused_kill_enemy takes the enemy from the boy's last collision ($CF5E, $CF5F, written by sprite_touch), so it may once have been enough to touch an enemy. The row above may be a shot's beginning: fire puts a second sprite on the boy, facing his way, with a sound, and it vanishes when it hits the scenery. No code has been found that moves that sprite along, so nothing left in the game settles which.

The sound player was silenced without being removed. Its stores all point at the last byte of memory, so it still steps through its notes and makes no sound.

12 · Secret

Secret: the magic crosses were once silver

Three of the first five levels carry a spare score line that is never shown. It says SILVER CROSSES where the game says MAGIC CROSSES.

13 · Unfinished

Unfinished: a PRESS CTRL FOR MENU prompt that never appears

After GAME OVER the game still calls a routine meant to put PRESS CTRL FOR MENU on the bottom row. The routine reads the text, the screen and the colours, and writes nothing. It looks like a feature left out before release, with its prompt stubbed so it never shows.

The text at $574A, drawn here as it would have looked. The routine loops 19 times over LDA $574A,X, LDA $07CA,X, LDA $D805, LDA $DBCA,X: four loads and no store.

An older forest card is left behind the same way: card_forest_title ($5D0D) is still there, and its printer, $5E00, is never called.

14 · Odds and ends

Things the manual never told you

Small things found in the code.

  • Fall damage was written, then switched off twice. Falls are counted, and on landing the count is compared with a limit. The branch after that compare has been overwritten with NOPs, and the limit is $FF anyway. (land $53EF; the limit at $457A)
  • The enemies can speed up, but nobody has seen it. Each enemy slot has a count the code calls lives. A life here is one entrance: coming back on screen uses one, and leaving by an edge gives it back. When every slot has used up its count and all five are off the screen, a LEVEL counter goes up, is shown over the play area, and every enemy's movement and animation delays are halved. Edges refund a life and most slots start with 200, so play does not seem to get there. (level_up $2C19; speed_up $5681; counts at $CF76, from $45E7)
  • The jump sound should rise as he falls. The routine adds 5 to the frequency's low byte, but its carry test branches to the very next instruction. The high byte goes down by one every step regardless, and the pitch keeps dropping. (jump_pitch_fall $5518; the branch at $5524)
  • The title should flicker two colours. On the title after a game, the flicker routine is meant to change both multicolour registers, $D022 and $D023. Its second store goes to $7523, a byte in the last screen's settings, so only one flickers. (title_flicker $7580)

15 · Open questions

What we're still unsure of

  • Whether the LEVEL counter can ever appear in real play. Nothing here proves it cannot.
  • Screenshots of the way back taken in an emulator show a stray fence patch on the cinema and a crosses counter reading 2S or 5S where the way to the house reads 05. They were taken with crosses moved beside the boy, so that may be the cause.
  • What $1000-$1FFF held. It repeats every 64 bytes like sprite data, but drawn as sprites it is noise.
  • Whether $4A90-$4AFF, shaped like more enemy paths, was ever used. No screen's settings point at it.
  • Which of the manual's ghouls, zombies, ghosts and bats is which shape.
