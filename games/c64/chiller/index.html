01 · The journey

To the haunted house and home again

In Chiller, a boy crosses a forest, a cinema, a ghetto and a graveyard to reach his girlfriend in the haunted house. He collects five blue crosses on each screen. On the way home the girl joins him, and together they must collect the blue and red crosses through the same places in reverse.

The return journey remembers what happened on the way out. Mushrooms eaten and ledges broken are still gone. Take the final cross and the game starts again in the forest with your score intact. There is no ending screen.

02 · The makers

A £1.99 Mastertronic game

Mastertronic published Chiller in 1984 as a £1.99 cassette game. The game's own title screen says it was programmed by David and Richard Darling. Games That Weren't attributes the code to Richard, graphics to David and sound to David Dunn. Those more specific roles come from that archive, rather than from the title screen.

The Darling brothers later established Codemasters. Its company history dates the founding to 1986. The price is recorded in a September 1984 review. Chiller sits near the beginning of a much larger story about British budget games and the people who made them.

03 · The two releases

The tune that had to change

The first release played David Dunn's rendition of Michael Jackson's Thriller during the game. Reports from November 1984 describe a dispute over the music. Mastertronic issued a later version with a different in-play tune.

The copy examined for this site is the first version: its music data matches the documented first-version transcription byte for byte. That establishes which version the analysis covers. On the Music tab, you can listen to both tunes through a port of the game's own sound driver and inspect the small change to the driver itself.

04 · What the code keeps

A game with traces of other ideas

Normal play gives you one energy bar and no way to kill enemies. The code still contains routines for lives, enemy deaths and points for kills. Some unused score data calls the pickups SILVER CROSSES, and a GAME OVER menu prompt is read but never drawn.
