1985 · Hewson Consultants

Gribbly's Day Out

Gribbly, his bubbles and every creature are hardware sprites, but a Gribblet sitting on a ledge is two characters of the scenery and only becomes a sprite when it leaves the ground. Each level begins with Gribbly assembled out of triangles, and ends with him taken apart the same way.

01 · Everything that moves

Every shape that moves, at the game's own rate

The game runs at the video chip's 50 frames a second and moves its creatures on every other frame, so most of them change picture 25 times a second. Each shape here steps as the game's code steps it, in the colours it gives it.

Colours of level

Drawn from the sprite shapes in the listing ($3000-$3FFF, and Seon's at $0E40-$0FFF) and the play tiles at $2800, over the level's background. A level's colours come from four tables by its number AND 7 ($7DBF-$7DDE), so levels 0 and 8 look alike, and so on. They set the background, the tiles' colours and the Gribblets', which are the tiles' third colour; Gribbly and the creatures have colours of their own. Every multicolour sprite shares white and black ($D025/$D026). The random picks are made here by the page, as the game makes them from the sound chip's noise.

Every creature has one mover, which runs on even frames (move_creatures, $584D) and takes a free hardware sprite only while the creature is on or near the screen. A seed pod steps through $D4-$D9, and is drawn eight pixels to the right on $D4 and eight to the left on $D7, so it shivers as it falls (seed_pod_move, $5BF8). A Topsy walks through $EC-$F0, counting up one way and down the other ($5213). When it turns into a chrysalis it keeps the Topsy's sprite, so its first picture, $F1, is still in the Topsy's colour; from $F2 it is multicolour light blue, and it holds $F4 until it hatches (chrysalis_move, $538A). A Stomper walks through $F5-$F9; a Flyer is drawn double width in white and changes only every fourth frame; Seon, double width and black, shows one of his four pictures at random every fourth frame, and once every 256 frames plays his other three in turn (seon_move, $49AF).

Fire blows a bubble every eighth frame while it is held (blow_bubble, $6429). It takes Gribbly's sprite settings, so it is drawn in his colour, but single-colour. move_bubbles ($64A5) carries it nine pixels a step away from him and counts its shape down from $CB, the smallest, to $C4, the burst, then lets the sprite go.

The Gribblets on the ledges are tiles $38-$3F, a left half and a right half four times over. On frame 2 of every eight, animate_chars ($6651) moves each of the four pictures on to the next character, so every Gribblet on the map looks about at once. A flipped Gribblet's legs are its top three rows, copied in from $7F67 and $7F77 at random on the same frame, and the psi-grub (tile $27) is redrawn from one of four pictures at $7FAC. Gribbly's own face is redrawn inside his sprite, a piece at a time (below).

The two sequences at the end are played by level_start ($4573) and end_sequence ($5A20) with sprites 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7, double size, between waits of one video frame or two (wait_frame, $5BBD, takes two when the frame counter it steps comes out even). At the start a small black star grows where Gribbly will stand and opens into a triangle; three pieces gather round it, turn white and fall away as dots, and Gribbly is there. The end is much the same played backwards: the pieces slide out from him and darken, and the last of them shrinks back to a star and goes. One of the star's colours is blue ($F6), so on a level with a blue background that step cannot be seen.

A Gribblet sitting on a ledge is two tiles in the map, drawn by the character screen like the scenery. Only when it hops, falls from a Flyer, or rides inside one does it borrow a hardware sprite. Sprites are scarce. Gribbly has one, and the game has seven to share between Gribblets, bubbles, Seon and the creatures. A creature off the screen gets none at all: it moves as numbers in a table ($0368-$03F7, sixteen slots) and takes a free sprite only when it comes into view (free_sprite $620B).

A Gribblet that hops (gribblet_hop $62E5) is erased from its ledge, flies as a sprite, and is drawn back into the map where it lands. Off the screen, a hop moves the tiles and nothing more.

In play the video chip shows the play screen at $0800 with the tiles at $2800 ($D018 = $2B), so the sprite pointers are the last eight bytes of that screen, $0BF8-$0BFF. Below the play area a raster interrupt switches to the status panel's screen at $0400, whose eight pointers all hold $C0, the blank shape, set once by new_game. The How it works tab shows how the switch is hidden.

Shape $FF holds stray bytes and nothing ever points at it. A mover sets its creature's shape every time it moves it, but its colour mostly when it gives it a sprite, and a creature that changes kind keeps the sprite it had: a seed pod that lands becomes a Topsy in the same sprite, and a Topsy curling up shows the chrysalis's $F1 in its own colour.

02 · Gribbly's face

Gribbly's face moves in pieces, each on its own timer

Gribbly is one sprite, and his face is not a series of pictures. The game redraws pieces of it inside the sprite while he plays: his antennae, his eyes, and his mouth, each at its own rate and for its own reason.

 

The shape on screen, enlarged, in level 0's colours, and every piece the game can draw into it, from its tables at $7DF5-$7F33, with the one in place outlined. Under them: one cycle of 128 frames, about two and a half seconds, and where the picture is in it. The face is drawn by a port of animate_face ($6AAD), run beside the game's own routine on the kit's 6502 simulator for 2,048 calls, every frame number eight times with random noise and points; the three shapes matched after every call. In the emulator, on level 0, the antennae changed only on frame 2 of every 8 and the eyes only on frames 2, 8, 10 and 16 of every 128.

The antennae change on every eighth frame, about six times a second. Rows 0-4 of all three of Gribbly's shapes get one of four poses, picked from the noise of the sound chip's third voice added to the raster line ($6AC2), so the antennae wave whichever way he faces. A pick can land on the pose already there, which makes the waving uneven.

The eyes run on a cycle of 128 frames, and only in the shape that faces you (eyes $6B36). On frame 2 of the cycle the lids come half down, on frame 8 they shut, on frame 10 they are half open again, and on frame 16 the eyes open wide, looking one of six ways. That look stays for the rest of the cycle, so Gribbly blinks every two and a half seconds and glances somewhere new as he opens his eyes. The look comes from the sound chip's noise as well. Its values 6 and 7 both give look 0, so look 0 comes up three times in eight.

The mouth is redrawn on every other frame, from the score ($6AE5). Gribbly smiles while points are waiting to be added and frowns while points are waiting to be taken off; otherwise his mouth is straight. Points reach the score at one a step, 25 a second (score_tick $6FF0), so the expression lasts as long as the points take to count: four seconds of smile for the 100 that saving a Gribblet earns, and under a second of frown for the 20 that putting one down away from the cave costs.

The joystick picks which of the three shapes is shown: left shows $C2, right $C3, and once he has stopped moving sideways, $C1, the shape that faces you (gribbly_physics $68F5). All three get the same antennae and mouth, so nothing jumps when he turns. The side shapes keep the eyes they were drawn with. The face goes on moving while the game is paused (pause $72E5) and between levels (wait_frame $5BBD).
