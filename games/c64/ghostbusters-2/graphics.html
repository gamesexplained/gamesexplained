The title sprite layers

Eight sprites form four pairs over the title picture. The setup assigns the first four white and the next four red; each pair shares a position. Switch the layers to see how they fit together. The shapes begin at $2800, and the stored setup assigns their pointers.

The displayed group reconstructs the captured pointers and setup positions; its backdrop is omitted.

Three shapes for the fireball

The game steers sprite 5 across Broadway and cycles its pointer through three shapes. Choose a shape to see its 24 by 21 pixels. The $851E raster code sets the position and pointer; the bitmaps begin at $40C0.

The VIC reads 63 bytes for each multicolour sprite. These colours come from the Broadway raster handler. Other moving shapes require their own identification.

The story’s lower-band letters

The story renderer represents each letter or digit with two character codes, one above the other. Choose a character to see its shape from the character set selected in the captured lower text band.

The text renderer selects the paired codes. The raster handlers select this character set in the captured opening-story frame. The colour here is illustrative.

Broadway movement frames

Two sprites move across the lower part of the scene. Every fourth movement update, the game copies a stored frame into each sprite slot. Select a frame to inspect its source bitmap.

The movement setup selects the source group. The inspector uses illustrative colours and multicolour mode; the moving objects are still being identified.

Airshaft character arrangements

The raster handlers assemble the character from six sprite slots in three rows. Choose one of 30 pointer-table records to see its pieces together.

Airshaft sprite pieces

Airshaft routines select sprite slots through pointer tables and individual state changes; the inspector also includes slots selected in a captured frame. The sprite 4 animation uses the sequences at $CB63 and $CB67.

The inspector shows the captured bytes in multicolour with illustrative colours. Slots $06 and $09 show their state in that frame and can change during play. The game may combine pieces or use different colours.

The airshaft status sprite

Space selects one of three modes. In one captured state the lower row displays 99, 05 or 02. Select a mode to see the 24 by 21 sprite after the game draws it. In VICE tests, holding fire and up changed mode 2’s 05 to 04 and mode 3’s 02 to 01; the meaning of the numbers remains open.

The captured sprite uses slot $06 at $4180; the mode drawing routine changes two columns in its final eight rows. Its source addresses can change during play.

The ten digit patterns begin at $928F, eight bytes per digit. Moving a pointer back eight bytes selects the preceding pattern. The upper counter routine uses three pattern pointers and handles the transition between 99 and 100.

Three pieces of a status sprite

Three code paths copy seven rows each into successive parts of sprite slot $09. Choose how many pieces to assemble. Each path also moves the upper counter’s tens-pattern pointer by eight bytes and increments the same count; what triggers them remains open.

The pieces begin at $0F00, $0F15 and $0F2A. The viewer leaves unselected rows blank to show how the pieces fit; it does not reconstruct the sprite’s earlier state. The colours come from the captured status area.

An animated museum detail

In the portrait room, a three-by-three area of the screen alternates between two character patterns. In a 20-frame VICE trace, the update ran once per frame and changed the pattern every six calls. Pause the animation to inspect either pattern.

The update routine copies codes from $837E and $838A. The portrait room was reached from the floor after forced success paths in the earlier episodes.

Character sets for three scenes

The Broadway raster handler switches the VIC between sets at $7000 and $7800. The airshaft and museum use their own sets at $7000 and $7000. Each set holds 256 shapes of eight bytes. The viewer uses a single chosen palette for all cells, so it shows the stored shapes rather than their individual screen colours.

In multicolour mode each pair of bits selects one of four colours. Some screen cells use single-colour characters instead.

Airshaft copied patterns

The game copies blocks of bytes within the video bank. Choose a group and frame to inspect them as eight-byte patterns. The first four groups skip byte zero on the copy paths shown here; the two short groups among them have alternate paths that copy it.

Patterns are shown in single colour and in memory order, not at their positions on the game screen. The destination's captured first byte is used when the selected copy skips it. See the long copy and short copy. Their role on screen remains open.

The opening story’s picture rows

The story switches to a multicolour bitmap for its picture. Choose one of the 15 captured character rows to see the 40 cells the VIC draws, together with their bitmap and colour-matrix addresses.

Each cell takes eight bitmap bytes, two colours from the matrix copied from $8F00 to the display, one from the captured colour RAM and the black background. A reconstruction of the complete frame differs from VICE by two pixels.
