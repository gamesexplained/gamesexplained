01 · The title

The title sprite layers

Eight sprites form four pairs over the title picture. The setup assigns the first four white and the next four red; each pair shares a position. Switch the layers to see how they fit together. The shapes begin at $2800, and the stored setup assigns their pointers.

The sprites where the title places them, without the picture behind.

02 · The airshaft

The airshaft: one Ghostbuster in six sprites, and what attacks him

Each figure is drawn from the airshaft’s own sprite shapes, in the colours the episode gives it, and moves at the game’s own speed. The figures in the shaft are shown on its brown background.

The poses come from the tables at $919F and $9217, the rising ghost’s shapes from $C6FB, the creature’s from $CB63 and the blast’s from $A829. The shield routine writes the four arcs into the code that draws them, and the panel’s raster handler sets the five sprites on the panel.

The panel’s weapon counts

Space switches between the three weapons, and a sprite on the panel shows a count under each one: 99 for the proton beam, 05 for the PKR bomb and 02 for the shield, in one game. Firing with the bomb or the shield chosen takes one off its count. Choose a weapon to see the sprite as the game draws it.

The sprite is slot $06 at $4180; the drawing routine rewrites two columns of its last eight rows.

The ten digit patterns begin at $928F, eight bytes per digit. Moving a pointer back eight bytes selects the digit before. The counter routine uses three pattern pointers and handles the step from 99 to 100.

The slime scoop, piece by piece

Each piece of the slime scoop picked up in the airshaft adds seven rows to sprite slot $09, and ten to the percentage on the panel. Choose how many pieces to show.

The pieces begin at $0F00, $0F15 and $0F2A. Rows for pieces not yet collected are left blank.

03 · Broadway

Broadway: the statue, the fireball, the people and the ghosts

Broadway draws more figures than there are sprites. It moves sprites down the picture as the picture is drawn, so one sprite can draw a ghost in the sky, a piece of the statue and a person on the road. The background is black.

The statue’s frames come from the statue routine, the ghosts’ shapes from the sequences named at $C4F5, the people’s from the Space routine, and the road figures’ frames from $F000, copied in by the road routine.

04 · The museum

The museum: two Ghostbusters, Janosz and Vigo

The museum shows two Ghostbusters and their opponent on a floor of light and dark blue. Each figure is drawn in the colours the episode gives it.

The joystick routine chooses the Ghostbusters’ shapes, and the tables at $9618 and $9620 swap the upper half when he picks up Oscar. Janosz and Vigo walk through the walking routines, and the figure that forms takes its pairs from $A31A and $A330.

The panel’s status words

Two single-colour sprites form each status word. The panel shows OK or DEAD; DAZED is stored with them and never shown.

The panel’s status setup selects the pointers, and the raster handler draws them in single-colour mode.

Oscar’s crib

In the portrait room, Oscar’s crib is a three-by-three block of characters that switches between two patterns every sixth frame. Picking him up selects the empty crib.

The update routine copies codes from $837E and $838A.

05 · Kept but not drawn

Shapes the game keeps without a known use

Each episode’s sprite memory holds a few shapes that none of its routines or tables is known to choose.

The museum’s eight shapes begin at $6800. Broadway’s ghost sequences are named in the table at $C4F5.

06 · Every shape

Every sprite shape, in the order the game stores them

Each episode keeps its sprite shapes together, one after another, and a sprite chooses a shape by its number. Choose an episode to see all of its shapes, then choose a shape to see what it draws.

A shape that a card above draws keeps that card’s colours. The others are drawn in plain colours. The museum’s equipment panel keeps its shapes in a different part of memory, with the status words, from $0D40.

09 · Character sets

The scenes’ character sets

The Broadway raster handler switches the VIC between sets at $7000 and $7800. The airshaft and the museum each load a set of their own at $7000 (airshaft, museum), and the airshaft has a second set at $7800, which holds its moving walls. Each set holds 256 shapes of eight bytes. The viewer draws every shape in one set of colours.

In multicolour mode each pair of bits selects one of four colours. Some screen cells use single-colour characters instead.

10 · The airshaft’s walls

The airshaft’s moving walls

Parts of the airshaft’s walls move. The game animates them by copying new patterns over characters in its second character set, a frame at a time. Choose a group and step through its frames.

The patterns are shown in single colour and in memory order, not where they sit on the screen. See the long copy and short copy.
