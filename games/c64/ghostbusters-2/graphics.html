The shapes stored for the Broadway fireball.

Three shapes for the fireball

The game steers sprite 5 across Broadway and cycles its pointer through three shapes. Choose a shape to see its 24 by 21 pixels. The $851E raster code sets the position and pointer; the bitmaps begin at $40C0.

The VIC reads 63 bytes for each multicolour sprite. These colours come from the Broadway raster handler. Other moving shapes require their own identification.

A Broadway frame, sprite by sprite

The VIC repositions hardware sprites during the frame. The $856F raster chain reuses sprites 6 and 7 in several bands. Select one sprite to see its uses in this captured picture.

The full reconstructed frame matches all 104,448 pixels of its VICE capture. The selector masks the sprite-enable register while keeping the captured raster changes.

Character sets for two scenes

The Broadway raster handler switches the VIC between sets at $7000 and $7800. The airshaft uses a different set at $7000. Each set holds 256 shapes of eight bytes. The viewer uses a single chosen palette for all cells, so it shows the stored shapes rather than their individual screen colours.

In multicolour mode each pair of bits selects one of four colours. Some screen cells use single-colour characters instead.

The opening story’s picture rows

The story switches to a multicolour bitmap for its picture. Choose one of the 15 captured character rows to see the 40 cells the VIC draws, together with their bitmap and colour-matrix addresses.

Each cell takes eight bitmap bytes, two colours from the $6000 matrix, one from the captured colour RAM and the black background. A reconstruction of the complete frame differs from VICE by two pixels.
