# Ghostbusters II — shared facts

The opening story and each gameplay episode have separate captured states and listings under `parts/`; the exact route and the two disk releases are in `orientation.md`.

- In the four captured states, all 4,096 bytes of RAM under `$D000–$DFFF` are identical. The airshaft transition at `$02A7` copies them back into `$1000–$1BFF` and `$0400–$07FF` before jumping to `$1000`. The shared copy is counted once in `parts/resident-stash`; its executable lower-memory copy is described in `parts/story-engine`. The Censor intro’s boot unpacker produces this complete stash before the first engine entry.
- All four captured states contain identical bytes at `$E464–$E5C1` and `$E000–$E0C1`. The three-voice update at `$E141–$E225` differs between them only in the immediate operands at `$E147` and `$E14A`, which its initialization and update modify. It writes all three SID voices at `$E176–$E218` and volume at `$E222`; the airshaft calls it through `$E13A`. The driver, its three voice-state records, effect-state records, and the per-load tune streams are described in the corresponding listings.
- The sound initializer at `$E000` selects a seven-byte record at `$EB5C`: one accumulator increment and three little-endian pointers. With A set to 1 from the airshaft snapshot, VICE stopped at its return with `$E147 = $24` and channel pointers `$EF44`, `$EF42`, `$EF46`; the kit's 6502 simulator produced the same values. That record is the first outcome selection; its audible result is available in the page player.
- The capture of a PAL airshaft frame was reconstructed with zero differing pixels by the kit's frame renderer. The page uses its trimmed visual data; `parts/airshaft/facts.md` records the capture and the interrupt chain.

All 11 part ledgers have full annotation coverage. The 17 media items have dispositions in `media.json`, including the damaged museum file and disk-directory decoration. Coverage includes retained bytes whose separate gameplay purpose is explicitly unresolved; it does not claim those bytes have an established active role.

- In the airshaft, the success path at `$0897` initializes sound selection 1; failure paths at `$08BB` and `$08CD` initialize selection 2. Their records are at `$EB63` and `$EB6A`. VICE and the kit processor agreed on channel state and all three voice frequencies for initialization and the first 30 calls to `$E13A` for each selection; `sound-vice.json` holds those observations. In the forced-success state, an execution checkpoint counted 30 calls to `$E13A` over 30 PAL frames. The page player and listing agree on every SID write for 500 updates per selection (`test_sound.js`).

- In the opening-story snapshot, the three active channel-list pointers are `$EB85`, `$EB9B` and `$EB73`; the three list pointers in sound record 0 are `$EB83`, `$EB99` and `$EB71`. The story and airshaft snapshots contain identical sound data at `$EB5C–$EFDF`. The driver reads each list in two-byte entries. Initializing selection 0 and playing 30 updates matched VICE on channel state and voice frequencies; a 12,000-update simulator comparison matched every SID write made from the listing and from the snapshot. This supports associating record 0 with the opening story; the complete sequencer-and-SID-register cycle was subsequently measured at 458,752 updates, after a 2,341-update prefix, with effects disabled. The independently advancing effect-noise state is excluded from that comparison.

- The ending snapshot has different sound data from the opening story: record 0 at `$EB5C` points to `$EB7F`, `$EB71` and `$EB89`. The active channel pointers in that snapshot match those values. The page takes its ending sound state and data from `work/ending.vsf`; the forced-success route is in `orientation.md`. Its first 30 updates match VICE channel states and voice frequencies. For 12,000 updates, the same sound code with those ending bytes produced the same SID writes as the complete ending snapshot. `test_sound.js` holds the first 500 writes to a snapshot-derived digest and the first 30 states to the VICE measurements.

- Broadway initializes selection 0 at `$CD2C` for the prompt; its stream data differs from the opening story. The player takes that state and data from `parts/broadway/work/prompt.vsf`. Its first 30 updates match VICE channel states and frequencies, and 12,000 updates match the prompt snapshot’s SID writes. In simulator comparisons, Broadway and museum selections 1 and 2 produced the same SID writes as the corresponding airshaft selections for 500 updates each.

- The Censor crack intro calls `$1000` from its raster handler at `$18AA`. A checkpoint counted 30 calls in 30 PAL frames. The page player begins from `work/censor-intro.vsf` and carries 1,459 bytes read or executed by the intro music over 120,000 simulated updates, plus the captured SID registers. That sparse image and the full snapshot produced identical SID writes for all 120,000 updates; the first 30 driver states and SID registers also match VICE (`test_sound.js`). This tune belongs to the crack intro, not an attribution to the game publisher.

- The sound initializer `$E000–$E0C1`, update `$E13A–$E225` and stop/clear code `$EB26–$EB57` were paired across the opening story, Broadway and museum with `carry.py`. The initializer and stop/clear ranges match the airshaft code. The update ranges differ only at the self-modified immediate operands `$E147` and `$E14A`; the initializer and update write those bytes. The annotations describe the operations rather than the snapshot-specific operand values.

- The 14 pointer pairs at `$E668–$E683` select short offset sequences at `$E684–$E6AC`. The driver at `$E43D` masks a sequence byte's high bit and resets the sequence index when that bit is set, before adding the resulting value to the voice state. These bytes match the airshaft, Broadway, museum, opening-story, second-story, third-story and ending snapshots. A complete-cycle CPU trace of all three selections in the airshaft, Broadway and ending datasets reached pointer-table indices 0, 2, 3, 6 and 8. The other nine entries were not selected in those complete music cycles.

## The listing's error rate

The sample with seed 121126 drew 80 comments from a population of 5,150: 60 of 3,907 written comments and 20 of 1,243 generated descriptions. The independent checker used gpt-6-astra. One written comment named the wrong source array for sorted high X coordinates in the museum; it was corrected, together with the matching source-address descriptions for the other sorted arrays. The raw result is 1/80, with a 95% Wilson interval of 0.2%–6.7%; weighting the two strata gives 1.3%. This is a lower bound on the true error rate, not a full audit.

A separate targeted review of the new state-group and field descriptions found 26 additional wrong comments. Corrections distinguish coordinates from pointers and flags, identify the actual equipment exchange fields, and describe both motion workspaces correctly. This selected review has no random error-rate interpretation. A full independent audit remains recommended.

A subsequent targeted mechanics review on gpt-6-astra checked 42 selected comments and found eleven with wrong details; all eleven were corrected. Seven shared the false assumption that both beam endpoints must remain active, rather than testing stored hit flags. The other corrections concern second-player pickup suppression, bomb movement before versus after activation, an effect selector passed in X, and the zero command in the Broadway census. These selected results are not a random sample or a full audit.

A continuation on gpt-6.1-sol drew 60 comments uniformly from all 5,159 comments across the eleven parts, seed 141126. The independent checker found one wrong detail (1.7%; Wilson 95% interval 0.3–8.9%): the voice writer's wording confused the decremented gate counter with the separately written control byte. The sampled comment and all twenty matching comments were corrected. Fifty-two comments passed every checkable detail. Seven included historical snapshot comparisons or read-watch observations whose original captures were unavailable; their static functional claims passed, but those historical observations were not independently reproduced. Twenty focused instruction-level CPU tests passed. The observed error count is a lower bound, and the seven evidence gaps are not verified passes.

A separate generated-template supplement drew 20 comments from a population of 742, seed 141127, on gpt-6.1-sol. Sixteen passed and four retained historical map-tail read observations that were not independently reproduced; no wrong detail was demonstrated. Eleven matrix-copy CPU cases passed. This separate supplement has no combined population-weighted rate with the uniform sample. Neither review is a full audit; a full independent audit remains recommended.

The analysis used model identifiers not recognised as proven by `kit/models.json`. The fresh checker's sample does not replace the maintainer's check required by `kit/CHECKING.md`; the published tier remains Bronze pending that review.

## Additional verification

The museum's four first-pair floor probes accepted codes $00 and $3F and rejected $40 and $FF in all 16 VICE trials, with an entry checkpoint firing once in each trial. The processor model agreed. Its 16 directional display routines were checked at all three animation phases. The six Broadway fire-cell routines passed 180 processor comparisons across screen bands, backgrounds, and terminal counts. The four airshaft short-contact tests were checked across all 256 input codes each.

Entry-path processor traces reached the first main-loop wait in each gameplay episode and identified state written before its first read. Those generated bytes are excluded from coverage; the declared ranges name their first writers. Authored maps, source bitmaps, tables, and retained load bytes stay counted. The sprite image bytes used by the Graphics page remain in the listing.


The corresponding music-state periods are 6,144 updates after a 2,521-update prefix for Broadway, and 49,152 after a 19,662-update prefix for the ending. The success and failure selections settle to stopped state after 150 and 159 updates respectively. The comparison includes every memory byte written by the update and the SID register values, except the four-byte effect-noise generator at `$EB58–$EB5B`. It does not assert an analog waveform period.

A subsequent mechanics pass verified the three airshaft weapon modes and the 17-step courage depletion in VICE, recorded all three scoop pickups and success in 1,124 control reads, and replayed those reads successfully in VICE. Broadway tests followed a ghost death to a ground drop, a pedestrian pickup to the gauge refill, one replacement-fireball charge and the three-contact statue charge. Museum tests verified weapon exhaustion and recharge, a slime hit and Janosz’s collapse transition, the two hit-flag requirement, Oscar pickup and the safe/fatal landing threshold. The per-part facts give the addresses and distinguish controlled fixtures from normal input routes.

The Jondi introduction calls its music update at `$C475` once per PAL frame: a non-stopping checkpoint counted 30 entries over 30 frames. The page carries 6,434 bytes of music code, data, and state. The extracted image and complete captured memory produce identical SID writes for 120,000 updates. The first 30 updates also match VICE on 177 driver-state bytes and all SID register fields; unused high bits of the three pulse-width registers are masked when comparing hardware state. The driver writes CIA timer registers `$DC04/$DC05`, but the measured introduction calls it from its frame loop. The player preserves that frame cadence. `sound-vice.json` and `test_sound.js` hold the compact observations and checks.

The Jondi bootstrap’s loader asks its drive code for filename prefix `$30,$30` (`00`). At `$0526/$052E` in the drive’s RAM, the directory search compares the first two filename bytes with `$06A5/$06A6`; those bytes hold `$30,$30` in the captured drive. No directory entry on this image begins with `00`. At the first request’s return to C64 `$19D0`, X and Y are both `$30` and the output pointer `$AE/$AF` remains `$1C00`. A subsequent request with X=Y=`$FF` returns to `$19D7` with the pointer wrapped to zero and every byte of `$1C00–$FFFF` equal to `$FF` (58,368 bytes). After the German Space prompt, the copy at `$0100` decodes that buffer with XOR `$41`, filling the BASIC program with `$BE`; VICE reaches BASIC’s line scanner at `$A544`. This identifies the failed file request and resulting invalid program in this image. It does not establish where the missing file went or which release originally supplied it.

Broadway has a complete ordinary-input win from `parts/broadway/work/play-clean.vsf`. The kit machine reaches `$C9D5` after 11,438 frames, with resource bypass `$8016=0`, supply cursor `$D5`, and column pointer `$0AE0`. It records 5,721 joystick reads at `$8C65` and 11,443 Space scans at `$9FBB`. VICE replays the same read sequences, reaches success once and neither failure checkpoint, and agrees on both read counts, the supply cursor, the column pointer, and the zero bypass flag. `broadway-route.json` stores the two streams as run-length pairs. The input harness replaces only those control reads and restores its temporary code and saved resident stash at success. This route begins from a captured playable state rather than power-on.

A font and display pass separates the title logo from the story alphabet. The title setup writes 240 consecutive logo codes and the frame renderer fetches all 240 corresponding `$2000` patterns. The story alphabet is at `$3000`; running every one of the 122 stored title/story/ending rows through its real renderer identifies the previously unassigned punctuation. The title-frame reconstruction has three differing pixels at raster boundaries and is not presented as exact. The detailed findings and corpus checks are in the title facts and `test_story_text.js`.
