These pictures reconstruct captured frames from the airshaft, Broadway and museum. They show one moment in each episode; the full scrolling layouts and their data are still being traced.

The How it works page traces controls and display code. The Graphics tab lets you isolate sprites from the Broadway and museum captures.

The airshaft, from its stored rows

The shaft data holds 153 records, twenty bytes apart. The game expands the first fourteen bytes of a record through one of two tables into two rows of character shapes. This reconstruction follows the table switches observed while moving down and omits moving sprites. It uses one palette from the captured frame throughout; colours during the full passage remain unverified. The other six bytes in each record and the complete play route remain open.

The row data begins at $1000; $A2C0 expands each row. The full passage has not been recorded in play, and the table changes are specific to a downward pass.

The Broadway backdrop

Broadway’s scrolling scenery is stored as twelve rows of 480 character codes. Scroll sideways to inspect the strip. This is the Censor capture: its first row begins with 298 bytes retained from the preceding airshaft, while the Jondi disk loads different bytes there. It uses the captured middle-screen character set and palette; moving sprites and later palette changes are omitted.

The rows start at $0900. The column fetch takes one byte from each row and advances until the pointer reaches $0AE0. VICE checks cover three columns; the full passage has not been recorded in play.

The museum screens

The museum stores two 40-by-25 screens and two colour layers in the same 1,000 bytes. Choose a screen to see its stored background without sprites. The first is used in the observed floor scene. From that state, moving the selected Ghostbuster up and then right reached the portrait room; the route to the museum used forced success paths.

The two screen arrays start at $1000 and $1400. The setup routine takes the low or high nibble from each colour byte at $1C00. Both views use the palette observed in VICE. A three-by-three area animates during the captured visit; sprites are omitted. The Graphics tab shows its two patterns.
