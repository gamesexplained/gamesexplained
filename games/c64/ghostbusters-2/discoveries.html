Discoveries

Bug: on the original disk, two Ghostbusters can trap each other in a doorway

On the museum floor, a Ghostbuster may not walk into his partner. A doorway can still put one down on top of the other, and then neither of them can take a step.

Before each step, the original game checks whether the other Ghostbuster is in the way. It looks at a box round the walking Ghostbuster, about 30 pixels wide and 8 lines tall, which reaches a little further in the direction of the step. If his partner stands inside that box, the step is refused. A Ghostbuster who walks towards his partner therefore stops a few pixels short of him.

Walking from the first room into the portrait room skips that check. The doorway sets the arriving Ghostbuster down just inside the door, always at the same distance from the wall and at the height he walked in at. The game does not look for his partner there. If the partner is still standing where he came in, the newcomer lands on top of him. Each of them is now inside every one of the other’s boxes, so up, down, left and right are all refused, for both of them. The game carries on, but walking can no longer part them. On the screen they look like one Ghostbuster, because the two figures are drawn in the same place.

It takes no trick to get there. Walk one Ghostbuster into the portrait room, press Space to take the other one, and follow him in along the same line.

The portrait room on the original disk, drawn from the game’s memory. Ghostbuster 2 has just come in by the door. Each press of a direction is one frame of walking: two lines up or down, two pixels left or right. Hold it to keep walking. The demonstration does not test the room’s walls, so walking is kept to the open floor near the door, and each Ghostbuster keeps the pose he stood in.

The cracked copies of the game do not have this bug. Each of the eight checks, four directions for each Ghostbuster, begins with an instruction that reads which room the walker is in. The crackers put a jump past the check in place of that instruction, so in a cracked copy the Ghostbusters walk through each other and the doorway does no harm. This is the only change the cracks make to how the game plays. The checks are in the museum’s code from address $B7FB to $BF69 (a $ marks a hexadecimal number), and the doorway is at $BBB5.

Secret: a switch in Broadway’s code turns off both ways of losing

The walk down Broadway can be lost two ways: the clock reaches twelve, or the slime runs out. One byte turns both off, and the game never sets it.

Before the game ends the walk at twelve o’clock, it looks at that byte. It looks again before a fresh fireball, or a ghost reaching the statue, takes a row from the slime supply. With the byte set, the clock carries on past twelve and the fireballs and ghosts take nothing. The byte is zero when Broadway loads, on the original disk as on the cracked copies, and Broadway’s code only ever reads it.

Broadway’s panel, with the clock moved on by the game’s own clock routine. Past twelve, the page moves it on a minute a frame, so that 15:59 comes quickly. Turn the switch on and run it again.

Secret: a third word for the panel, DAZED

In the museum, the equipment panel writes OK or DEAD under each Ghostbuster. A third word, DAZED, is stored with them, and the panel never shows it.

Each word is drawn from two sprites, and DAZED is stored right next to the other two, in the same style. The code that chooses the word for each Ghostbuster only ever picks OK or DEAD, so DAZED never reaches the screen.

The three words as the museum stores them, drawn from their sprites: the two the panel shows, and DAZED.
