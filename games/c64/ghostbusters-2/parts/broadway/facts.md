# Broadway facts

The listing's captured play state is `work/play.vsf`; its route uses a forced airshaft success and a retry, as documented in the game's `orientation.md`. `work/entry.vsf` catches the next boot at `$8009`, and `work/play-clean.vsf` records that boot in play without a retry. The two play states differ in runtime-patched operands and need to be reconciled before changing the listing's snapshot.

- The saved machine has `$01 = $35`, the hardware IRQ vector `$FFFE` pointing to `$84C9`, and the RAM vector at `$0314` pointing to `$84E2`. At `$84C9` the program saves A, X and Y, inspects the stacked status and jumps through `$0314`.
- The short executed-address capture from this play state recorded 2,078 instruction starts. It covers only that interval; the disassembler's additional control-flow walk and the unclassified loaded memory need examination.
- The Broadway prompt snapshot `work/prompt.vsf` has the same hardware IRQ entry `$84C9`. Its `$1000–$1BFF` and RAM under `$D000–$DFFF` are byte-identical to the play snapshot; 19,914 of 20,480 bytes in `$8000–$CFFF` agree. These observations support treating the prompt as a state of this part. They do not identify the loader hand-over.

At `$8C59`, the game reads joystick port 2 and calls four direction helpers. `$91DD/$91EE` subtract or add 3 to the immediate operand at `$8524`, which the raster code writes to sprite 5 Y; `$91FF/$9210` do the same to `$851F`, written to sprite 5 X. The comparisons are made before the three increments or decrements, so a result can cross a threshold. From `work/play-clean.vsf`, ten frames of right, up or down changed `(X,Y)` from `(112,120)` to `(127,120)`, `(112,105)` or `(112,135)` respectively; left reached `(103,120)` and then stopped on its boundary test. A checkpoint at `$8C65` counted five joystick reads over ten frames in the rightward trial. `test_broadway.js` checks all byte values and directional combinations against the four routines.

Sprite 5 uses three shapes at `$40C0`, `$4100` and `$4140`: the raster handler selects pointer values 3, 4 and 5 in VIC bank 1, incrementing the pointer after four visits to `$853A` and wrapping after 5. The three 63-byte shapes are unchanged between the Broadway entry and play snapshots. A captured frame reconstructed from the video registers and memory matched all 104,448 pixels of the VICE capture.

The Broadway raster chain starts at `$84E2` and continues through `$856F`, `$8595`, `$85C6`, `$85FB`, `$8637`, `$866B`, `$86A0` and `$8718`. In a 20-frame VICE trial each of the nine handlers ran 19 times. The middle handlers reposition sprites 6 and 7 at several vertical bands, and `$8718` calls the sound update before scheduling the first handler again.

In the captured frame the VIC uses bank 1. `$8595` writes `$1D` to `$D018` at the middle split, selecting the character set at `$7000`; `$8718` writes `$1F` at the lower split, selecting `$7800`. Both 2,048-byte sets are unchanged between the entry and play snapshots. The complete captured frame, including the register changes, matched the VICE image pixel for pixel.

The fire path at `$8CB5` uses `$8C10` as a latch. In a VICE trial from `work/play-clean.vsf`, holding fire for two consecutive ten-frame intervals reached the new-press path once and set `$96F0`; after ten frames released, another ten-frame press reached it again and set `$96F2`. The path checks the first flag before moving to the next candidate. The six slots draw cell-based shots. Their updaters restore the previous character, advance one cell and stop at step `$26`; a 180-case test covers all six slots and terminal restoration.

The Broadway backdrop occupies twelve 480-byte rows at `$0900–$1F7F`; entry, play and clean play snapshots agree across the range. The scroll routine at `$87F4` takes one code from each row, using a stride of `$01E0` (480), and writes them to screen positions 40 bytes apart. The pointer starts at `$0900`. While `$8023` is zero, `$876F` runs a preliminary pass until the high byte reaches `$0A`, resets the pointer to `$0900` and sets `$8023`. The following 480-column pass ends at pointer `$0AE0`, where `$87D2` enters success at `$C9D5`. A sustained VICE trace observed both the reset and the success entry with the resource/countdown bypass `$8016=1`; that artificial run does not prove a legal winning route.

Three direct VICE calls with column offsets 0, 78 and 479 produced the corresponding twelve source bytes. `test_broadway_map.js` checks all 480 columns against the processor model and those three VICE fixtures. The Levels tab draws the strip with the `$7000` character set and the palette of the captured middle-screen region; sprites and later palette changes are omitted. A complete input recording and its VICE replay reach the native success entry; the recording begins from the captured clean play state.

The scroll routine reads the snapshot bytes in `$0900–$1F7F` as backdrop data, although a static disassembly had labelled some bytes near `$1000` as code. In the Censor route, `$0900–$0A29` is unchanged from the preceding airshaft snapshot: the Censor Broadway file begins output at `$0A2A`. The Jondi Broadway file writes from `$0900`, and all 298 bytes of that prefix differ from the captured Censor state. Across `$0900–$1F7F` the captures differ in 4,891 bytes; their `$4000–$7FFF`, `$8000–$CEFF` and `$E000–$F7FF` ranges match. The captured Broadway execution map has no instruction starts in this region. The later story transition overwrites the saved engine into lower memory before jumping there, so the transition’s jump does not establish that these backdrop bytes execute.

The routines at `$AD07` and `$AD39` use X to select an entry, and Y as an index into a table selected by `$B03A,X` or `$B054,X`. The first adds the signed table byte to a 16-bit base at `$B0D6/$B0D7,X`; the second subtracts it from the base at `$B0F0/$B0F1,X`. The resulting pairs go to `$AF9E/$AF9F,X` and `$AFB8/$AFB9,X` respectively. A direct VICE trial with base `$0180`, selectors `$70` and `$38`, and Y values 0, 64, 128 and 192 recorded eight results in `broadway-curve-vice.json`; `test_broadway_curve.js` checks those and all 256 indices of the two tables against the processor model. It also checks all indices of the 32 views selected by the two lookup routines. Each view matches in the entry and play snapshots; some overlap. These coordinates belong to the scripted ghost slots. Each slot stores its curve phase and selects one of the two signed offset tables; the command stream advances or reverses the phase.

The Censor Broadway unpacker writes `$0A2A–$CEFF`, `$E000–$EF76` and `$F000–$F7FF`; its 56,397 stable output bytes match the handover snapshot. The part lies over the second story and owns those output ranges. The map comparison assembles the retained `$0900–$0A29` prefix from the airshaft listing with the rest from Broadway. The separate Jondi file writes from `$0900` and matched 56,695 stable bytes at its own handover; its differing backdrop and coordinate data are described in the separate Broadway-Jondi listing and the coordinate-view checks.

The setup at `$802A` initializes state, copies 1,000 screen bytes from `$3C00–$3FE7` to `$4400–$47E7`, prepares colour RAM and selects VIC bank 1. The source bytes match in the entry and play snapshots; the initialization then patches several destination cells. At `$8260` a keyboard-bit sequence can gate the loop, with waits for three changes of `$801D` between transitions; this is the P-key pause handshake, also verified directly in VICE below.

The routine at `$A540` expands an entry’s computed position into four sets of offsets and values at `$B1C0–$B1FA`. Its choice at `$AFD3` changes two values, and a non-1 value at `$B172` writes `$08` to the last three slots. The raster routine at `$C901` reads these arrays as X and Y positions and sprite pointers for sprites 0–4, limiting Y to `$9D`. The entries are the scripted ghosts; the raster places their one-, two- or four-piece animations and clips them below the play area.

The sequence routine at `$A4EE` selects a pair of bytes for the sprite layout. At a `$FF` terminator, its mode either holds the previous pair, restarts the sequence, or clears the entry’s active flag.

The paired-state routine at `$9D05` alternates patched addresses between two groups in `$F000–$F8xx`, then dispatches separate updates according to `$9C8C/$9C8E`. The routines at `$9E05/$9EC6` move two X coordinates and copy 64-byte frames into `$4280/$42C0` every fourth movement update. The raster handler at `$86D6–$86FE` uses those low X bytes and selects sprite pointers `$0A/$0B` for sprites 3 and 4. These are the two independently moving street-level figures, distinct from the three Space-controlled collectors. Their 64-byte frames are copied into the two live sprite slots before the raster displays them.

The frame sources at `$F000–$F7FF` comprise 32 records of 64 bytes. The two movement routines select records in `$40` steps and copy them to the sprite slots; all 2,048 source bytes match between the captured entry and play snapshots. The Graphics tab displays their first 63 bytes with illustrative colours.

The ledger includes 19,852 bytes previously outside the count. Forty stretches match stable unpacker output at the entry snapshot; another is a copy visible in play whose source is present at entry. Their descriptions are in the completed listing; separate purposes remain unresolved where stated.

The pointer table at `$B33D` names 29 lists at `$B377–$BD8A`, containing 176 records in total. `$A367` reads each 14-byte record: its first byte chooses an array index; offsets 2 and 4 supply two little-endian words; offsets 6, 8, 10 and 12 supply state and selectors. The selector at offset 8 indexes the pointer table at `$BD8B`, and offset 10 indexes the animation pointer table at `$C4F5`. A first byte of `$FF` ends the list, and the byte two positions later is copied to `$16`. The pointer table and lists match the entry and play snapshots. `test_broadway_scene_records.js` checks the initializer against all 29 lists in the processor model. These initialize the scripted ghost slots. Fields are stored as little-endian words even where the initializer only needs the low byte; the skipped high bytes are part of that fixed 14-byte record layout.

The 52-byte order at `$C8CB` chooses among the record lists and ends with `$FF`; `$A353` wraps on that marker. The main loop calls it and initializes the next list when `$AE87` returns 1. With `$16` zero, that check requires the five state entries at even offsets 0–8 of `$B172` to differ from 1 and the entry at offset `$18` to be zero. With `$16` nonzero, it requires the five entries to differ from both 1 and 2. State 1 is active movement, state 2 runs a terminating/death animation, state 3 follows the special motion path, and state 0 is inactive. A new wave waits for the applicable active and death states to finish.

The record field at offset 8 selects a pointer from `$BD8B`; the first 114 entries point into byte streams at `$BE6F–$C4F4`. The loop at `$A492` reads a stream command, selects a handler from the 58-entry table at `$A6E1`, advances the stream pointer and calls it. It stores the resulting pointer and repeats when the handler returns with Y nonzero. All 113 distinct stream segments are decoded in the listing, including command widths, parameters and branch selectors. The table has 114 entries because one stream is shared.

Path commands `$00–$0C` have been traced in the listing. `$00` returns immediately; `$01–$0B` consume between one and three parameter bytes and set indexed state, including coordinate-derived values in `$B0D6/$B0D7` and `$B0F0/$B0F1` for commands `$02–$09`. `$0C` selects a sprite sequence through `$C4F5`, sets state 2 and tells the dispatcher to continue. The commands configure ghost motion, waits and animation; `$0C` enters state 2 for the selected terminating animation.

The listing also traces path commands `$0D–$10`, `$13`, `$16–$18` and `$1C`. The `$0D` handler copies X to the processor stack pointer, changes X, and later copies the stack pointer back to X before executing RTS. None of the 113 decoded stream segments contains command `$0D`. This stored handler changes the hardware stack pointer; it is not required by the supplied wave scripts.

Commands `$14`, `$15` and `$19–$1B` have also been traced. `$19` and `$1A` conditionally replace the stream pointer using a selector from the following byte; `$1B` updates a three-step counter and can advance two stored operands. `$15` leaves a ground-slime droplet; `$1B` counts a ghost contact with the statue and charges one supply row every third contact. Both were followed in VICE.

Commands `$11` and `$12` compare the low bytes of differences between two indexed coordinate pairs after adding half of a parameter. When both comparisons pass, they select an animation for the current or the other entry respectively. On its successful path `$12`, like `$0D`, copies X into the stack pointer and later reads it back before RTS; neither `$11` nor `$12` occurs in the 113 decoded stream segments. The malformed stack-changing success path is therefore outside these supplied scripts.

The dispatcher also selects coordinate handlers: `$1E` adds signed eight-bit values to the two 16-bit coordinates; `$1F–$22` advance a phase and use it in both curve lookups, while `$23–$26` subtract the phase step. `$31` adds four to the second coordinate. The supplied streams use their configured signed curves to move the ghosts. These handlers define additional motion commands; the decoded supplied streams use commands `$02–$09` for curve setup.

The 114 path pointers have 113 distinct targets. Taken in address order, those targets divide `$BE6F–$C4F4` into 113 segments; linear decoding with the command handlers accounts for all 1,670 bytes without crossing a target boundary. The listing records each segment and its parameter bytes. The script contains 20 distinct command values, including `$00`; none is `$0D` or `$12`, whose stored handlers change the hardware stack pointer. Branch selectors stay within the same 114-entry pointer table.

## Slime, shots, and pedestrians

The five positions at `$A33F-$A348` belong to ground-slime droplets, with active flags at `$A349`. A ghost hit advances `$B1A6,X`; on the threshold in zero-page `$16`, `$97F1` switches to its death script. The captured first scene uses threshold zero with an initial counter of `$FF`, so the first hit triggers that script. Its command `$15` creates a droplet from the ghost X position. VICE followed the native death-path updater through this creation.

`$B22A` scrolls the droplets with the road; `$B309` draws them as code `$FC` in the street row. `$B26C` checks each against the three walking figures. A matching figure removes the droplet, moves the gauge cursor back one three-byte row, and sets the refill flag consumed by `$8314`. VICE confirmed the pointer change and the removed droplet.

The displayed supply gauge at `$4180` copies depletion rows from `$41C0` and refill rows from the full source `$4240`. `$91A2` charges one row for a replacement fireball and resets the 22-cell shot bar. Script command `$1B` at `$AC21` charges one row per three ghost contacts with the statue. Both charges were measured in VICE. A nonzero stored flag at `$8016` bypasses these charges and the countdown-failure branch; the shipped value is zero.


The coordinate source contains 21 separator spans totalling 167 bytes between its lookup views. Across both datasets, 8,192 direct calls per dataset (32 selectors × 256 phases) read none of these separator bytes, with a positive read of `$2000`. During the artificial native VICE run to `$C9D5`, watches on the first eight separator spans and the backdrop tail `$1F80–$1FFF` also remained at zero; the column-update control and success checkpoint fired. These results establish the coordinate reader’s bounds and this run’s accesses, not the absence of any other conceivable consumer.

The ordinary winning route in `../../broadway-route.json` starts from `work/play-clean.vsf`. It pulses fire for eight frames, releases it for eight, alternates up and down every 80 frames, and presses Space for the first four frames. The second scroll pass begins at frame 3,137 (joystick read 1,569; Space scan 3,138). Success arrives at frame 11,438, joystick read 5,721 and Space scan 11,443. VICE replay agrees on both final read counts, column `$0AE0`, supply cursor `$D5`, zero resource bypass and one success entry, with zero failures. These measurements establish an ordinary winning control route for this captured state; the earlier episodes on its capture route used a forced success.
