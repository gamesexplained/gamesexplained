# Broadway facts

The listing's captured play state is `work/play.vsf`; its route uses a forced airshaft success and a retry, as documented in the game's `orientation.md`. `work/entry.vsf` catches the next boot at `$8009`, and `work/play-clean.vsf` records that boot in play without a retry. The two play states differ in runtime-patched operands and need to be reconciled before changing the listing's snapshot.

- The saved machine has `$01 = $35`, the hardware IRQ vector `$FFFE` pointing to `$84C9`, and the RAM vector at `$0314` pointing to `$84E2`. At `$84C9` the program saves A, X and Y, inspects the stacked status and jumps through `$0314`.
- The short executed-address capture from this play state recorded 2,078 instruction starts. It covers only that interval; the disassembler's additional control-flow walk and the unclassified loaded memory need examination.
- The Broadway prompt snapshot `work/prompt.vsf` has the same hardware IRQ entry `$84C9`. Its `$1000–$1BFF` and RAM under `$D000–$DFFF` are byte-identical to the play snapshot; 19,914 of 20,480 bytes in `$8000–$CFFF` agree. These observations support treating the prompt as a state of this part. They do not identify the loader hand-over.

At `$8C59`, the game reads joystick port 2 and calls four direction helpers. `$91DD/$91EE` subtract or add 3 to the immediate operand at `$8524`, which the raster code writes to sprite 5 Y; `$91FF/$9210` do the same to `$851F`, written to sprite 5 X. The comparisons are made before the three increments or decrements, so a result can cross a threshold. From `work/play-clean.vsf`, ten frames of right, up or down changed `(X,Y)` from `(112,120)` to `(127,120)`, `(112,105)` or `(112,135)` respectively; left reached `(103,120)` and then stopped on its boundary test. A checkpoint at `$8C65` counted five joystick reads over ten frames in the rightward trial. `test_broadway.js` checks all byte values and directional combinations against the four routines.

Sprite 5 uses three shapes at `$40C0`, `$4100` and `$4140`: the raster handler selects pointer values 3, 4 and 5 in VIC bank 1, incrementing the pointer after four visits to `$853A` and wrapping after 5. The three 63-byte shapes are unchanged between the Broadway entry and play snapshots. A captured frame reconstructed from the video registers and memory matched all 104,448 pixels of the VICE capture.

The Broadway raster chain starts at `$84E2` and continues through `$856F`, `$8595`, `$85C6`, `$85FB`, `$8637`, `$866B`, `$86A0` and `$8718`. In a 20-frame VICE trial each of the nine handlers ran 19 times. The middle handlers reposition sprites 6 and 7 at several vertical bands, and `$8718` calls the sound update before scheduling the first handler again.

In the captured frame the VIC uses bank 1. `$8595` writes `$1D` to `$D018` at the middle split, selecting the character set at `$7000`; `$8718` writes `$1F` at the lower split, selecting `$7800`. Both 2,048-byte sets are unchanged between the entry and play snapshots. The complete captured frame, including the register changes, matched the VICE image pixel for pixel.

The fire path at `$8CB5` uses `$8C10` as a latch. In a VICE trial from `work/play-clean.vsf`, holding fire for two consecutive ten-frame intervals reached the new-press path once and set `$96F0`; after ten frames released, another ten-frame press reached it again and set `$96F2`. The path checks the first flag before moving to the next candidate. The full lifetime and collision behavior of these slots remain open.

The Broadway backdrop occupies twelve 480-byte rows at `$0900–$1F7F`; entry, play and clean play snapshots agree across the range. The scroll routine at `$87F4` takes one code from each row, using a stride of `$01E0` (480), and writes them to screen positions 40 bytes apart. The initial column pointer is `$0900`, and the check at `$87D2` enters the success path at `$0AE0`, after 480 columns.

Three direct VICE calls with column offsets 0, 78 and 479 produced the corresponding twelve source bytes. `test_broadway_map.js` checks all 480 columns against the processor model and those three VICE fixtures. The Levels tab draws the strip with the `$7000` character set and the palette of the captured middle-screen region; sprites and later palette changes are omitted. The whole sequence has not been recorded in play.

The scroll routine reads the snapshot bytes in `$0900–$1F7F` as backdrop data, although a static disassembly had labelled some bytes near `$1000` as code. In the Censor route, `$0900–$0A29` is unchanged from the preceding airshaft snapshot: the Censor Broadway file begins output at `$0A2A`. The Jondi Broadway file writes from `$0900`, and all 298 bytes of that prefix differ from the captured Censor state. Across `$0900–$1F7F` the captures differ in 4,891 bytes; their `$4000–$7FFF`, `$8000–$CEFF` and `$E000–$F7FF` ranges match. The captured Broadway execution map has no instruction starts in this region. The later story transition overwrites the saved engine into lower memory before jumping there, so the transition’s jump does not establish that these backdrop bytes execute.

The routines at `$AD07` and `$AD39` use X to select an entry, and Y as an index into a table selected by `$B03A,X` or `$B054,X`. The first adds the signed table byte to a 16-bit base at `$B0D6/$B0D7,X`; the second subtracts it from the base at `$B0F0/$B0F1,X`. The resulting pairs go to `$AF9E/$AF9F,X` and `$AFB8/$AFB9,X` respectively. A direct VICE trial with base `$0180`, selectors `$70` and `$38`, and Y values 0, 64, 128 and 192 recorded eight results in `broadway-curve-vice.json`; `test_broadway_curve.js` checks those and all 256 indices of the two tables against the processor model. It also checks all indices of the 32 views selected by the two lookup routines. Each view matches in the entry and play snapshots; some overlap. The identity of the moving objects and the indices used during normal play remain open.

The Censor Broadway unpacker writes `$0A2A–$CEFF`, `$E000–$EF76` and `$F000–$F7FF`; its 56,397 stable output bytes match the handover snapshot. The part lies over the second story and owns those output ranges. The map comparison assembles the retained `$0900–$0A29` prefix from the airshaft listing with the rest from Broadway. The separate Jondi file writes from `$0900` and matched 56,695 stable bytes at its own handover; its differing backdrop and coordinate data still need their own analysis.

The setup at `$802A` initializes state, copies 1,000 screen bytes from `$3C00–$3FE7` to `$4400–$47E7`, prepares colour RAM and selects VIC bank 1. The source bytes match in the entry and play snapshots; the initialization then patches several destination cells. At `$8260` a keyboard-bit sequence can gate the loop, with waits for three changes of `$801D` between transitions; the key’s gameplay purpose remains open.

The routine at `$A540` expands an entry’s computed position into four sets of offsets and values at `$B1C0–$B1FA`. Its choice at `$AFD3` changes two values, and a non-1 value at `$B172` writes `$08` to the last three slots. The raster routine at `$C901` reads these arrays as X and Y positions and sprite pointers for sprites 0–4, limiting Y to `$9D`. The entries’ gameplay identities remain open.

The sequence routine at `$A4EE` selects a pair of bytes for the sprite layout. At a `$FF` terminator, its mode either holds the previous pair, restarts the sequence, or clears the entry’s active flag.

The paired-state routine at `$9D05` alternates patched addresses between two groups in `$F000–$F8xx`, then dispatches separate updates according to `$9C8C/$9C8E`. The routines at `$9E05/$9EC6` move two X coordinates and copy 64-byte frames into `$4280/$42C0` every fourth movement update. The raster handler at `$86D6–$86FE` uses those low X bytes and selects sprite pointers `$0A/$0B` for sprites 3 and 4. The objects’ identities remain open.

The frame sources at `$F000–$F7FF` comprise 32 records of 64 bytes. The two movement routines select records in `$40` steps and copy them to the sprite slots; all 2,048 source bytes match between the captured entry and play snapshots. The Graphics tab displays their first 63 bytes with illustrative colours.

The ledger includes 19,852 bytes previously outside the count. Forty stretches match stable unpacker output at the entry snapshot; another is a copy visible in play whose source is present at entry. Their individual purposes remain open.

Six groups of animation sequences start at `$C597`, `$C5DF`, `$C627`, `$C66F`, `$C6B7` and `$C6FF`. Each group has four `$FF $FF`-terminated sequences of pointer/mode pairs. The modes are zero and the groups select the eight sprite pointers `$60–$67`, `$68–$6F`, `$70–$77`, `$78–$7F`, `$80–$87` and `$88–$8F` respectively. The selector at `$A4EE` supplies a pointer to the layout code, which writes the sprite pointer for the raster handler. The corresponding 48 slots occupy `$5800–$63FF`; their sequence and bitmap bytes match in the entry and play snapshots. The objects represented and the final byte of each slot remain open.

Other pointer/mode sequences at `$C561`, `$C573`, `$C585`, `$C747`, `$C781` and `$C78B` select slots `$28–$37`, `$44–$4A`, `$4C–$56` and `$58–$61`; all use mode zero and end with `$FF $FF`. The selector table at `$C4F5` points to each sequence. Their bitmap slots and the sequence bytes match in the entry and play snapshots.

The sequence at `$C757` selects sprite pointers `$90–$95` and the blank slot `$08`, each twice; `$C775` selects `$0C`. Both use mode zero and end with `$FF $FF`. Their selected bitmap bytes match between entry and play.

Three sequences at `$C849`, `$C853` and `$C85D` supply base sprite pointers and modes 1 or 2. For an active entry, the mode 1 layout at `$A579` supplies the base and the next three pointers; mode 2 at `$A5E3` supplies the base, the next pointer and `$BC/$BD`. Together the sequences can select slots `$9E–$BD`, at `$6780–$6F7F`. The sequence and bitmap bytes match in the entry and play snapshots; the objects remain unidentified.

The pointer table at `$B33D` names 29 lists at `$B377–$BD8A`, containing 176 records in total. `$A367` reads each 14-byte record: its first byte chooses an array index; offsets 2 and 4 supply two little-endian words; offsets 6, 8, 10 and 12 supply state and selectors. The selector at offset 8 indexes the pointer table at `$BD8B`, and offset 10 indexes the animation pointer table at `$C4F5`. A first byte of `$FF` ends the list, and the byte two positions later is copied to `$16`. The pointer table and lists match the entry and play snapshots. `test_broadway_scene_records.js` checks the initializer against all 29 lists in the processor model. The objects and the skipped bytes remain open.

The 52-byte order at `$C8CB` chooses among the record lists and ends with `$FF`; `$A353` wraps on that marker. The main loop calls it and initializes the next list when `$AE87` returns 1. With `$16` zero, that check requires the five state entries at even offsets 0–8 of `$B172` to differ from 1 and the entry at offset `$18` to be zero. With `$16` nonzero, it requires the five entries to differ from both 1 and 2. The gameplay meaning of these states remains open.

The record field at offset 8 selects a pointer from `$BD8B`; the first 114 entries point into byte streams at `$BE6F–$C4F4`. The loop at `$A492` reads a stream command, selects a handler from the 58-entry table at `$A6E1`, advances the stream pointer and calls it. It stores the resulting pointer and repeats when the handler returns with Y nonzero. The handler entries are disassembled, but their command formats and the streams remain open.

Path commands `$00–$0C` have been traced in the listing. `$00` returns immediately; `$01–$0B` consume between one and three parameter bytes and set indexed state, including coordinate-derived values in `$B0D6/$B0D7` and `$B0F0/$B0F1` for commands `$02–$09`. `$0C` selects a sprite sequence through `$C4F5`, sets state 2 and tells the dispatcher to continue. What these states mean during play remains open.

The listing also traces path commands `$0D–$10`, `$13`, `$16–$18` and `$1C`. The `$0D` handler copies X to the processor stack pointer, changes X, and later copies the stack pointer back to X before executing RTS. Its caller stack state and whether the command executes during play remain open.

Commands `$14`, `$15` and `$19–$1B` have also been traced. `$19` and `$1A` conditionally replace the stream pointer using a selector from the following byte; `$1B` updates a three-step counter and can advance two stored operands. The listing records the conditions and remaining unknown effects.

Commands `$11` and `$12` compare the low bytes of differences between two indexed coordinate pairs after adding half of a parameter. When both comparisons pass, they select an animation for the current or the other entry respectively. On its successful path `$12`, like `$0D`, copies X into the stack pointer and later reads it back before RTS; the caller stack state and whether the path executes in play remain open.

The dispatcher also selects coordinate handlers: `$1E` adds signed eight-bit values to the two 16-bit coordinates; `$1F–$22` advance a phase and use it in both curve lookups, while `$23–$26` subtract the phase step. `$31` adds four to the second coordinate. Their reachability and effect on the scene remain open.

The 114 path pointers have 113 distinct targets. Taken in address order, those targets divide `$BE6F–$C4F4` into 113 segments; linear decoding with the command handlers accounts for all 1,670 bytes without crossing a target boundary. The listing records each segment and its parameter bytes. Conditional pointer changes, timing and actual reachability remain open.

Broadway’s first tune record at `$EB5C` points to three voice lists containing 96, 48 and 11 stream pointers. Their 14 distinct entries cover `$ECAD–$EED6`, with `$EE92` entering a tail shared with the stream at `$EE8C`. The listing records an ordered command decode for those 554 unique bytes. The other two tune records select six short streams at `$EEDD–$EF75`; each ends with `$91`. Entry and play snapshots match across the records and streams. Timing and the reachability of every command remain open.

At `$8260`, the game scans the P key and waits for release, another press and another release. A short VICE trial from the clean play snapshot changed `$8027` from 0 to 1 on the first press; after release the program waited at `$827F`. A second press and release cleared `$8027` and returned the program to the main-loop wait at `$82A2`. The CIA port also reads joystick-port-1 lines.

At `$9FB6`, the game scans Space and uses `$9C81` as a held-key latch. A new press sets `$9C82`, toggles `$9C80` between 0 and 1, and resets three raster pointer operands to `$50/$53/$56` or `$58/$5B/$5E` respectively. In a short VICE trial from the clean play snapshot, two separate Space presses changed `$9C80/$9C81/$9C82` from `01 00 00` to `00 01 01`, then after release to `00 00 01`, and on the second press to `01 01 01`. The identity and full movement of these sprites remain open.

The three Space-controlled sprite positions use the low/high pairs at `$9C6A–$9C6F`, which the raster code writes as X coordinates. In a short VICE trial, their low bytes began at `$20/$28/$30`, reached `$29/$31/$39` after the first Space press and twelve released frames, and returned to `$20/$28/$30` after the second press and twelve released frames. The ordinary paths at `$A036/$A084/$A0D2` increment the coordinates, while `$A120/$A171/$A1C3` decrement them and select the opposite animation range. The extracted `$50` and `$58` bitmaps depict opposite-facing human figures; collecting slime remains unverified.

At `$9976`, the game compares three figure X positions with two other sprite positions after halving them. If the low-byte test falls inside the other sprite’s 24-unit range, `$9A1B` can set the corresponding `$973B` flag and start sound effect `$18`; pointer value `$08` suppresses the relevant check. `test_broadway_overlap.js` checks a matching position and the blank-pointer guard in the processor model. The gameplay meaning of this flag and the other sprites remains open.

Sprite 5 uses the immediate X and Y operands at `$851F/$8524`. At `$9AEA`, when its Y is at least `$AA`, the game checks its halved X against the three Space-controlled figure positions. A match calls `$9B71`, which sets the figure flag at `$973B,Y`, resets the sprite coordinates to `$66/$74`, and starts sound effect `$18` if the flag was clear. The processor-model check in `test_broadway_overlap.js` exercises the match, vertical threshold, and already-set flag. `$9A4B` checks the sprite against two other positions and changes their state on a match; their gameplay identities remain open.

`$9B9B` compares six active entries against the three figure positions when each entry’s `$9708,Y` value is in `$B5–$C9`. On a match with a figure whose flag is not 1, it writes `$26` to `$8C11,Y`; `$9C4A` sets a clear figure flag and starts sound effect `$18`. The entries and the meaning of the state byte remain open.

`$9821` makes a similar comparison between the six active entries and two other positions; a match writes `$26` to `$8C11,Y`, changes that position’s state to 2 and starts sound effect `$18`. `$98DA` compares the sprite 5 position with bounds in five scene entries. A match invokes `$91A2` and advances a counter; when the counter reaches `$16`, `$97F1` selects a new path pointer and changes the entry state. These objects’ identities remain open.
