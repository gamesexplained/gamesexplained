# Ghostbusters II — orientation

The first two scenes use Censor Design's 1989 crack, [CSDb release 78781](https://csdb.dk/release/?id=78781), in `work/Ghostbusters2.d64`. The museum capture uses the Jondi/Censor release, [CSDb release 227946](https://csdb.dk/release/?id=227946), in `work/ghostbusters-ii.d64`. `media.json` lists both disks. These are cracked releases, so their code must not automatically be attributed to the publisher.

On VICE release v3.13.1, `v3.13.1-linux-x86_64-gui.zip`, the emulator check passed 56 of 57 checks. `pause-at-instruction` failed; use `pause()` in `kit/c64/vice.py`. The test used PAL, and VICE wrote sound to `/dev/null` through the `fs` sound device.

## From power-on to the first episode

Power-cycle VICE and autostart the disk. Hold fire on joystick port 1 at the Censor intro, release it, and press fire at the end of the first story. Warp mode shortens the loads. A stopping execution checkpoint at `$8009` caught the start of the airshaft program; this clean-boot state is `parts/airshaft/work/entry.vsf`. Delete the checkpoint, turn warp off and resume; `parts/airshaft/work/play-clean.vsf` shows the player on the rope. The Source listing uses that play snapshot, with the entry snapshot for the loaded-data audit. The named-loader observations below confirm transfer to `$8009`; a control-read recording from power-on remains a follow-up.

On this build `port: 1` pulls bits on `$DC01`, and `port: 2` pulls bits on `$DC00`, as the emulator check measured. The Censor intro accepted fire on port 1; the airshaft's success prompt reads fire on port 2.

To reach the next scene without playing the airshaft to completion, load `parts/airshaft/work/play-clean.vsf`, pause safely, set the PC to `$0897`, and resume. This enters the game's success-message path without satisfying its gameplay conditions. Release fire, then press fire on joystick port 2: the game restores the story and loader code. A checkpoint at `$8009` catches Broadway's initialization in `parts/broadway/work/entry.vsf`. Remove the checkpoint, turn warp off, resume and press fire at the Broadway prompt to reach `work/play-clean.vsf` in that part. The listing's `work/play.vsf` came from an earlier attempt which ran at warp speed to its retry screen; fire at normal speed then restarted play. This is an artificial route for capturing the next part, not evidence of a completed airshaft.

On the Jondi/Censor disk, autostart the named `GHOSTBUSTERS II` file: the wildcard selects a different directory entry. Its intro responds to port 2 fire, shows a loading screen and then asks `BITTE SPACE DRUECKEN`. Pressing Space left a black screen for 5,500 frames; the stopped CPU was at `$A544`. This attempt did not establish a complete boot route.

For the museum, load `parts/broadway/work/play-clean.vsf`, attach the Jondi/Censor disk to drive 8, pause, set PC to `$C9D5`, and resume. This enters Broadway's success-message path without meeting its win conditions. Press fire on port 2. A checkpoint at `$8009` catches the museum's initialization in `parts/museum/work/entry.vsf`; remove it, turn warp off, and resume to reach the rope scene in `work/play.vsf`. Continuing from the Censor disk instead reached a garbled BASIC screen without hitting `$8009`. Its `7.THE MUSEUM` file is 15,554 bytes including the load address; the Jondi/Censor file is 28,376 bytes. The two versions differ after their first 10,668 bytes, and the shorter file does not yield the complete museum image: its third decompressed segment diverges and its fourth exhausts the input. The media ledger identifies it as a damaged partial copy.

From `parts/museum/work/play.vsf`, holding down for two frames and up for five frames, repeating that pair 110 times with `vice.frames()` from a stopped machine, reached the first landing in two VICE trials; the state is `parts/museum/work/floor.vsf`. The 377 reads captured from that segment are in `museum-rope-route.json`. The kit's C64 machine replayed them from the same snapshot and reached the landing. A recording from power-on remains open.

For `parts/museum/work/floor-two.vsf`, advance 120 neutral frames from the first landing, repeat the same input pair 110 times for the second descent, then advance 120 more neutral frames. This continuation was observed once in VICE; a control-read recording and second-machine replay remain open.

## Parts and loader

The manual describes three gameplay episodes: the airshaft, Broadway and the museum. The transition routes above capture each episode; the title, story scenes, ending, shared stash, and restored engine each have an ownership ledger and listing.

The loader uses the KERNAL's internal OPEN entry `$F3D5`, not its LOAD entry. Break there and read the filename length at `$B7` and pointer at `$BB–$BC` to see its requests. From the opening-story fire prompt, the Censor disk opens `3*` and reaches the airshaft entry at `$8009`. After the forced airshaft success it opens `4*`; at the following story's fire wait, pressing fire opens `5*` and reaches Broadway at `$8009`. After the forced Broadway success on the Jondi/Censor disk it opens `6*`; at the following story's fire wait, pressing fire opens `7*` and reaches the museum at `$8009`. Each wildcard has a single corresponding numbered filename in that disk's directory. The newly captured Broadway and museum entries agree with the earlier entry snapshots in `$1000–$3FFF`, `$8000–$CFFF` and `$E000–$FFFF`. The airshaft entries agree in the first two ranges and differ only in the two hardware IRQ-vector bytes in the last range.

The shared stash also contains the lower-memory loader dispatch copied to `$0400–$04EA`. Its eight jump entries at `$0400–$0417` select the patterns `1*` through `8*` from `$0497–$04A6`. The entries for `3*`, `5*` and `7*` restore the ROM environment, load, write `$1B` to `$D011` and jump to `$8009`. The other five call the common loader and return. This code supports the observed request sequence, but does not by itself establish each packed file's complete output.

On a fresh boot with the Censor disk, a breakpoint at `$F3D5` catches `1*`; the load reaches the title prompt saved as `work/title-after-1.vsf`. After pressing Space, a trace with non-stopping checkpoints counted one entry to the loader at `$0651`, one entry to the KERNAL routine at `$F3D5` and 2,931 calls to the byte receiver at `$067B` over 510 frames; `$B7/$BB–$BC` named `2*`. A separate continuation reached the first-story wait captured as `work/story-from-2.vsf`. Compared with the earlier opening-story snapshot, the title has three different bytes in `$1000–$1BFF` and the first-story capture has none; the two first-story captures are also identical at `$C200–$C819`.

From the saved museum floor state `parts/museum/work/floor-two.vsf`, hold up on port 2 for 30 frames, then right for 180 frames and release both directions for two frames. The selected Ghostbuster enters the portrait room; the resulting state is `parts/museum/work/alternate-observed.vsf`. The earlier episodes on this route were advanced through forced success paths.

From `parts/museum/work/alternate-observed.vsf`, hold left on port 2 for 90 frames to return to the previous room. This continuation was observed in VICE; a control-read recording and second-machine replay remain open.

To inspect the ending, load `parts/museum/work/floor-two.vsf` with the Jondi/Censor disk attached, set the PC to `$0A16` and resume. This enters the congratulations path without satisfying the museum's win conditions. After its fire prompt, the loader opens `8*`; the story wait is `work/ending.vsf`. The scene displays a congratulations message. Its `$1000–$1BFF` differs in three bytes from the opening-story snapshot, while `$C200–$C819` differs in 1,136 bytes. This is a forced route, not a recorded win.

The second and third story waits are saved as `work/second-story.vsf` and `work/third-story.vsf`. Relative to the opening-story snapshot, the second has 43 different bytes in `$1000–$1BFF` and the third has three, while their `$C200–$C8FF` contents differ extensively. That comparison suggests a reused story engine with different content; which bytes each compressed file writes and whether it contains code overwritten before the snapshot remain open. The file ledger therefore keeps the numbered files open.

On the play snapshot, `$01` read `$35`; the RAM hardware IRQ vector at `$FFFE` read `$8D53` and the KERNAL IRQ vector at `$0314` read `$8D6F`. A captured frame recorded nine successive IRQ-vector values, listed in `parts/airshaft/facts.md`. The loader shows a Censor intro and loads the title, story and first episode in sequence. The decoded load ranges are recorded in `unpack-map.json`.

Every disk file was compared with both airshaft snapshots in 16-byte pieces at file offsets divisible by 16, omitting the two-byte PRG load address. None of the eight numbered files had an exact piece in either RAM image. The boot file had two matching pieces at a shared offset, with 111 of its 6,607 payload bytes equal there. This does not rule out transformed or shorter matches; the file-to-memory mapping remains open.

From the story's fire prompt, `loads.py` watched `$FFD5` for 40 seconds as fire was pressed. With warp enabled the game loaded the airshaft and reached the retry screen during that watch, which recorded no KERNAL LOAD call. This route therefore did not pass through that entry; the loader it used and the file-to-memory mapping remain open.

From the forced airshaft-success route, a checkpoint at `$1000` catches the return to the story code immediately after the copy at `$02A7`. At that stop, all 3,072 bytes of `$1000–$1BFF` match the RAM beneath `$D000–$DBFF`, and all 1,024 bytes of `$0400–$07FF` match the RAM beneath `$DC00–$DFFF`. The state is `work/resident-copied.vsf`. The source bytes also match across the four earlier scene snapshots. Other possible consumers and the precise ownership of this shared stash remain open.

A separate VICE memory-map trace cleared the access map at the opening-story fire prompt, continued through the `3*` load and stopped at `$8009`. Reading the map required briefly resuming VICE after that stop, so it includes an unmeasured amount of airshaft start-up as well as the loader and the preceding story wait. It recorded writes at every address in `$0400–$CCFF` and `$E000–$EFE1`. Comparing RAM at the prompt and entry found 46,086 changed bytes overall, all of them among the recorded writes. The map is an upper bound on the load's writes, not a proof that every recorded byte belongs to the file; the transfer's exact ownership and transient writes remain open. The monitor command was `memmapshow 12`: VICE reads `12` as hexadecimal, selecting the RAM and ROM-addressed write bits.

Equivalent traces from the later story waits to the `$8009` entry stops recorded 57,554 written addresses for Broadway and 59,157 for the museum. Their largest continuous runs were `$0A2A–$CEFF` and `$0400–$CFFF` respectively. The prompt-to-entry RAM comparisons changed 50,733 and 51,770 bytes, all within their respective recorded write maps. As with the airshaft trace, the map also includes the story wait and some execution after the breakpoint; the numbers do not by themselves identify every file's exact output.

During the loading animation on another pass, the CPU was observed in a routine at `$062F–$0634` which calls `$067B`. That routine banks the KERNAL in and calls `$EE13` (`KERNAL_ACPTRj`, the serial receive entry), then banks it out again. This identifies a byte-receive path but does not identify the file or establish the unpacked ranges.

The captured loader at `$0500` was run against the numbered files with two different initial RAM fills. `analyse_unpack.js` records the ranges whose resulting bytes agree under both fills, the file offsets and the comparison with each captured snapshot in `unpack-map.json`. For the opening story, airshaft, Broadway and Jondi museum, all respectively 15,488, 54,498, 56,397 and 58,034 stable bytes match the captured state. The title, later stories and ending were captured after their loads. Their differences all fall within the sound-driver area: `$E0C2–$E14A`, `$E43B–$E43C` and `$EB58–$EB5B`, plus `$E464` in the ending. These ranges include mutable voice state, self-modified operands and the four-byte sound state; the cause of each changed byte remains open. The Censor museum file ends while the decoded fourth segment is still requesting input; its fourth header declares 24,660 bytes with only 3,381 remaining. This is consistent with an incomplete packed segment, but does not establish why the observed museum transition failed.

The title prompt reached after loading `1*` is also saved in `parts/title/work/entry.vsf`. The following Space press loads `2*` and reaches the opening story; the separate title load owns the ranges in `parts/title/part.json`.

For an alternate Broadway entry, load `parts/second-story/work/story.vsf`, attach the Jondi/Censor disk, and press fire on port 2 for two frames. The snapshot was still completing screen setup after that press; twenty neutral frames reached serial loading. Continue with warp and a stopping checkpoint at `$8009`, inspecting the loader state between bounded waits. The handover is `parts/broadway/work/jondi-entry.vsf`. All 56,695 stable bytes decoded from Jondi `5.THE LIBERTY` match that snapshot. The Censor file begins its first output at `$0A2A`, so `$0900–$0A29` in the Censor Broadway snapshot remains byte-identical to the preceding airshaft. All 298 of those bytes differ in the Jondi entry; the two captures also differ elsewhere in their backdrop.

The shared executing engine is represented by `parts/story-engine/work/entry.vsf`, a copy of the handover at `$1000` described above. It lies over the resident stash. Later story transitions restore this engine again; their own load ranges include that copy, but their coverage excludes it in favour of the shared engine listing. The later waits differ from the copied handover in runtime state and text buffers as recorded in `parts/story-engine/facts.md`.

For `parts/broadway-jondi/work/play.vsf`, advance 32 neutral frames from the alternate entry. At the observed wait at `$CA03`, press port 2 fire for two frames and release it; the resulting state waits in the game loop at `$82A2`. This route starts from a forced earlier success, not an unbroken playthrough.

An [alternative image of the Censor release](https://csdb.dk/release/?id=78781) is saved as `work/0805b.D64` (SHA-256 `7b30fc61675c3978738e8ee898c73af6aab839ce5711595305e0c500333fabb4`). Its complete Broadway and museum files match those on the Jondi disk byte for byte; the first Censor image has a shorter, incomplete museum file. Its additional files and allocated stray sectors are recorded in `media.json`.

A partial decode of the shorter Censor museum file consumed all 15,554 raw bytes. The first two complete segments produced 6,144 and 8,192 stable bytes matching the complete museum entry. The third complete segment produced 12,288 stable bytes, of which 3,158 differ from that entry. The following header at file offset 12,168 was `$CE $BB $CC $54 $60`; its segment exhausted the remaining input. These comparisons do not establish why the disk copy differs or whether its partial output can be used.


A normal port-1 fire press from the Censor intro reaches the boot unpacker. A stopping store checkpoint on `$D000` caught PC `$01A9`, immediately after the store at `$01A6`, with processor port `$01=$38` and A=`$8E`. This bank setting writes physical RAM below I/O. Continuing to the first `$1000` entry produced a resident stash at `$D000–$DFFF` identical in all 4,096 bytes to the later restored-engine capture. The unpacker reads through the patched load at `$0193`, treats `$F4` as a run/address marker, and writes through the patched store at `$01A6`. Its destination-address command changes that store’s two operand bytes.
