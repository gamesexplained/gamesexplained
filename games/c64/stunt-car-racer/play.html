01 · Differences

What differs from a C64

The game plays as it does on a C64, at the C64's pace. These are the places where the page cannot be the machine, and what it does instead.

On a C64On this page
The game loads from disk behind a loading picture, and its own loader jumps to the start-up at $3FFB.There is no loading. The page starts at $3FFB with the memory as the loader leaves it, and the main menu comes up after the start-up's work.
The start-up adds up the game's code and data, and on a sum that is not $9DFA it turns the border yellow and stops.The page carries the game's data and none of its code, so it cannot add up the code. It leaves the sum where the game's leaves it, takes as long as the game does, and goes on.
Each instruction takes its cycles, and the video chip takes some of the processor's on the lines where it fetches characters and sprites.The port executes no instructions. Each routine costs the cycles it costs the game, and the view costs what its lines cost: each pixel tested and drawn, each step of a line off the view. A pass of a race comes within a few per cent of the game's, so the view moves and the clocks count as they do on a C64. The video chip takes its cycles as the beam passes those lines.
The raster interrupt splits the screen into bands: the view's bitmap above and the dashboard below, the sprites placed again in each.Each band runs whole when its interrupt comes, and its writes to the video chip land where the game's land in that band on average, within a few cycles.
The SID plays the engine and the effects, written by the game as it runs.The site's model of the SID, with the filter of the C64's original 6581, plays what the port writes as each frame is shown.
A league, a Hall of Fame or a game for several is saved to a disc or a tape, and loaded from it.Both go to a disc the page keeps in this browser: what is saved there is there the next time the page is opened. The KERNAL's routines for it are written in JavaScript, as the page has no ROM.
The game reads a joystick in control port 2, and the keyboard.The arrows and Right Ctrl are the joystick, or the pad on a touch screen, and the letters, figures, Return, Space, = and F1 are the C64's keys of the same names; Backspace is DEL and Left Ctrl is C=. A tap shorter than a pass of the game is held until the game has read it.
