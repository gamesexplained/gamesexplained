The track is drawn in lines about seven times a second, into whichever of two pictures is hidden. The race's clocks count those pictures rather than time, and the races the player does not drive are settled by a roll of the dice.

01 · The view

The view is redrawn about seven times a second

The dashboard, the wheels and the flame change every frame, fifty times a second. The view of the track changes once every seven frames or so, when a new picture of it is finished.

Sixteen frames in a row of a practice race on Little Ramp, from the Play tab's game. A blue square marks a frame whose view is new: the interrupt has swapped the bitmap on show, from the one at $4000 to the one at $6000 or back.

Each pass of the race (RACE_LOOP, $3D05) reads the controls, moves the car and the opponent one step, and draws the view into the bitmap that is not on show. Then it asks the raster interrupt to swap the two (REQUEST_FLIP), and the interrupt does so at the top of the next frame, so the picture never tears. A pass on Little Ramp takes about seven frames.

The view is drawn piece by piece from the car forwards, four pieces of track a pass (DRAW_VIEW, $167A). Each piece's corners are turned into the eye's frame, projected onto the screen, joined by lines and filled. Drawing more pieces costs more time: with the count at $173E raised from 2 to 6, eight pieces were drawn a pass, and a pass took 8.8 frames against 7.1.

02 · The clocks

The race clock counts passes, not seconds

The clock on the dashboard adds 0.14 seconds every pass of the race, the time seven frames take. When a pass takes longer than seven frames, the clock falls behind the real time.

The clock is a minute of real racing at fifty frames a second. TICK_CLOCK ($109C) adds $14, 14 hundredths in decimal, to the clock once a pass. A pass on Little Ramp took 7.25 frames on average over 500 frames (69 passes), so the clock showed 9.66 seconds for ten seconds of play.

Lap times and the best lap are read off these clocks too. A track whose view takes longer to draw has a slower clock, so a lap there reads faster than it took.

03 · Damage

Hard landings crack the car, and big bumps put holes in it

The car rests on three springs. A landing that presses a spring past the track's damage line adds to the damage, and the crack across the top of the windscreen grows to match.

The springs keep a count of presses in a row ($E1), and a press adds damage only while that count is below a number the track sets: 3 on Roller Coaster, 10 on Big Ramp (WHEELS_ON_ROAD, $0A97). The Tracks tab lists the number for every track.

The crack is cut into the bitmap a pixel pair at a time (CRACK, $1B88). When it reaches the far side, or the damage reaches 128, the car is wrecked. A bump of 20 or more also takes one of the car's ten whole parts ($C719), drawn as a hole in the damage bar. A crack that grows into a hole adds damage rather than length.

The holes stay from race to race through a season and into the next. The car gets all ten parts back only after a season that ends with the player in the bottom division or as the winner of the top one (CHANGES, $375D). A practice race puts the parts back as they were before it.

04 · Tracks

Each track is a loop of pieces on a grid

The eight tracks are stored as descriptions, and the game builds a track from its description before every race.

Little Ramp from above, in VICE, before a practice race. The Tracks tab shows every track this way, from all four sides.

BUILD_TRACK ($EA34) reads a track's description (the pointers at $B220) into the tables at $0400-$0773: each piece's kind, the square of the map it sits on, its heights, its ends and its distance round the track. Little Ramp has 44 pieces, and Roller Coaster and Draw Bridge have 78 each. The same description sets the track's boost and how forgiving its springs are.

05 · The league

The races you do not drive are settled by dice

Each division has three drivers and one race a round. Every race the player is not in, in every division, is worked out from two numbers.

The odds count every pair of the two drivers' rolls as equally likely. Before each round of races RACE_OTHERS ($E87F) gives each driver a rating: its base from the table at $E8B6, 120 for Hot Rod down to 20 for Road Hog, plus 0 to 63 at random. RACE_RESULT ($E92C) then gives the race to the higher rating, a tie to a coin, and the fastest lap to the winner unless a draw of 96 in 256 gives it to the loser.

A win is worth two points and the fastest lap one. Points tie-break on wins, then on a coin (SORT_DIVISION, $E9A3). A season is six races, four of them the player's, and at its end the winner of each division goes up and the last comes down. Out of Division 1 only the player goes up, into the Super League.

06 · Sound

Sound: the engine is two voices an octave apart

There is no music in this game. The engine is two of the SID's voices an octave apart, and the sound effects come and go over it.

The engine's note follows the car's speed along the road, moving an eighth of the way towards it each pass (ENGINE_SOUND, $E104), and the raster interrupt moves voices 1 and 3 on by a step every frame. The filter's cut-off follows the note too. A sound effect is seven bytes: its voice, its waveform, its envelope, its pitch, its pulse width and how many frames its gate is held (START_EFFECT, $8655).
