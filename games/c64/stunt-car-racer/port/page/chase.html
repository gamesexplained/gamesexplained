The Chase View switch under the keys puts the camera behind your car and above it, while the game draws a race. The track and both cars are drawn again each time the game draws a picture, from the game's own memory, in its colours and its fat pixels, over everything above the dashboard. The dashboard is the game's own.

The track

The track is the one the game builds for the race (BUILD_TRACK, its pieces at $0400-$0773). Each piece's points are worked out by the game's own routines (PIECE_LOAD, PIECE_POINTS, PIECE_POINT_IN_VIEW), run on a copy of the machine with the eye put at the map's corner, so that they come out in the map's units: a square is $800 each way and the road $180 wide, the flat road is at height $500 and the ground at $200. The points come in pairs, the road's two edges, and between two pairs the road is a solid block down to the ground. Its lines are the ones the game draws (POINTS_TO_LINES): the edges white and black by turns for the road just ahead and black further on, a line across at each piece's end and a white one at the start, and a line down to the ground wherever a side of the road shows.

The cars

Both cars are the shape the game draws the opponent's car (DRAW_OPPONENT, $E1B1): a box on four wheels, worked out from four corners. The game builds it on the screen from the corners' places there; Chase View builds the same shape in the track's units. The car is 81 across and half as long again ($C34D is $1B of the road's 256 to each side). The wheels are boxes a quarter of the width across and as tall. The body is a wedge, half the width across: at the back from half a wheel's height up to twice it, and at the front from half a wheel's height to its top.

  • The opponent's car is placed by its corners as OPP_CORNERS leaves them, and by the heights of its wheels (OPP_WHEELS).
  • Your car is placed by its position ($0100-$0108), its heading ($0122, $0125), and the heights of its three points, which the game works out from its pitch and its roll (CAR_POINT_HEIGHTS). A point's wheel sits $28 below it, or on the road when its spring is pressed into it.
  • The crane's chains hang straight down to the front wheels while the game draws them (CRANE_CHAINS, $8B51). From the driver's seat the game draws them at pixel columns $54 and $A8, from row $AF up, ending on the front tyres. As the crane lets go, the game lifts their ends by 8, 24, 48 and 80 rows over four passes and then stops drawing them; Chase View lifts them by the same rows at the chains' distance from the driver.
  • Your car's front wheels turn while you steer. The game steers all the way or not at all (CONTROLS sets $C5 to $F1 for left, $0F for right, 0 for straight), and the car turns slowly after it, so the wheels show at once what the stick is doing. The wheels are the mod's own: the game never draws them on the opponent's car.

The camera

The camera is 280 units behind the car and 95 above it, looking 400 ahead. It turns after the car, closing 6 % of the gap each video frame (a third of it over a pass of seven), and moves to the car at once when the game puts it somewhere new.

The game's lens is a cylinder. A point's place across the screen is its bearing, 256 fat pixels to a quarter turn (PROJECT_X), and its place up the screen is its height over its distance along the ground, about 83 lines for a unit of height at a distance of one (PROJECT_Y). So on this page's square pixels the game draws a unit of height about a quarter as tall as a unit across is wide, and Chase View draws heights the same. Its lens is flat and wider than the game's: it sees 76 degrees across, where the game sees 56.

What it leaves out

The dust thrown up at the road's edge, the crane itself, which the game never draws, and the crack across the windscreen, which is a part of the cockpit. The game itself is unchanged: its memory is only read, and with Chase View on or off it runs byte for byte the same race. The drawing costs the game no time.
