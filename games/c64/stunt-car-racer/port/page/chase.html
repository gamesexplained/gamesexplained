The Chase cam button under the picture puts the camera behind your car and above it, while the game draws a race. It and Heli cam (section 02) take turns: pressing one switches the other off, and pressing it again goes back to the driver's seat. The track and both cars are drawn again each time the game draws a picture, from the game's own memory, in its colours and its fat pixels, over everything above the dashboard. The dashboard is the game's own.

The track

The track is the one the game builds for the race (BUILD_TRACK, its pieces at $0400-$0773). Each piece's points are worked out by the game's own routines (PIECE_LOAD, PIECE_POINTS, PIECE_POINT_IN_VIEW), run on a copy of the machine with the eye put at the map's corner, so that they come out in the map's units: a square is $800 each way and the road $180 wide, the flat road is at height $500 and the ground at $200. The points come in pairs, the road's two edges, and between two pairs the road is a solid block down to the ground. Its lines are the ones the game draws (POINTS_TO_LINES): the edges white and black by turns for the road just ahead and black further on, a line across at each piece's end and a white one at the start, and a line down to the ground wherever a side of the road shows.

The cars

Both cars are the shape the game draws the opponent's car (DRAW_OPPONENT, $E1B1): a box on four wheels, worked out from four corners. The game builds it on the screen from the corners' places there; Chase cam builds the same shape in the track's units. The car is 81 across and half as long again ($C34D is $1B of the road's 256 to each side). The wheels are boxes a quarter of the width across and as tall. The body is a wedge, half the width across: at the back from half a wheel's height up to twice it, and at the front from half a wheel's height to its top.

  • The opponent's car is placed by its corners as OPP_CORNERS leaves them, and by the heights of its wheels (OPP_WHEELS). The game works the corners out on every pass, wherever the car is, but draws it only when it is ahead of you and more than 10 apart along the track (OPP_DRAW_HERE, $E195, with the gap at $39). Chase cam and Heli cam draw it wherever it is, behind you and beside you too.
  • Your car is placed by its position ($0100-$0108), its heading ($0122, $0125), and the heights of its three points, which the game works out from its pitch and its roll (CAR_POINT_HEIGHTS). Points 0 and 1 are the front wheels, on the left and the right, and point 2 is the middle of the back (WHEEL_OFFSETS, $0897). The roll that lifts point 0 over point 1 is the one that turns the game's own picture, so the car leans as the horizon tilts from the driver's seat. A point's wheel sits $28 below it, or on the road when its spring is pressed into it.
  • The crane's chains hang straight down to the wheels while the game draws them (CRANE_CHAINS, $8B51). From the driver's seat the game draws them at pixel columns $54 and $A8, from row $AF up, ending on the front tyres. As the crane lets go, the game lifts their ends by 8, 24, 48 and 80 rows over four passes and then stops drawing them. Chase cam hangs a chain to each of the four wheels and lifts them by the same rows at the front chains' distance from the driver.
  • The specks the game throws up over its view while a wheel is over the road's edge (DUST, $2C6F) are drawn where its driver sees them, a car's length ahead: white, a fat pixel wide and two lines tall, as the game draws them. The game draws them on the screen, not on the track, so a car's length is this view's choice, and the higher ones show over the car's roof from behind.
  • When the car is wrecked, the game covers its view with a cloud of dust: up to sixteen puffs, each a few lines joined end to end over the ground's colour, thrown up and falling back (DUST_WRECKED, DEBRIS, $29E3). The game draws its cloud from the horizon down, so here the puffs ring the car's front, a car's length out. Each sits as far round from the nose as it is across the game's view, with the view's edges level with the car's sides, and as high as it is in the game's picture. Each keeps the game's shape and turns to face the camera.
  • While the game shows the boost's flame ($72 bit 7, which BOOST, $F60F, sets), sparks fly up and back off the outside of both front wheels. The game's flame is two sprites over the bonnet, yellow with white, on its left and its right by turns each frame (IRQ_SPRITES, $CE1B). The sparks take its two colours and are drawn as the specks are, a fat pixel by two lines each, thrown afresh each video frame. They are the mod's own: the game shows the boost only by its flame and the boost left on the dashboard.
  • Your car's front wheels turn while you steer. The game steers all the way or not at all (CONTROLS sets $C5 to $F1 for left, $0F for right, 0 for straight), and the car turns slowly after it, so the wheels show at once what the stick is doing. The wheels are the mod's own: the game never draws them on the opponent's car.

The camera

The camera is 280 units behind the car and 95 above it, looking 400 ahead. It turns after the car, closing 6 % of the gap each video frame (a third of it over a pass of seven), and moves to the car at once when the game puts it somewhere new.

The game's lens is a cylinder. A point's place across the screen is its bearing, 256 fat pixels to a quarter turn (PROJECT_X), and its place up the screen is its height over its distance along the ground, about 83 lines for a unit of height at a distance of one (PROJECT_Y). So on this page's square pixels the game draws a unit of height about a quarter as tall as a unit across is wide, and Chase cam draws heights the same. Its lens is flat and wider than the game's: it sees 76 degrees across, where the game sees 56.

What it leaves out

The crane itself, which the game never draws, and the crack across the windscreen, which is a part of the cockpit. The game itself is unchanged: its memory is only read, and with Chase cam or Heli cam on or off it runs byte for byte the same race. The drawing costs the game no time.
