1986 · U.S. Gold

Gauntlet

The arcade hit squeezed into 64 KB, where every monster on screen is a set of letters in a font, and the game keeps armour values for its heroes that it never reads. Eat poison with under 100 health and you can wander the dungeon for hours.

01 · The game

Four heroes, endless dungeons

One or two players each pick a hero and fight down through a scrolling maze, level after level, until their health runs out.

The heroes are Thor the Warrior, Thyra the Valkyrie, Questor the Elf, and Merlin the Wizard. Each level is a maze of walls and doors, seen through a window that scrolls with the players. Generators pour out ghosts, grunts, demons, lobbers, and sorcerers, and Death walks among them. Health starts at 2000 and drains by one point every 64 frames whatever you do, so the food lying in the maze matters as much as the fighting.

Keys open doors, potions blow up every monster on screen, and treasure scores points. Each level ends at an exit, a whirlpool in the floor. A second player can join at any moment by pressing fire.

02 · One frame, rebuilt from memory

Characters for the maze and its monsters, sprites for the heroes

The picture below is drawn by this page from the game's memory and the video chip's registers, as the emulator recorded them for one frame of level 2.

Level 2, both players in. Drawn by the site's renderer from the frame kit/c64/frame.py recorded: all 104,448 pixels match the emulator's own picture of the same frame. Switch the sprites off and only the heroes and their two-sprite black masks at the play area's edges go: the walls, the generator, and the field of ghosts are all characters.

The maze is a grid of 32 by 32 tiles at $0C00, each tile 2 by 2 characters. The game draws the grid into a character map of 64 by 64 at $3000 and a colour map at $2000, then copies the 33 by 20 window the players can see into whichever of its two screens is hidden, and swaps (maze_draw at $8A0B). Scrolling moves the window 2 pixels a step with the video chip's fine scroll and redraws on each whole character.

Monsters are drawn into the same maps as four characters each (monster_draw, $CA7F). The character map is also the collision map: a player's four cells hold $5B, a character that looks like floor, and anything above $5A blocks the way (cells_free). The only sprites are the two heroes, the shots, the level number's digits, and two black masks that cover the columns the scroll brings in at each side.

03 · The music

Music: three tunes on two players

The disk carries three pieces of music by Ben Daglish, played by two different music drivers.

The game's own players, running in this page. Each button runs the game's code on a small 6502 core in the page and plays what it writes through the site's model of the SID. The title music and the level jingle go through the player in PLAYER-$E000; the treasure-room tune goes through the second player at $A036. test_music.js runs the page's core beside the kit's 6502 simulator on the committed listing: every register write of every frame is the same for all three tunes, to their ends. The treasure tune's bass goes down to about 35 Hz, which laptop and phone speakers barely play.

The title music is the file DATATITLE, 18 seconds long, and the level jingle DITTIDATA, 4 seconds. Both are data for the full driver at $E000, which has 33 commands, vibrato, slides, pulse sweeps, and arpeggios (music_commands, $E643). The level jingle plays before each level (level_intro, $9BEF), with the game waiting until it ends.

The treasure-room tune, DATATREASURE, plays on a simpler driver built into the game program at $A036, called once a frame from the raster interrupt while a treasure room runs. Its 96-note frequency table (note_freqs, $A29E) is tuned for an American machine: 95 of its notes are within 10 cents of true pitch at the NTSC clock. On the PAL machines this release was sold for the tune plays about 65 cents flat, two thirds of a semitone. The $E000 driver's table is tuned for PAL.

04 · The monsters

Three fonts animate every monster at once

The game has three copies of its maze font, identical except for the monsters. Changing font is how the monsters move their arms and legs.

The six monsters, drawn from the tile table at $F400 (four characters a tile), the tile colours at $F600 and the three maze fonts at $4800, $5000 and $5800, in the order panel_flash steps through them ($8255: font selects 2, 4, 2, 6), one step every eight frames.

A monster is a tile from $40 to $6F: six kinds, each with a tile for every one of eight facings. The lobber has one picture for all eight. The three fonts at $4800, $5000 and $5800 hold the same walls, floor, and treasure, and the monsters in three different poses. Once a frame panel_flash ($8C7C) counts, and every eighth frame it points the video chip at the next font in the cycle: one write to $D018 animates every monster on screen.

A monster's byte in the table at $0580 holds its facing (bits 0-2), kind (bits 3-5) and level (bits 6-7). The same byte is its strength: a hand-to-hand blow takes $40 off it, so a level-one monster dies at the first blow and a level-three one at the third. Death's byte starts at $C0 or above, and shots do not hurt it.

05 · Poison

Bug: poison can make you nearly immortal

Poison takes 100 health, and a potion if there is one, from a hero with no special powers to lose. It does the subtraction in the wrong number system, and with under 100 health left the result wraps round to over 16,000.

The game's arithmetic, ported. The panel digits are drawn with the game's own panel font ($6000), glyph $10 plus each digit. test_poison.js holds the port against the game's code in the kit's 6502 simulator: poison_health ($9F62) for all 256 values of the hundreds byte, and one tick of health_tick ($B933) from all 65,536 health values, every result and every death the same.

Health is kept as four decimal digits in two bytes ($B931 and $B92F for player one): 2000 is $20 $00. The clock takes one off with the processor in decimal mode (health_tick, $B944). Poison, when the hero has no power to lose, takes a potion if it carries one, then one off the high byte with decimal mode off (poison_health, $9F62). From 2000 the high byte becomes $1F, which is not a decimal number: the panel shows a glyph from the word SCORE in the hundreds place, and the decimal countdown treats F as fifteen hundreds, so the hero has 600 more health than it should.

Below 100 there is nothing to borrow from. $00 less one is $FF, the routine never tests for it, and the clock counts down from $FF50 in decimal: 16,550 ticks, nearly six hours of play. In the emulator, a Warrior poisoned with 50 health had $FF $47 after the message, two stray glyphs on the panel, and $FF $07 forty ticks later, still in the game. Poison jugs appear on their own from level 8.

06 · Armour and fight power

Bug: the heroes' armour does nothing

The manual gives each hero an armour rating and sells a potion of extra armour. The game keeps the numbers, then never reads them.

The same for every hero. In the emulator a level-three grunt was put beside a standing player: in 187 attacks the game read monster_damage 93 times, on alternate attacks, and the six armour values not once. Health fell by 961: 93 blows of 10 and 31 ticks of the clock.

At the start of a game each player gets six damage values from its hero's table, and the armour potion replaces them with lower ones (power_apply, $9D10; damage_taken $9E49). No instruction in the program reads them. A Warrior with 20 % armour and a Wizard with none lose health at exactly the same rate.

Hand-to-hand is the same story. A blow takes $40 off a monster for any hero (fight_damage, $ACCA); the fight-power potion writes a stronger value one place along, at $ACCC, which nothing reads. The potion's other half, more power against generators (fight_power, $ACC8), does work.

07 · Waiting

The idle timer turns walls into exits

Stop shooting for long enough and the game helps you out of the level.

30 s: doors and keys become floor
150 s more: every wall becomes an exit

Game seconds. A game second is twelve passes of the main loop, 48 frames with the screen still, 0.96 s on PAL. In the emulator, from 129 seconds left the walls became exits within 6,250 frames.

idle_timer ($B557) counts game seconds whenever nobody fires, and every shot sets it back (shot_fire). After 30 every door and key turns to floor (idle_doors_open, $B581). After 150 more, every wall, crumbling wall, and key becomes an exit (idle_walls_exit, $B59C). The manual says 30 and then another 30, and that the walls vanish first.

08 · Levels

128 files, endlessly reshuffled

The box promises hundreds of levels. The disk holds 128 level files, and after level 7 the game draws them at random.

Every one of the 128 files, built by the game's own code, is on the Levels tab.

The rule is level_pick ($9AC4) and level_start ($99CF).

Levels 1 to 7 load files 1 to 7. From level 8 on, level_pick chooses a file at random from 8 to 117. A countdown runs out every four to seven levels and then a treasure room comes instead, one of files 118 to 128, and the level number is put back by one so it does not count. Two exits on level 1 skip ahead, to levels 4 and 8, and one on level 4 to level 8 (exit_targets). The game never ends while a player has health.

A level file is under 512 bytes: a four-byte header, a list of wall lines and the objects run-length coded. The header can make the maze wrap at its edges, make shots hurt or stun the other player, or keep only one exit, chosen at random. From level 8 the game scatters extra food and the odd poison jug (extras_place) and, in six rolls of sixteen, prints "FIND THE HIDDEN POTION". One of those six, the shot-speed potion, prints the message and hides nothing (hidden_potion_place, $A35E).

09 · Left on the disk

Secret: a cable link from the developers' desk

Among the files the game loads is a small program that sends and receives memory through the user port, and nothing in the game calls it.

PINN232 loads at $1388, 5000 in decimal. SYS 5000 receives a block, SYS 5003 several, SYS 5006 sends one, a bit at a time on the user port with a handshake, the border changing colour with each byte (pinn_send_byte, $142A).

Loaded every game, never run. load_files ($9964) loads it with the music and graphics; no instruction in the game program jumps into it.

Its entry points take their numbers from a BASIC statement, so it was driven by hand from the BASIC prompt. It looks like the tool the programmers used to move code between machines while they worked, left in the load list when the game shipped. Beside it, the program carries the prompt "INSERT SIDE 2 OF DISK / THEN PRESS SPACE BAR / TO CONTINUE GAME" with code to wait for it ($A693), which nothing reaches either: every level is on side 1.
