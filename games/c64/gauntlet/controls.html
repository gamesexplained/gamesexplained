1986 · U.S. Gold

Gauntlet

Two joysticks and five keys, read every eighth frame. Joystick 1 comes in through the keyboard, which turns SPACE into a second fire button.

01 · The sticks

A joystick for each player, and one key for magic

Player one plays on joystick 1 and uses magic with SPACE. Player two plays on joystick 2 and uses magic with any of the four function keys. The game puts this on a screen of its own before the heroes are chosen.

The controls in play, as the game reads them: the sticks in scroll_decide ($872E), the magic keys in magic_keys ($BA67), the join in players_join ($A56A).

With the screen still, the game looks at both sticks and the keyboard every eighth frame, about six times a second, and each look moves each hero on one step (scroll_decide, $872E, through players_move, $93F9). While the screen scrolls, it looks again as each step of the scroll is done. Between looks nothing is read, so a tap shorter than eight frames can fall between two of them and be lost.

Fire does two things at once. A pressed fire button makes the direction the game keeps for that player "none" ($0F in dir_p1 and dir_p2, $8EB2 and $8EB3), so the hero plants its feet; and shot_fire ($A718) throws a shot whenever the player's shot slot is empty. The shot goes the way the stick points, or the way the hero faces when the stick is centred. To walk and shoot, the player lets go of fire between shots. Every shot also resets the idle timer, which is why the walls never turn into exits while anyone is firing (How it works).

Fire on the panel of a player who is not playing brings that player in (players_join), as long as somebody is. The new hero appears on a free square next to the other one (join_beside, $A584). On the hero selection screen, joystick 1 moves a gauntlet to the number of players and the heroes, and fire picks.

02 · The keyboard

Joystick 1 is wired into the keyboard

On the Commodore 64, joystick 1 shares its five wires with one column each of the keyboard. Gauntlet reads player one's stick from the keyboard, so a few keys work as player one's stick, and SPACE is one of them.

The game's own code, running in this page. Press the buttons to hold controls down. The page runs the keyboard scan (keys_scan, $87D7), the stick reads after it ($8782-$87AE) and magic_keys ($BA67) on the kit's 6502, with both players in the game and a potion each. The grid is the eight bytes the scan leaves at $8821-$8828, a dark square a key the game sees as pressed. The page wires the controls as the machine does: joystick 1 pulls a column low in every row, and joystick 2 pulls its row lines low while the scan drives the others.

The scan, keys_scan ($87D7), drives each of the eight keyboard rows low in turn and reads the columns, from row 7 to row 0, and keeps the eight answers at $8821-$8828. Player one's stick is the low five bits of the first of them, row 7's (stick_p1, $8EB0). Joystick 1 pulls its columns low whichever row is driven, so its directions and fire turn up there, but so do the keys of row 7 that sit on the same columns: 1 is up, ← down, CTRL left, 2 right and SPACE fire. Player two's stick comes straight from $DC00 (stick_p2, $8EB1), which only joystick 2 reaches.

So SPACE is player one's fire button as well as the magic key. Pressing it throws a shot and plants the hero's feet as fire does, and uses a potion in the same moment.

magic_keys has to tell SPACE from the fire button, since both show as bit 4 of row 7. It looks at row 6 as well, where bit 4 is right SHIFT: the fire button pulls bit 4 low in every row and SPACE only in row 7, so magic needs row 7's bit low and row 6's high. The cost is that while player one holds fire, SPACE does nothing. Player two's test is the same idea. F7 and F1 are bits 3 and 4 of row 0, the columns of joystick 1's right and fire, so the game wants bits 3 and 4 of row 1 high before it believes one, and while player one pushes right or holds fire, none of player two's four keys uses a potion.

Pressing a magic key once uses one potion. magic_use ($BAA5) sets magic_debounce ($BA66) to 2 and the game's clock counts it down (health_tick, $B933, every 64 frames), so a key held down uses another potion 65 to 128 frames later, between about one and a half and two and a half seconds. A press with no potion carried only plays a sound.

03 · Nothing else

No pause, and RESTORE does nothing

Beyond the sticks and the magic keys, the game in play reads nothing.

The scan's eight bytes are read in two places only: the stick read and magic_keys. RUN/STOP and every other key go unread, so play runs on until the last hero's health is gone, with no pause and no way back to the title. The RESTORE key, which interrupts the processor through its NMI line on any C64, goes to a bare RTI at $827F, so pressing it leaves the game as it was.

Thirty bytes after the scan, at $87F3, are a routine that ORs the eight rows together, which keeps only what is low in every row, joystick 1, and stores it where player two's stick goes ($8EB1); and at $8CBD eight more wait for player one's fire button. Both are left over: the game never calls them.
