1986 · U.S. Gold

Gauntlet

Every shape the game moves, drawn from its own bytes: four heroes in eight facings, monsters that are letters in a font, and the sprites for everything else.

01 · The heroes

Four heroes, eight facings, three steps

Each hero has 24 walking shapes and eight for its shot. The game keeps only the two heroes in play in memory, and draws them as sprites over the maze.

Every shape of every hero, from the file PLYRS.SPR (walking, shapes $C0-$D7 once loaded) and GAUNT CHR (the shots, $D8-$DF), as multicolour sprites in the hero's colour (hero_sprite_colours, $9118) with the shared light red and white. The top row walks in all eight facings at the game's rate; the bottom row is the shot, one shape for each direction.

A hero's sprite shape is its base, $C0 for player one and $E0 for player two, plus an offset for the step of its walk and one for its facing (players_sprites, $915C). Every fourth frame, panel_flash ($8C7C) moves each player whose stick is pushed on one step, and the four steps take their offsets from anim_frames ($9128): 0, 8, 0 and 16. So the walk is first, second, first, third, and the legs swing through the middle pose both ways.

The Warrior's shot is a thrown axe, and it is the only one that turns: every fourth frame shots_move ($A768) adds one to its shape or takes one away, by its direction (axe_spin, $A87C), and keeps it among the eight. The same eight shapes show the hero shrinking into an exit: for the last part of the spin, exit_spin ($BACE) adds $18 to the hero's base, which points it at the shot's shapes.

02 · The monsters

Three fonts animate every monster at once

The game has three copies of its maze font. They share the walls, the floor and most of the treasure, and differ in the monsters, the transporter, the trap and the treasure chest. Changing font is how all of them move.

The font on show, all 256 glyphs, sixteen to a row from $00, switching with the monsters above.

Point at a glyph to see its number.

The six monsters, drawn from the tile table at $F400 (four characters a tile), the tile colours at $F600 and the three maze fonts at $4800, $5000 and $5800, in the order panel_flash steps through them ($8255: font selects 2, 4, 2, 6), one step every eight frames.

Point at a tile to see its number and what it is.

All 128 tiles, $00 to $7F, sixteen to a row, each four characters from the tile table at $F400 in the colours at $F600, switching font with the monsters above. The walls are level 1's. Generators, treasure, the trap and the transporter move with the same switch as the monsters.

A monster is a tile from $40 to $6F: six kinds, each with a tile for every one of eight facings. The lobber has one picture for all eight. The three fonts at $4800, $5000 and $5800 hold the same walls and floor, and the monsters in three different poses. Once a frame panel_flash ($8C7C) counts, and every eighth frame it points the video chip at the next font in the cycle: one write to $D018 animates every monster on screen.

The fonts differ in more than the monsters. Glyphs $34-$37 make the transporter, $38-$3B the trap, and $1A-$1B are two of the treasure chest's four, and each has a different picture in each font, so the same switch that animates the monsters animates those too. Of the 256 glyphs, 137 differ between the first font and the second, 135 between the first and the third, and 149 between the second and the third. The cycle shows the first font twice as often as either of the others.

The three maze fonts in full, from the listing, each glyph in the colour of the first tile that uses it (the tile table at $F400, its colours at $F600). The one framed in yellow is on show in the animation above. Glyphs $01-$0C are the walls as maze_style ($8CC5) set them for level 1: every level copies its own wall set into all three fonts. Glyph $5B looks like floor and is the mark that a player stands on a cell.

A monster's byte in the table at $0580 holds its facing (bits 0-2), kind (bits 3-5) and level (bits 6-7). The same byte is its strength: a hand-to-hand blow takes $40 off it, so a level-one monster dies at the first blow and a level-three one at the third. Death's byte starts at $C0 or above, and shots do not hurt it.

03 · Everything else

Thirty-two sprite shapes for the rest

Below the heroes, the game keeps 32 more sprite shapes: the masks at the screen's edges, the digits, the lobber's rock and the demon's fireball.

Point at a shape to see its number and what uses it.

Shapes $A0-$BF, from the listing's bytes at $6800-$6FFF, in the colours the game gives them: the fireball and the rock yellow, the digits in player one's hero's colour (the Warrior's red here). The masks are black sprites, drawn here in grey so they show. $B0-$B7 are drawn in white: the search of the listing found no code choosing them.

Sprites 0 and 1 are the masks: each is shape $A0, a solid block, doubled in height and moved down the side of the play area four times a frame by the raster interrupt ($8663), so the column the scroll brings in at each edge stays black. Sprites 2 to 5 carry the shots: slots 0 and 1 the monsters', 2 and 3 the players'. A demon's fireball is shape $B8 plus its direction (fireball_fire, $AA2E). A lobber's rock starts at $AD, grows to $AF as it rises and shrinks back as it falls (rock_fly, $A7BF).

The digits $A2-$AB do two jobs on the shot sprites: the level number while a level begins (level_digits_sprites, $9B10), and the clock in a treasure room (treasure_clock, $AD87).

04 · One frame, rebuilt from memory

Characters for the maze and its monsters, sprites for the heroes

The picture below is drawn by this page from the game's memory and the video chip's registers, for one frame of level 2.

Level 2, both players in. Drawn by the site's renderer from one frame of the game. Switch the sprites off and only the heroes and their two-sprite black masks at the play area's edges go: the walls, the generator, and the field of ghosts are all characters.

The maze is a grid of 32 by 32 tiles at $0C00, each tile 2 by 2 characters. The game draws the grid into a character map of 64 by 64 at $3000 and a colour map at $2000, then copies the 33 by 20 window the players can see into whichever of its two screens is hidden, and swaps (maze_draw at $8A0B). Scrolling moves the window 2 pixels a step with the video chip's fine scroll and redraws on each whole character.

Monsters are drawn into the same maps as four characters each (monster_draw, $CA7F). The character map is also the collision map: a player's four cells hold $5B, a character that looks like floor, and anything above $5A blocks the way (cells_free). The only sprites are the two heroes, the shots, the level number's digits, and two black masks that cover the columns the scroll brings in at each side.
