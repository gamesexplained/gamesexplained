The game moves the player a fixed step each time its main loop goes round, and on a C64 a round takes from a fifth of a second to two seconds, most of it spent drawing the view. So in the Wilderness the player walks in strides of 240 of the game's units, and turns 15 degrees at a time. With Fast CPU on, the page runs the main loop as a C64 with a much faster processor would, once for every frame the screen shows, and each round moves the player by the share of a step that its time is of a C64 round.

The interrupt keeps the C64's time

Only the main loop's own work goes faster. Everything the game times by its interrupt runs as before: the music, the drain on strength while a sensor watches, the countdown to the next lightning, and the scripts' timer every ten seconds. So does every wait on the interrupt: a door's animation, the viewport opening, the pause after a shot. A minute of play is a minute on either machine.

A C64 round is the time the C64 would take over the view in front of the player now. The page counts what each round costs as the game runs, at the cycles of the C64's own routines (section 01), whether Fast CPU is on or off. Facing the castle from where the game starts, a round that draws the view takes 1.8 seconds; looking up into the sky, two-thirds of a second. So the player walks from the start at 132 of the game's units a second on either machine, and turns faster where the view is simpler, as on a C64.

The view between two of the game's angles

The game keeps the sines and cosines of its 72 angles in 64ths. At those angles each picture is the game's own. Between them the rounding of the 64ths jumps, and a wall close to the player moves a few pixels at once. So with Fast CPU on, the game works out each view at the angle between, for what it uses the view for (which objects are in sight, what a shot hits), and the page paints the picture from the same objects at that angle, as Smooth motion paints its views (section 02): in the C64's pixels, or with Native resolution at the screen's own. The page draws a view for every frame of the screen: 60 a second on most screens, and more on faster ones.
