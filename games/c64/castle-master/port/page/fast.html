The game moves the player a fixed step each time its main loop goes round, and on a C64 a round takes from a fifth of a second to three seconds, most of it spent drawing the view. So in the Wilderness the player walks in strides of 240 of the game's units, and turns 15 degrees at a time. Nothing in the loop waits, so a C64 with a much faster processor would go round as fast as it could and take a whole step each time, hundreds a second. With Fast CPU on, the page runs the main loop 60 times a second and gives each round a sixtieth of a step, so that every step takes a second, whatever is in view.

The interrupt keeps the C64's time

Only the main loop's own work goes faster. Everything the game times by its interrupt runs as before: the music, the drain on strength while a sensor watches, the countdown to the next lightning, and the scripts' timer every ten seconds. So does every wait on the interrupt: a door's animation, the viewport opening, the pause after a shot. A minute of play is a minute on either machine.

Every round lasts one tick of the game's clock

The game's clock is timer A of the C64's first CIA chip, which interrupts the processor every 16,422 cycles, just under 60 times a second. Each tick moves on the scripts' delays, the lightning's countdown, the drain on strength and the scripts' timer. With Fast CPU on, each round of the main loop starts at the same count of that timer, one tick after the last unless the game waited on the interrupt in between. So the same keys, held for the same rounds, make the same game on any computer and at any frame rate.

The page shows the view a round behind, part of the way from one round's picture to the next as Smooth motion draws it, so that the view moves at every frame of a screen that shows 120 or 144 a second. A computer too slow to run 60 rounds a second plays slower than the C64. Its rounds stay one tick long, so the player's steps stay the same size.

A step a second, whatever is in view

On a C64 the pace of play follows the view. Facing the castle from where the game starts, a round that draws the view takes 1.8 seconds, and the player walks 132 of the game's units a second; looking up into the sky, a round takes two-thirds of a second; with nothing in view, a fifth. In play, half the rounds take between 0.7 and 1.3 seconds. With Fast CPU on, a step takes a second wherever the player looks: a whole turn takes 24 seconds, and in the Wilderness the player walks 240 units a second.

The game counts some things in rounds and others in ticks of its clock, and with Fast CPU on the two keep one ratio. The player covers the same ground between two of the scripts' ten-second ticks whichever way they face, where on a C64 they cover less facing the castle. The scripts that a step sets off run each round, as on a C64, so 60 times a second while the player moves. On a step they only show or hide objects by the game's flags, which comes out the same however often it is done.

The view between two of the game's angles

The game keeps the sines and cosines of its 72 angles in 64ths. At those angles each picture is the game's own. Between them the rounding of the 64ths jumps, and a wall close to the player moves a few pixels at once. So with Fast CPU on, the game works out each view at the angle between, for what it uses the view for (which objects are in sight, what a shot hits), and the page paints the picture from the same objects at that angle, as Smooth motion paints its views (section 02): in the C64's pixels, or with Native resolution at the screen's own.
