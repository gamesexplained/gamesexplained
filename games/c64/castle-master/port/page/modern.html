Native resolution changes how the page draws the 3D view, the window in the middle of the castle's frame, and nothing else. The game runs underneath as it does without it: two copies of it, one with the view watching it, keep every byte of memory the same at every frame.

The game draws the view into a buffer of 120 by 120 pixels, each twice as wide as it is tall on the screen, and copies the buffer into the bitmap when the picture is done. Many of its colours are patterns: two colours in alternating pixels, which the screen blurs into a shade between them.

With Native resolution on, the page works each picture out again from what the game keeps for it: where the player stands and which way they look, and the records of the area's objects. It applies the game's own rules to them: the objects the game queued for the picture, each one's corners from its record, each face's colour from the side of it the player is on or from the way it winds, the faces that turn away left out by the game's own test on the corners as the game rounds them, and the order from farthest to nearest by the game's own bubble sort on the same bounds. Then it draws the same faces as sharp shapes at the screen's own resolution, each pattern as the shade the eye mixes from it, with the sky, the floor and the Wilderness's mountains where the game puts them, and the sight in the middle in the colours the game's XOR gives it.

The pictures are the 1,633 the game drew in 82,600 frames of play, from the loader's jump and from 199 moments in every area, walking, turning and looking up and down. The game works out each corner in whole numbers and drops the fraction toward the middle of the view. The page works it out exactly, which moves a corner by less than 2 pixels. The 60 faces that are further out are all cut by an edge of the view, where the game's cut and the page's put a corner up to 35 pixels apart; why is not known.
