1988 · SSI

Pool of Radiance

GEN, the part of the game that makes and trains characters, keeps rules the rulebook never prints. A halfling can't become the 6th level fighter it promises, training throws experience away, and a seventh race and six more classes sit in its tables with no way onto the menus.

01 · Character sheet

Roll a character by GEN's own rules

Pick a race, a gender and a class, and roll. Each score comes from the game's own dice, adjusted for the race and held to its limits, and the hit points, gold and age follow as CREATE NEW CHARACTER sets them. Raise or lower a score as MODIFY CHARACTER allows, or change a level, and the rest of the sheet follows.

The classes are those the race may take (race_classes, $0E64); a class whose lowest scores the roll misses is marked, as the game would leave it off its menu (class_minimums, $0E75).

    02 · Level limits

    A halfling never reaches the 6th fighter level the rulebook promises

    The rulebook gives each race one limit as a fighter: 6th level for a halfling, 7th for an elf. GEN lowers it by a level for each point of strength below 18, two at most. A halfling man's strength can't go above 17 and a woman's above 14, so no halfling fighter gets past 5th level.

    RaceRulebookStrongest manStrongest womanStrength 16 or less

    The rulebook's limits are on page 3, and the training hall's, 8th level for a fighter, 9th for a thief and 6th for a cleric or a magic-user, on page 7. The race's limit comes from race_level_table ($1E60), and race_level_limit ($24A0) lowers it for each class part that training looks at (level_limits, $1E21). A character stopped there is told NO MORE ADVANCEMENT POSSIBLE.

    Intelligence lowers a magic-user's limit the same way, but the training hall's 6th level comes first: an elf's limit is 11th and a half-elf's 8th. A half-elf cleric stops at 5th level, which the rulebook leaves out, giving the half-elf's limits as a magic-user and a fighter only. Try the halfling fighter on the sheet.

    03 · Training

    Training throws away experience past the next level

    A character goes up one level each time it trains. Then GEN cuts its experience to one point short of the level after that. A 1st level fighter who saves up 100,000 points trains to 2nd level and keeps 4,000, and the other 96,000 are gone.

    Experience is never cut as it is won (add_exp, $0C69), so it piles up until the character trains. Then cap_experience ($23D4) holds it to one point less than the next level needs, the highest such figure over the character's classes. It runs after every training, and also when a level limit stops the character. The rulebook says only that the character advances a level (page 7). The sheet's last line shows the most a character keeps.

    04 · The seventh race

    The half-orc is in every table and on no menu

    The rulebook names six races. GEN's tables hold a seventh, the half-orc, with everything a race needs, and the race menu skips its number.

    The menu reads its races from race_numbers ($121A): 1 to 5, then 7, the human. Race 6 has its own adjustments to the scores (strength +2, constitution +2, charisma −2) and limits (wisdom at most 14 and charisma 12), its classes (cleric, fighter, thief, cleric/fighter, cleric/thief and fighter/thief), its level limits (4th as a cleric, 8th as a thief, 10th as a fighter), its adjustments to the thief skills, a starting age of 16 and death of old age at 80. The engine's own words include HALF-ORC (messages, $3243). The sheet offers it.

    05 · Cut classes

    Druids, paladins, rangers and monks were cut, with their rules left in

    The class tables have 17 entries, and no race may take five of them: the druid, the paladin, the ranger, the monk and the cleric/ranger. A sixth, the cleric/thief, belongs to the half-orc alone. Each still has its lowest scores, its alignments, its first hit dice and its starting gold.

    ClassLowest scoresAlignmentsFirst hit diceGold

    None of the first five has a row in the experience, to-hit or saving throw tables, which have rows for the magic-user, the cleric, the thief and the fighter, and none has a prime ability for the experience bonus. The monk's row of alignments allows none at all. The engine's list of class names has DRUID and MONK, but PALADIN and RANGER have no words of their own: the pointers for those two names lead to MAGIC-USER (messages 13 and 14, $3439). One piece of the adventure still looks for a ranger. The dungeon's surprise check notes whether anyone in the party has the ranger's bit in their class, for the area scripts to read (cmd_party_surprise, $1D6B).

    06 · Thief skills

    Three of the eight thief skills are never used

    Training sets a thief's eight skills from its level and race. The game reads five of them, most in one place only. Hide in shadows, hear noise and read languages are worked out and never looked at again, and dexterity changes none of the eight.

    Skill1st levelRead by

    Move silently counts in the surprise check only when every standing member of the party rolls under their own figure, and a member who is not a thief has 0, so a party with anyone else in it never passes that part (cmd_party_surprise, $1D43). The skills come from thief_skills ($102E) and race_thief_adjust ($1076) in set_thief_skills ($1FEC).

    07 · Saving throws

    Dwarves, gnomes and halflings save better against everything

    The rulebook says dwarves and halflings save better against magic (page 23). GEN takes two-sevenths of the constitution off all five saving throws of a dwarf, a gnome or a halfling, so they also save better against breath, poison and petrification. A gnome fighter with constitution 18 saves against a dragon's breath on 12, where a human needs 17.

    con_save_bonus ($2359) does it for the races whose byte in race_save_bonus ($2380) is 1. The bonus is 2 for a constitution of 7 to 10, 3 for 11 to 13, 4 for 14 to 17 and 5 for 18 and 19. Try the gnome fighter on the sheet.

    08 · Hit points

    A new character's hit die never rolls low

    A new character's first hit dice are raised to a floor near their top: a fighter's d10 gives 9 or 10, a cleric's d8 7 or 8, a thief's d6 5 or 6, and a magic-user's d4 always 4. When a fighter trains, its d10 never gives less than 4, where the rulebook says 1 to 10 (page 4).

    ClassFirst hit diceAt leastConstitution adds at most

    CREATE NEW CHARACTER rolls class_hp_dice ($0F7C) dice of class_hp_sides ($0F8D) and raises the total to class_hp_least ($0F9E). A character of two or three classes rolls one die between its classes', some with 9, 7 or 5 sides. Training rolls a die for each class that goes up (4, 8, 6 or 10 sides), divides it by the number of classes, rounds a remainder up by chance, and gives a fighter of one class at least 4 (train_hp, $2037). Constitution adds to each die from 15 up.

    09 · Experience

    The experience bonus starts at 16, where the rulebook says 15

    A character of one class with 16 or more in its class's prime ability gets a tenth more of every award. The rulebook puts the line at 15 (page 3). A character of two classes gets half of each award and one of three a third, and neither ever gets the bonus.

    prime_bonus ($15E3) compares with 16. It works the tenth out in two pieces, from the award's low two bytes and from its high byte, and drops the high byte's remainder, so an award of 65,536 points gets no bonus and one of 100,000 gets 3,446.

    10 · Ability scores

    Every race but the halfling adds to strength, humans included

    The rulebook says only that the computer generates the scores (page 3). GEN adds at least one to the strength of every race but the halfling, and to the constitution of every race but the elf, humans included. That comes on top of scores that start high: each is the best of six rolls of three dice (How it works), so 44 scores in 100 come out at 15 or more before the race's adjustment, and the average is 14.2.

    roll_ability ($0D1F) keeps the best of the six; adjust_ability ($0D4A) adds ability_adjust ($0D60) and holds the score between ability_minimum ($0DB4) and ability_maximum ($0E08). An 18 strength rolls a percentage only in a class with a fighter's part, up to 75 for an elf man and 50 for a gnome (strength_pct_cap, $0FC0).

    11 · Age

    Every HASTE adds a year to its target's age

    A character starts at a fixed age for its race and grows older with the days that pass. Each HASTE cast on it, in combat or in camp, and each potion of speed adds a year as well, which the rulebook's HASTE doesn't mention (page 27). At its race's limit a character dies of old age.

    RaceStarts atDies atHASTEs to get there

    The ages come from race_ages ($1004) and POST.COM's old_age ($215D). HASTE in combat adds the year once for each casting (kind_haste, $A977). In camp it adds one to every member it reaches, even one already hasted (haste_member, $AAEA). How the days are counted is on the Discoveries tab, in A month of rest in camp ages the party one day.

    12 · The training fee

    Training costs 1,000 gold at every level, and the change becomes platinum

    The fee is the same 1,000 gold pieces for 2nd level as for 9th. The trainer adds up all of a character's coins, takes the fee, and pays the rest back as platinum, one piece for each 5 gold, keeping the change: a character with 1,234 gold leaves with 46 platinum, worth 230.

    has_money ($1D50) counts the five kinds of coin in copper and asks for 200,000. pay_training ($1D63) clears them all and gives back a platinum piece for each 1,000 copper left. Gems and jewellery stay as they are. A hired companion trains free: for a character who is not a player's, has_money always says yes and the payment is skipped (train_choice, $1B84).

    13 · PENDRAGON

    PENDRAGON is a player's fighter, saved onto a game disk

    The copy of game disk 3 kept on archive.org holds a saved character, PENDRAGON, a 1st level human fighter. The C64 Preservation Project's images of that disk, of versions 1.0 and 1.1, end with three blocks free, and PENDRAGON's file takes exactly those three: its owner saved him there. The game lets that happen, because it never checks which disk is in the drive.

    When CREATE NEW CHARACTER saves, it asks for the save disk and writes to whatever disk is there (ask_save_disk, $22EF; save_character, $10F6). ADD CHARACTER TO PARTY reads the directory the same way and lists every file whose name starts with the byte 1 (dir_byte, $1825), so with that copy of game disk 3 left in the drive, PENDRAGON is offered and joins the party with armour class 7 and 13 hit points. He has strength 17, intelligence 17, wisdom 12, dexterity 17, constitution 18 and charisma 13, 80 gold, no items and no experience, and is neutral good. The Items tab's sheet uses him, and the button above puts him on this one.
