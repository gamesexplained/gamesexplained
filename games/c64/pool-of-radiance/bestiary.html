1988 · SSI

Pool of Radiance

Every monster file on the eight disk sides, drawn with the pictures the game shows it with, and given the numbers it fights with, worked out by the game's own code.

01 · Bestiary

Every monster the scripts can send against the party

A monster meets the party three times over: as sprites standing in the 3D view, as a close-up picture that animates while the party decides what to do, and as a little figure on the battle map. The game keeps each in its own file, numbered by kind, and keeps the monster itself in a fourth, a character record like a party member's. Pick a monster to see all of its pictures and its numbers.

The monsters are drawn by the site's shared scripts, which the built site serves.

The close-ups are decoded as the game's picture driver, ANIMATE00, decodes them, each frame shown for its own delay in sixtieths of a second. The 3D-view sprites stand where draw_monster_sprites ($28FA) puts them in the middle of the view at each distance, and the figures are painted in the battle's colours. The numbers in the boxes are the ones the battle uses: the combat preparation module runs strength_index ($3ED2) and compute_combat_stats ($3729) on every combatant ($1630), and this page runs the same two routines over each monster file, on a 6502 in your browser.

02 · Side by side

The whole roster, sorted however you like

Every distinct monster file, one row each. Several names come in more than one version: the same monster copied to another disk side with a different weapon or purse, or a different monster that shares a name. Click a column to sort by it, and a row to show that monster above.

03 · How a monster is made

A monster is a character with a figure, a sprite and a picture

The game has no table of monsters. A monster is a file of the same shape as a party member's character, and a script loads one when it wants a fight.

Each disk side holds the MON files its areas need, each with a two-digit hexadecimal number, so the same kind of monster can sit at a different number on another side, and one number can be two different monsters: MON16 is RESTAL, a fighter, on the first side and a GIANT SKELETON on the fourth. A file is 480 bytes, or 544 for a few with more items. It is a character record: the name, the six ability scores, THAC0 and armour class (both kept as 60 minus their value), hit points, how many half-attacks it makes a round with which dice, its move, its money and up to sixteen items.

The script command LOAD MONSTER (cmd_load_monster, $1AC6) takes three numbers: the MON file, how many, and the combat figure. It loads the file into the working character, into one of four slots kept for monsters, and gives each of the monsters one, two or four places on the battle map by the size byte in the file (monster_entry_sizes, $1B6F): a giant takes two squares, one above the other, a snake or a basilisk two side by side, a wyvern or Tyranithraxus four. Every monster of one command is a copy of the same record, so all of them start with the hit points the file gives. Before that, SETUP MONSTER (cmd_setup_monster, $1F0A) names the sprites the party sees in the 3D view, how far off they stand, and the close-up. Outdoors the close-up is never loaded, and the monsters stand as sprites in the middle of the view.

The figure file holds a block of 162 bytes for each square: nine characters for the ready pose, nine for the attacking pose, and a colour for each of the eighteen. A battle copies four blocks for every monster slot, one for each place it can fill, and lays them out by the monster's size (fit_combatant, $0FBD, with size_squares_across and size_squares_down). The 3D-view file holds twelve sprite pictures, some for each of the three distances, with the place of each and the two colours they share. The close-up is a sequence of frames, each stored as the bytes that change from the frame before, run-length coded.

A monster's numbers in a battle are worked out from its record by the same routine as a party member's (compute_combat_stats, $3729), so a monster that carries a weapon and armour and has them readied fights with them. The 4TH LVL FIGHTER's record says THAC0 18 and armour class 8; in a battle it has THAC0 16, armour class 2 and does 1d8+2, from the LONG SWORD +1, CHAIN MAIL +1 and SHIELD it has readied and its strength of 17. Its move can change as well: one HILL GIANT file says move 12, and in a battle it moves 3. When the battle is won, each monster that fell is worth its experience value plus a number of points for each of its hit points ($09BA), and its money and items join the treasure: each kind of money varied to between 51 and 150 per cent (vary_amount, $0CBD), unless a non-player character in the party has pocketed that monster's share.

04 · The rest of the pictures

Pictures no monster above is shown with

The disks hold 65 close-ups, 56 sets of 3D-view sprites and 66 combat figures that differ from each other. These are the ones the bestiary does not use: places and people the scripts show with PICTURE, the treasure chest, and monsters the scripts meet some other way, such as the two-headed giant in PIC47, the number of the ETTIN's file on the same side.

The pictures are drawn by the site's shared scripts, which the built site serves.
