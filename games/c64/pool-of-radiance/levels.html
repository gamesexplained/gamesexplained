01 · Every map

Every map, and what happens on each square

The game loads a block's map into memory at $0400 (an address in the C64's memory, written in hexadecimal) when the party enters it, together with a script file, named ECL and a number, which decides what happens there. A script is a small program in the game's own language of commands, which the game runs every time the party arrives on a square (the scripts). Each square's byte in the map's third page tells the script which of its events the square holds.

A dot marks every square where the script shows the party something, coloured by the most serious thing that can happen there. Click a square for the script's own words and what it can do: the fights, the treasure, the damage, the words it checks and where it leads. Some events need Search on, some depend on the way the party faces, and some come only by chance or later in the story; the square says which.

Anywhere on this map

White lines are walls, a gap with yellow ends is a door that opens, and red is a locked door, read from the map's four pages as the How it works page explains (the maps). North is at the top. A filled dot is something a party meets on its first visit in the morning, a ring is something the script holds for another time or a later point in the story, and a dashed ring around a dot means the party needs Search on.

MapDiskIts script says

Three maps have no script of their own number. The script of the lizard men's keep, ECL10, loads GEO1E for its tunnels, ECL18 loads GEO1F for the Temple of Bane itself, and ECL1D loads GEO20 for the catacombs under Kuto's Well. The demonstration, ECL1E, walks its party round Podol Plaza's map, GEO12. The city hall's script, ECL08, and the training hall's, ECL0B, run on New Phlan's map. ECL13, the silver dragon's cave, loads no map at all: everything in it happens in words.

Valjevo Castle's scripts share their maps. ECL03 loads GEO03 or GEO05, and ECL05 loads GEO03, GEO05 or GEO06, chosen from a table by the way the party faces as it comes in. The same square then means something different to each script, so those maps have a button for each script that uses them.
