01 · Morale

Who runs, who surrenders and who fights on

Before each turn it takes, a combatant the computer runs checks its morale. Pick one, set how hurt it is and how the fight is going, and the panel runs the game's own check.

The panel runs the game's own code, with the site's shared scripts that the built site serves.

Each change runs COMBAT's morale ($20C1) and hurt_check ($211C) on the kit's 6502 over the listing's bytes. BLESS and CURSE answer the event that the check runs, and here they do what kind_bless ($A73F) and kind_curse ($A74F) do: 5 off the figure, at least 0, or 5 on.

The check starts with the combatant's hit points. Each monster and NPC carries a figure in its record, and it counts as hurt when its hit points are at or below that share of its full hit points. Almost every monster file holds a figure over 100, so a monster always counts as hurt. Five files hold a figure of 0, so those never do: HASSAD, the SHAMAN, and one file each of the EFREETI, the NOMAD and the 4TH LVL FIGHTER.

A hurt NPC on the party's side runs. A hurt monster looks at how much of its side's hit points are gone, in steps of 5 from 0 to 100 (side_losses, $203C), and holds while that is no more than the morale the area's script set for the fight. When it breaks, it runs if it moves as fast as the party's fastest member. If it is slower, it SURRENDERS when its intelligence is 5 or more, and fights on when it is lower. Every fight with undead is set to 99, 100 or 101, so undead break only when all but a twentieth of their side's hit points are gone, if at all.

BLESS takes 5 off both figures for a blessed combatant, and CURSE adds 5, so a blessed hireling holds on a little longer and a cursed monster breaks a step sooner. The rulebook says that BLESS raises the morale of friendly NPCs by 1 (page 23), and that giving an NPC treasure may help its morale. Nothing that moves items or money writes to the byte that holds the figure.

02 · ANIMATE DEAD

Bug: a zombie raised from one of the party runs from every fight

The rulebook says ANIMATE DEAD turns a dead human into a zombie that fights for the caster (page 24). Raised from a monster or a hired NPC, it does, to the end. Raised from one of the party's own characters, it runs at its first turn in every fight.

The spell sets the zombie's morale figure from the byte that holds it. A monster or an NPC has the top bit of that byte set, and the spell writes $B2 there, a figure of 0: never hurt. For a character of the party it ORs the old byte with $FE, which leaves a figure of 102 or 104. The zombie counts as hurt at full hit points and, being on the party's side, runs. The spell cast in a fight ($AB76) and the one cast in camp ($A9FA) are the same six instructions.

03 · NPCs

Each hired companion runs at its own point, and the MAD MAN at once

The rulebook says that NPCs may run if things are going badly for the party. What counts is the NPC's own hit points: the party's losses never come into it. The figure comes from the script command that adds the NPC to the party (cmd_add_npc, $2724), and the training hall gives each of its eight hirelings a different one.

The share of its full hit points at which each NPC runs, before BLESS or CURSE. The MAD MAN joins with a figure of 100, so he counts as hurt at full hit points and runs at his first turn in every fight.

04 · Surrender

A monster that surrenders is worth as much as one killed

SURRENDERS takes the monster off the field with the condition the party's own unconscious have. After the fight, every monster that went down and did not flee counts for experience ($0990), so one that surrendered gives its full share, and one that ran gives nothing.

05 · Turning undead

What a cleric's turning does to each undead in the game

Pick the cleric's level and roll. One d20 serves the whole turning. Then up to a d12 undead are tried, nearest first, until one is not beaten (turn_undead, $24AA).

Each cell runs turn_roll ($252B) on the kit's 6502 for every roll of the d20, over the tables at $7AD7, $7AE4 and $7AF1, with the turning figure GEN gives each level of cleric (cleric_figures, $239A). A number is the least roll that turns, and a roll outlines the cells it beats; turned (T) and destroyed (D) happen whatever the roll.

06 · The message

Bug: an undead that a cleric destroys IS CURSED

A turned undead gets the message IS TURNED. One that is destroyed is laid down and gets message 18 of the spell file, SPELLN00, which reads IS CURSED ($24E2). The file has no message that says destroyed. A 6th level cleric destroys skeletons and zombies whatever the roll, so each of them falls with the words IS CURSED beside its name.

07 · Starting squares

Bug: on the wilderness map, the monsters start in the middle of the field

A fight on the wilderness map faces the party a random way and gives each side four of the field's 25 big squares to start in, the monsters' from the opposite facing. One instruction stores the monsters' four in the wrong place. Three times in four they start in the middle square, 5 or 6 squares from the party instead of 10 or 11. With the party facing south they start 16 squares away instead of 11.

The 56 by 26 squares of the field, with where the game put a party of six and twelve goblins for each facing, and where it puts them with the one instruction corrected.

The loop that copies the monsters' four squares ($15C2) stores them by X, its index into the table, where the party's loop stores by Y, which counts 3 down to 0. Only when the monsters take the table's first facing, north, does X fall on their four bytes, and then in the wrong order, so their own square is the one meant to lie beyond them. Otherwise the four bytes keep the zeros they hold on the disk, and square 0 is the middle of the field. Fights in towns and dungeons place the two sides another way.

08 · Long shots

Bug: the penalty for a long shot never applies, and would make it easier

range_penalty ($695D) gives a shot beyond a third of the weapon's range −2, and beyond two thirds −5. It works out the thirds from the byte that missile_leg ($6652) has just set to 128 to draw the arrow's flight, not from the weapon's range. That puts the first third at 42 squares, and no bow or thrown weapon in the game reaches so far: the long bows' 22 is the longest. Only item type 127, which no item has, would shoot 60. Had the weapon's range been used, the penalty would still have helped the archer: the 2 or 5 comes off the target's armour class figure ($11F1), as a backstab's bonus does, and that makes a hit easier.

09 · Surprise

A surprised character with dexterity 16 or more can still act

The rulebook says that monsters that surprise the party get a series of attacks before the party can reply (page 16). In the game, surprise lasts one round, in which the surprised side's initiative is cut by 6 (surprise_round, $15D9). Initiative is a d6 plus the dexterity bonus to hit, at least 1 ($0895), and a combatant cut to 0 does not act. So a character with dexterity 16 still acts on a 6, one with 17 on a 5 or 6, and one with 18 or more on a 4, 5 or 6. It works the other way too: the QUICKLINGS, the thieves, NORRIS THE GRAY and the other monsters with dexterity 16 or more can strike back in a round the party surprised them.

10 · Dexterity

VIEW leaves dexterity out of a new archer's THAC0

Dexterity's bonus to hit with a bow is kept in the character's record, and only the setting up of a fight writes it ($1639, from dex_hit_bonus, $1682). A character who has never fought has 0 there, so VIEW gives its bow a THAC0 without the bonus until its first fight. PENDRAGON, the fighter on the Items tab, has dexterity 17 and has never fought: with a bow his THAC0 reads 20 on VIEW and is 18 in a fight.

11 · The computer's side

The computer goes for enemies it cannot see

A combatant the computer runs picks a target at random among the enemies it can see. When it sees none, it searches again with its line of sight let through the walls, and heads for whatever it finds (ai_target, $670A). A magic-user with no other class stays where it is unless it flees. One that flees runs the way the party faced when the fight began, or the opposite way for the party's own side ($6884).
