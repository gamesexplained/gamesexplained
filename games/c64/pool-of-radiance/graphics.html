1988 · SSI

Pool of Radiance

In a battle every combatant is a little figure of nine characters with two poses, ready and attacking. A party member's figure is put together from a head and a weapon in the game's own editor. Below them, the 3D view's walls on every map, every portrait and the ending's two scenes.

01 · Everything that moves

Every figure the icon editor makes, and the sprites around them

A party member's figure in a battle is three characters by three, drawn in two poses. The combat icon editor builds it from a weapon and a head, and every choice it offers is laid out here by its own code. Each figure strikes as a battle draws an attack: the ready pose, then the attacking one, then ready again. The monsters' figures come from a file for each kind of monster, loaded for the battle: the Bestiary draws every one of them, with each monster's close-up and 3D-view sprites.

The figures are drawn by the game's own code in the built site.

Point at a figure for its pieces.

Each figure is laid out by the editor's own lines for a choice (save_head, $B26F, then pick_piece from $B14C), run on the kit's 6502 in the page over the bytes of the icons part, starting from the editor's own icon with the size's first weapon and first head. The cells are drawn as the video chip shows the characters draw_figure ($B361) makes from the pieces' pictures, on the battle's colours: dark grey, light red and black (combat_colours, $146A).

A figure has nine cells in each pose. A weapon choice fills the cells below the head from a string of codes kept for that choice (cell_strings, $ABC0), and a head choice fills the top middle cell, and for some heads the cells beside it. A cap or a lock of hair never takes a top cell from a piece of another kind. The large figures are for the man-sized races, and the small ones for dwarves, gnomes and halflings.

The battle's sprites are few. The cursor that marks the square in hand is a hollow box built by code, 24 pixels wide and 12 tall, doubled in height and drawn behind the characters (set_sprites, $147D). A fallen combatant is covered by two sprites, a red one in front of a black one, stretched for its size (lie_down, $1BF9). A missile is sprite 6, given one of twenty pictures for the kind of shot and the way it flies, with a colour pair for each picture (load_missile_picture, $6A57). The battle copies those twenty pictures under the input and output chips, and only the first four are among the bytes the Source tab lists.

The title has the remaining sprites: three red pieces that fill the TSR logo from behind the picture, and three white sparkles. Each sparkle grows from a dot to a four-pointed star and shrinks again over fifteen steps of sparkle_frames, taking a step at random, 15 times in 256, and then jumps to a random place on the picture.

02 · The 3D view

Walk any of the 29 maps

Every step redraws the view, eleven characters by eleven, from the walls around the party's square. Choose a map and walk it with the buttons or the arrow keys. Each view is drawn by the game's own 3D view driver from the map and wall files on the disks.

The view is drawn by the game's own code in the built site.

The view is draw_view ($C183) run on the kit's 6502 in the page over the view part, the map's GEO file at $0400 and the walls as the map's script loads them (install_wall_def, $1485), coloured for the square by set_wall_colour ($1157). A step goes ahead when the side faced has no wall or a door that opens ($10EC), and wraps at the map's edge as step_forward ($C3FA) does; in the game the map's script takes the party elsewhere there. The map beside the view: white for walls, yellow for a door that opens, coral for a locked door. Click a square to stand on it.

A map's script sets its walls up when the party arrives. It names three wall sets, and each set is two files: a WALLDEF of five wall shapes, and a WALLSET of the fifty characters they are drawn with. A shape is 156 character codes, the pieces the view is built from, near and far, ahead and to the sides, each with a colour. A wall's number on the map, 1 to 15, picks a set and a shape: 1 to 5 are the first set's shapes, 6 to 10 the second's and 11 to 15 the third's. The three sets' characters go into the character set at $6A, $9C and $CE, so a WALLDEF drawn for the first set has its codes moved up by 50 or 100 when it is loaded as the second or third (renumber_wall_chars, $14CB). WALLDEF01 is the first set in most areas, and the third in the slums, Valjevo Castle, Kuto's Well and the catacombs under it. New Phlan is the exception: its fifteen shapes are one file, WALLS00, drawn with 192 characters of their own, SECSET00. Every other map shares SECSET02, 42 characters that include the bands of sky and ground the view starts from ($40-$42).

There are two wall colours. Bit 7 of a square's byte on the map's third page picks the second for the walls seen from that square, and the same colour fills the sky. Each colour byte in the wall shapes that is not a fixed colour takes it ($1157), so a whole block can be recoloured in one pass of 2,340 bytes. Most scripts set the first colour by the clock: cyan while the hour is under 14, black from hour 14 on. The buttons above the view switch between the two where the map's script does.

Every wall shape on the disks

Each of the eighteen WALLDEF files, and New Phlan's WALLS00, drawn by the same driver down a short corridor of one shape, with the shape again across its end. The maps that draw each set are named under it.

Point at a shape for where it is drawn.

Five of the shapes are drawn on no map: the first of WALLDEF02, the third and fourth of WALLDEF07, the last of WALLDEF08 and the last of WALLDEF17, marked in coral. Each file is loaded by some map, but no map that loads it has a wall of that number in its GEO file.

Two wall sets drawn as one

Fifty characters are not enough for the Valhingen Graveyard's walls or for the hedges of Valjevo Castle. Their scripts load two wall sets that share their hundred characters: WALLDEF15 and WALLDEF16 in the graveyard, and the hedges of WALLDEF19, which use the castle walls' characters as well as their own. Their codes are already numbered for the two places, so before loading them the script sets a flag for each of the first two sets ($49E7, $49E8), and the game leaves the codes as they are. The bailey sets the flags as the party arrives in it from anywhere else, and the ways back out of the castle, and out of the graveyard, clear them.

The castle's hedge maze, from the square the walk above starts on there: as the game draws it, and with the two flags clear, so that the hedges' codes are moved up by 50 into the third set's characters.

03 · Portraits

A head drawn over a body

A character's portrait is two pictures: a body eleven characters square, and a head five rows deep drawn over the top of it. The disks hold 41 heads and 21 bodies. Choose any of them, or one of the people who join the party.

 

The portraits are drawn in the built site.

Drawn as ANIMATE00 ($8400) draws a portrait: the body as a whole picture ($8406), then the head ($8409) as a picture of the second kind, which does not clear the characters first. The head's 55 characters take the place of the body's first 55, the top five rows, with the head's colours, and the head's three shared colours ($D021-$D023) are the screen's. Every head and body is a single frame.

Portraits are shown in two places only: when a character is made, where the player steps through 14 heads and 12 bodies (choose_portrait, $12B6), and on the VIEW screen in the civilised part of New Phlan ($48A4). New Phlan's script turns them on when the party arrives and off as it leaves ($49EB). The scripts use the same files for the people met on the way, a head over a body in place of a close-up picture (load_picture_files, $27BC). A person who joins the party is a monster file with a head and a body of its own, at bytes $FE and $FF of the record, the same places a character keeps them.

HEAD03, on disk 3 side B, is on no list and in no script: nothing in the game shows it. Two heads and three bodies are offered only when a character is made: HEAD12 and HEAD33, and BODY12, BODY1A and BODY23. HEAD44 is otherwise only the training hall's THEURGIST's.

04 · A missing file

Bug: three people who join the party have portraits no disk holds

The MAD MAN of Mendor's library has head 23, and the LEVEL 6 MU of the graveyard and PRINCESS FATIMA of the kobold caves have body 00. There is no HEAD23 or BODY00 on any of the eight disk sides.

What VIEW shows for one of the three in New Phlan: the game asks for side 3 to load the file, and asks again after every key, whichever side is in the drive (load_resource_file, $40B9, and disk_prompt, $4378).

The game draws a portrait only on the VIEW screen in New Phlan's civilised streets, so while these three are away in their own areas their missing files never matter. If one of them is still with the party in the city and the player chooses VIEW for them, the game cannot go on: it loads the head, then the body, from disk 2 side A, finds no file of that number, and asks for the disk again and again (load_resource, $4222), whichever side is put in the drive. The title's demonstration party has body 00 too, for RESTAL, TARRAN and DIANE.

05 · The ending

The ending's two scenes

When the dragon falls in the last battle, the game plays two scenes in the picture window: the dragon's death, then the spirit of Tyranthraxus, which rises from its body and is called back through the pool.

The scenes as FINAL ($0800) plays them: each background is 15 by 15 characters from SPELLE20, drawn with a SECSET's characters (draw_scene, $09A9); the pictures PIC34, PIC36 and PIC35 are drawn frame by frame at their own pace; the six sprites are PIC3A's eleven images, placed by sprite_x and sprite_y; and the waits between steps are the game's own delay loops, timed at the C64's speed. The sounds are left out.

The first scene loads every picture both scenes need at once and keeps them out of the way (load_scenes, $08E7), so nothing is loaded between the two. The spirit's figure is six sprites: a yellow one, and five in grey that swap between two sets of images on every round of the flash (swap_images, $0B68), while the picture's shared colour and the sprites' flash white and grey. When the spirit cries out, the pool's blue goes grey (PIC35, drawn over the window as a portrait's head is drawn over its body), the five grey sprites fade to black until only the yellow eyes are left, and the eyes fall, faster at each step. They turn red after the first step: the test at $0B4C sets the colour whenever the wait is 50 or more, and the wait starts at 100.

06 · A figure

Nine characters, each a piece with its own colour

The large figure the editor starts from, cell by cell. Each cell's code names a piece in CHARPIC00, and the piece's kind gives it a colour.

The editor's starting icon with the first large weapon and the first large head laid out by its own code. The kinds come from piece_types ($A700), the colours from kind_colors ($B645) plus 8 for a multicolour piece, as put_piece ($B209) gives them.

A figure is 36 bytes: the ready pose's nine character codes, the attacking pose's nine, and a colour for each of the eighteen cells. The party's are kept at $4BE0, 36 bytes for each of the eight slots. Each code names a piece, eight bytes of picture at $9900 + 8 times the code in CHARPIC00. piece_types tells the editor what each piece is, weapon, body, cap, hair, shield, arm or leg, and whether it is drawn in multicolour, with wide pixels in three colours, or in hires, with narrow pixels in one.

The colours are one to a kind of piece, so a figure's whole body takes one colour and its legs another; the editor's COLOR menu changes them a kind at a time. Rather than put the pieces' codes on the screen, draw_figure copies each piece's picture into a character of the figure's own, $60 plus the cell's number among the 36 the editor shows, and puts that character on the screen. A battle does the same with all twelve slots: assign_icon_codes ($0942) gives each slot's two poses, and a mirror image of each for facing the other way, codes of their own in the battle's character set.
