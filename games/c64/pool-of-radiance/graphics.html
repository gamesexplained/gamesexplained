1988 · SSI

Pool of Radiance

In a battle every combatant is a little figure of nine characters with two poses, ready and attacking. A party member's figure is put together from a head and a weapon in the game's own editor.

01 · Everything that moves

Every figure the icon editor makes, and the sprites around them

A party member's figure in a battle is three characters by three, drawn in two poses. The combat icon editor builds it from a weapon and a head, and every choice it offers is laid out here by its own code. Each figure strikes as a battle draws an attack: the ready pose, then the attacking one, then ready again. The monsters' figures come from a file for each kind of monster, loaded for the battle.

The figures are drawn by the game's own code in the built site.

Point at a figure for its pieces.

Each figure is laid out by the editor's own lines for a choice (save_head, $B26F, then pick_piece from $B14C), run on the kit's 6502 in the page over the bytes of the icons part, starting from the editor's own icon with the size's first weapon and first head. The cells are drawn as the video chip shows the characters draw_figure ($B361) makes from the pieces' pictures, on the battle's colours: dark grey, light red and black (combat_colours, $146A).

A figure has nine cells in each pose. A weapon choice fills the cells below the head from a string of codes kept for that choice (cell_strings, $ABC0), and a head choice fills the top middle cell, and for some heads the cells beside it. A cap or a lock of hair never takes a top cell from a piece of another kind. The large figures are for the man-sized races, and the small ones for dwarves, gnomes and halflings.

The battle's sprites are few. The cursor that marks the square in hand is a hollow box built by code, 24 pixels wide and 12 tall, doubled in height and drawn behind the characters (set_sprites, $147D). A fallen combatant is covered by two sprites, a red one in front of a black one, stretched for its size (lie_down, $1BF9). A missile is sprite 6, given one of twenty pictures for the kind of shot and the way it flies, with a colour pair for each picture (load_missile_picture, $6A57). The battle copies those twenty pictures under the input and output chips, and only the first four are among the bytes the Source tab lists.

The title has the remaining sprites: three red pieces that fill the TSR logo from behind the picture, and three white sparkles. Each sparkle grows from a dot to a four-pointed star and shrinks again over fifteen steps of sparkle_frames, taking a step at random, 15 times in 256, and then jumps to a random place on the picture.

02 · A figure

Nine characters, each a piece with its own colour

The large figure the editor starts from, cell by cell. Each cell's code names a piece in CHARPIC00, and the piece's kind gives it a colour.

The editor's starting icon with the first large weapon and the first large head laid out by its own code. The kinds come from piece_types ($A700), the colours from kind_colors ($B645) plus 8 for a multicolour piece, as put_piece ($B209) gives them.

A figure is 36 bytes: the ready pose's nine character codes, the attacking pose's nine, and a colour for each of the eighteen cells. The party's are kept at $4BE0, 36 bytes for each of the eight slots. Each code names a piece, eight bytes of picture at $9900 + 8 times the code in CHARPIC00. piece_types tells the editor what each piece is, weapon, body, cap, hair, shield, arm or leg, and whether it is drawn in multicolour, with wide pixels in three colours, or in hires, with narrow pixels in one.

The colours are one to a kind of piece, so a figure's whole body takes one colour and its legs another; the editor's COLOR menu changes them a kind at a time. Rather than put the pieces' codes on the screen, draw_figure copies each piece's picture into a character of the figure's own, $60 plus the cell's number among the 36 the editor shows, and puts that character on the screen. A battle does the same with all twelve slots: assign_icon_codes ($0942) gives each slot's two poses, and a mirror image of each for facing the other way, codes of their own in the battle's character set.
