1988 · Imagine Software · Commodore 64

Target Renegade

What the manual, the magazines, and the code say about getting through five stages in the time given.

01 · The aim

Each stage runs on its own clock

The game ends in two ways. Running out of lives, or out of time, sets a negative end flag and returns to the title ($8283). Defeating the final opponent sets a positive one and plays the ending ($9ABA). Each stage has its own clock. The table at $A8C2 holds six starting values, 8, 4, 4, 4, 4, and 4 minutes, indexed by stage. If it is indexed from the first stage, the car park gives eight minutes and every later stage four. Zzap!64's level 1 screenshots show the clock at 6:18 and 6:24, which fits a start at eight minutes.9

No route through the game has been played to the end for this page. The captures of later stages were reached with explicit state changes, and no fight was completed. The advice below comes from the manual, the magazines, and the tables in the code, and says which is which.

02 · Staying alive

A bullet costs twice what a blow costs

FactWhere
Endurance is 44 units, refilled on every new life.$8D67
An enemy's blow costs 2 units, so a life lasts 22 blows.$9BD1
An object that hits you, such as a bullet, costs 4 units.$8B88
Each enemy you defeat gives back 2 units, up to 44.$9ABA
A new game starts with five lives. An extra life comes at 50,000 and then every 100,000, to a maximum of nine.$8000, $FC00

Two things follow. A bullet costs twice what a blow costs, so a gunman is worth avoiding, not trading with. And because every defeat returns two units, finishing an enemy quickly is worth more than avoiding every blow. Both are consequences of the numbers, not advice from a source.

04 · Advice

Advice from the people who played it

The manual's hints are to use weapons wherever possible, because a weapon has a greater effect and also gives "a better points bonus", and to "eliminate your enemies before you progress".1 Paul Glancey found that many opponents fall to the stick held in the punch position and repeated presses of fire.9 Gordon Houghton found the timing "frustratingly precise and sometimes illogical" at first, and wrote that practice "really does encourage progress".9 The review's own caption warns: "Turning your back on the opposition isn't advisable, even when they're on their knees."9

Whether the game can be finished is not in doubt. A reader at Lemon64 wrote in 2024 that they had finished it again, after about thirty years.2 No source read for this page says how many players have done it. The score and lives are the other way to measure a run: see Gameplay.
