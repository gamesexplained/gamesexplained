1988 · Imagine Software · Commodore 64

Target Renegade

What the joystick does, move by move, as the manual, the magazines, and the code describe it.

01 · The joystick

One stick, one button

The game reads a joystick in port 1. The title screen starts on fire from that port, and a test with the same press on port 2 changed nothing. The Commodore key pauses and resumes play, and the manual says so.1 The title screen names Space as the music switch. In the normal play tested, Space changed nothing.

The manual draws two diagrams. With the fire button up, the stick walks in eight directions. With fire held, each direction is a different move.1 Zzap!64 put it as "everything is done from the joystick", and Commodore User noted that the first game's moves no longer needed the space bar.9,10

02 · The moves

Eight directions with fire held

Stick, fire heldThe manual calls itWhat the code does
UpJump upStarts a jump.
Up and left, or up and rightJump kickStarts a jump and adds a horizontal step.
Left or rightBack kick, and punch or swing weaponA front attack, a bat swing, or a back kick, chosen by the way the player faces ($A278).
DownDuck, or pick up weaponDucks, and checks for a dropped weapon within reach ($A2D6).
Down and left, or down and rightDuck punch, and "shrug" opponentTwo special moves with their own poses ($A1A4, $A21C). Which pose is the duck punch and which the shrug is not settled.

The code finds the direction by combining four on or off bits from the stick into a number, which indexes a table at $A035. The overview has a control pad that shows the result for each direction and each facing: try it. Thirty-six combinations of stick and facing were tested against the real code.

03 · Technique

Mashing the punch

Two of the reviewers described what the controls reward. Gordon Houghton wrote that "frustratingly precise and sometimes illogical timing" was needed at first, and that practice then makes it addictive.9 Paul Glancey found that many opponents fall to the stick held in the punch position and repeated presses of fire.9 A weapon "replaces the punch", according to Commodore User, and the manual says weapons work better and score a better bonus.10,1

Readers on Lemon64 complain about the timing, and one calls it exact to the frame. The timing of a punch against the enemy's hit window has not been measured here, so it is open.
