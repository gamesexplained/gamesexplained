1988 · Imagine Software · Commodore 64

Target Renegade

How a fight is won and lost: two enemy slots, nine enemy types, and an endurance bar of 44 units.

01 · The fight

Two enemies at a time

The game keeps two enemy slots. When the player attacks, the code tests slot 1 and then slot 0 ($A0B7), and a new enemy goes into a free slot, sometimes with a partner on the opposite side ($8631). That fits what Commodore User wrote: "Attackers come at you in pairs or singly."10

There are nine enemy types, numbered 0 to 8. The game gives each a starting health ($8704) and a score for each hit ($A0AD), and a stage adds a base number of 0, 0, 2, 4, or 6 to pick which types it spawns ($8738). The fifth stage forces type 7 instead. The table below is the data, by type number. Which picture on the screen carries which number is not settled, so only the traced behaviours are named.

Scores are in displayed points: the game stores them in hundreds. The score table holds a tenth value, 900, that has no health entry beside it. Each landed hit takes one health point ($A0F9); a weapon may take more, and that has not been checked. Type 8 has the most health and the highest score, and its defeat ends the game, so it is almost certainly Mr. Big.

02 · Endurance

Forty-four units, two for a blow

The player's endurance starts at 44 and is restored to 44 each time a life is lost ($8D67). The bar on the panel shows it as eleven cells of four units each ($8544).

From full endurance a life is lost on the 22nd ordinary blow or the 11th object hit. At zero endurance a life is lost. With one life left, zero endurance sets the end flag to negative, which skips the ending and returns to the title ($8283). Each defeat returns two units, which is one blow's worth.

03 · Lives and time

Five lives, an extra one past 50,000

The supplied cartridge starts a new game with five lives ($8000). The manual says three, and Zzap!64's review says five.1,9 The first extra life comes when the score passes 50,000, and then one more for every 100,000 (Discoveries). Lives stop at nine.

The time on the panel counts down once every 51 passes of the main loop ($8771). When it reaches zero the same negative end flag is set as when the last life goes.

04 · The hold

One enemy holds, the other hits

Paul Glancey described "a biker who sneaks up behind the player and holds him while his partner beats hell out of him from the front".9 The code has a matching rule. A type 0 enemy starts a hold only when its partner slot is active ($9418). It then places the player 24 units to the side it faces, one lane unit above its own, and gives the player the held pose. Whether type 0 is the sprite Glancey called a biker has not been checked.

Turning your back is not safe. The review's own caption says so: "Turning your back on the opposition isn't advisable, even when they're on their knees."9

05 · The gun

A limited supply of bullets

The manual says the boss of stage 2 has a gun with a limited number of bullets, and advises waiting until they run out before attacking him.1 The armed type shows why that works. When it is 30 to 34 units away and within six lane units, a tick-based test may start pose $60. Closer than 30 units it uses up one from an ammunition byte at $0674, starts pose $65, and sets the shared weapon state ($9232). The routine does not set the starting ammunition: that happens when the enemy is created. The size of the supply is not yet read from the game.

06 · Motorbikes

Kicking a rider off his bike

In the car park, riders on motorbikes try to run the player down. The bike's code tests whether the bike overlaps the player ($8E44). On the ground, an overlap hurts the player. A player who is in the air meets a narrower window, and an overlap there marks a dismount hit. That fits the jump kick the manual describes, though the code tests only that the player is elevated. The dismount ($902B) puts a rider at the bike's position and facing, with its own health, in slot 0, and starts two sound effects. At the end of the dismount animation the game leaves an object on the ground if none is there and spawns an ordinary enemy ($8F8E). The manual says a rider kicked off his bike is knocked unconscious for a very short time.1 How long that is has not been measured.
