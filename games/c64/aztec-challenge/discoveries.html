Commodore 64 · 1983 · Cosmi

Aztec Challenge

A snake that is set up and never shown, a fire button that makes the swimmer bite-proof, and a bridge that pays for holding the stick the wrong way: what the code does that the manual never said.

01 · The Vermin

Bug: the Vermin's green snake is placed but never appears

The Vermin brings on six creatures, one for each of the low six bits of a random number ($6700). For each bit that is set, it checks whether that creature is already out, by the same bit of the byte $2A, and if not it marks it out and places it. Marking is an ORA of the creature's bit into $2A. For the fifth creature, bit 4, the value is #$00 where it should be #$10 ($6772). The creature is placed ($681A: sprite 6, shape $9C, a coiled snake, in green) but never marked, so the routine that would move it and switch its sprite on ($6A80) is never called. Each time bit 4 comes up again the snake is placed again, its sprite still switched off.

The six marking instructions, read from the listing's bytes. Live in VICE on 8 November 2026: 60 seconds of the Vermin, the sprites on screen sampled 281 times; sprites 0-5 and 7 all came on, sprite 6 never, and bit 4 of $2A was never set. A second copy of the checks at $6860, which nothing calls, has the same #$00 ($68C7).

02 · The Piranha

Secret: holding fire with a direction keeps the swimmer safe from the piranhas

The manual warns that fire must be pressed with the stick centred, "or neither function will work". On the Piranha that is half true. The swimmer's controls ($1DE0) dive only for fire alone, and move only for a direction alone; fire with a direction does neither. The bite check ($1EA0) asks a different question: is the fire bit pressed at all? If so it empties the collision register and returns ($1E97), and no fish can bite. The drowning count only runs while diving ($7F9D), and fire with a direction is not a dive, so the swimmer never drowns either. It also never moves, so the lake is never crossed: it is a way to wait, not to win.

03 · The Bridge

Secret: holding the stick right on the Bridge scores 10 points a pass

Every level counts "achievements" in one byte, $68, worth 10 points each when the attempt ends ($1456). The Bridge counts one on every pass of its loop in which the stick is held in any direction but fire alone ($7D4C), before it looks at which direction it is. Down, left and up then start a jump and the count stops; right and the diagonals start nothing, so the runner stays on the bridge and the count goes on. Held right for 10 seconds, it went from 0 to 72, 720 points. The count is one byte, so after 255 it starts again at 0: the most it can pay is 2,560 points (255 achievements and the 10 every attempt adds), and holding on too long loses them.

At the rate measured in VICE (72 passes in 10 seconds, 7.2 a second, on 8 November 2026), with the byte wrapping as INC $68 does. The points are added when the attempt ends, won or lost.

04 · The Piranha

Bug: a lost life on the Piranha throws away all the progress

The Piranha counts the swimmer's way up the lake by the passages off its top edge ($1AB8): each counts an achievement, and every second one raises the progress stage, which wins at stage 9. When a life is lost the level restarts through the music's reset ($1CA0), which also clears the stage. Other levels clear their progress too, but a crossing of the lake has to be done in one life, eighteen passages without a bite.

Swimming up in VICE: stage 0, then 1 at the second passage; a bite; stage 0 again. Repeated five times over two minutes, the stage never passed 1.

05 · The Temple

A falling spear can strike the runner's head and do no harm until it reaches the legs

The runner is two sprites, the upper half and the lower half (Graphics). The Temple's trap check ($79F0) reads the sprite collision register and keeps its two lowest bits, one for each half. Only the value 2, the lower half touching something while the upper half does not, catches the runner. A spear or a stone that strikes the head, or both halves at once, passes on, and the runner is caught only when it has fallen past the head onto the legs. Until then the runner can still run out from under it.

06 · Hopaztec

Secret: Hopaztec's trapped tiles come from one fixed sequence

The game picks its trapped tiles from voice 3's noise generator ($74C2), reading it after a fixed delay each time ($74EC). The noise generator is a shift register that runs on the clock, so read at the same intervals it gives the same numbers in the same order. Which part of that sequence a player gets depends only on how long the scorecard waited for fire: two attempts started at the same moment get the same traps, and an attempt started a little later gets the same list shifted on by a few numbers.

Twenty lists drawn in VICE on 9 November 2026, phase 1, each started a little over half a second later than the one before. Matching runs of numbers are marked in the same colour: list 2 is list 1 four numbers on, and lists 6 and 7, 10 and 11, 13 and 14 are the same list.

07 · Hopaztec

Bug: four hops take the hopper off the floor and into the walls

Hopaztec stops a hop at the floor's edges with tables ($5BE8-$5C20): a hop forward is refused on the back row, a hop back on the front row, and a hop sideways at each row's left and right edge. The floor is not a rectangle, though. Its right edge steps in three times on the way to the back, and its left edge steps out once from the front row, so at four tiles a hop forward or back lands on no tile at all: from tile 60, 66 or 72 forward, into the right-hand wall, and from tile 1 back, under the left border, where the hopper is almost out of sight. Out there the tile numbers go on (61, 67, 73 and 0), the hops go on, and so does the check for traps, which knows only numbers. The hopper can hop back onto the floor, and it can go on hopping round the outside of the room.

The four exits are on the Levels tab's map of the floor, worked out from the tables and held against the game's own hop checks, from every tile in every direction (test_hop.js). Live in VICE, with the traps switched off, the hopper hopped forward from tiles 60, 66 and 72 off the floor, came back from the first two, and, once off the floor, hopped right past the edge of the screen and came round again from the left as its X passed 511.

08 · Smaller things

Quirks in the controls, the traps and the night

  • The Stairs move left for up and down. The climber's stick ($71A0) sorts the input by size: anything below 7 moves left, so up and down alone move the climber left as well; anything of 7 or more moves right, fire included.
  • The third block always rolls down the same line. A stone block that reaches the foot of the Stairs comes back at the top ($7000): the first at the climber's X, aimed at the climber, the second at a random X from CIA 1's timer, and the third at the X it reads from CIA 2's timer ($70AD). Nothing in the game ever starts that timer, which stands at $FFFF in every snapshot of it, so the read always gives 255 and the third block always comes down at X 255, right of the middle.
  • Some creatures appear on the screen instead of walking in. The Vermin places its scorpion, spiders, snake and lizard at 256 plus a random byte ($67A0); the screen's right edge is at 343, so about a third of them pop up already on it.
  • The doorway can be a trapped tile. Hopaztec's list of trapped tiles ($74C2) can hold 44, the doorway's number, and repeats, and numbers that are no tile of the floor (0 and 61-66 lie off it); the check after a hop ($1FE8) skips 44, so a trapped doorway is harmless and a repeat or a number off the floor is a wasted trap.
  • Night falls on four levels only. Phase 4's two colours darken the Gauntlet, the Piranha, the Bridge and Hopaztec's doorway; the Stairs, the Temple and the Vermin never read them and look the same by night.
  • Left-overs. Two sprite shapes no routine uses ($B2, $BA), a second copy of the Vermin's creature checks nothing calls ($6860), and 52 bytes of what look like screen positions at $457A that nothing reads.
  • The Gauntlet punishes holding the stick. Any input held for 8 passes of the main loop makes the runner fall ($614F), the manual's "staying in either mode for too long", which also catches a jump held too long.
