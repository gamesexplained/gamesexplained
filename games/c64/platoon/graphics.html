Commodore 64 · 1987 · Ocean

Platoon: graphics

The picture the cassette draws while it loads, from the tape's own bytes and at the tape's own pace.

01 · The loading picture

The film's poster, a row of cells at a time

The 1987 cassette first prints PLATOON (C) 1987 OCEAN SOFTWARE in white on black, with blue stripes running in the border. Two minutes into the tape the screen turns white, and the film's poster fills in on it a cell at a time, left to right along each row of cells and down the screen, over 29 seconds, while Jonathan Dunn's title tune plays (Music).

The cassette's own bytes, recorded in VICE once the picture was in, and drawn as the video chip draws them. The bitmap appears at the tape's own pace, where it had got to every fifth of a second.

The picture recreates the film's poster: a soldier with his arms raised in a jungle clearing, and two dog tags that hang over the title and stand in for both letters O. The line along the top reads SEE THE AWARD WINNING FILM AVAILABLE NOW ON VIDEO FROM RCA COLUMBIA PICTURES, beside the video company's logo.

The screen colours and colour RAM are in place when the screen turns white, so each cell comes in finished. The picture is a multicolour bitmap, 8,000 bytes, read in the order it lies in memory: the eight rows of an 8 by 8 cell together, forty cells across, then the next row of cells down. It arrives at about 270 bytes a second, from 122 seconds into the tape to 151, and stays up while the rest of the first load comes in.

The disk shows the same picture, byte for byte, but puts it up whole and in silence, about 25 seconds into its load.
