A fighter’s shirt and legs share the same hardware sprites. The game swaps their shapes as the screen is drawn.

Four shapes make one fighter

Visible pieces
Opening pose with separate colour and outline layers.

Loading the shape records from the listing.

Each half uses two hardware sprites in the same place. The black outline can draw a single pixel. The colour layer draws in pairs of pixels but has three colours to work with. Turn either layer off to see what it contributes.

The game draws the upper pair first. The routine at $3003 schedules the switch to the lower pair while the display is drawing the fighter. The original instructions mention a gap at the waist. This opening pose shows the stored layers at their nominal spacing. The exact pixels visible at the switch depend on when the game changes the pointers.

The outline and colour shapes for this pose are at $EE40 and $EE00 for the top, and $E8C0 and $E880 for the bottom.

Shape pieces stored on disk

One stored pair of VIC sprite shapes.

Shape bytes in the listing

Most shape pieces stay in memory. Three of the level files replace the slots starting at $D000. Those addresses also select the C64 chips, so the picture bytes live in the RAM under the chip registers. The video chip can see that RAM while the CPU is using the chips.
