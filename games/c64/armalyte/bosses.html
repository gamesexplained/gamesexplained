1988 · Thalamus

Armalyte

Fifteen boss fights, run on this page by the game's own boss routines: how each boss moves, which of its sprites shoot, what it lets loose, and how long it lasts before it blows itself up.

01 · The fights

How each boss moves and shoots

A boss is up to 15 sprites flying in formation. Only its core, and the guards that shield the core, can be hurt. The rest is armour, and the enemies a boss lets loose cannot be shot at all.

The viewer runs the game's routines for paths, firing, and releases, written again in JavaScript. The two ships stay where they start, and shots that reach them burst without harm.

A level's boss table places each sprite of the boss and gives it a path: a list of steps, each a speed across and a speed down held for a number of frames. The sprites that keep together run paths with the same steps, so the boss moves as one ship, and a part with steps of its own, such as a guard on levels 4, 6, and 7, flies its own course. Every boss drifts in from the right at a pixel a frame. An end boss arrives with the end of the map, which scrolls in beside it for its first 199 frames. For a mid-level boss the scroll stops first and the boss flies in to meet the ships. Level 8 has one mid-level table, so it meets the same boss twice, at full strength both times.

The core never fires. Whenever one of the boss's shots is free, the game looks through the boss's sprites from the last to the first, and the shot goes to the first one on a step of its path marked for firing (enemies_fire, $A664). One or two sprites therefore do nearly all of a boss's shooting. Many of them fire only at a target below them, or only at one above. A shot is aimed at player 1, or on alternate shots at the second ship, and a new shot never starts within 45 lines, up or down, of one already flying.

Only the core and its guards can be hurt. Once a frame the game looks for the players' fire in three character squares inside each of their sprites (enemies_hit_by_fire, $A54C), and fire found there counts one hit and is wiped from the screen. That includes a super weapon's fire, which goes on through ordinary enemies but is spent on a boss part like a single shot. While a guard is alive, fire that reaches the core is wiped and not counted (boss_part_hit, $AC07). A part takes at most one hit a frame, so the 480 hits of level 5's boss take nearly ten seconds even if every frame scores.

Most bosses also let enemies loose, one or two at a time, into the sprites the boss leaves spare (boss_spawn, $AD89). The core releases them while it is on a step of its path marked for firing, the same mark that lets the other parts shoot. These enemies cannot be shot. In a boss fight the game looks for hits only on the core and its guards, so a released enemy flies through the players' fire untouched and has to be dodged. Released enemies can shoot as well.

02 · Timers

Every boss can be outlasted

A boss fight stops the scroll for a time the level sets. When the time is up, the boss blows itself up, and the game pays out as if the player had destroyed it.

From each level's script (the stop times, $9730 in a level, in units of 48 frames) and its two boss tables ($9778 for the end boss, $97D8 for the mid-level boss). The core takes no hits until its guards are gone. The seconds are at the PAL frame rate, 50.125 a second.

The script runs once every eight frames, at the end of each scroll step, and it keeps running while the scroll is stopped ($A93E). A stop counts down in units of six runs, 48 frames, and 0 counts as 256. When the count runs out the script calls level_end ($AC20), the same routine that destroying the boss's core calls, and every object on the screen explodes.

What happens next depends on where the scroll is. Away from the map's end, a mid-level fight pays 50,000 points and the scroll moves on. At the end, the end-of-level sequence plays for 148 frames and level_complete ($AE25) pays one million points and an extra life to each player. A player who hides from the end boss for four minutes gets exactly what a player who shot it gets. In level 1, with no input at all, the mid-level boss went at the end of its 2,880 frames with the score going from 0 to 50,000, and the end boss went after 12,288 frames and the level was completed.

Levels 5 and 7 have no mid-level boss: no event in their scripts starts one, though level 7 carries a copy of level 4's in its table. Level 8 starts its mid-level boss twice, at columns 167 and 242.
