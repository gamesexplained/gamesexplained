1988 · Thalamus

Armalyte

The ships, the enemies, the bosses and their shots are all sprites; only the players' own fire is drawn into the screen as characters. Each level brings its own 192 sprite frames into the same place in memory, and every enemy, boss and shot is a short run of those frames played in a colour of its own.

01 · Everything that moves

Every sprite of a level, played at the game's rate

The ships, the drone and the pods are the same on every level. The enemies, their shots and the bosses come with the level's file. Here each one plays its frames as the game's own tables say, at 50 frames a second.

Drawn from the level's sprite frames ($4000 + 64 × the frame number in its listing) and the engine's tables. Every sprite is multicolour, with white and dark grey shared by all of them (video_setup_play, $AB0F) and its own colour from its object type ($03E6 in the level). An enemy moves on through its frames once every second frame, held for the delay its type gives (objects_animate, $F022), and only while the step of its path is marked for animation. Here every enemy animates all the time.

An enemy's look is five bytes in five tables, one entry per object type (objects_init_new, $E3AA): its first and last frame, a byte holding its firing mode, its frame delay and whether it plays back and forth, its colour, and its hit points. The tables hold 162 types, and many enemies share their frames with other types that differ only in colour or behaviour. A run of frames is played forwards and starts again, or plays to its last frame and back, holding each end for one more step.

A boss is built from the same objects as a wave. Its table names an object type for each of up to 16 parts, and most parts show a single frame, so a boss is a picture cut into sprite-sized pieces that fly in formation. The Bosses tab runs every boss fight with these parts in place.

The ships tilt rather than turn. Player 1's ship has seven frames with level flight in the middle. Every fifth frame the game moves it one frame towards one end while the stick is pushed up, towards the other while it is pulled down, and back towards the middle when the stick is let go (ship1_bank, $F407). A second player's ship does the same with frames $07-$0D. The drone has no tilt at all: it turns through eight frames of its own, a step every fourth frame while it is attached and every second frame when it is set loose (drone_spin, $9F0E).

02 · The sprite frames

A level's 192 sprite frames, and what uses each one

The ships, the drone, the scoreboard, the pods and the bursts have the same frames in every level's file. The rest belong to the level, and a few of them nothing ever shows.

Point at a frame to see what uses it.

Frames $00-$BF, sixteen to a row, from $4000 to $6FFF in the level's listing, each in the colour of the first thing that uses it. The bar under a frame says what uses it; a frame with no bar is never shown. What uses a frame comes from the level's object types, those its waves place, its bosses are built from and its bosses let loose, and from the engine's fixed frames.

The scoreboard's eight frames are drawn into while the game runs: panel_update ($99FA) writes the score and each player's figures into them from the engine's font, one changed item a frame, so what the level file holds there is only a starting picture. Frame $BF is blank in every level and is what an exploding ship shows after its last burst frame ($EA18).

03 · The screen

One bank of the video chip's memory holds the sprites, both screens and the characters

The video chip sees 16K of memory at a time. In play it sees $4000-$7FFF ($DD00 = 2), and most of what it holds comes from the level's file.

The sprite frames come from the level file. The two screens at $7000 and $7400 are drawn by the scroll, each ending in its eight sprite pointers ($73F8, $77F8). Characters $20-$FF are the level's own set, copied to $7900 from $D000 in its file at the level's start, and the game builds the stars' characters below them.

The playfield is a multicolour character screen, and the map is drawn into whichever of the two screens is hidden, one column on over the eight frames of a scroll step. At line 0 the interrupt shows the finished one ($D018 = $CE or $DE, from $24), so the map moves a character left with no redrawing seen. The How it works tab draws the screen from a frame of the game's memory and shows every level scrolling.

Characters $F6 to $FF of each level's set are the players' fire, written into the screen (How it works), and the stars are characters too. Everything else that moves is a sprite, and the How it works tab shows how the game gets up to thirty of them out of the machine's eight (Sprites). The eight that make the scoreboard below the playfield are grey (panel_sprites_setup, $9D6C).
