Commodore 64 · 1989 · Domark

Licence To Kill: gameplay

Six scenes in five levels, a helicopter whose throttle is also its height, and a clock that pays out what is left of it.

01 · The levels

Six scenes, five levels

The reviews counted six sections, and the game counts five levels. The dive and the water-ski are one level in the code, number 3, and harpooning the seaplane switches from one to the other.

Each level has a 25-byte record in the table at level_records, which start_level reads: where its map ends, its colour scheme and playfield colour, how fast its fuel runs down, its list of enemy launches, its tiles, and its table of character heights. Most of the values are written straight into the code as it runs, so the same routines serve every level. After level 5 the level number goes back to 0 (level_complete).

02 · Energy, lives and the clock

Seven shots, four lives

A shot or a crash takes energy, and Bond loses a life when the energy is gone. The level then starts again from its beginning.

The energy is a count at $039E. Every level starts it at 47 (start_timer), and levels 1 and 3 then set it to 6. An enemy shot takes 1 on level 1 and 8 on level 2 (enemy_shots_hit_player), so either way Bond survives six shots and the seventh costs a life. On level 4 each touch of a diver takes 1; on level 5 the tanker has an energy count of its own, and a Stinger missile takes 3 of it (move_stinger). A crash into the scenery sets the count below zero at once.

The lives start at 3 ($19F6), and the panel shows 03. Each loss takes one away, but the game ends only when the count goes below zero, so Bond plays on at 00 and has four attempts.

The countdown in the panel loses one every other frame, 25 a second (count_down_timer), and stops at 0000; running out of time is not a loss. Whatever is left when a level ends is added to the score as a bonus. Measured in the emulator, 200 frames of play took the clock from 1920 to 1820.4

03 · Crashes

Fly higher to clear the buildings

On level 1 the stick's up and down set the helicopter's throttle, from 1 to 47. The throttle picks how fast the scenery scrolls, and it is also how high the helicopter flies: the game crashes it into anything taller than that.

The opening picture of level 1, drawn from its map, tiles and character set. Every character the helicopter would crash into at this throttle is hatched in white and outlined in black. The test is the game's own: a character crashes the helicopter when its height times 4 is at least the throttle (player_hits_scenery).

The C64 can report when a sprite touches the background, but the game never reads that register. Instead each level has a table of heights, one for every character, four bits each (heights_a for levels 1 and 4, heights_b for the others). Each frame on level 1, player_hits_scenery copies the throttle into the compare instruction at $333F, works out which screen characters lie under the helicopter, and looks up the heights of up to seven of them, chosen by where the helicopter sits within a character from the lists at scenery_offsets. One that is high enough sets the energy below zero. Run on the game's own code over a moment of play, with the helicopter placed at 530 points over the screen, throttle 1 crashed at 100 of them, throttle 8 at 55, throttle 16 at 5, and throttle 24 or more at none.4

The same heights decide where shots stop: a character taller than a shot ends it (move_shots). On level 2 the routine is entered further in, at $32D1, and a high character puts Bond back where he stood, which is how the boatyard's walls stop him. The Maps tab can show every character's height.

04 · The scroll

Two screens and a four-part copy

The scenery moves down the screen a pixel or more each frame, by the C64's fine-scroll register (scroll_step). Each time it has moved a whole character, every row must be copied one row down, which is too much work for one frame. The game keeps two screens, at $4000 and $4400, shows one while it copies into the other in four parts of six rows (scroll_copy_rows_a to _d), draws the new top row from the map, and then flips the display to it (set_scroll_regs).

Enemies arrive from a list for each level, three bytes an entry: a scroll position, a column, and a kind of enemy (launch_from_map). When the scroll reaches the position, a group of up to six sprites appears. Each of the 45 kinds names its shape, colour, hit points, hit box and where its shots start from, in the tables from type_state.
