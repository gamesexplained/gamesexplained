Tutti Frutti

The first block that you push after loading the game waits two and a half seconds before it moves. In the block orchards, a Bad Apple in each corner wins the round.

Bug: The first block that you push after loading waits two and a half seconds

In the block orchards (levels 3 and 6, and every third level after them), Super Strawberry pushes cherry blocks and Bad Apple blocks with the fire button. A pushed block slides away at once, except the first one after the game is loaded. That block stays where it is for about two and a half seconds, and then it slides. Every block pushed after that moves at once, so the wait comes back only when the game is loaded again.

The top of the first block orchard. The strawberry stands one step up and one step left of where it starts the round, and pushes down. The bar under each picture is the block's countdown. Both pictures come from the game's own push, countdown and drawing routines, run one frame at a time. The only difference is the starting countdown: 126 on the left and 0 on the right.

Each moving fruit has a countdown, and the fruit moves only when its countdown is zero. A Bad Apple uses its countdown to hang for 32 frames before it falls, which gives the strawberry time to get out from underneath. A pushed block is meant to move at once. The push routine at $2D34 gives the block a position, a direction and a speed, but it never sets the block's countdown, because the countdown should already be zero. The routine that starts each orchard does not set it either.

The block's countdown is one byte of memory, at the hexadecimal address $033F, and the game never writes a starting value into it. In the version of the game on this site, loading leaves the number 126 in that byte. The game takes one off the countdown each frame, 50 times a second, and moves the block on the frame after the countdown reaches zero. That is 127 frames, or about two and a half seconds. After the first push the countdown stays at zero, so every block pushed after it moves on the next frame.

Players noticed the wait too. Warren Pilkington's review calls it a known bug: on the first game after loading, the first block on level three "does not always push straight away but does so after a couple of seconds".

Secret: A Bad Apple block in each corner wins a block orchard

Every orchard ends when its last Acid Apple is gone. Each of the three kinds of orchard also has a second way to win, which pays extra points. In a cherry orchard it is to eat every cherry. In a falling-apple orchard it is to eat every cherry and drop all six Bad Apples onto the bottom row (how an orchard is won). The cassette inlay says only that the job is to collect the cherries.

In a block orchard, crushing every cherry block does not end the round. The second way to win there is to push the four green Bad Apple blocks into the four corners of the orchard. The round then ends at once, and the game adds 3,000 points on top of the bonus that is left on the counter.

The game's own map routine draws the orchard. Yellow squares mark the four Bad Apple blocks, and dashed squares mark the four corners. The goal picture shows the same orchard with its four Bad Apple blocks moved into the corners. The line under the picture is the result of the game's own corner check at $2C78.

The four blocks start near the middle, inside rings of cherry blocks. A pushed block slides until the next square is filled, so a block can leave its ring only through a gap. Pushing a cherry block against something crushes it, which opens a new gap. A Bad Apple block cannot be crushed. In the tunnel that runs round the edge of the orchard, a block pushed along the tunnel slides all the way to its end, which is a corner.

Secret: Shift pauses the orchard tune

Hold Shift, or latch Shift Lock on the computer, to quiet the melody while playing. The code at $1780 switches off voice 1 and leaves its note counter where it was. Voice 2 still handles effects.
