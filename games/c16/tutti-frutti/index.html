1985 · Mastertronic · Commodore 16 / Plus/4

Tutti Frutti

The same orchard engine turns cherries into something to collect, then something to slide at an enemy.

Super Strawberry in the orchard

Super Strawberry cuts paths through soil to reach the cherries. The Acid Apples follow open tunnels from a flashing box in the middle. A Bad Apple can fall through a tunnel and crush the strawberry or an Acid Apple.

In the third kind of orchard, cherries and Bad Apples become sliding blocks. The same fire button that launched a bouncing ball now pushes the fruit.

Music: One theme for two voices

The title plays both halves of each byte in the music table. The orchard uses the high half for the melody, freeing the second voice for the ball, falling fruit, and hits. Shift pauses the orchard melody.

TED register writes recorded from the PAL engine. The theme comes from $3F00, with separate pitch tables for the clear and Game Over tunes at $3EC8 and $3480.

One frame from the character grid

Each square on the screen takes a pattern from memory and a separate byte for its color. The strawberry and the lettering use the same grid. As the fruit moves between cells, the game chooses different pattern pieces to draw it.

The 320×200 reconstruction matches every pixel of the emulator’s display window. The character positions come from $0C00, the colors from $0800, and the patterns from $3800.

Three ways to use the fruit

Clearing the Acid Apple roster works in every orchard. The game also checks a second way to win, based on the rules of that orchard. That route pays an extra award.

OrchardsFruit objectiveExtra points
1 and 4Collect all the cherries.1,000
2 and 5Collect all the cherries and bring six Bad Apples to the bottom row.2,000
3 and 6Slide one Bad Apple block into each corner.3,000

Two four-bit tile values share each byte at $3500. The fruit checks at $2C00 read a working copy of the board. The level viewer shows all six layouts in order.

Turns happen at tile boundaries

The strawberry follows the joystick between tiles. At a junction, the code reads the four neighboring tiles and chooses a legal exit. In a block orchard it can also remember a blocked direction. Fire then tries to slide the fruit ahead, or crushes a cherry if the cell beyond it is occupied.

The keyboard scan at $3100 also accepts A, Z, 3, and 4 for up, down, left, and right. Port 1 supplies fire. The push test starts at $2CB3.

Acid Apples pick a turn

At each tile boundary, an Acid Apple checks the nearby tunnels. It drops the direction it came from if it can go another way, then samples directions until it finds an open one. With a dead end ahead, it turns back. The turn routine starts at $1C80.

A faster round adds more to an eight-bit counter each frame. When the sum crosses 255, the Acid Apples move a step. That same step drives the bonus countdown at $1A00, so later rounds also spend the bonus faster.
