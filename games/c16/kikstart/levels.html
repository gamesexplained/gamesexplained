Kikstart

Every course, drawn from its stored terrain columns, with its time and traffic settings.

01 · The courses

The whole course fits in a narrow strip

Each stored byte selects a vertical strip of five character cells. Joining those strips makes the terrain. The sixteen course starts point into one continuous stream, so the transition shown at an edge can belong to the adjacent course.

All sixteen strips are decoded from $1E50/$1E60 and $2F00 onward. The five expansion tables begin at $1EC0. The rendered ground uses the stored character pixels and TED colours.

02 · The format

The same byte controls the picture and the finish

The low six bits select a terrain column. A separate property table supplies the bike's collision threshold and flags. The timer routine also checks the full byte sixteen columns ahead: 254 enables countdown and 253 requests the finish sequence.

The stream pointer wraps at $3FD8, while the display reads forty columns at a time. That look-ahead can reach the loaded bytes after the wrap address. The viewer divides the stream at its selected starts; those boundaries are different from the moment the rider passes a finish marker. Evidence: $239C, $23EC, $2318, and $2E28.

03 · The difficulty

Time and traffic change by course

Course nine begins with sixteen seconds, while course sixteen begins with forty-eight. The score multiplier rises in steps. Upper-band drift and object-spawn settings change independently, so the course number changes more than the layout.
