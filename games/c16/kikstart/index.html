1986 · Mastertronic · Commodore 16 / Plus/4

Kikstart

A moving road and a bike built from small character squares. Explore the courses, the music for two voices, and the rules behind a jump.

01 · The game

A bike against the clock

Ride through sixteen scrolling courses, clearing obstacles and keeping enough time to reach the finish. Left and right change the bike's speed. Fire jumps. Crashes cost bikes; the course bonus rewards the time and bikes you keep.

02 · The picture

One screen uses two character sets

The score panel and the road share a grid of character codes in memory. Each code selects a small square of pixels. TED, the computer's display chip, changes the set of shapes it reads partway down the screen. Nine character cells make the moving bike.

A held gameplay frame, rebuilt from screen bytes and the character sets. 0 of 64,000 display pixels differ from the matching emulator image. The raster handler at $2118 selects banks $1000 and $1800.

Horizontal scrolling also changes between bands. The bike uses shapes shifted by a few pixels so that the character grid can move underneath it. Inspect the individual shapes.

03 · The music

Music: Two notes fit in one byte

The scrolling demonstration plays two notes from each byte, a small unit of memory. Each half selects from sixteen pitch values for one voice. A step lasts six game updates, about 0.12 seconds. The 192 steps make a loop of about 23.1 seconds.

Writes to TED's sound registers, the chip's control settings, captured for one complete loop. The test regenerates every pitch and control value from $2AA0 and the tables at $1948–$1A47. The captured write times keep the original pacing.

04 · The sound

Sound: The effects share one voice

One voice carries the engine pitch, which follows speed. The other carries a short rising or falling pitch, with noise for crashes and lightning. The effects share four memory locations, so a new effect can replace the one already playing.

These previews play the pitch changes from the original effect routines and $2AEC, updated once per game pass. The engine stays at speed 128 while each effect plays.

05 · The controls

Speed changes continue in the air

Ground controls change speed by three units per update. In the air they change it by two. That smaller step lets a jump be adjusted after take-off.

The button arithmetic is checked across all 256 input speeds against $2784 and $2E44. The printed keyboard legend is reversed in this copy; see the comparison.

06 · The reward

The extra bike comes after the bonus is measured

The game looks up the bonus for remaining bikes before granting an extra bike. Three bikes therefore contribute 40 units even though the next course begins with four. Remaining time contributes one unit per tenth of a second. The course multiplier scales both rewards.

Bike units for one through five bikes: 10, 25, 40, 75, 99. Tables $1D38/$1D40, panel setup $2964, award $2A4A, multiplier $1E10. Compare all course parameters.
