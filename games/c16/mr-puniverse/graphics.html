1985 · Mastertronic

Mr Puniverse

The C16 has no sprites, so every shape that moves is one of the game's 256 characters. The game moves them by rewriting a character's picture, or by writing a different character into the cell. Here is each of them, from the listing's bytes, moved by the game's own routines.

01 · Everything that moves

Every moving shape, at the speed of the main loop

Each picture below is drawn from the character set at $3800, in the colours a room gives it, and moves at 20 passes of the main loop a second, about as fast as the game runs it.

The spinning head, the conveyor belt, the force field, the lift and the vitamin are the game's animate_chars ($1D13) and rotate_vitamin_chars ($2844), run on the kit's 6502 over the listing's memory once a pass. They rewrite the characters' pictures, and the page draws whatever those routines leave there. Mr Puniverse is the game's copy_under_glyphs and merge_player on the same 6502, a pose a pass and a column every four. The crusher's head takes the stages update_crusher ($2F63) gives it, a stage a pass, and the crumbling floor the ones crumble_step ($23A5) gives it, a stage for each pass he stands on it. Each takes the colour of the first cell holding it in rooms 0 to 24. No room has a lift or a bridge, so the lift is drawn in the conveyor's colours.

Two kinds of animation run here. Some characters stay in place on the screen while the game rewrites their pictures. The vitamin's four frames, $D7 to $DA, swap pictures on every pass, so the vitamin turns wherever it is. Every third pass animate_chars does one of three jobs: it spins the random cannon's head, rolls the conveyor belt a pixel left, or scrolls the lift and the force field up a line. One rewritten picture changes every cell that shows that character, all over the room.

Everything else moves by putting a different character in a cell. A cannon's shot is one character, $C5, rubbed out and written again a cell further on, in the cannon's colour. A crusher's head has four pictures for its four heights in a cell, $CD to $D0 beside $D2 to $D5, and leaves its shaft, $CC beside $D1, behind it as it comes down. The crumbling floor goes through eight pairs of characters, two cells wide, and is gone after the eighth.

Mr Puniverse is the exception. His eight poses are not characters at all but 256 bytes at $3A68, where characters $4D to $6C would be. On every pass the game copies the six characters behind him into a buffer and draws his pose over them, and that becomes characters $78 to $7D, the only six he is ever drawn with (How it works).

02 · The character set

One set of 256 characters draws the rooms, the panel and everything that moves

The screen is TED's multicolour text mode: 40 columns and 25 rows of characters, every one from this set. Point at a character to see what it is.

Point at a character.

The set at $3800-$3FFF as the listing has it, sixteen characters to a row from $00 at the top left, each in the colour of the first cell that shows it in rooms 0 to 24, or else on the panel. His poses are in his colour, and a character nothing shows is plain white. It moves with the cast above: the characters the game animates are the same ones here.

A character is eight bytes, one for each of its rows. In a multicolour cell each byte is four pixels two bits wide, and the two bits pick the background, one of the room's two shared colours, or the cell's own colour (How it works). Characters $00 to $4C are the panel's letters and digits, $4D to $6C hold his poses instead, $78 to $7D are his six, and $7E to $DA build the rooms and everything in them. The last 48 bytes, where $FA to $FF would be, are merge_player's buffer, so those characters change every pass.
