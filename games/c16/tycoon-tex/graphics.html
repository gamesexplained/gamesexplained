1985 · Gremlin Graphics

Tycoon Tex

The C16 has no sprites, so Tex, his bullet and everything that comes at him are characters of the game's set, drawn into the play field. Here is each of them in each of the three scenes, from the listing, moved by the game's own routines.

01 · Everything that moves

Every moving shape, in each scene's colours

Tex, his bullet and the explosion run at the game's 50 frames a second. Pick a speed to see his legs run as the game runs them.

Speed

Tex's legs are the game's animate_legs ($1EF2) on the kit's 6502, every leg_delays frames at the speed chosen. His death is turn_tex ($23CE), run once, then sink_tex ($24CD) every second frame, 23 times, as tex_death counts sink_steps down from 24. The explosion is start_explosion, then tick_explosion and draw_explosion every frame. The bullet is character $52 moved a cell a frame, as move_bullet moves it. The enemies are each scene's shapes as set_scene_graphics ($36B6) copies them in at the start of a level, laid out by arrow_cells, jet_cells, bouncy_cells and explosive_cells. The star is twinkle_star ($2C68), every fourth frame. Everything is drawn in the play field's colours for the scene, the play field being TED's multicolour text mode.

None of the enemies is animated: each is a block of characters moved across the field a column at a time, the arrow and the jet by the interrupt and the bouncing bomb and the crash explosive by the main loop (How it works). What changes is the scene. At the start of every level set_scene_graphics copies the arrow, the bouncing bomb and the crash explosive for the desert, the ice or the surface pipe into the character set, so the same characters are a different picture in each. The jet and its bomb are the same in all three.

Tex himself is 15 characters, $40 to $4E, three across and five down, drawn into the play field like the letters of the score. draw_tex puts them on the screen at his cell, row by row from tex_layout, and looks at the pipe under him on the way (check_hole). Only his bottom two rows ever change while he runs: animate_legs copies one of four pictures from leg_frames over characters $49 to $4E, in the order 1, 3, 0, 2 (leg_frame_order), every 22 frames at speed 1 and every frame at speed 7.

When he dies, tex_death saves his characters at $0400, and turn_tex turns each of them a quarter turn in place: its eight rows become its eight columns. draw_tex and erase_tex are switched to tex_lying_layout, five columns by three rows, so that he lies on his side. Then sink_tex slides the five columns down a pixel every second frame, 23 times, while the pipe slows to a stop. His characters are put back, a life goes, and he starts again at column 1 on a new stretch of pipe.

02 · The two character sets

One set draws the field and the panel, another the skyline and the pipe

Each band of the screen points TED at a character set of its own. Point at a character to see what the listing says it is.

128 characters at $3C00, eight to a row: the panel, the play field and the message row.

256 characters at $1000, sixteen to a row: the skyline and the pipeline.

Point at a character.

Both as level 1 leaves them, in the desert's colours: the first in the play field's, with Tex in his colour, the enemies in theirs and the rest in white; the second in the pipe's. The scene's enemy shapes and the twinkling star change in them as the cast above does.

The screen is five bands, each set by its own raster interrupt: the panel, the skyline, the play field, the pipe and the message row (How it works). The panel, the play field and the message row read the 128 characters at $3C00, which hold the letters, the digits, Tex and the enemies. The skyline and the pipe read the 256 at $1000, and scroll sideways at their own speeds, which is what makes the parallax.
