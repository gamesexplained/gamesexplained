1986 · Mastertronic

Prospector Pete

Everything on the screen is one of the game's 256 characters. Pete and the creatures are pictures ANDed and ORed into characters of their own every frame; the water, the digging, the fuse and the blast change by swapping or rewriting characters. Here is each of them, drawn from the listing and moved by the game's own routines.

01 · Everything that moves

Every moving shape, frame by frame

Each picture runs at the game's 50 frames a second, at the speeds of level 1, in the mine's colours.

Pete digging is the game's move_pete ($1F89), called every other frame with the joystick held right and then left, and the figures are drawn by stamp_figures, draw_figures and unstamp_figures, all run on the kit's 6502 over a mine that start_level built. The creature is drawn the same way and moved a step every four frames, as level 1 moves it by day; the page chooses its way. Every step of every move is from the frames build_figures ($2236) shifts at start-up into $0400-$06FF. The fuse and the blast change as irq_play changes them, the fuse every fourth frame and the blast every frame, with the page's random numbers in place of TED's timers. The water steps as water_fill steps a cell, once every five frames on level 1.

Pete and a creature each have two frames for each way they move, a picture character and a mask character, stored at $1600-$16BF, and Pete one more for standing still ($A0, with its mask $A1). At start-up build_figures turns them into four steps for each way, and every frame draw_figures lays the step over what is behind the figure (below).

The rest moves by changing characters. A cell Pete digs goes through three stages of earth eaten away, drawn for each way he can come at it and for each of the four characters the earth is tiled with: 48 characters. The water has four stages for each way it can come into a cell, and a seventeenth for a full cell. The fuse of a stick of dynamite is one character whose two top rows irq_play swaps every fourth frame, and the blast is one character it fills with random bits every frame.

02 · Pete and the creatures

Moving figures are characters redrawn every frame

Pete and each creature are two characters of their own, rebuilt every frame from the earth or tunnel behind them, so they glide between cells. Here one of them moves along a tunnel, a step at a time.

The game's stamp_figures, draw_figures and unstamp_figures, as irq_play calls them before and after copying the view, run on the kit's 6502 over frames that build_figures made at start-up. On the right, the figure's two characters as draw_figures left them, four times the size.

Each figure has two characters, Pete's $CE and $CF and the creatures' the pairs after them. Before the view is copied to the screen, stamp_figures puts a figure's pair into the map over the cell it is leaving and the cell it is entering, saving what was there. draw_figures then builds each of the two characters' eight rows from the saved character's row, ANDed with a mask and ORed with a picture, so the earth or tunnel shows round the figure. Once the view is copied, unstamp_figures puts the map back.

A move from one cell to the next takes four steps: one multicolour pixel (two screen pixels) a step across, or two rows a step up and down. The pictures and masks for every step are shifted once, at start-up, from the frames stored in the character set (build_figures, shift_frames): pages $0400 for up and down, $0500 for left, and $0600 for right, Pete's in the first half of each page and a creature's in the second. Pete standing still is one character, his standing picture over the cell he is in (draw_standing).

03 · The character set

One set of 256 characters draws the mine, the title and everything that moves

The screen is TED's multicolour text mode, every cell one of these characters. Point at a character to see what it is.

Point at a character.

The set at $1000-$17FF, sixteen characters to a row from $00 at the top left, in the mine's colours, as the listing has it before the title and play have rewritten any of it. The fuse and the blast move with the cast above.

A character is eight bytes, one for each of its rows, and in a multicolour cell each byte is four pixels, two bits each: the background, one of two colours shared by the band, or the cell's own (How it works). The surface's three colours step through the day, from light blue by day to black at night, while the mine's three never change. The characters for the figures do two jobs: as loaded, $C0 to $D7 hold the frames build_figures reads, and once it has read them the title uses some for its scroller and play uses $CE to $D7 for the figures as drawn.
