01 · The twelve phases and the bonus round

Every phase, drawn from its layout record

Twelve phase layouts and one bonus round, then the sequence repeats from phase 4.

Each picture is built by the page from the game's own tables, the way $D293 builds the screen: the block lists for the platforms, the clouds as 4 by 4 tiles, the propellers as 3 by 3, the stars where a cell is blank, and the palette for the phase's group of four. The birds stand where their list puts them, in the colour their type gives; the player waits at the left. Held against the emulator's own screen for all sixteen layout indexes, every cell below the status bar matches (work/test_phases_vs_emulator.js; the stars twinkle, so a star cell in any of its tiles counts). The pictures are in jsnes's palette.

The order is phases 1, 2 and 3, a bonus round, 4, 5 and 6, a bonus round, 7, 8 and 9, a bonus round, 10, 11 and 12, a bonus round. After the fourth bonus round the layout index goes back to 4 ($F358), so phase 13 is phase 4's screen with faster birds, and the game cycles through phases 4 to 12 and their bonus rounds for as long as the player lasts. Phase 9 is phase 4 with two propellers added; phase 12 is phase 6 again.

02 · Balloon Trip

The opening of Balloon Trip is the same every time

The first screens of Balloon Trip come from a 122-byte list; only after it do the balloons and sparks come at random.

The fixed opening ($C4AD) read as the trip reads it: each byte is a balloon (green) or a spark (white) at a row, entering from the left, 8 pixels of scrolling apart; a 0 ends it. The list is run twice, then the random generators take over ($C3BF). Press a balloon or a spark to see its byte.
