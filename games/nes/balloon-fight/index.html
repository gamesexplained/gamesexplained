1985 · Nintendo

Balloon Fight

How a 16 KB cartridge flies: the flap, read from the code and run on this page; the birds' three speeds; the storm's clock; and a sound driver that keeps its counters inside the Balloon Trip ranking table.

01 · The game

A balloonist against the birds

You fly by flapping. The birds fly the same way, and whoever is higher when two meet wins.

The player hangs under two balloons and flaps to rise: once for each press of A, or continuously with B held. Birds sit on the ledges pumping up a balloon of their own, take off, and chase. Meeting one from above pops its balloon and it drops on a parachute; a second hit on the way down sends it into the water, and a bubble rises from the spot for the player to pop. A bird that reaches land pumps a new balloon and comes back a faster colour. The player's two balloons go the same way: a bird from above takes one, and with none left he falls. The water has a fish in it, and a phase that goes on too long gets a storm: a cloud flashes and throws a spark that bounces around the screen.

Clearing every bird clears the phase. Every three phases there is a bonus round of balloons rising from chimneys, and a third mode, Balloon Trip, scrolls the sky past a single balloonist who collects balloons and dodges sparks for a place in a table of fifty scores.

The title screen, phase 1 as play begins, and phase 1 after half a minute with a spark loose. Screens from the emulator.

02 · The music

Music: every tune the cartridge holds, played by the game's own driver

The phases have no music. Eleven short tunes mark everything else, and one of them loops.

The game's sound driver ($F500 to $FBC9) and its eleven tunes ($FBCA to $FFF6) run on this page on the kit's 6502, called once a frame as the NMI handler calls $F758, with the same request bit the game sets to start each one; every byte it writes to the APU goes to the page's model of the chip. Held against the emulator: started from the same paused game, the page's driver and the console's made the same writes, in the same order, frame for frame, for all eleven tunes and the fourteen effects below. The roll shows the two pulse channels in blue and orange and the triangle in red; the noise channel is along the foot. The triangle's bass goes down to 55 Hz, which a laptop or phone speaker barely plays.

A tune is a header of five entries ($FBCA): a tempo and the address of a byte stream for each of the four channels. A byte from $80 to $BF sets the next note's length, its low nibble added to the tempo to index a table of frame counts ($F660); $C0 to $FE opens a repeat; $FF jumps back while repeats remain; a 0 ends the channel; anything else is a note, an even index into the period table at $F600, where note 2 holds a period of 0 and is the rest. The driver counts each channel's note down once a frame and reads the next byte when it reaches 0 ($F504). Only the bonus round's tune loops: when its streams run out the driver sets a flag ($07E8) and starts it again.

The driver runs on the title screen too, with the APU switched off. It is switched on when a game starts ($F1E9) and off again for the demonstration, so a game that starts after a demonstration plays sound and the demonstration does not.

03 · The flap

A flap every nine frames at most, and the pad read every third

The balloonist's flight is four numbers from a table and one state machine; this is the game's own arithmetic, run on the page.

Height against time over four seconds, from the start of flight at mid-screen. The dots along the foot mark the frames on which a flap was taken. The port runs $EA18, $EC49 and $EA58 as the game runs them; it was held to those routines on the kit's 6502 over 35,358 frames of random taps and holds with 1,641 flaps, and never differed in height, velocity, action or frame.

Every actor has a vertical velocity in 8.8 fixed point. Each frame $EA58 adds gravity to it, holds it between a top speed down and a top speed up, and moves the actor by it. A flap takes a fixed amount off the velocity ($EC9B). All four numbers come from tables by actor type ($E601 on), and the type is also the colour.

The pad is not read every frame. Each actor has a timer that reloads with 3 ($E6B8), and only on the frame it runs out do the animation step, the steering and the controls run; the physics runs every frame. A press of A shorter than three frames can fall between two readings. After a flap the actor is set idle with its animation frame at 1, and a flap is not accepted again until the frame has counted round to 0 and the action back to flapping, which takes three more readings: nine frames ($EC75). B held gives a flap every nine frames, so 14 flaps in two seconds; A gives one per press, and taps faster than the limit are dropped.

04 · One frame, rebuilt

The screen, drawn again from the PPU's memory

A reconstruction, not a screenshot: the nametable, the sprite table and the palette as the snapshot holds them, drawn by the page.

Phase 1 at frame 365, the snapshot the listing was built from: the 960 tiles of nametable 0, the 64 sprites of the sprite table ($0200, sent by the NMI's DMA), the 32 palette entries and the two pattern tables, drawn by the site's NES renderer. Compared pixel by pixel with the emulator's own picture of the same frame, 0 of 61,440 pixels differ. The boxes are the 16 by 24 pixel rectangles the collision code uses ($EE25): the player's and each bird's.

Everything the game draws goes through one queue. A routine that wants tiles on the screen appends a block to $0300: the PPU address, a count and the tiles ($C131); the NMI handler sends every queued block in the next vertical blank ($C188), then resets the scroll to 0, 0. The status bar is written the same way on request ($D798), one star twinkles each frame ($D60D), and the sprites are a page of RAM the handler copies with the sprite DMA. Balloon Trip's scrolling is the one exception: after the handler's work it waits for the end of vertical blank, spins for about 35 scanlines and sets the scroll for the rest of the picture ($C0D1), so the status bar stays while the sky drifts. There is no sprite-zero hit and no timer; the split is a delay loop.

05 · The birds

Pink, green and white: the same bird with faster numbers

A bird's colour is its type, and its type indexes every table that makes it move and think.

A bird decides what to do when its timer runs out ($E712), and reloads the timer with a random number of frames whose range depends on its colour: 32 to 63 for pink, 16 to 31 for green, 8 to 15 for white ($E762). Each decision looks at one player, alternating between the two: if that player is alive and not freshly arrived, the bird flaps when the player is higher than it and steers toward the player's X; with no player to chase it only flaps ($E731). Below Y $A0, near the water, it always flaps. The colours' physics are in the table above: a white bird falls faster, flaps harder and flies faster than a pink one.

A bird that loses its balloon drops on a parachute, swaying on a spring ($E7B1): the sway's displacement gains a sixteenth of the sideways speed each frame and the speed loses a sixteenth of the displacement, which never decays. On the ground it pumps up a new balloon in steps whose length comes from a table by phase ($F3BA), and takes off a colour faster if it had been hit before ($ECAE); white stays white. Hits count as the manual says: a bird's balloon is worth 500, 750 or 1,000 by colour, its parachute torn in the air 1,000, 1,500 or 2,000, and a kick while it is on the ground 750, 1,000 or 1,500 ($F06A, $F076).

Who hits whom is decided by height: when two actors' 16 by 24 boxes overlap, the one underneath loses a balloon if the other is more than 4 pixels higher ($EF72). Two birds never hurt each other, and a hit is followed by 20 frames in which the victim cannot be hit again ($EF7F).

06 · The phases

Twelve layouts, then phases 4 to 12 for ever

A phase is a record of clouds, propellers, birds and platforms; the Maps / levels tab draws every one.

Sixteen records are listed at $DB2A. Each names the block lists that paint its ledges, then its clouds as cell coordinates, its propellers with their starting angle, its list of birds with a position and a colour each, and four tables of platform edges that the collision code reads. Every fourth record is the bonus round's, which has no birds; that empty list is what makes the phase a bonus round ($D394). After the sixteenth record the index goes back to 4 ($F358), so the layouts of phases 4 to 12 and their bonus rounds repeat, while the phase number keeps counting and the difficulty tables keep climbing.

The first two phases override the steps to 3, and the sparks have a schedule of their own: the first lightning comes after 15 counts of 128 frames in phase 1, 32 seconds, falling to 5 counts by phase 25 ($C761), and each spark thrown is faster than the last ($C89F). The platforms' colours change every four phases ($D45A), and a fanfare plays on phases 1, 4, 7 and 10, the first after each bonus round.

07 · The storm and the fish

A spark every so often, from a random cloud

The storm is a counter that ticks every 128 frames, and the fish is a creature that only bites what is exactly above it.

Once every 128 frames the storm's counter goes down ($C790). At 0 a cloud is chosen at random, flashes through four attribute colours for half a second ($C831), and throws a spark: one of four directions at random, a speed from the spark table plus a random 0 to 31, and the next spark's speed level one higher. Two sparks can be in the air; while both are, the counter waits at 2. A spark bounces off the top and the sides of the screen and off every platform edge it meets ($CA67), keeping its speed, and is lost off the bottom. It hits a player within 8 pixels sideways and 12 down ($CB1C): both balloons gone at once and a stunned tumble into the water.

The fish swims right under the water a pixel a frame and wraps from X $B1 to $40 ($C64B). Every frame it looks for an actor whose X equals its own and whose Y is $B4 or lower, which is just above the surface ($C614); finding one, it rises a pixel a frame for 24 frames and bites if the victim's bottom edge is still at or below it ($C658), and the victim becomes a falling type carried in its mouth. A victim that moves off makes it sink back. It takes birds as readily as players.

08 · Balloon Trip

Balloon Trip: a fixed opening, then random stretches, and 10,000 points for twenty in a row

The third mode is the main engine with the birds taken out and a scroller put in.

The scene moves on odd frames only, one pixel at a time ($C216). Every eight pixels a generator runs to put balloons and sparks at the left edge. The first is a reader of a 122-byte list ($C4AD) that gives the same opening every game; it runs twice. Then a step counter that rises every 32 generator runs picks from a table of generators ($C3BF): five steps of random sparks that occasionally add a bubble at the water line, one step whose generator is an RTS and makes nothing, a step of sparks alone, and a step of balloons with sparks. A balloon is 300 points. Popping twenty without letting one drift off the right edge pays 10,000 points with the perfect-bonus jingle and raises the balloon's value to 500, then 700 ($C348); a balloon lost resets the run. Popping a bubble stops the scrolling for two seconds ($ECBA).

The rank in the status bar is the score's place in a table of 50 five-digit scores at $0700, filled at power-on with 25,000 down to 500 in steps of 500 ($C043). The rank is recomputed after every score change ($C539), and at the end the score is written into the table at its rank ($C579). The table shares its last entries with the sound driver, which is a discovery of its own.

09 · The bonus round

Twenty balloons from four chimneys

The bonus round reuses the balloons of Balloon Trip and pays by the round.

Every fourth layout is the bonus round. Twenty balloons rise one at a time from the four chimneys ($CEAE), 40 to 103 frames apart, swaying on the same spring as a parachute and rising at a speed set by the round ($D112). A balloon within 24 pixels vertically and 16 horizontally of a player is popped and counted for that player ($CECE). When the last has gone the results screen shows each player's count times the round's value, 300, 500 or 700 points, and counts it into the score 100 points a tick ($D213). All twenty popped between the players is PERFECT!!! and a super bonus of 10,000, 15,000, 20,000, 25,000 then 30,000 points ($D117). A player cannot die here: one who falls bounces back up at Y $C8 with both balloons restored ($EB62).

10 · The sound effects

Sound: fourteen effects, most of them one write of four bytes

The effects are request bits the game sets and the driver answers the next frame.

Each effect started by its request bit ($F0 to $F3) and played by the game's driver on the page, as the tunes above. Most are a block of four register bytes handed to one channel and left to the chip's own envelope, sweep and length counter ($F683); the lightning steps the noise period up every three frames ($F87D), the spark's bounce and the fish's rise pick random pitches ($F8A6, $F993). The flap is the first four bytes, $94 $AB $FD $58: a half-volume pulse with a fast downward sweep.

Thunder is the one effect without a sound. The cloud's flash sets bit 3 of the second request byte ($C852), and the driver's dispatch shifts that bit out without testing it ($FA59). Bit 4, which the driver does answer there, is a bird's flap.
