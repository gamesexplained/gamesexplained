Parachute: Discoveries

Things the program does that the manual and the wiki never mention.

01 · The late catch

A paratrooper can be caught after he lands

When a paratrooper reaches the bottom of his lane and the boat is somewhere else, the program does not count the miss at once. It sets one bit for his lane ($29) and starts the miss at the next step. A press that brings the boat under his lane before then finds the bit, clears it and counts a catch instead ($030F). The player has one step for it, about 0.2 seconds at the start of a game and 0.14 at the top speed. The miss itself is on How it works, section 05.

02 · Game B

Game B is no faster than Game A

The wait between steps and the number of paratroopers allowed in the air come from the score alone, by the same table in both games ($00C0). Game B differs in two other ways. Its paratroopers can be caught in the tree (section 06), and its jumps are rarer at the start: in Game A, with one or two in the air, the next always jumps, while Game B asks the chip's divider every time, five chances in sixteen ($0124).

03 · The shark

A fin circles the water every ten seconds

The shark's fin and its leap are one chain of segments with the swimmer's path ($0340). Besides a miss, the clock puts a token at the start of the chain each time the tens of seconds go up, except at the minute ($0239). So every ten seconds a fin circles the water and the shark leaps, in the clock and in a game, with nobody in the water. The patrol makes no sound and does not stop the paratroopers.

04 · The clock

The helicopter waits while the seconds end in 8 or 9

In a game, the chance of a jump is read only when the clock's seconds end in 0 to 7 ($0124). For two seconds in every ten the helicopter holds its paratroopers back, unless the sky is empty, when one always jumps.

05 · The first jump

The first paratrooper falls alone

A new game sets the number allowed in the air to 0 ($05E8). The one paratrooper who jumps because nobody is in the air is the only one until the first catch, when the score sets the limit to 1 and then higher.

06 · The tree

The tree lets go only on even tens of seconds

In Game B and in the clock's demo, a paratrooper leaving the right lane's third place goes into the tree when the tree is empty and the number in the air is even ($01C3). He swings between two poses at each move of the right lane. The program tries to drop him only when the clock's tens of seconds are even ($01BA), so for ten seconds in every twenty he cannot fall; only when the right lane below him is empty; and only when he is in the pose the helicopter's rotor picks ($01E1). The tree is drawn on How it works, section 07.

07 · The test pads

A pad held at power on turns the misses off

MAME's notes give the board an unpopulated "Infinite Lives" pad on the chip's R3 row. Any key of that row held while the unit powers on sets a flag that stops misses being counted ($03D3, $0370), and the chip waits for it to be let go before it starts. The row's fourth key, which MAME marks "alarm test?", rings the alarm at once and flips the same flag each time it is read ($05A7).

08 · Buttons

Three keys at once open the alarm's setting

The program works out the mode from the sum of the keys held ($0553): the keys are worth 1 (Time), 2 (Game B), 4 (Game A) and 8 (Alarm); Time alone is the clock, a sum of 2 or 3 is Game B, 4 to 6 is Game A, and 7 or more the alarm's setting. So Time with Game B gives Game B, Game A with either gives Game A, and Time, Game A and Game B together give the alarm's setting.

09 · The demo

In the clock all three lanes move each second

The clock's demo runs once a second and moves the three lanes in one go, middle, right and left ($010D), where a game moves one lane a step. It never runs the step's own routine, so the helicopter's rotor stands still in the clock.
