1981 · Nintendo

Parachute: Graphics

Every shape is fixed on the glass. A paratrooper falls by lighting the next shape down his lane, and the boat, the fin and the misses move the same way.

01 · Everything that moves

Every position of every moving object

Each figure lights its object's shapes in the order the game does, one change a move.

At the start of a game a step takes about 0.2 s and each lane moves once every three steps, so a paratrooper drops a place about every 0.6 s; these figures move at that pace. The boat moves as fast as the presses come, one place a press.

Each place in a lane is a whole figure: a paratrooper falling with his parachute closed near the helicopter, then open lower down, and the last, just above the boat, drawn as he lands. The swimmer and the fin take turns along the bottom of the screen, and the shark's leap is one large shape.

02 · The bits behind the shapes

Where each object lives in memory

Each shape is one bit of one cell. Hover over or tap a row to light its shapes.

A cell's four bits drive the four shapes of one LCD latch; rows 0 and 1 of memory are the two halves of the screen ($0640). The lanes and the water's chain cross from cell to cell, so the code that moves them names each bit it passes.

03 · Fixed shapes

The bell is a spare digit segment

The chip's digit table gives each of the three digits one spare segment, and the game uses all three ($0647): the first is the clock's colon, the second the bell swung up, which also shows that the alarm is on, and the third a place in the left lane.
