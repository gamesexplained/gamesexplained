1981 · Nintendo

Popeye: Graphics

Every shape is fixed on the glass. An object moves by lighting the next shape along its path, at the pace of the game's steps.

01 · Everything that moves

Each object, through every place it can be

Each figure lights its object's shapes in the order the game does, at the game's pace at the start of Game A.

At the start of Game A a step takes about 0.19 s, and a falling item moves one place every third step, so about 0.57 s a place. Bluto's weapon comes down in two moves, three steps apart, and goes back up on the step after. Popeye moves only when the player presses, so his figure steps at a fixed half second. The outlines are MAME's, traced from the unit's LCD; the bits that light them are the game's ($0640).

The paths have different lengths: five places for the bottles, six for the pineapples and seven for the cans, the last of each over Popeye's head on that side, where a caught item sits for a step.

02 · The bits behind the shapes

Where each object lives in memory

Each shape is one bit of one cell. Hover over a row to light its shapes.

A cell's four bits drive the four shapes of one LCD latch; rows 0 and 1 of memory are the two halves of the screen. The paths cross from cell to cell, so the code that moves them names each cell in turn ($032F, $00B8, $01C0).

03 · Shapes that stay put

Olive Oyl's arms, and the clock

Olive never moves, but her arm points at the path the last item went down.

Her body is always lit in a game. Each throw turns her arm toward its path; on the pineapples' step her arm turns toward them whether she throws or not ($01C0). The two other arm poses and the bell belong to the clock and the alarm.
