One ant runs nearly twice as fast as the rest, the grenade has more fuses than the instructions say, and an aeroplane hides among the pictures.

Secret: one ant moves almost twice as fast as the others

Five ants hunt you through the city. Four of them stop on every other turn, which gives you time to get away. The first ant hardly ever stops. It moves on 19 turns out of 20, so on your first rescues it covers nearly twice the ground of the others.

Each ant moves one square on the turns the game lets it move. On its other turns it stands still. The race starts again when ant 1 reaches the red line.

Later rescues make the other four faster. After four rescues they move on two turns out of three, and after eight rescues on three turns out of four. Ant 1 stays ahead the whole game.

Each ant has a small counter that counts down once a turn. When it reaches zero, the ant misses that turn and the counter starts again. The BASIC program gives ant 1 a counter of 20 and the others 2. Before each rescue it sets the other four again, to 2, 3 or 4 as the game goes on, but it leaves ant 1 out. Whether the fast ant was meant is not known.

The counters are set by BASIC lines 140 to 190. The test that skips a turn is at $8A5D. How the ants hunt shows the ants on a piece of the city.

Secret: hold several grenade keys at once for more fuse lengths

The instructions give four keys for throwing a grenade: S, D, F and G. Each one sets a different fuse, so the grenade flies a different distance before it goes off. You can also hold more than one. The game adds the keys together, which gives 15 different fuses.

Each square is one turn of the game. The grenade leaves your hand on turn 1 and goes off on the red square. Holding several keys still uses only one grenade.

Each key stands for a number: S is 2, D is 4, F is 8 and G is 16. The game reads all four keys at once and adds up the ones held down. That sum is the fuse. So S alone gives the shortest fuse, 2 turns, and all four keys give the longest, 30.

A long fuse is not always a long throw. A grenade stops when it hits a wall, and it waits there until the fuse runs out. You cannot throw another one until it has gone off.

The grenade keys are read at $8D00.

Cut: an aeroplane the game never shows

The pictures of the ants, the boy, the girl and the grenade are stored together. Among them are four more that the game never picks, so they never appear in play. They show an aeroplane.

The four are the same plane facing four ways. They are stored like the four views of every person and ant, one for each way a figure can face on the screen, and each one is the exact mirror image of another.

The plane, drawn over the striped wall pattern the way the game draws the others. Graphics shows every picture that does appear.

There are no planes in Ant Attack, and the game never says what this one was for. Unseen64 reported the plane in 2009, from a tip by Robert Seddon, and gave no reason for it either.

The pictures are stored at $BC00, between the boy and the puff of a blast.
