1983 · Quicksilva · ZX Spectrum

Ant Attack

The moving figures, their masks, and the pictures retained on the tape.

01 · Ant Attack

The figures fit inside sixteen pixels

Each picture has a background mask and ink for a sixteen-by-sixteen square. Two sets of human poses supply the boy and girl. The ants have two walking poses for each direction. Recovery and grenade bursts share four direct shape numbers.

The viewer includes all sixty stored masked shapes. Walking alternates at 6.7 engine updates per second, the measured opening-view rate. The game can run at another rate when its drawing or sound takes more time. Pausing allows a pose to be inspected.

The AMMO cube is the grenade's inactive base picture. During a throw, the game selects the small dot and burst shapes instead. The four retained F0–F3 pictures are shown under their numbers because their intended gameplay role is unresolved. Shape decoding is at $80A0; animation selection is at $8980 and $8600.

02 · Ant Attack

Masks let the scenery remain visible

A set mask bit keeps the old background pixel. An ink bit then sets the new pixel. This two-step operation preserves the empty space around a person while drawing the person's body.

Blocks and people share the same projected depth order. The renderer sorts the eight objects and draws each when it reaches the matching point among the city blocks. A wall drawn later can cover the figure. Sources: $8500, $8580, and $85D0.

The city itself is a bitmap. Its colour attributes cover eight-by-eight squares, so the engine largely uses a single grey view while reserving the coloured panel for counters and messages. The rebuilt play screen shows that arrangement.
