1983 · Quicksilva · ZX Spectrum

Ant Attack

A city of six-bit columns, five ants, and a rescue that can take you far into the desert.

01 · Ant Attack

Rescue someone from Antescher

Ant Attack puts a boy or a girl outside a walled city inhabited by giant ants. The other person waits somewhere among its roofs and stairways. Getting close at the same height makes the captive follow. Both people must reach the desert before time runs out.

A failed attempt restores health and ammunition for another try. A successful rescue earns a time bonus and moves the next captive to a harder location. Ten rescues complete the mission.

02 · Ant Attack

A longer throw means a longer fuse

The four grenade keys set how many engine updates the grenade travels. S sets two, D four, F eight, and G sixteen. The grenade moves on the launch update and stops when its countdown expires. A wall can interrupt that path.

A throw on clear ground, shown one engine update at a time. The demonstration holds height constant and leaves out walls and humans.

Ants at the grenade's height enter recovery when their distance is below five cells. Ants five to seven cells away are stunned. Distance adds the horizontal and vertical gaps, so a diagonal gap counts twice. Human hits use a separate exact-position test. The grenade code starts at $8D00; ant damage starts at $87A0.

03 · Ant Attack

Music: the logo has its own rhythm

The title flashes through colours while BASIC plays two adjacent pitches, then follows them with a rising run of notes. The timeout screen descends instead. The completion message mixes printed words with a longer sequence of beeper commands.

Choose a sequence to hear it.

The game's beeper level changes, recorded from the running title, timeout, and completion sequences, play through the Spectrum sound model. The recording uses 15,600 samples per second.

The title and timeout BEEPs live in BASIC at $77DA and $7A8C. The final event begins at $920F. The game uses the 48K speaker rather than an AY music chip.

03 · Events

Sound: messages choose their own notes

A fall, a bite, a successful shot, and a rescued captive have separate event streams. Each stream interleaves text with beeper commands. The rescue congratulations include a rising phrase, while a bite has a shorter warning.

The game's event sounds, played through the Spectrum speaker model. The pauses for printing are omitted. Noise starts from the new-game random state, so a noise burst can differ during play. Counter-only events have no sound.

The event interpreter is at $8E0D. Its tone and noise routines are at $8B80 and $8B4A. The congratulations stream begins at $9160.

04 · Ant Attack

The scanner measures progress toward the captive

Before rescue, SCAN compares the distance to the captive with the previous reading. Green means the distance fell; red means it did not. The scanner can guide someone behind a building, because it uses world coordinates rather than the picture.

The captive starts following within three cells when both people have the same height. A follower normally stops one cell away. A gap of six cells or more also stops the following movement, so a rescuer can leave the captive behind. These rules are at $8F80 and $8AB6.

The Map tab shows every rescue location. Later rescues choose among four positions using the previous rescue's remaining time modulo four. The time bonus is remaining time multiplied by the number rescued, including the person just saved. With 600 time units left on rescue three, the bonus is 1,800.

Ants step toward the player and compare their distance before and after a move. A move that fails to improve the distance makes the ant turn using the game's random state. The schedule becomes faster for four of the five ants. Sources: $8A00, $8A5D, and BASIC $5E49.

05 · Ant Attack

The city is built from columns

The opening play screen, drawn from its bitmap and colour attributes. The black outer border is omitted.

The city stores one byte for each column of ground. Six bits describe six possible block heights. The renderer samples a diagonal window through those columns, then draws blocks and characters in depth order. Changing the camera rotates that sampling window.

Every moving object uses a masked picture. The mask preserves the background around the figure, while the ink supplies its pixels. That lets a person disappear behind a wall without erasing the wall. The Graphics tab shows the masks and every stored shape. Sources: $84A0, $80A0, and $C000.

06 · Ant Attack

The keys keep movement separate from the camera

KeysAction
V / CWalk forward / jump; together they climb.
M / Symbol ShiftTurn in opposite directions.
0 / P / Enter / SpaceChoose one of four camera views. 0 is the digit zero.
S / D / F / GThrow farther by allowing more travel updates.
1Return to BASIC's retry path.

The engine reads the keyboard directly. Its city loop disables interrupts and redraws before the next update, so the time counter follows engine work rather than seconds. Key 1 returns from that loop without clearing the rescue or total-score variables. Sources: $8000, $8060, and $8400.

The unused-looking AMMO picture has a visible place in the desert. The Discoveries tab includes a route to it.
