ZX Spectrum · 1986 · Durell Software

Fat Worm Blows a Sparky

Everything that moves, drawn by the game's own drawing routines a pass of its loop at a time, about seven passes a second. Nothing here is a sprite the hardware moves: each pass the game clears its picture and draws every creature into it again, the worm and the Crawlies worked out afresh from where they stand and which way they face.

01 · The worm

The worm: four sections, one drawn thinner each pass

The worm is four records in the list of things to draw, one for each section. Each section is a four-sided shape between two pairs of edge points, outlined and filled with a dotted pattern.

Speed

Each card is the game's own loop from the start of a game, with only the worm's records drawn, by draw_object ($81DB). An address such as $81DB is a numbered place in the Spectrum's memory, written in hexadecimal (base 16). The head is the point in the middle of the view, and the worm moves left.

Each pass one section is drawn narrower than its neighbours. The loop sets a byte at $8067 to the frame counter's two lowest bits plus one, and the section it names takes its width from further along a table of 31 widths ($E48C). So the thin place moves along the worm and comes back every four passes, and the worm ripples as it goes.

The view follows the head and never turns. Steering turns the worm, so a turning worm swings round its own head on the screen: with right held, the heading goes down by 12 of the 256 steps of a circle each pass, a whole circle in about 21 passes. A section that carries a bug is filled with another pattern and gets a speckled disc. A bug moves one section toward the tail each pass, as long as the section behind it has none.

02 · The Crawlies

Crawlies: two discs and six legs, drawn from the heading

A Crawly is not a stored picture. Each pass the game draws two discs for its body and six legs at angles worked out from the way it faces.

Speed

Each card is a Crawly put into the game's list of objects and moved by the game's own walk_objects ($D691), a pass at a time, and drawn by draw_crawly ($840A). It is held in the middle of the card; in the game it walks across the board.

Three bytes of a Crawly's record hold its legs, two legs to a byte and four bits to a leg. Each pass it walks, the game adds $20 to each byte and then takes 2 away twice ($D97D, $D996), so each leg creeps round a few degrees a pass and springs back, out of step with the others. A turning Crawly gets only one of the two steps, which one depending on the way it turns. Each leg is a strip drawn out to a point at its angle ($B491), and the body is two discs, the smaller one, the head, set off toward the way it faces ($B36A).

Late in a game Crawlies come bigger. From pass 4,352 a Crawly that a pad lets out on every eighth pass is 14 units wide instead of 10, and the first sparky to hit it shrinks it to 10. The small Crawly an eruption point lets out is three stored pictures: a body that swaps between two pictures each pass, with a head and a tail set on either side of it by its heading ($B4E0). A sparky that hits a Crawly stops it, and it is drawn turned round until, two of its updates after the hit, it becomes an explosion: the first picture for three passes, the second and the third for one pass each, and then nothing.

03 · The Sputnik

The Sputnik comes down onto its own shadow

A Sputnik is a spiked ball high above the board, drawn with its shadow on the ground beneath it. The ball is one of four stored pictures, chosen by how wide it is on the screen.

Speed

A Sputnik moved by the game's walk_objects and drawn by draw_sputnik ($8667), seen from over the worm, which is in the middle of the card. It starts 12 units off the worm on each side.

A Sputnik is made at height 207 in one of the squares round the worm and homes in on it. Once it is within 20 units of the worm on both sides it drops 5 units a pass, still homing, so it swings past the worm and back as it falls. The view looks down from 384 units above the board, so the high ball is drawn further from the middle than its shadow, and the two meet as it lands. On the board it becomes a Crawly.

Under 13 pixels wide the ball is the 12-row picture, under 16 the 15-row one, under 20 the 18-row one, and wider than that the 22-row one ($86D9). The routine keeps the smallest width it has drawn in the Sputnik's record and never draws it wider again.

04 · Sparkies and eruption points

A sparky has a picture for every size

A sparky is a stored picture chosen by its width on the screen: twenty pictures, from 1 row to 20.

Speed

The sizes are drawn by plot_spark ($B797), one for each width. The eruption point is put into the game's list of objects and moved by walk_objects, and drawn by $863F.

To draw a sparky the game averages its outline at its two heights and gives the width to plot_spark, which takes that size's picture from a table at $BF27. A sparky wider than 20 pixels gets the 20-row picture, moved to stay centred. Its picture changes only when its size does.

An eruption point can be put down at the worm when the pass before listed nothing from the board. It counts down from 20, a pass at a time. While the count is 9 or more it is not drawn; from 8 to 1 it is its first picture; at 0 it lets out a small Crawly and is its second picture from then on.

05 · On the board

Spindles, pads and de-buggers move without going anywhere

Three kinds of thing on the board change their picture with the frame counter, the number the loop adds 1 to every pass.

Speed

Each is drawn by draw_object in the middle of the view, where only its top shows, with the frame counter going up by 1 a pass.

A spindle is one of four stored pictures at $CB4B, 12 rows each, and turns a step every pass ($860A). The picture is the frame counter's two lowest bits, or the same bits of the counter's negative when the spindle's top height is an even number, so 27 of the board's 58 spindles turn one way and 31 the other.

The four triangles on an eruption pad, where Crawlies come out, grow and shrink over 32 passes ($8786). A de-bugger's stripes slide across its top, swapping colours as they go, and the picture repeats every 16 passes ($8833).

06 · The ending

The hand that takes the disk

The disk on the board turns its hub every pass. At the end a hand of 15 four-sided shapes comes down from the top of the view, closes on the disk and lifts it away.

Speed

Drawn by draw_floppy_disk ($BB06) a pass at a time, the hand's counter stepped as the ending loop ($78EF) steps it. Before it ends, the game slides the view until the worm is at the top edge of the disk's square, which puts the disk just below the middle of the view.

The hub swaps between two pictures on the frame counter's lowest bit. In the ending a counter at $BAAD goes up by 1 a pass, and the hand is drawn that many rows down from the top, from a list of corners at $BAAE. When the hand passes the top of the disk the drawing routine sets the counter's top bit, and from then on it goes down by 3 a pass, with the disk drawn rising in the hand, until it is under $80 and the menu comes back. The How it works tab tells why the ending does not need a single spindle.

07 · The loading picture

A picture that loads in the dark

The worm, coiled on a microchip under a magnifying glass, is the first 6,912 bytes of the tape's one long block. The program before it makes ink and paper both black, so for 15 seconds the bitmap comes in unseen. Then the colours sweep down the screen a row of cells at a time, in under two seconds.

The tape's own bytes, at its own pace from the picture's first byte, 21 seconds into the tape. The red and black stripes are the loader's: it turns the border over at every edge of the tape's signal, so each stripe is one pulse, a thin one a 0 and a thicker one a 1.

The picture takes 16.8 seconds, about 410 bytes a second, a little over twice as fast as the Spectrum's own loader would bring it in. It stays up for nearly two more minutes while the rest of the block, the game, loads behind it, and the game clears it when it starts.
