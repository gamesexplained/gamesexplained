Eighty-one places and thirty-six characters with minds of their own, in a game written mostly in BASIC. Every picture is drawn from its place's number, every fight is settled before the first blow, and food makes a better weapon than an axe.

01 · The game

The game

You are a mortal in the world of the Norse myths, and you play it by typing English.

The loading screen, from the tape in the emulator, and Valheim, where the game starts, from Spectrum Computing. The names under the figures are the characters standing there.

The top of the screen shows where you are: a picture of the place, with you and everyone else there drawn as figures in a row along the ground. Below it, you type what to do: go north, get the axe, Thor, give me the food, attack Loki with the sword. The game answers in a few lines of text.

Thirty-six characters share the world with you, most of them from the myths, and two animals, Snake and Raven. Each has its own goodness, charisma, strength and brains. Those where you are walk off, eat and drink, pick things up, give them away and fight one another without being told, and they can be asked to do things for you. Six quest objects lie locked in chests and cupboards around the 81 places of Midgard, Asgard and Hell: Ofnir, Drapnir, Skornir, Skalir, Felstrong and Grimnir. Grimnir opens the way to the hall of Valhalla, and when you reach it the characters come one by one to congratulate you.

Most of the game is a BASIC program of 20,623 bytes. Machine code at the top of memory, 12,185 bytes, draws the pictures and the figures, reads the keyboard and checks the spelling of every word typed.

02 · The map

The world, place by place

Hell fills the five southern rows of a nine by nine grid. North of it, the five western columns are Midgard and the four eastern ones Asgard. Each place is one row of eighteen bytes.

The map and the panel read the map table, $BF37, and the special places, $C644, from the listing as the tape loads them. Colours are the terrain; a thick border is a special place; a solid line is an exit that is always open and a dashed one an exit of a special place, which needs something carried or someone present. The number in a corner is where the place's ringway leads. The pictures are the game's own drawing: the commands each place sends run through the graphics code at $F7AF in a Z80 simulator, and 13 of them, read from the emulator's screen, were the same to the byte.

A row of the map holds the terrain, the exits as eight bits from north round to north-west, the place a ringway leads to, the crowns lying there, six pairs of an object and its column on the screen, and a scene feature for the picture. An object in a chest or cupboard has the column 255. A place holds six objects at most, which comes back in the bugs below.

Thirty-six places are special, with 128 plus a number in place of the terrain. The number picks a row of nine bytes in s$: the real terrain, the place's name, the object or character it needs and the one it forbids, the extra exits those two govern, and up to four more scene features. The description the game prints is put together from these (line 3902): a name only for a special place, "hall" after a name ending in an apostrophe, the terrain, and the world, which is Hell for places 1 to 45, Asgard for the four eastern columns above that, and Midgard for the rest.

03 · The pictures

Every picture comes from its place's number

No picture is stored. The game draws each one as you arrive, from the terrain here, the terrain one step north and random numbers seeded with the place's number, so a place looks the same every time.

    The commands

    The graphics commands lines 7010 to 7039 send for the place chosen above, in order, worked out by a port of those lines with the ROM's random number generator. test_pictures.py draws all 81 pictures from these commands with the game's code in SkoolKit's simulator and holds the sheet the map shows against them.

    The BASIC builds each command as a string and hands it to one machine-code entry, $F7AF, with LET x$=STR$ USR gi. Command 1 draws the place: the terrain picks one of eight drawers from the table at $E352 (icy waste, marsh, plain, lakes, forest, mountains, cave and hall), and the terrain one step north picks the view on the horizon from $E362. Before drawing, $DE35 seeds the machine code's own 32-bit shift register at $DAB0 from the place's number, so the ridges, dots and towers it scatters fall the same way on every visit. Valheim left and entered again gave a picture the same to the byte.

    The windows are chosen in BASIC. Line 7010 runs RANDOMIZE h*100*5, which sets the ROM's generator to the place's number times 500, and the following lines draw RND to choose which windows each castle gets and where a hall's tall windows go. Line 7010 also sends command 19 first for every place in Hell and for places 56, 57, 65, 66 and 81, which turns their buildings red on black.

    04 · Fights

    A fight is settled before the first blow

    Each side's score is its strength now, plus 40 for a weapon and 40 more for a weapon numbered above 66. The difference between the scores decides the fight. The two or three blows on the screen come after.

    Attacker

    Defender

    A port of lines 1362 to 1376. Choosing a character fills in its full strength and what it carries on the tape; change any of it. The five buttons are fights tried in the emulator, with Idun's strength poked and two lines of the program changed so that nobody walked off before the blow: each ended as the port says.

    The attacker's weapon is the object named after "with", so attack Thor is bare hands even with a sword in your pack. The defender always uses whatever is in its first slot. The side behind then adds 32 for a shield and 64 for a helmet, but only if it has something in its first slot (line 1365).

    Scores within 20 of each other are a draw, and both lose 32 strength. A win by less than 64 sends the loser off to a random place with 64 less, and costs the winner 32. A win by 64 or more kills the loser, and so does any loss of 20 or more by you: you are never merely defeated (line 1374). Nothing random enters the result.

    An attack you order also moves the attacker's goodness one time in two: up a step for hitting a bad character, down for hitting a good one (line 1356). From a goodness of 0 the step down is the character code of a negative number, and the program stops with "B Integer out of range, 1356" (seen in play, with your goodness poked to 0).

    05 · Weapons

    Secret: food is a better weapon than an axe

    The test for the better weapon is only the object's number. The axe is 63 and food is 96.

    You, at 175 strength, against Idun at 215 in the emulator. With the axe, 215 against 215 is a draw; with the food, 255 against 215 is a win by 40. The table is every object you can carry, by its number in the dictionary.

    The weapon's score in line 1362 is (w>o)*40+(w>66)*40, where o is the variable that holds 0. Every object counts for 40, and every one numbered above 66 for 80, which puts the sword and Skalir with the rock, the ring, the wine and the food. The axe, Felstrong, the shield and lightning only reach 40. Thrown, the axe and Felstrong do 20 damage and food does 2 (line 1471), so the numbers only favour food in a fight hand to hand.

    06 · Characters

    A character you have just left can follow you

    Up to ten characters get a turn on each pass of the main loop. A character still standing where you were can come after you, if it is restless enough and feels strongly about you either way.

    You start at 15. An attack you order can move it.

    From the characters' table at $BC23 and the extra bytes at $BAEF, as the tape loads them, and the figures' shapes from $E8AA. Qualities are the game's nibbles, 0 to 15; the inlay prints them divided by three and rounded up. Highlighted rows can follow you at the goodness chosen, and Follows is how often one does when a turn picks it. Click a heading to sort.

    A turn (line 4010) first picks one of the 38 others at random. If it is not here, it arrives when it is at the place you just left, its restlessness beats RND*8, and the gap between its goodness and yours is under 5 (it likes you) or over 11 (it loathes you). It also arrives if this place is its home, the third byte of its row in p$, wherever it is. The turns stop as soon as you press ENTER, so a player who types quickly gives followers fewer chances. That is the manual's "the faster you move, the harder it is for others to keep up", and it is how it works.

    A character that is here instead does one of six things at random: leave, take or eat or drink something, drop something, attack the one here it likes less, give something to the one it likes more, or step aside. Restlessness, the last nibble, is not on the inlay. Bravery is on the inlay and in the table, and no line of the program reads it (FN b is defined in line 50 and never used).

    Strength falls only when someone acts: every command costs the one doing it 5, and waiting costs nothing. Food gives back 64 and wine 32, up to full strength, which is 16 times the strength nibble plus 15. You start at 175.

    07 · Typing

    The spelling checker forgives one wrong letter

    Each word is looked up among the 255 rows of the dictionary as you press ENTER. One letter wrong, missing or extra still finds it, and so does an "s" on the end.

    Each word goes through VH.findWord, a port of $D937 and $DA11, on the dictionary at $AFE0. test_parser.py runs it and the game's own code in SkoolKit's Z80 simulator on 746 words: every word in the dictionary that can be typed, each with an "s", three misspellings of each and 100 random strings. They agree on every one.

    The editor collects letters into a word, lower-cased, and digits into a number; any other character ends the word ($D7CD). The matcher walks the dictionary from row 1. A row that matches with no allowance used wins at once; otherwise it keeps the row that matched the most letters, and at the end takes it if the letters matched are within two of the word's length. The first word it cannot place is turned red and the line stays for editing ($D8C8), so BASIC never sees a word it does not know.

    The last byte of each row is its class: 1 a name or object, 2 a verb, 3 a preposition, 5 a word passed over ("the", "please" and the place names), 6 a swear word and 7 a game command. A swear word is not an error to the editor. The parser sends it to line 120, where Mary appears and slaps you: "Mary is not amused...".

    08 · Quests

    Secret: Felstrong can be the first quest

    The manual puts Felstrong fifth. On the map's own tables, nothing stands between the start and its cave but a ring.

    The route walked in the emulator from the start, with a ring poked into your pack: two steps west, a ringway, one step west, a ringway, north-east, north into Rankle's hall and its ringway to the cave. The game answered "You are not strong enough to open the cupboard."

    The table is a search of the map and the special places, which takes every condition but a quest object as met: the right ordinary object or character at hand, a ring for every ringway and someone strong enough for every lock. Each quest needs only the ones listed beside it. A chest or cupboard opens with the key and (35-sp)*6 strength, sp being the special place's number (line 1955). That is 174 for Ofnir, which you can manage at your full 175, and 180 to 204 for the other five, so for those you must tell someone stronger, with a key of its own, to open it.

    09 · Money

    Bug: picking up crowns pays one crown

    The routine that adds money, line 3410, adds the number of the one receiving it instead of the amount.

    Line 3410 is LET x=FN m(n)+n, with n the character and m the amount. In the emulator, picking up a pile of 50 printed "You get 50 crowns." and then "You have 151 crowns."

    The same routine pays the seller in a trade, so a character paid 100 crowns for its sword gets its own number. Thor is 4. The rest of the money leaves the game.

    10 · Help

    Bug: help never says what is forbidden

    At a special place, help is meant to say what the extra exits need and what they forbid. What they forbid never reaches the screen.

    Every special place from s$. An extra exit is open when what it needs is carried or present and what it forbids is neither (line 3860).

    Line 6555 skips the extra exits altogether where a place both needs and forbids something. Where it only forbids, line 6563 starts the sentence again with "You can go ", over the forbidding text. Both were seen in play with the two bytes set by hand. Sixteen places forbid something, and help names none of them.

    11 · Chests

    Bug: a chest at a full place stops the game

    Putting an ordinary object into a chest or cupboard where all six places are taken stops the program with "3 Subscript wrong, 5310:5".

    An object put down where there is no room brings Klepto, who takes it to a neighbouring place: "Klepto steals the rock." Line 5309 works out where Klepto should stand from the column of the object, and an object in a container has the column 255, so the subscript of the screen's column array is out of range. Both were seen in play.

    12 · Saving

    Bug: BREAK during a save resets the Spectrum

    save and load mean to catch BREAK on the tape and carry on. Pressing it resets the machine instead.

    Both routines, $5CF0 and $5D0B, change only the low byte of the address BASIC jumps to on an error, to $25, meaning $5D25. In play that address is the game's own handler, $DA3C, so the jump goes to $DA25, in the middle of the spelling checker, and the Spectrum resets. Seen in play, for both.

    13 · Sound

    Sound: the beeper

    There is no music. The machine code clicks each key, and the BASIC has five sounds made with BEEP.

    Square waves at each sound's pitch and length: BEEP's pitch p is 261.63 × 2p/12 Hz, and the key sounds call the ROM's BEEPER at $03B5 with the counts in $D5B7 and $D5AE. The chest and the death are 110 and 131 Hz, which small speakers barely play.
