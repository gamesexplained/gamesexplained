With Fast CPU off, the game plays as it does on a 48K Spectrum, at the 48K's own speed. These are the places where the page cannot be the machine, and what it does instead.

On a SpectrumOn this page
The interrupt can fall between any two instructions of the main program.The interrupt runs between two of the port's routines, at the clock's time for it. The bytes the interrupt changes and the main program reads in the middle of a routine (the wait counter, the clock's frame count, the minutes, the energy and the shield, and the sensors' beams) are read after the interrupts due by then have run.
Each instruction takes its T-states, and a pass takes as long as its drawing does.The port executes no instructions. Each stretch of the main loop, each routine and each row and byte it fills costs what it cost the game, measured or fitted on the game's own timings (01, The pace), so a pass takes about as long as on the Spectrum: within 5 % but for a few scenes, the slowest of them in Mitral, 9 % slower.
A sound effect plays with the interrupts off, and the frames it lasts are lost to the game's clock.The page's effects take the game's time with the interrupts off too, and lose the same interrupts, as does a sensor's hit, whose sound plays inside the interrupt.
The beeper is toggled from the effects' timing loops.The site's model of the beeper plays the port's writes to it, each at the T-state the game's loops make it, as the frame is shown.
The ULA draws the screen a line at a time as the beam crosses it.The page takes the screen whole at the moment the beam would reach its first line, so a change made while the beam is drawing shows a frame later, all at once.
The information screen's S and L save and load a position on tape.S gives a .tap file to download, with the game's two blocks; L asks for a .tap file and gives the game its blocks in turn. Cancel is BREAK.
The joystick is a Kempston interface, a Sinclair stick or the cursor keys, as the control options choose.The arrow keys, and the pad under the screen, are whichever joystick the control options chose, with Left Ctrl for fire; every other key is the Spectrum's own.
