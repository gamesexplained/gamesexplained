The whole game, playable in the browser: the security question, the control options, the moon's eighteen sectors in solid 3D, the probe and the jet, the rigs, the sensors, the clock and the information screen, with saving and loading to a tape file. Every routine is ported from the game's own Z80 code and was run beside it until the two agreed.

  • Tap a key in this list to press it in the game.
  • Space swaps moving for aiming the crosshair
  • D drills: a rig is put down in front of the probe; C collects it
  • R F rise and fall, P L look up and down
  • I the information screen: S saves, L loads (a .tap file), 1 abandons the game, any other key goes back
  • The security question: the word from the Federation Briefing, shown under the screen with a button that types it, then Enter
  • The control options: 1 keyboard, 2 Sinclair, 3 Kempston, 4 cursor; Enter begins
  • A Z the angle of a turn, S X the size of a step
  • B shows or hides the crosshair, N M tilt
  • O K forward and back, Q W turn left and right, or the arrow keys
  • 0 or Ctrl fires the laser
  • U turns round

The game's own screen, drawn from the port's memory each frame as the Spectrum would show it, at the Spectrum's own speed: the game draws each view into a buffer of its own and then copies it to the screen. Fast CPU plays the game as a Spectrum with a much faster processor would, with a new frame 60 times a second, and Speed sets how fast you move: 1× as fast as a Spectrum at its quickest, and 2× and 4× faster still (section 02). The sound is the port's writes to the beeper, each edge at the T-state the game's loops make it, played by the site's model of it as each frame is shown.
