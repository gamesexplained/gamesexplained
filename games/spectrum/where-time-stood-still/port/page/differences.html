The game plays as it does on a Spectrum 128, at the 128's pace. These are the places where the page cannot be the machine, and what it does instead.

On a Spectrum 128On this page
The interrupt can fall between any two instructions of the main program.The interrupt runs between two of the port's routines, at the clock's time for it. The bytes the interrupt changes and the main program reads in the middle of a routine (the clock's count to midnight, the light and its count) are read after the interrupts due by then have run.
Each instruction takes its T-states, and a pass takes as long as its drawing does.The port executes no instructions. Each stretch of the main loop, each routine and each byte, row and character it draws costs what it cost the game, fitted on the game's own timings (01, The pace), so a pass takes about as long as on the 128.
Copying the view to the screen takes longer than a frame, with the interrupts off, so in play the 128 misses about one interrupt in five, and the game's clock, which counts them, loses a fifth of its time.The page's copy takes the game's time with its interrupts off too, and loses the same interrupts.
The AY chip plays the music and the effects, and the beeper clicks with the drum.The site's models of the AY and the beeper play the port's writes to them, each at the time in the frame it was made, as the frame is shown.
The ULA draws the screen a line at a time as the beam crosses it.The page takes the screen whole at the moment the beam would reach its first line, so a change made while the beam is drawing shows a frame later, all at once.
The 128's ROM holds the editor and BASIC. The game calls none of it, but reads 171 of its bytes as the bottom of area 14's map.The page carries those 171 bytes of the original 128's ROM and nothing else of it. A +2's ROM differs in two of them, which are two blocks of that map; the page has the original 128's.
The joystick is a Kempston interface, or the keys.The arrow keys are a Kempston joystick, with Left Ctrl, Left Shift, Right Ctrl or Right Alt for its fire, and the pad under the screen is one too; every other key is the Spectrum's own, Left Ctrl and Left Shift as well.
The game has no mouse.The mouse works the menus. In play a click opens the first menu, as SPACE does. In a menu that is open, the pointer goes where the mouse is in its window, and a click there is fire. With the game paused from its menu, a click is fire, which carries on.
