Three maps of 36 by 36 squares, each drawn here from its scenario file: a green land of houses and rivers, a dungeon of rock and lava, and a sorcerer's hall of traps, teleports and walls you can walk through.

01 · Scenario 1

The Many Coloured Land: the portal can open on the map's edge

Up to four wizards start on the four pentacles. Three giant spiders roam between the corners, where four locked chests wait to be filled with treasure at the start of each game.

Choose a square.

Every square drawn from the scenario's map, things and graphics, the way the game draws its map window ($A94C). The creatures and objects are the ones the scenario places itself ($FF15). Each game also scatters objects at random over the open ground and into the chests, and shows a square's contents only once one of the player's creatures has seen it.

Each wizard takes one of the four pentacles, at rows and columns 9 and 26. The portal they leave by opens on turn 12 to 15, on one of four squares, three of them on the edges of the map, and stays open for 12 turns, so nobody knows in advance where to wait for it.

The corners hold the treasure. The start of each game fills each locked chest there with one to six objects drawn at random ($FE03). The giant spiders, which belong to nobody, patrol the corners: two walk route 1, round all four, and one walks route 8, from corner to corner.

In a game against the computer, TORQUEMADA's creatures each take one of the eleven routes that suits their kind. A scenario hook keeps them, and only them, out of water, tangle vine and fire ($E16A).

02 · Scenario 2

Slayer's Dungeon: opening the Slayer's chest wakes three groups of guards

The Slayer, worth 30 points, lies in a locked chest with a magic elixir. Its key is in another chest in the north, and opening the Slayer's chest wakes three groups of guards at once.

Choose a square.

Drawn the same way as scenario 1. Choose a trip square to see which creatures it wakes, or a creature to see the route it walks. A red waypoint is one that lies past the end of the scenario's table of waypoints.

Twelve of the fourteen independent creatures start out waiting, each in a numbered group (bit 6 of their orders, $D4E5). A trip square wakes a group when anything changes the square ($A716): a door unlocked, opened or closed, a chest opened, or its terrain changed by any other means. Eight of the eleven trip squares in the list are doors, with their creatures waiting behind them. The other three all name the Slayer's square, at row 16, column 33, so opening its chest wakes groups 1, 2 and 3 at once: the two spectres at the west end and the demon two rows above the chest.

Two pieces of the scenario's own code change the rules in the dungeon. One makes the dungeon things invisible again at the start of every turn ($E39E). The other makes the river of molten lava, which costs no more to walk into than the floor, take 7 points from a creature a player moves into it ($E1F0). The computer's creatures cross the lava unharmed, as Discoveries shows.

Three of the seven routes the computer's creatures choose from, routes 0, 2 and 6, name waypoint 28, but the table holds only waypoints 0 to 27. The game reads the two bytes after the table as the square ($9971), which gives row 8, column 7, a square of rock wall. A creature moves on to its next waypoint only when it stands on the one it is heading for ($9992), and nothing but a ghost can stand in rock, so a creature that takes one of these routes never gets past it. Discoveries has more.

03 · Scenario 3

Ragaril's Domain: every trap the scrolls hint at

One wizard plays against RAGARIL, and the portal opens late, on turn 44 to 51, in the middle of the hall at the centre. The scenario's own code adds teleports, trap floors, a floor that kills and an altar that takes a life.

Choose a square. A scroll shows its message.

Drawn the same way as scenario 1. The orange marks come from the scenario's own code: the table of teleports ($E3AD), the trap floors ($E324), and the floor and altar that kill ($E3C9). The walls marked are the ones a creature can walk through or break with ease.

The scrolls hold the scenario's hints. A wizard reads one with READ, a menu option only this scenario adds ($E2F8). "Offer a living sacrifice on the altar of Sondoman": a creature that starts its turn on the altar, at row 18, column 9, dies, and four walls in row 16 become locked doors ($E3C9). The last of them is a trip square, and wakes the spectre waiting beside it. "When is a wall not a wall?": five squares look just like the walls round them and are named WALL, but cost 4 action points to cross, the same as the floor.

Several squares look like floor and are something else. A creature that steps onto one of the three trap floors turns the eight squares round it into tangle vine, water or green fire. Three floor squares side by side in row 11 are teleports to row 5, column 4. The floor at rows 23 to 25 kills any creature that starts its turn on it.

The four teleport pads at the corners of the hall in the middle each lead to a square of its own. A teleport costs no action points, but walking onto one of those squares costs 254.

Opening the locked chest at row 5, column 30, which holds the scroll about the green flame, wakes three zombies.
