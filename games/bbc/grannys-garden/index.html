1983 · 4Mation Educational Resources

Granny's Garden

A school adventure in two BBC BASIC programs, every picture typed into PRINT statements as teletext codes. Its rooms are entered and never left, so after enough wandering BASIC stops with an error, and the game quietly starts again.

01 · The game

The game

A wicked witch has locked away the King and Queen of the Mountains and hidden their six children, and the player, helped by the King and Queen's blue raven, has to find them.

The title. Every picture in the game is Mode 7 teletext: letters, and characters of six blocks, in seven colours.

The woodcutter's house, where Esther is hidden. The secret word is on the wall.

The player starts in Granny's garden, finds the magic tree that leads to the Kingdom of the Mountains, and is asked a question at every turn: which way to go, what to take, which creature to call. Answers are typed. Many wrong choices bring the Witch, who sends the player home to start again.

The game is two programs on one disc. The first finds Esther in the woodcutter's house and Tom in the Giant's garden; the second finds Clare and Anna in the City of the Dragons, and Jessica and Daniel in the Land of Mystery. Each search but the last ends with a password, and the password, typed into the next run, is the only thing that carries a player on from one search to the next.

02 · The theme

Music: the theme's top line runs ahead of the tune

The theme plays each note on three channels at once, in octaves. The top channel is told to drop whatever it is playing and start the new note at once, so it races ahead of the other two.

A recording of every byte the machine sent the sound chip while each tune played, replayed through the page's model of the SN76489. Each bar on the roll is a note: blue is SOUND 3, orange SOUND 2, red SOUND 1. Under the player are the theme's 103 steps as the program reads them from its DATA at $6578, a pitch and a length; the step sounding on channel 2 lights up as it plays. The lowest note is 295 Hz, which a phone's speaker plays.

PROCsnd1 reads a pitch n and, unless n is 0, a length t. Each step is three SOUND statements: channel 2 at the pitch (n+5)×4, channel 3 an octave higher, both at full volume, and channel 1 two octaves higher and a little softer. A step with n = 0 is a rest of no length at all, which leaves a gap of about 10 ms between notes. The length is divided by 1.5, and SOUND drops the fraction, so a length of 4 plays for 2 twentieths of a second, 9 for 6 and 14 for 9.

Channel 1's notes are given as SOUND &00011. The channel number is read as hex digits, and the second from the right, here a 1, tells the operating system to flush the channel: drop the notes waiting and play this one now. BASIC waits only when a channel's queue is full, five notes behind the one playing, and channel 1's never fills. So while channels 2 and 3 play from their queues, channel 1 plays each note the moment its SOUND statement runs, up to 1.2 seconds early, cut off by the next. The orange and blue bars on the roll are in step with each other; the red ones lead them, and the first four are cut to 20 or 30 ms while BASIC fills the other queues. Channel 1's last note ends a second before the tune does.

The kingdom's tune, fifteen random notes, has the opposite trouble. Its channel 1 is SOUND &00111, and the third digit asks for each note to wait for a note on another channel that also asks to wait. None does, so channel 1 waits for good, and only two parts of that tune are ever heard. In the recording channel 1's volume register is never written with anything but silence. Channel 2 is flushed, as the theme's channel 1 is, and ends five seconds before channel 3.

The third button plays the Witch: three rounds of five notes on channel 1, all at the same pitch, with ENVELOPE 1 redefined before each note with larger steps, and a note on channel 2 after each round. In the first round BASIC queues the five notes in a few hundredths of a second (in the later rounds the last few wait for room in the queue), and the operating system reads an envelope while its note is playing, so the first note's steps grow as the definitions change under it, and the notes after it play whichever definition is in force while they play, for most of them a round's last, the same warble.

03 · A screen, rebuilt

The City of the Dragons, drawn from its 1,000 bytes

A Mode 7 screen is one byte a character: 40 by 25, about a kilobyte, and the teletext chip draws the colours from codes among the characters.

Point at a cell to see its byte.

The emulator's picture of the same moment.

Left, the page's own drawing of the 1,000 bytes jsbeeb showed at this moment, with BBC.drawTeletext from the site's library. The block graphics are drawn exactly, as checked against jsbeeb on 3,000 cells; the letters are in the page's font, as the teletext chip's own letters are in its ROM. The screen had scrolled two rows when this was taken, so its first byte is at &7C50.

Nothing on this screen was drawn with a graphics command. Each row is a PRINT of a string, and starts with two codes: a colour, then 157, New Background, which makes that colour the background for the rest of the row, cyan for the sky's three rows and white for the snow below them. The rest of the row is characters from 160 to 255, each drawn as six blocks, two across and three down, in the graphics colour last set. Each change of colour takes a cell of its own, which shows as the background, so the castle, the river and the pagoda are drawn around the cells their colours need. The black stripe down the left is the first code of every row. Ticking the box shows each control code where it sits.

04 · The magic trees

Only six of the twelve trees can be magic

The first question in the game is which of twelve trees is magic. The program picks it with two random numbers, and half of the trees can never be the one.

Left, the trees as jsbeeb showed them, redrawn by the page; the six that can be magic are outlined. Right, one tree printed from the 59 numbers of the DATA at $4C26, read from the listing.

Line 290 picks the magic tree's column as RND(3)×9+4 and its row as RND(2)×6. RND(3) is 1, 2 or 3, which gives the columns b, c and d; RND(2) is 1 or 2, the middle and bottom rows. Column a and the top row are never picked.

All twelve trees are the same 59 numbers. Each is added to 160 to make a character: most are graphics, and −150 and −152 become 10 and 8, the codes that move the cursor down a line and back a character, so one string draws the whole tree when it is printed.

05 · Ready to start?

Secret: answer T before Y, and the magic tree is always a1

The game's first question, Ready to start?, takes one more answer than yes. Type T, then Y, and the magic tree is the top tree on the left, one the game never picks otherwise.

Line 220 asks the question until the answer begins with Y, and line 230, in the same loop, sets a flag, t%, when it begins with T. The question comes back with nothing said. Line 290 then picks the tree as usual and, if t% is set, puts it at column 4, row 12: a1. The second button above, Pick it after T, picks it that way. The flag is cleared each time the program starts again, so it holds for one game.

The flag has a variable of its own and a test of its own, so it was put there on purpose. Nothing on the screen mentions it, and neither does any review or guide to the game found online. The teacher's booklet that came with the game, which has not been found, may say what it was for.

06 · Typing an answer

Most answers are recognised by a few letters inside them

The game seldom compares an answer with a whole word. It looks for a short piece of one, so Stars reaches the stairs and Riverbank is a password.

Click the black box and type. This is PROCi, the game's own input routine (Part 1 line 1020, $24EC), ported to the page key for key; the box is as wide as the field on the screen. Checked against the game in jsbeeb on 35 sequences of letters, DELETEs and other keys, 236 keys in all: after every key the port's answer, count and cursor matched the game's own in$, l and x, and its field matched the screen.

Every answer goes through PROCi. PROCi takes letters, DELETE and RETURN and ignores any other key, shows the first letter as a capital and the rest in lower case, and builds the answer the same way. Then the program looks for pieces of words in the answer with INSTR: no, ky and iv for snow, sky and river, itch, ack, upb and rs for the four doors of the hallway. The pieces never include a first letter, so the capital never gets in their way.

A field keeps one letter fewer than it shows. When the cursor reaches the end, PROCi takes the next key as DELETE, whatever it is, RETURN included. At Ready to start? the field shows four letters, and an answer ending in f starts the game without music. Yf does; Yesf loses its f to the RETURN, and a second RETURN starts the game with the music on.

07 · DELETE

Bug: one DELETE too many and the answer no longer matches the screen

A DELETE pressed when the answer is empty still counts, and PROCi's count of letters falls one behind the screen. From then on each DELETE takes a letter too many off the answer, or none at all.

Keys at Ready to start?On the screenThe answerWhat happens
X DELETE Y RETURNYYthe game starts
DELETE X DELETE Y RETURNYXYReady to start? again
DELETE s n o x DELETE wSnowSnw

Typed in jsbeeb on 14 October 2026 at the first question of Part 1, the answer read from the game's variable in$. The button above the black box types the second row.

PROCi keeps the number of letters in l, and a DELETE does l=l−1 and in$=LEFT$(in$,l). A DELETE in an empty field still counts, so l becomes −1, and stays one less than the letters on the screen. Each later DELETE cuts the answer to l letters, one fewer than the screen keeps; when l is below zero it cuts nothing, because BBC BASIC's LEFT$ with a negative length gives back the whole string (tried on its own: LEFT$("AB",-1) is AB). A child who presses DELETE once too often and then corrects a mistake sees the right word on the screen while the game reads another: Snow, typed that way at the password, is Snw, which holds no no.

08 · The baby dragons

Taming the baby dragons

Four baby dragons guard Clare in their cave. Each likes some foods and hates others, and the player throws food until each one has come out alone and been given a magic collar.

The game's own rule (Part 2 lines 1110-1230, $2E44) and its own words: the foods' effects come from the DATA at $32F8 and the sentences from $3334, read from the listing. The dragons are cut from the game's picture of all four, and a dragon in the cave is drawn faint. Checked against the game in jsbeeb on 30 games of random throws, 208 in all: before every throw the port's counters and its c() and e() matched the game's, and each game ended the same way, in the Witch, four collars or neither.

A throw adds the food's effect to each dragon, and keeps each dragon between in (0) and out (1). The table is in line 1270: buns bring the red dragon out and send the yellow one in, lollies the green out and the red in, oranges the blue out and the green in, chips the yellow out and the blue in. When exactly one dragon is out and has no collar, it gets one. A fourth throw of any food brings the Witch, so every food can be thrown three times.

Buns, lollies, oranges and chips, in that order, collar all four in four throws, and so does any order that keeps that round, such as chips, buns, lollies and oranges. The sentences come from a table of their own, in which each dragon loves, hates or sometimes eats each food, and they match the effects.

09 · The Land of Mystery

Every road is known by the sum of its two ends

The Land of Mystery is five places and the roads between them. What happens on a road depends only on the sum of the numbers of the two places at its ends.

SumRoadsOn the road

PROCw, Part 2 lines 1640-1750 ($3F11). The places are numbered forest 1, cottage 2, lake 3, castle 4 and hill 5.

PROCw adds the number of the place the player is at to the number of the place they ask for. A sum of 6 is a road that does not exist, and 5 brings the Witch; 4, 8 and 9 meet the dwarf, the troll and the beast. The player starts at 10, which no place is, and the program allows only the forest and the cottage from there. The map's buttons make journeys by the same rule.

Each journey is an ON np GOSUB to the place, and no place ever returns from it: it calls PROCw again for the next journey. That is the second of the ways the game starts itself again (section 12).

10 · The Giant's garden

Bug: call the bee in the wood and Tom can never be found

In the Giant's garden each creature helps at one stage of the way to Tom. The bee is the only one that can get past the Giant's dog, and if it was called in the wood before, it will not.

The stages are in Part 1 lines 2840-3060 ($5CAB). The route that calls the bee in the wood was played to the dog in jsbeeb.

Calling the bee in the wood sets be to 1 for the rest of the run. At the dog, only an unhurt bee chases the dog away, and every other creature brings the Witch, so a player who tried the bee in the wood can no longer reach Tom. The bee says it must go to the doctor, and the toadstool says You will have to try again., as it does after any creature that cannot help in the wood. Nothing says that the bee is needed later, and the dog is two stages on.

12 · The giant

Bug: answer the giant "maybe" and the hill turns into the forest

The giant on the hill asks Shall I eat you?

After Maybe, then Y: the forest's words under the hill's picture. Played in jsbeeb on 14 October 2026.

PROCgiant asks again when the answer is neither yes nor no, by calling itself. When the inner call ends at Y, it returns to the end of the outer call's last line, line 2090, and BASIC carries on to line 2100. That line is DEF PROCfor, the forest, and BASIC passes over a DEF it meets on the way as though it were a REM, so it runs the forest's lines inside PROCgiant: the words about the creepy forest appear under the hill, no stone is offered, and the map then says the player is at the hill.

13 · Starting again

The 20th visit to the hallway starts the game again

The rooms of the house and the places on the map are entered and never left. Each visit leaves something open on BASIC's stack, and after enough of them BASIC stops with an error, which the program turns into a fresh start.

REPEATs open
GOSUBs open

The counts at each visit and each journey, read from BASIC's own counters (&24 for REPEATs, &25 for GOSUBs) in jsbeeb, on routes that go in and out of the backroom and back and forth across the map.

The hallway is a REPEAT loop that asks Where do you wish to go?, and every room ends by calling the hallway again, so a REPEAT is left open each time. BBC BASIC allows 20 at once. At the 20th visit the hallway's twenty are open, and the REPEAT in the input routine is one too many. On the map each journey is a GOSUB that never returns, and BASIC allows 26. Both programs start with ON ERROR RUN, so the 20th visit to the hallway and the 27th journey stop with Too many REPEATs or Too many GOSUBs, and RUN starts the program from the top, back at Ready to start?, with no message. Hearing the lake's name leaves one GOSUB more open, so after it the 26th journey is the last. The stairs leave two GOSUBs open each trip once the snake is dead, so a player who keeps climbing them meets the GOSUB limit first, at the 15th visit to the hallway. A guess at the trees that the game rejects for its form, a letter alone say, leaves the trees' own REPEAT open for the rest of the game, because lines 440-450 run on into the kingdom, and then the 19th visit is the last.

14 · Memory

Bug: on this disc, Part 1 runs out of memory before Tom

Each program was written to move itself down to &0E00 when it is loaded higher, as a disc system's workspace makes it. On this disc that test is turned off, and Part 1, left at &1900, stops with No room before the end.

The bytes free between BASIC's variables and its stack at each of the game's waits, in jsbeeb, on the way to Tom. At &0E00 the run reaches Tom with about 2,200 bytes to spare. Loaded at &1900, as the disc puts it, the same answers stop at the pond with No room at line 2670.

The first line of each program was a test, IF PAGE<>&E00 GOTO 3280 (2590 in Part 2). The lines it goes to select the tape filing system, copy the program down to &0E00 four bytes at a time and run it there, which gives it 2,816 bytes more. The second line then writes a REM over the first, so that the program, running at &0E00, does not test again. Both files on this disc were saved after such a run: their first lines are REMs, and CHAIN runs each program where the disc system loads it, at &1900.

Part 1 has about 1,000 bytes there when it starts, and its variables and BASIC's stack need about 1,600 more as it goes. It stops at the pond, or on the stairs by the route through the house, and No room is the one error ON ERROR cannot catch, so the game ends at BASIC's prompt. Part 2 is smaller and runs to its end either way. Typing LOAD"GRAN1" and then GOTO3280 runs the program's own move and gets round it. Whether this is 4Mation's own disc is unknown; no other copy was compared.
